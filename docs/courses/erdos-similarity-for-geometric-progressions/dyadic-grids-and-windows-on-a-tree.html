Dyadic grids and windows on a tree

Written by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), October 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. Public domain (CC0).

The periodic hitting set of Proposition 3.1 of Measure-universal sets and periodic hitting sets is built from random tables indexed by the cells of finitely many grids. This lesson sets up the grids and the bookkeeping of the indices \(n\) of the points \(x+tq^n\) [OpenAI-ES, Section 3]. The indices are grouped into windows attached to the edges of a finite ordered tree, and two facts are proved. For all centers \(x\) outside a periodic set of small density, the points \(x+tq^n\) with \(n\) in a window lie in the same cells as \(x\) in the grids of all earlier windows. And within one window, the points \(x\) and \(x+tq^n\) lie in pairwise distinct cells of that window's grid, at every center.

  • Windows on an ordered tree (Section 2).
  • Stable centers (Section 3).
  • Separation within a window (Section 4).

Throughout, \(q\in(0,1)\) is fixed, \(m\) is Lebesgue measure, and \(\rho\) is the density of a measurable \(1\)-periodic set, as in Measure-universal sets and periodic hitting sets. We write \(\{z\}=z-\lfloor z\rfloor\) for the fractional part and put \[ c=\frac4{1-q}. \]

1. Dyadic grids and keys

For each integer \(b\ge1\) put \[ \begin{gathered}N_b=2^{\lceil\log_2(cq^{-b})\rceil},\qquad\Gamma_b=N_b^{-1}\mathbb Z,\\ J_b(z)=\bigl\lfloor N_b\{z\}\bigr\rfloor\in\{0,1,\ldots,N_b-1\}.\end{gathered} \] The number \(J_b(z)\) is the key of \(z\) at resolution \(b\). The points with key \(k\) form the cell \(\bigl[\frac k{N_b},\frac{k+1}{N_b}\bigr)+\mathbb Z\): cells are closed on the left and open on the right, so a point of \(\Gamma_b\) belongs to the cell on its right.

Lemma 1.1 (grids).

  1. \(cq^{-b}\le N_b<2cq^{-b}\).
  2. \(N_b\) is a power of two and nondecreasing in \(b\). If \(b'\ge b\), then \(N_{b'}/N_b\) is an integer and \(J_b(z)=\bigl\lfloor J_{b'}(z)\big/(N_{b'}/N_b)\bigr\rfloor\) for every \(z\): a finer key determines every coarser key.
  3. \(J_b\) is \(1\)-periodic. On a segment \([u,v]\) with \((u,v]\cap\Gamma_b=\varnothing\), \(J_b\) is constant.

Proof. (1) For \(y>0\), \(2^{\lceil\log_2y\rceil}\) is the least power of two that is at least \(y\), so it lies in \([y,2y)\). (2) \(cq^{-b}\) increases with \(b\). Put \(K=N_{b'}/N_b\), an integer power of two, and \(y=N_{b'}\{z\}\), so \(J_{b'}(z)=\lfloor y\rfloor\) and \(J_b(z)=\lfloor y/K\rfloor\). Write \(\lfloor y\rfloor=Kj+i\) with integers \(j\) and \(0\le i<K\). Then \(j\le y/K<(Kj+i+1)/K\le j+1\), so \(\lfloor y/K\rfloor=j=\lfloor\lfloor y\rfloor/K\rfloor\). (3) Periodicity is clear, and \(J_b(z)=\lfloor N_bz\rfloor-N_b\lfloor z\rfloor\) because \(N_b\lfloor z\rfloor\) is an integer. Let \(u\le w\le v\). An integer \(k\) with \(N_bu<k\le N_bw\) would give the point \(k/N_b\) of \(\Gamma_b\) in \((u,v]\); so \(\lfloor N_bw\rfloor=\lfloor N_bu\rfloor\). Likewise \(\lfloor w\rfloor=\lfloor u\rfloor\), since the integers belong to \(\Gamma_b\). Hence \(J_b(w)=J_b(u)\). \(\square\)

So each cell of the grid \(\Gamma_b\) has width \(1/N_b\le q^b/c=\frac{(1-q)q^b}4\). The grids are dyadic even when \(q\) is not, so that coarser keys are functions of finer ones; their resolutions follow \(q^{-b}\).

2. Windows on an ordered tree

Fix integers \(M\ge2\), \(d\ge1\), \(g\ge1\) and \(r_0\ge1\). Take the complete ordered \(M\)-ary tree of height \(d\): every vertex of height \(h\ge1\) has \(M\) children \(P_1,\ldots,P_M\), in this order, of height \(h-1\), and the leaves have height \(0\). Write \((P,i)\) for the edge from \(P\) to \(P_i\); there are \[ K=M+M^2+\cdots+M^d \] edges. The preorder of the edges lists, for \(i=1,\ldots,M\) in turn, the edge \((P,i)\) followed by all edges of the subtree rooted at \(P_i\) in preorder, starting at the root. So an edge and the subtree below it form a consecutive block.

Put \(r_1=r_0\), \(\sigma_1=Mr_1+(M-1)g\), and for \(2\le h\le d\) \[ \begin{gathered}r_h=\max\{r_0,\ g+\sigma_{h-1}\},\\ \sigma_h=M\bigl(r_h+g+\sigma_{h-1}\bigr)+(M-1)g .\end{gathered} \] Choose an integer \(n_0\ge1\) with \(2q^{n_0}\le\frac14\). Now assign to the edges, in preorder, consecutive sets of integers \(W_e=\{a_e,a_e+1,\ldots,b_e\}\), the windows: the first window starts at \(n_0\), an edge leaving a vertex of height \(h\) gets a window of exactly \(r_h\) integers, and between consecutive windows exactly \(g\) integers are left unused. Put \[ \mathcal N=\bigcup_eW_e , \] and for an edge \(e=(P,i)\) let \(b_e^*\) be the largest endpoint among \(W_e\) and the windows of the edges below \(P_i\). The grid of the edge \(e\) is \(\Gamma_{b_e}\).

Lemma 2.1 (spans; [OpenAI-ES, Lemma 3.1]). For a vertex \(P\) of height \(h\ge1\), the windows of all edges below \(P\), together with the gaps between them, occupy exactly \(\sigma_h\) consecutive integers. For an edge \(e=(P,i)\) leaving a vertex of height \(h\), \[ b_e^*-a_e+1\le2r_h . \tag{2.1} \]

Proof. By induction on \(h\). If \(h=1\), the edges below \(P\) are \((P,1),\ldots,(P,M)\), consecutive in preorder, with windows of length \(r_1\) and \(M-1\) gaps between them: \(\sigma_1\) integers. If \(h\ge2\), preorder lists the blocks \((P,i)\), subtree of \(P_i\), for \(i=1,\ldots,M\). A block consists of a window of length \(r_h\), a gap, and the windows below \(P_i\), which occupy \(\sigma_{h-1}\) integers by induction; consecutive blocks are separated by a gap. This gives \(M(r_h+g+\sigma_{h-1})+(M-1)g=\sigma_h\).

For (2.1): \(b_e^*-a_e+1\) is the number of integers occupied by the block of \(e\). If \(h=1\), \(P_i\) is a leaf and the block is \(W_e\), of length \(r_1\). If \(h\ge2\), the block has length \(r_h+g+\sigma_{h-1}\le2r_h\) by the choice of \(r_h\). \(\square\)

So the finest grid used anywhere in the block of an edge has index at most \(2r_h-1\) beyond the start of its window. This is what keeps the number of grid boundaries under control in Every scale and every center; the gaps serve the next lemma.

3. Stable centers

Definition 3.1. A point \(x\in\mathbb R\) is stable if for every window \(W_e\) other than the first, with \(b'\) the right endpoint of the window immediately before \(W_e\), \[ (x,\ x+2q^{a_e}]\cap\Gamma_{b'}=\varnothing . \] We write \(\mathcal S\) for the set of stable points.

The left endpoint \(x\) is excluded because a point of \(\Gamma_{b'}\) at \(x\) itself belongs to the cell on its right, which the points \(x+tq^n\) do not leave; the right endpoint is included because reaching the next point of \(\Gamma_{b'}\) does change the key.

Lemma 3.2 (stable centers keep earlier keys; [OpenAI-ES, Lemma 3.2]). The set \(\mathcal S\) is measurable and \(1\)-periodic, and \[ \rho(\mathbb R\setminus\mathcal S)\le4Kc\,q^{g+1}. \tag{3.1} \] If \(x\in\mathcal S\), \(n\in W_e\) and \(t\in[1,2]\), then every edge \(f\) before \(e\) in preorder satisfies \[ J_{b_f}(x+tq^n)=J_{b_f}(x). \tag{3.2} \]

Proof. For a window \(W_e\) other than the first, with \(b'\) as in Definition 3.1, the condition fails exactly when some \(\gamma\in\Gamma_{b'}\) satisfies \(x<\gamma\le x+2q^{a_e}\), that is, on the union of the intervals \([\gamma-2q^{a_e},\gamma)\), \(\gamma\in\Gamma_{b'}\). This union is \(V+\mathbb Z\), where \(V\) is the union of the \(N_{b'}\) intervals \([\gamma-2q^{a_e},\gamma)\) with \(\gamma\in\Gamma_{b'}\cap[0,1)\); it is \(1\)-periodic and measurable. For any measurable \(V\), translation invariance and countable subadditivity give \[ \begin{aligned}\rho(V+\mathbb Z)&\le\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}m\bigl((V+k)\cap[0,1)\bigr)\\&=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}m\bigl(V\cap[-k,1-k)\bigr)\\&=m(V).\end{aligned} \] So the density of the union is at most \[ 2N_{b'}q^{a_e}<4c\,q^{a_e-b'}=4c\,q^{g+1}, \] by Lemma 1.1(1) and \(a_e=b'+g+1\). There are at most \(K\) such windows, which proves (3.1); \(\mathcal S\) is the complement of a finite union of measurable periodic sets.

Let \(x\in\mathcal S\), \(n\in W_e\) and \(t\in[1,2]\). If \(e\) is the first edge, (3.2) is empty. Otherwise \(0<tq^n\le2q^{a_e}\), so \((x,x+tq^n]\) contains no point of \(\Gamma_{b'}\), and \(J_{b'}(x+tq^n)=J_{b'}(x)\) by Lemma 1.1(3). An edge \(f\) before \(e\) has its window before \(W_e\), so \(b_f\le b'\), and Lemma 1.1(2) turns the equality of the keys at resolution \(b'\) into (3.2). \(\square\)

4. Separation within a window

Lemma 4.1 (separation; [OpenAI-ES, Lemma 3.3]). Let \(e\) be an edge, \(x\in\mathbb R\) and \(t\in[1,2]\). The points \[ x\quad\text{and}\quad x+tq^n\ (n\in W_e) \] have pairwise distinct keys under \(J_b\) for every \(b\ge b_e\).

Proof. All these points lie in \([x,x+2q^{n_0}]\), an interval of length at most \(\frac14\), since every window consists of integers \(n\ge n_0\). The distance from \(x\) to \(x+tq^n\) is \(tq^n\ge q^{b_e}\). For \(n<n'\) in \(W_e\), \[ \begin{aligned}t(q^n-q^{n'})&=tq^n(1-q^{n'-n})\\&\ge(1-q)q^{b_e}.\end{aligned} \] If two points \(z,w\) have the same key at resolution \(b\), their fractional parts lie in one interval \(\bigl[\frac k{N_b},\frac{k+1}{N_b}\bigr)\), so \(|z-w-j|<1/N_b\) for the integer \(j=\lfloor z\rfloor-\lfloor w\rfloor\). If moreover \(|z-w|<\frac14\), then \(j=0\), since otherwise \(|z-w-j|\ge1-|z-w|>\frac34>1/N_b\); so \(|z-w|<1/N_b\). But by Lemma 1.1(1) \[ \begin{aligned}\frac1{N_b}&\le\frac1{N_{b_e}}\le\frac{q^{b_e}}c\\&=\frac{(1-q)q^{b_e}}4<(1-q)q^{b_e},\end{aligned} \] smaller than every distance between our points. \(\square\)

At a stable center, the random construction of Random routing and independent tests will therefore read the same earlier table entries at \(x\) and at \(x+tq^n\), while the points of one window read pairwise distinct entries of their own table.

5. Exercises

5.1. For \(q=\frac12\), compute \(c\), \(N_b\) and the width of the cells of \(\Gamma_b\).

5.2. Show that \(\lfloor\lfloor y\rfloor/K\rfloor=\lfloor y/K\rfloor\) can fail when \(K>0\) is not an integer.

5.3. Let \(M=2\), \(d=3\), \(g=1\) and \(r_0=1\), and take \(n_0=1\), which is allowed when \(q\le\frac18\). Compute \(r_1,r_2,r_3\) and \(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3\), list the windows in preorder, and check (2.1) for the two edges leaving the root.

5.4. Show that \(K=(M^{d+1}-M)/(M-1)\) and that the windows occupy \(\sigma_d\) consecutive integers in total.

5.5. Show that the bound (3.1) tends to \(0\) as \(g\to\infty\) with \(M\) and \(d\) fixed, but that the windows then become longer: show that \(r_h\ge g\) for \(h\ge2\).

6. Solutions

5.1. \(c=8\), \(cq^{-b}=2^{b+3}\) is a power of two, so \(N_b=2^{b+3}\) and the cells have width \(2^{-b-3}\).

5.2. For \(K=\frac12\) and \(y=\frac12\): \(\lfloor\lfloor y\rfloor/K\rfloor=0\) but \(\lfloor y/K\rfloor=1\).

5.3. \(r_1=1\), \(\sigma_1=3\); \(r_2=\max(1,1+3)=4\), \(\sigma_2=2(4+1+3)+1=17\); \(r_3=\max(1,1+17)=18\), \(\sigma_3=2(18+1+17)+1=73\). Write \(R\) for the root, \(A,B\) for its children and \(A_1,A_2,B_1,B_2\) for theirs. In preorder, with one unused integer between windows: \((R,1)\): \(1\)–\(18\); \((A,1)\): \(20\)–\(23\); \((A_1,1)\): \(25\); \((A_1,2)\): \(27\); \((A,2)\): \(29\)–\(32\); \((A_2,1)\): \(34\); \((A_2,2)\): \(36\); \((R,2)\): \(38\)–\(55\); \((B,1)\): \(57\)–\(60\); \((B_1,1)\): \(62\); \((B_1,2)\): \(64\); \((B,2)\): \(66\)–\(69\); \((B_2,1)\): \(71\); \((B_2,2)\): \(73\). The windows fill \(1\)–\(73\), that is \(\sigma_3\) integers. For \((R,1)\), \(b^*=36\) and \(b^*-a+1=36=2r_3\); for \((R,2)\), \(b^*=73\) and \(73-38+1=36=2r_3\).

5.4. There are \(M^j\) vertices of height \(d-j\), and each vertex other than the root has exactly one incoming edge; so \(K=\sum_{j=1}^dM^j=(M^{d+1}-M)/(M-1)\). The second claim is Lemma 2.1 for the root.

5.5. \(4Kcq^{g+1}\to0\) as \(g\to\infty\) because \(q<1\). For \(h\ge2\), \(r_h\ge g+\sigma_{h-1}>g\). Choosing \(g\) large makes most centers stable, at the price of longer windows above height one; the choice of parameters in Every scale and every center takes \(g\) after \(M\) and \(d\), and the window lengths do not enter the bound that \(g\) must satisfy.
