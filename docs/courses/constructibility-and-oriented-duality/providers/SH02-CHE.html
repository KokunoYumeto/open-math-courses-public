A restriction can create a singularity even when the covectors directly above the restricted submanifold do not predict it. Nearby covectors can become arbitrarily large while their tangential components remain finite. Normal deformation records this process. Applying the same construction to a graph gives inverse-image estimates, and applying it to a diagonal gives tensor and Hom estimates.

SH02-CHE-SETUP — Categories, maps, and the proof dependencies

Let kk be a commutative ring of finite global dimension. All manifolds are finite dimensional, Hausdorff, and countable at infinity; maps are smooth. The real analytic case is included. Unless a different range is expressly discussed, complexes belong to Db(kX)D^b(k_X). Their cohomology sheaves can be arbitrary. We assume neither a field of coefficients nor constructibility, perfect stalks, compact support, or orientability.

For f:Y→Xf:Y\to X write

Ef=Y×XT*X,fπ(y,ξ)=(f(y),ξ),fd(y,ξ)=(y,dfytξ),q:Ef→Y. E_f=Y\times_XT^*X,\qquad f_\pi(y,\xi)=(f(y),\xi),\qquad f_d(y,\xi)=(y,df_y^t\xi),\qquad q:E_f\to Y.

The graph lies in X×YX\times Y, in that order. Its conormal is identified with EfE_f by (y,ξ)↦(f(y),y;ξ,−dfytξ)(y,\xi)\mapsto(f(y),y;\xi,-df_y^t\xi). The relative orientation complex is

ωf=ωY⊗f−1ωX⊗−1,ωX=or⁡X[dim⁡X]. \omega_f=\omega_Y\otimes f^{-1}\omega_X^{\otimes-1}, \qquad \omega_X=\operatorname{or}_X[\dim X].

The comparison ϑf:f−1F⊗ωf→f!F\vartheta_f:f^{-1}F\otimes\omega_f\to f^!F is the mate of the relative trace under Rf!⊣f!Rf_!\dashv f^!. This fixes its direction and normalization. A morphism is a microlocal isomorphism on VV if the microsupport of its cone misses VV.

SH02-CHE-OPERATIONS — Finite limits and escape directions

Let A⊂T*XA\subset T^*X and B⊂T*YB\subset T^*Y be closed conic sets. Their graph normal cone is

Cf(A,B)=CTΓf*(X×Y)(A×Ba),(CHE1) C_f(A,B)=C_{T^*_{\Gamma_f}(X\times Y)}(A\times B^a), \qquad\text{(CHE1)}

viewed as a subset of T*EfT^*E_f through the normal-cone and Fourier identifications. This is the operation denoted CμC_\mu in the asymptotic-estimates lesson. For a vector-bundle projection q:Ef→Yq:E_f\to Y, let qπ:Ef×YT*Y→T*Yq_\pi:E_f\times_YT^*Y\to T^*Y be projection and qd:Ef×YT*Y→T*Efq_d:E_f\times_YT^*Y\to T^*E_f the transpose differential. Define

f#(A,B)=qπqd−1Cf(A,B),f∞#(A,B)=q̇πq̇d−1Cf(A,B).(CHE2) f^\#(A,B)=q_\pi q_d^{-1}C_f(A,B), \qquad f^\#_\infty(A,B)=\dot q_\pi\dot q_d^{-1}C_f(A,B). \qquad\text{(CHE2)}

Dots remove the zero section of EfE_f. The biconic normal-cone geometry also identifies the first set with T*Y∩Cf(A,B)T^*Y\cap C_f(A,B). Put f#A=f#(A,TY*Y)f^\#A=f^\#(A,T^*_YY) and similarly for f∞#Af^\#_\infty A.

In local coordinates, (y0;η0)(y_0;\eta_0) belongs to f#(A,B)f^\#(A,B) precisely when there are (xn;ξn)∈A(x_n;\xi_n)\in A and (yn;ηn)∈B(y_n;\eta_n)\in B with

yn→y0,xn→f(y0),dfyntξn−ηn→η0,|xn−f(yn)||ξn|→0.(CHE3) y_n\to y_0,\quad x_n\to f(y_0),\quad df_{y_n}^t\xi_n-\eta_n\to\eta_0,\quad |x_n-f(y_n)|\,|\xi_n|\to0. \qquad\text{(CHE3)}

For f∞#(A,B)f^\#_\infty(A,B) require in addition |ξn|→∞|\xi_n|\to\infty. The norm can be any coordinate norm. The last condition controls the error from replacing a nearby point by a point on the graph. It must not be omitted.

For A,B⊂T*XA,B\subset T^*X put

A+̂B=(1X)#(A,Ba),A+̂∞B=(1X)∞#(A,Ba).(CHE4) A\widehat+ B=(1_X)^\#(A,B^a),\qquad A\widehat+_\infty B=(1_X)^\#_\infty(A,B^a). \qquad\text{(CHE4)}

Thus the sum uses ξn+ηn\xi_n+\eta_n, and permits different base points whose distance multiplied by |ξn||\xi_n| tends to zero. Ordinary fibrewise addition uses the same base point and finite covectors. For closed conic AA,

f#A=fdfπ−1A∪f∞#A.(CHE5) f^\#A=f_df_\pi^{-1}A\ \cup\ f^\#_\infty A. \qquad\text{(CHE5)}

Indeed, a sequence in (CHE3) with BB the zero section has either a bounded subsequence of ξn\xi_n, producing the ordinary image by closedness, or a subsequence with norm tending to infinity. Conversely constant sequences give every point of the ordinary image. The same argument gives A+̂B=(A+B)∪(A+̂∞B)A\widehat+B=(A+B)\cup(A\widehat+_\infty B).

We say ff is noncharacteristic for AA on V⊂T*YV\subset T^*Y when

f∞#A∩V=∅.(CHE6) f^\#_\infty A\cap V=\varnothing. \qquad\text{(CHE6)}

The condition makes sense for an arbitrary subset VV, not just an open or conic subset. It is a condition on escape directions over nearby points, and is stronger than properness of the ordinary cotangent correspondence over an open VV.

SH02-CHE-001 — What specialization can contribute

Let M↪XM\hookrightarrow X be a closed submanifold and F∈Db(kX)F\in D^b(k_X). There are canonical identifications

T*NMX≃T*NM*X≃NNM*X(T*X).(CHE7) T^*N_MX\ \simeq\ T^*N_M^*X\ \simeq\ N_{N_M^*X}(T^*X). \qquad\text{(CHE7)}

Under these identifications,

SS⁡(νMF)⊂CNM*X(SS⁡(F)),SS⁡(μMF)=SS⁡(νMF).(CHE8) \operatorname{SS}(\nu_MF) \subset C_{N_M^*X}(\operatorname{SS}(F)), \qquad \operatorname{SS}(\mu_MF)=\operatorname{SS}(\nu_MF). \qquad\text{(CHE8)}

The equality means transport by (CHE7), not literal equality of subsets of two different cotangent bundles.

Proof. Use adapted coordinates (u,z)(u,z) with M={u=0}M=\{u=0\}. The deformation has coordinates (v,z,t)(v,z,t) and p(v,z,t)=(tv,z)p(v,z,t)=(tv,z). Its central fibre is NMXN_MX, and p+p_+ is a submersion on t>0t>0. Write (α,β,τ)(\alpha,\beta,\tau) for the covectors dual to (v,z,t)(v,z,t). The transpose differential gives

(α,β,τ)=(tξ,ζ,⟨v,ξ⟩).(CHE9) (\alpha,\beta,\tau) =(t\xi,\zeta,\langle v,\xi\rangle). \qquad\text{(CHE9)}

Hence every point of SS⁡(p+−1F)\operatorname{SS}(p_+^{-1}F) has

(tv,z;t−1α,β)∈SS⁡(F),tτ=⟨v,α⟩.(CHE10) (tv,z;t^{-1}\alpha,\beta)\in\operatorname{SS}(F), \qquad t\tau=\langle v,\alpha\rangle. \qquad\text{(CHE10)}

Apply the boundary contract to s−1Rj*p+−1Fs^{-1}Rj_*p_+^{-1}F. If (v0,z0;α0,β0)(v_0,z_0;\alpha_0,\beta_0) belongs to its microsupport, that contract and the normal-cone sequence criterion yield points satisfying (CHE10) with

tn>0,tn→0,(vn,zn;αn,βn)→(v0,z0;α0,β0),tn|τn|→0.(CHE11) t_n>0,\quad t_n\to0,\quad (v_n,z_n;\alpha_n,\beta_n)\to(v_0,z_0;\alpha_0,\beta_0), \qquad t_n|\tau_n|\to0. \qquad\text{(CHE11)}

This use of the boundary theorem is legitimate even though only the positive chamber occurs: extend the sheaf by zero to the other component of the complement of t=0t=0. No point from the empty negative component can enter the sequence.

Conicity allows multiplication of the covector in (CHE10) by tnt_n, so

(tnvn,zn;αn,tnβn)∈SS⁡(F).(CHE12) (t_nv_n,z_n;\alpha_n,t_n\beta_n)\in\operatorname{SS}(F). \qquad\text{(CHE12)}

The conormal bundle NM*XN_M^*X consists of (0,z;α,0)(0,z;\alpha,0). Relative to the point (0,zn;αn,0)(0,z_n;\alpha_n,0) on this bundle, the normal displacement of (CHE12), divided by tnt_n, is (vn,βn)(v_n,\beta_n). Its base tends to (z0,α0)(z_0,\alpha_0). This is exactly membership in the normal cone on the right of (CHE8).

For clarity, the identifications in these coordinates are

(v,z;α,β)↔(α,z;−v,β)↔((0,z;α,0);v,β).(CHE13) (v,z;\alpha,\beta) \longleftrightarrow(\alpha,z;-v,\beta) \longleftrightarrow \bigl((0,z;\alpha,0);\,v,\beta\bigr). \qquad\text{(CHE13)}

The minus sign is in the cotangent component dual to the conormal-fibre coordinate. The second assertion of (CHE8) is now the Fourier microsupport transport theorem applied to the bounded conic object νMF\nu_MF. This finishes both deductions. The construction is local, so a locally closed submanifold is handled in an open ambient neighborhood where it is closed. ▫\square

There is useful extra information in the argument. Equations (CHE10)–(CHE11) imply ⟨v0,α0⟩=0\langle v_0,\alpha_0\rangle=0. Thus the microsupport of the specialized object annihilates the radial vector field of NMXN_MX. This agrees with conicity and also checks that the positive deformation parameter did not introduce a spurious radial covector.

SH02-CHE-002 — A single triangle controls a comparison defect

Let HH be a bounded conic sheaf on a vector bundle q:E→Yq:E\to Y. Let C⊂T*EC\subset T^*E be a closed biconic set containing SS⁡(H)\operatorname{SS}(H). Write a:Y↪Ea:Y\hookrightarrow E for the zero section and j:Ė↪Ej:\dot E\hookrightarrow E for its complement. Then

Rq!H⟶Rq*H⟶Rq̇*(H|Ė)→+1(CHE14) Rq_!H\longrightarrow Rq_*H\longrightarrow R\dot q_*(H|_{\dot E})\xrightarrow{+1} \qquad\text{(CHE14)}

is the conic recovery triangle, and

SS⁡(Rq!H),SS⁡(Rq*H)⊂T*Y∩C,SS⁡(Cone⁡(Rq!H→Rq*H))⊂q̇πq̇d−1C.(CHE15) \begin{aligned} \operatorname{SS}(Rq_!H),\ \operatorname{SS}(Rq_*H) &\subset T^*Y\cap C,\\ \operatorname{SS}\bigl(\operatorname{Cone}(Rq_!H\to Rq_*H)\bigr) &\subset\dot q_\pi\dot q_d^{-1}C. \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(CHE15)}

Proof. Apply Rq*Rq_* to a*a!H→H→Rj*j−1H→+1a_*a^!H\to H\to Rj_*j^{-1}H\xrightarrow{+1}. The left term is a!Ha^!H, which the proper conic contraction identifies with Rq!HRq_!H. Under this identification the map is the canonical comparison from proper to ordinary direct image. Composition of ordinary direct images identifies the third term with Rq̇*j−1HR\dot q_*j^{-1}H. This proves (CHE14) with its map, not merely with the three objects. Apply the full and punctured conic projection estimates to the three terms and enlarge SS⁡(H)\operatorname{SS}(H) to CC. These are precisely the inclusions in (CHE15). ▫\square

Consequently the comparison is a microlocal isomorphism wherever the punctured image of CC is absent. There is no claim that qq is proper: the punctured term measures what ordinary direct image retains in the noncompact radial direction.

SH02-CHE-003 — Hom along a graph

For F∈Db(kX)F\in D^b(k_X) and G∈Db(kY)G\in D^b(k_Y), set A=SS⁡(F)A=\operatorname{SS}(F) and B=SS⁡(G)B=\operatorname{SS}(G). With p:X×Y→Xp:X\times Y\to X, r:X×Y→Yr:X\times Y\to Y, define

ℋf+(G,F)=μΓfRℋom(r−1G,p!F),ℋf−(F,G)=aE−1μΓfRℋom(p−1F,r!G),(CHE16) \begin{aligned} \mathcal H_f^+(G,F)&=\mu_{\Gamma_f} R\mathcal Hom(r^{-1}G,p^!F),\\ \mathcal H_f^-(F,G)&=a_E^{-1}\mu_{\Gamma_f} R\mathcal Hom(p^{-1}F,r^!G), \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(CHE16)}

where aE(y,ξ)=(y,−ξ)a_E(y,\xi)=(y,-\xi). These are sheaves on EfE_f. Let b:T*Ef→T*Efb:T^*E_f\to T^*E_f be the cotangent-fibre antipodal map, b(e;θ)=(e;−θ)b(e;\theta)=(e;-\theta). Then

SS⁡(ℋf+(G,F))⊂Cf(A,B),SS⁡(ℋf−(F,G))⊂b(Cf(A,B)).(CHE17) \operatorname{SS}(\mathcal H_f^+(G,F))\subset C_f(A,B), \qquad \operatorname{SS}(\mathcal H_f^-(F,G))\subset b(C_f(A,B)). \qquad\text{(CHE17)}

If GG is cohomologically constructible, the natural map

DY′G⊗f−1F⊗ωf⟶Rℋom(G,f!F)(CHE18) D'_YG\otimes f^{-1}F\otimes\omega_f \longrightarrow R\mathcal Hom(G,f^!F) \qquad\text{(CHE18)}

is a microlocal isomorphism on T*Y\f∞#(A,B)T^*Y\setminus f^\#_\infty(A,B).

Proof. The external Hom estimate puts the microsupport of the first kernel in A×BaA\times B^a. Apply SH02-CHE-001 with submanifold Γf\Gamma_f to obtain the first inclusion. The second kernel instead has microsupport in Aa×BA^a\times B. This is the image of A×BaA\times B^a under the antipodal map of T*(X×Y)T^*(X\times Y). Its induced map on the normal-cone cotangent model is the cotangent lift of aEa_E, followed by bb. One can verify this in (CHE13): negating an ambient covector negates α\alpha and β\beta, keeps vv unchanged, and therefore sends (α,z;−v,β)(\alpha,z;-v,\beta) to (−α,z;−v,−β)(-\alpha,z;-v,-\beta). SH02-CHE-PROPER — What the local hypothesis implies about properness

Suppose A⊂T*XA\subset T^*X is closed conic, V⊂T*YV\subset T^*Y is open, and f∞#A∩V=∅f^\#_\infty A\cap V=\varnothing. Then

fd:fπ−1A∩fd−1V⟶V(CHE26) f_d:f_\pi^{-1}A\cap f_d^{-1}V\longrightarrow V \qquad\text{(CHE26)}

is proper.

Proof. Fix compact K⊂VK\subset V and consider a sequence (yn,ξn)(y_n,\xi_n) in the inverse image of KK. The points yny_n have a convergent subsequence, because they lie over the compact projection of KK. A finite collection of bundle charts over that compact set reduces compactness to a bound on |ξn||\xi_n|. If no bound existed, pass to a subsequence on which the norms tend to infinity and fd(yn,ξn)f_d(y_n,\xi_n) converges to a point of KK. In (CHE3) take xn=f(yn)x_n=f(y_n) and take the zero covector as the second input. The distance error is identically zero. This produces a point of f∞#A∩Kf^\#_\infty A\cap K, a contradiction. The fibre coordinates are therefore bounded. The inverse image of KK is closed, since AA and KK are closed, so it is compact in a finite bundle-chart cover. This is properness. ▫\square

The converse fails. SH02-CHE-EXAMPLES computes a case where (CHE26) has empty domain, hence is proper, but a tangential singularity is created by inverse image.

SH02-CHE-DIAGONAL — Why a diagonal produces the asymptotic sum

Let A,B⊂T*XA,B\subset T^*X be closed conic and let δ:X→X×X\delta:X\to X\times X be the diagonal. Then

δ#(A×B)=A+̂B.(CHE27) \delta^\#(A\times B)=A\widehat+B. \qquad\text{(CHE27)}

Proof. The sequence criterion for the left side uses points xn,yn,zn→x0x_n,y_n,z_n\to x_0 and covectors (ξn,ηn)(\xi_n,\eta_n) with

SH02-CHE-006 — Tensor and internal Hom without a transversality assumption

For F,G∈Db(kX)F,G\in D^b(k_X),

Proof. The exact inverse image of the diagonal gives F⊗LG=δ−1(F⊠LG)F\otimes^LG=\delta^{-1}(F\boxtimes^LG). Apply the external tensor bound, then (CHE22), then (CHE27). All three operations on conic sets are monotone, so enlarging the external microsupport preserves the inclusion.

For Hom use

Rℋom(G,F)≃δ!Rℋom(q2−1G,q1!F).(CHE30) R\mathcal Hom(G,F) \simeq\delta^!R\mathcal Hom(q_2^{-1}G,q_1^!F). \qquad\text{(CHE30)}

This is exceptional inverse-image adjunction for internal Hom: δ−1q2−1G=G\delta^{-1}q_2^{-1}G=G and δ!q1!F=F\delta^!q_1^!F=F by composition. The external Hom bound places the kernel microsupport in SS⁡(F)×SS⁡(G)a\operatorname{SS}(F)\times\operatorname{SS}(G)^a. Apply the exceptional part of (CHE22), followed by (CHE27). This proves the second inclusion without replacing internal Hom by a tensor with a dual. ▫\square

When the escape term is empty, (CHE5) recovers the usual estimate with ordinary fibrewise addition. In general that replacement is false, even for constant sheaves on smooth closed submanifolds.

SH02-CHE-EXAMPLES — Tangency, escape, and a comparison that fails

Assume k≠0k\ne0. On X=ℝ2X=\mathbb R^2 use coordinates (s,t)(s,t), with covectors (σ,τ)(\sigma,\tau). Let Y={t=0}Y=\{t=0\} and Z={t=s4}Z=\{t=s^4\}. Both are closed smooth submanifolds. Their constant sheaves, extended by zero to XX, have conormal microsupports:

First let f:Y↪Xf:Y\hookrightarrow X and F=kZF=k_Z. Direct restriction gives f−1F=k{0}f^{-1}F=k_{\{0\}} on YY. Second, kY⊗LkZ=k{(0,0)}k_Y\otimes^Lk_Z=k_{\{(0,0)\}}. Stalkwise there is no higher Tor: the nonzero stalk modules of each factor are kk. Its microsupport at the origin is all of T0*XT^*_0X. Finally consider the closed line Y⊂ℝ2Y\subset\mathbb R^2 and F=G=kYF=G=k_Y. This GG is cohomologically constructible. The closed-submanifold duality calculation gives

Problem 1. Let f:Y→Xf:Y\to X be a submersion. Show that f∞#Af^\#_\infty A is empty for every closed conic A⊂T*XA\subset T^*X, and identify f#Af^\#A.

Problem 3. In the tangency example, replace s4s^4 by s2ms^{2m} with m≥1m\geq1. Determine the required growth of the normal multiplier and check the weighted error.

Solution. A conormal covector is (−2ms2m−1λ,λ)(-2m s^{2m-1}\lambda,\lambda). To keep its tangential component equal to c≠0c\ne0, take λ=−c/(2ms2m−1)\lambda=-c/(2m s^{2m-1}). Problem 4. Distinguish the two antipodes in (CHE17) in a one-dimensional fibre coordinate ee.

Solution. In coordinates (y,e;η,ϵ)(y,e;\eta,\epsilon), the cotangent lift of the bundle antipode is (y,−e;η,−ϵ)(y,-e;\eta,-\epsilon). The cotangent-fibre antipode is (y,e;−η,−ϵ)(y,e;-\eta,-\epsilon). SH02-CHE-RANGE — The full input range and its unbounded proof

The full intended tensor and internal Hom estimate allows two inputs in D+(kX)D^+(k_X). The deductions in SH02-CHE-006 establish the bounded-input theorem. SH02-UCE-SUM supplies both estimates for arbitrary inputs in the classical unbounded category, with the same coefficient ring and manifold assumptions. Its restriction to two bounded-below inputs proves the full printed range, including a possibly unbounded-below Hom output. The proof uses uniform local tests, raw specialization and radial recovery; it does not require the unresolved Fourier comparison.

The issue is substantial for internal Hom. Already at a point over a field, let G=⨁n≥0k[−n]G=\bigoplus_{n\geq0}k[-n] and F=kF=k. Both are bounded below, but

RHom⁡(G,F)≃∏n≥0k[n](CHE34) R\operatorname{Hom}(G,F)\simeq\prod_{n\geq0}k[n] \qquad\text{(CHE34)}

has nonzero cohomology in arbitrarily negative degrees. Thus membership of the two inputs in D+D^+ does not keep the Hom output in D+D^+. This calculation does not refute a correctly formulated unbounded microsupport estimate. It shows why a proof must specify that framework and justify the local tests and derived limit operations there. Degreewise truncation alone is insufficient: truncating a complex need not preserve its microsupport bound. Those steps are proved in SH02-UCE-WINDOWS, SH02-UCE-TESTS and the subsequent geometric arguments. The finite windows extend natural functor comparisons; the support tests themselves are applied to the original complex on neighborhoods chosen uniformly in degree.

SH02-CHE-REGULARITY — A C1C^1 chart can change an asymptotic sum

The asymptotic operations above are invariant under smooth coordinate changes by their normal-cone construction. Their behavior under C1C^1 changes is different from the C1C^1 invariance of microsupport. Here is a direct calculation.

Fix 1<r<21<r<2 and put g(s)=|s|rg(s)=|s|^r, Z={(s,g(s))}⊂ℝ2Z=\{(s,g(s))\}\subset\mathbb R^2, and

A=NZ*ℝ2={(s,g(s);−g′(s)λ,λ):λ∈ℝ}.(CHE35) A=N_Z^*\mathbb R^2 =\{(s,g(s);-g'(s)\lambda,\lambda):\lambda\in\mathbb R\}. \qquad\text{(CHE35)}

The graph is a C1C^1 submanifold, and AA is a closed conic subset of the ordinary smooth cotangent bundle. We claim

A+̂A=A∪T0*ℝ2.(CHE36) A\widehat+A=A\cup T^*_0\mathbb R^2. \qquad\text{(CHE36)}

To create a prescribed covector (c,d)(c,d) at the origin, use graph points with parameters sn>0s_n>0 and −sn-s_n, where sn→0s_n\to0. The derivatives there are an=rsnr−1a_n=rs_n^{r-1} and −an-a_n. Choose

λn=−c2an,μn=d−λn. \lambda_n=-\frac{c}{2a_n},\qquad \mu_n=d-\lambda_n.

The covectors (−anλn,λn)(-a_n\lambda_n,\lambda_n) and (anμn,μn)(a_n\mu_n,\mu_n) sum to (c+and,d)(c+a_nd,d), which tends to (c,d)(c,d). The graph points have distance 2sn2s_n, and the first covector has norm O(sn1−r)O(s_n^{1-r}) when c≠0c\ne0. Their weighted separation is therefore O(sn2−r)→0O(s_n^{2-r})\to0. If c=0c=0, the same construction has first covector zero and still satisfies the criterion. Thus every covector over the origin lies in the asymptotic sum.

It remains to exclude other extra points. The base of any asymptotic limit belongs to the closed graph. Near a point with parameter s0≠0s_0\ne0, the derivative g′g' is Lipschitz. Suppose two conormal covectors at parameters un,vn→s0u_n,v_n\to s_0 have a convergent sum. Write their normal coefficients as λn,μn\lambda_n,\mu_n. Then λn+μn→d\lambda_n+\mu_n\to d. The tangential component of their sum can be rewritten as

−g′(vn)(λn+μn)+(g′(vn)−g′(un))λn. -g'(v_n)(\lambda_n+\mu_n) +(g'(v_n)-g'(u_n))\lambda_n.

The second summand tends to zero: its norm is bounded by a constant times |un−vn||λn||u_n-v_n|\,|\lambda_n|, and this is bounded by the weighted separation in the definition of the asymptotic sum. The limit is therefore (−g′(s0)d,d)(-g'(s_0)d,d), a conormal covector. This proves (CHE36).

Now use the C1C^1 diffeomorphism Φ(s,t)=(s,t−g(s))\Phi(s,t)=(s,t-g(s)). It sends ZZ to the horizontal line, and its cotangent transport sends AA to that line’s conormal bundle BB. Since every covector in BB has tangential component zero, B+̂B=BB\widehat+B=B. But (CHE36) transports to B∪T0*ℝ2B\cup T^*_0\mathbb R^2, which is strictly larger. The operation +̂\widehat+ is therefore not invariant under all C1C^1 coordinate changes.

The same calculation also exhibits failure of C1C^1 invariance for the generalized inverse-image operation. Indeed (CHE27) writes A+̂AA\widehat+A as the generalized inverse image of A×AA\times A by the diagonal. The pair of coordinate changes Φ\Phi and Φ×Φ\Phi\times\Phi preserves the diagonal map and sends A×AA\times A to B×BB\times B. If generalized inverse image were invariant under these C1C^1 coordinate changes, (CHE27) would force the false equality just obtained. No claim of this invariance is needed by the smooth-manifold estimates.

Exact range of the readable comparison

In Astérisque 128, Theorem 5.2.2 allows bounded-below tensor inputs under finite weak global dimension, but its internal-Hom assertion additionally requires the first Hom input to be bounded. This is an actual restriction of that edition. Schapira’s 2016 review, Corollary 2.12, is a noncharacteristic estimate and cannot replace the full asymptotic-sum theorem used in CHE-006. Thus the bounded proof above is supported by the normal-deformation and graph argument, while the larger intended Hom range remains dependent on the explicitly linked unbounded supplement and its uniform tests. The point example in CHE-RANGE is retained because it shows exactly why bounded-below inputs do not make the Hom output bounded below. No source citation discharges that extra proof obligation.
