Near normalizers at every time

Written by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), October 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. Public domain (CC0).

Let \(M,N\) be a prepared pair with common vector \(\Omega\) and modular groups \(U_M(t)=\Delta_M^{it}\), \(U_N(t)=\Delta_N^{it}\). The unitaries \(W(t)=U_N(t)U_M(t)^*\) nearly normalize \(M\): conjugation by \(W(t)\) moves \(M\) by at most twice the completely bounded distance. This lesson proves that each \(W(t)\) is close to an exact normalizer of \(M\), with a bound uniform in \(t\) (Theorem 1.1). The proof makes the comparison of modular cuts into a statement about the frequency nest, replaces the field \(W\) by analytic multipliers, and smooths them into a norm-continuous path of approximate normalizers. The path is then factored by the infinitesimal estimates for type III factors, and a completely positive limit recovers an exact normalizer at each time [OpenAI-289, Section 7].

We use: Close operator algebras (Lemmas 1.4 and 1.5); Averaging and the completely positive criterion (Lemma 1.1 and Proposition 4.1); Preparing infinite factors (Definition 5.1); Rescaled Tomita graphs (Corollary 3.1, with its constant \(C_{\rm cut}=2.1\cdot10^6\)); Infinitesimal normalizers of type III factors (Lemma 3.1 and the group \(\mathcal G_M\)); Frequency nests and analytic multipliers (the notation \(\mathcal K,P_a,C_s,M_F\), Lemmas 3.1 and 4.1 and Proposition 4.2); Stone's theorem (a strongly continuous unitary group \(s\mapsto e^{isA}\) determines the spectral measure of its generator); and the weak compactness of the unit ball of \(B(H)\).

1. The statement

In this lesson \(C\) denotes absolute constants, whose values may change from one occurrence to the next, and \(\gamma_0>0\) an absolute threshold; all smallness conditions below are met by decreasing \(\gamma_0\).

Theorem 1.1 (near normalizers). There are absolute constants \(\gamma_0>0\) and \(C\) such that, for every prepared pair \(M,N\subseteq B(H)\) with \(d_{\rm cb}(M,N)\le\gamma<\gamma_0\), and every \(t\in\mathbb R\), there is a unitary \(n_t\) with \[ n_tMn_t^*=M,\qquad\|n_t-W(t)\|\le C\gamma . \]

No measurability or continuity of \(t\mapsto n_t\) is claimed. Throughout the proof write \(L_P=\log\Delta_P\), so \(U_P(t)=e^{itL_P}\).

2. Initial approximate normalizers

Lemma 2.1. For every \(t\), \(k\ge1\) and \(x\in M_k(M)_1\), \[ \begin{gathered} \begin{aligned} &\operatorname{dist}\bigl(W(t)^{(k)}xW(t)^{(k)*},M_k(M)_1\bigr)\\ &\quad \le2\gamma, \end{aligned}\\ \begin{aligned} &\operatorname{dist}\bigl(W(t)^{(k)*}xW(t)^{(k)},M_k(M)_1\bigr)\\ &\quad \le2\gamma . \end{aligned} \end{gathered} \] Consequently \(d_{\rm cb}(W(t)MW(t)^*,M)\le2\gamma\).

Proof. \(U_M(t)\) normalizes \(M\) and \(U_N(t)\) normalizes \(N\). Given \(x\), the element \(U_M(t)^*xU_M(t)\) lies in \(M_k(M)_1\); approximate it by \(y\in M_k(N)_1\) within \(\gamma\) plus a small slack; then \(U_N(t)yU_N(t)^*\in M_k(N)_1\) is within \(\gamma\) plus slack of some \(z\in M_k(M)_1\). So \(W(t)xW(t)^*\) is within \(2\gamma\) plus slack of \(z\). The second estimate approximates \(x\) by \(y\in M_k(N)_1\), then \(U_N(t)^*yU_N(t)\) by \(z\in M_k(M)_1\), and uses \(U_M(t)zU_M(t)^*\in M_k(M)_1\). The two estimates give the two directed distances between the unit balls of \(M_k(W(t)MW(t)^*)\) and \(M_k(M)\). \(\square\)

3. The frequency nest sees the modular comparison

On \(\mathcal K=L^2(\mathbb R,H)\) let \(\mathcal U_P\) be multiplication by the field \(t\mapsto U_P(t)\), for \(P=M,N\), and \(\mathcal W=\mathcal U_N\mathcal U_M^*\), multiplication by \(W\). Let \(T_s\) be the translation \((T_sf)(t)=f(t+s)\), so that \(P_a\) is the spectral projection of the generator of \(s\mapsto T_s\) for \((a,\infty)\).

Lemma 3.1. For every \(a\in\mathbb R\), \[ \begin{gathered} \|(1-P_a)C_1\mathcal WP_a\|\le C_{\rm cut}\gamma,\\ \|(1-P_a)C_1\mathcal W^*P_a\|\le C_{\rm cut}\gamma . \end{gathered} \]

Proof. A direct computation gives \(\mathcal U_P^*T_s\mathcal U_P=T_s\otimes U_P(s)\), because \(U_P(t)^*U_P(t+s)=U_P(s)\). So conjugation by \(\mathcal U_P\) carries the spectral projections of the generator of \(s\mapsto T_s\) to those of the generator of \(V_P(s)=T_s\otimes e^{isL_P}\). Under the Fourier transform in \(t\), \(T_s\) becomes multiplication by \(e^{is\xi}\), so \(V_P(s)\) becomes the decomposable unitary field \(\xi\mapsto e^{is(\xi+L_P)}\). The decomposable field of spectral projections \(\xi\mapsto1_B(\xi+L_P)\), \(B\) a Borel set, is a projection-valued measure whose unitary group is \(V_P\). By Stone's theorem it is the spectral measure of the generator of \(V_P\). Hence \[ \begin{gathered} \mathcal U_P^*P_a\mathcal U_P\ \text{has fibers}\\ 1_{(a,\infty)}(\xi+L_P)=1_{(a-\xi,\infty)}(L_P). \end{gathered} \] By (3.1) of the analytic multiplier lesson, \(C_1^*(1-P_a)C_1=1-P_{a-1}\), and so \[ \begin{aligned} &(1-P_a)C_1\mathcal WP_a\\ &\quad=C_1\mathcal U_N\bigl[\mathcal U_N^*(1-P_{a-1})\mathcal U_N\bigr]\\ &\qquad\cdot\bigl[\mathcal U_M^*P_a\mathcal U_M\bigr]\mathcal U_M^* . \end{aligned} \] The bracketed product is decomposable with fibers \(1_{(-\infty,a-1-\xi]}(L_N)\,1_{(a-\xi,\infty)}(L_M)\). With \(b=a-\frac12-\xi\), this is a product of the cuts of Corollary 3.1 of Rescaled Tomita graphs at \(b\mp\frac12\) (the open half-line gives a smaller projection), so each fiber has norm at most \(C_{\rm cut}\gamma\). By Lemma 4.1 of the analytic multiplier lesson the norm of a decomposable operator is the essential supremum of the norms of its fibers. The second estimate is the same with \(M\) and \(N\) exchanged, since \(\mathcal W^*=\mathcal U_M\mathcal U_N^*\). \(\square\)

4. Analytic multipliers and their extensions

Put \(\delta=C_{\rm cut}\gamma\), \(F(s)=e^{is}W(s)\) and \(\tilde F(s)=e^{is}W(s)^*\); then \(C_1\mathcal W=M_F\) and \(C_1\mathcal W^*=M_{\tilde F}\). By Lemma 3.1 and Proposition 4.2 of the analytic multiplier lesson there are analytic bounded fields \(B\) and \(S\) with \[ \begin{gathered} \|B-F\|_\infty\le\delta,\\ \|S-\tilde F\|_\infty\le\delta .\quad(4.1) \end{gathered} \] Let \(B(z)\) and \(S(z)\), \(\operatorname{Im}z>0\), be their extensions (Lemma 3.1 there).

Lemma 4.1. For \(\operatorname{Im}z>0\), every \(k\) and every \(x\in M_k(M)_1\), \[ \begin{aligned} &\|B(z)S(z)-e^{2iz}\|\le2\delta+\delta^2,\\ &\|S(z)B(z)-e^{2iz}\|\le2\delta+\delta^2,\\ &\operatorname{dist}\bigl(B(z)^{(k)}xS(z)^{(k)},M_k(M)\bigr)\le\beta,\\ &\operatorname{dist}\bigl(S(z)^{(k)}xB(z)^{(k)},M_k(M)\bigr)\le\beta, \end{aligned} \] where \(\beta=2\gamma+2\delta+\delta^2\).

Proof. On the boundary, \(F\tilde F=e^{2is}\), \(\|S\|_\infty\le1+\delta\), and \(BS-e^{2is}=(B-F)S+F(S-\tilde F)\) has norm at most \(2\delta+\delta^2\) almost everywhere. The scalar \(e^{2is}\) is an analytic field with extension \(e^{2iz}\), so by the product rule of Lemma 3.1 of the analytic multiplier lesson the extension of the analytic field \(BS-e^{2is}\) is \(B(z)S(z)-e^{2iz}\), and its norm is bounded by the boundary bound. The same holds for \(SB\).

For the second estimate, \(F^{(k)}x\tilde F^{(k)}=e^{2is}W(s)^{(k)}xW(s)^{(k)*}\) is within \(2\gamma\) of \(M_k(M)\) by Lemma 2.1, and \(\|B^{(k)}xS^{(k)}-F^{(k)}x\tilde F^{(k)}\|\le2\delta+\delta^2\); so on the boundary the distance is at most \(\beta\) almost everywhere. Let \(\omega\) be a normal functional on \(B(H^k)\) of norm at most \(1\) vanishing on \(M_k(M)\). The fields \(B^{(k)},S^{(k)}\) are analytic on \(L^2(\mathbb R,H^k)\), so the extension of \(B^{(k)}xS^{(k)}\) is \(B(z)^{(k)}xS(z)^{(k)}\), the Poisson integral of its boundary values; testing \(\omega\), a norm-convergent sum of vector functionals, gives \[ \begin{aligned} &|\omega(B(z)^{(k)}xS(z)^{(k)})|\\ &\quad \le\int\frac{r\,|\omega(B(s)^{(k)}xS(s)^{(k)})|}{\pi((t-s)^2+r^2)}\,ds\le\beta \end{aligned} \] for \(z=t+ir\), since \(|\omega(y)|\le\operatorname{dist}(y,M_k(M))\). As \(M_k(M)\) is weak* closed, the distance to it is the supremum of \(|\omega(y)|\) over such \(\omega\), by the Hahn–Banach theorem applied in the predual. The case \(S\,x\,B\) is the same. \(\square\)

5. A norm-continuous path

For \(z=t+ir\) with \(0<r\le\gamma\) put \[ \begin{gathered} A_r(t)=e^{-iz}B(z),\\ C_r(t)=e^{-iz}S(z). \end{gathered} \] Since \(|e^{-iz}|=e^r\le e^\gamma\), Lemma 4.1 gives, uniformly in \(r,t,k\) and \(x\in M_k(M)_1\), \[ \begin{aligned} &\|A_r(t)\|,\ \|C_r(t)\|\le1+C\gamma,\\ &\|A_r(t)C_r(t)-1\|\le C\gamma,\\ &\|C_r(t)A_r(t)-1\|\le C\gamma,\\ &\operatorname{dist}\bigl(A_r(t)^{(k)}xC_r(t)^{(k)},M_k(M)\bigr)\\ &\qquad\le C\gamma,\quad(5.1) \end{aligned} \] and the same with \(A_r\) and \(C_r\) exchanged in the last line.

Polar parts. For small \(\gamma\), \(A_rC_r\) and \(C_rA_r\) are invertible, so \(A_r(t)\) has a right and a left inverse, which agree, and \(\|A_r(t)^{-1}\|\le1+C\gamma\). With \(\|A_r(t)\|\le1+C\gamma\), the spectrum of \(|A_r(t)|\) lies in \([1-C\gamma,1+C\gamma]\), and the unitary polar part \[ V_r(t)=A_r(t)\bigl(A_r(t)^*A_r(t)\bigr)^{-1/2} \] satisfies \(\|V_r(t)-A_r(t)\|=\|1-|A_r(t)|\|\le C\gamma\) and, comparing \(A_r(t)^{-1}=|A_r(t)|^{-1}V_r(t)^*\) with \(C_r(t)=A_r(t)^{-1}A_r(t)C_r(t)\), \(\|V_r(t)^*-C_r(t)\|\le C\gamma\). By (5.1), at every matrix level, \(V_r(t)xV_r(t)^*\) and \(V_r(t)^*xV_r(t)\) lie within \(C\gamma\) of \(M_k(M)\) for \(x\in M_k(M)_1\).

Continuity. For fixed \(r\), \(t\mapsto A_r(t)\) is norm continuous, by holomorphy of \(B\), and its inverses are bounded, so \(V_r\) is norm continuous by the continuous functional calculus. Put \[ E_r(t)=V_r(t)V_r(0)^*, \] a norm-continuous unitary path with \(E_r(0)=1\). For \(x\in M_1\), \(V_r(0)^*xV_r(0)\) is within \(C\gamma\) of some \(m\in M\) with \(\|m\|\le1+C\gamma\), and \(V_r(t)mV_r(t)^*\) is within \(C\gamma\|m\|\) of \(M\); so \[ \sup_{x\in M_1}\operatorname{dist}\bigl(E_r(t)xE_r(t)^*,M\bigr)\le C\gamma . \] \(M\) is a separably acting type III factor. Lemma 3.1 of Infinitesimal normalizers of type III factors, applied to the path on the interval between \(0\) and \(t\), gives \(a_r(t)\in\mathcal U(M)\) and \(b_r'(t)\in\mathcal U(M')\) with \[ \begin{aligned} &\|E_r(t)-a_r(t)b_r'(t)\|\\ &\quad \le C\gamma ,\quad(5.2) \end{aligned} \] where a slack of \(\gamma\) allows for an infimum that is not attained. No continuous choice of the factors is needed.

6. Back to the real axis

The field \(F_0(s)=e^{is}W(s)\) is strongly* continuous, since modular groups are strongly continuous. Put \[ D_r(t)=e^{-i(t+ir)}\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{r\,F_0(s)}{\pi((t-s)^2+r^2)}\,ds . \] By (3.2) of the analytic multiplier lesson and (4.1), \(\|A_r(t)-D_r(t)\|\le e^r\delta\), so \[ \begin{gathered} \|V_r(t)-D_r(t)\|\le C\gamma,\\ \|D_r(t)\|\le e^r .\quad(6.1) \end{gathered} \] At every fixed \(t\), \(D_r(t)\to W(t)\) strongly* as \(r\downarrow0\): on a fixed vector, split the integral into \(|s-t|<\eta\), where strong continuity controls \(F_0(s)\xi-F_0(t)\xi\), and the complement, whose Poisson mass tends to \(0\); the same holds for the adjoint field, and \(e^{-i(t+ir)}\to e^{-it}\). Since \(W(0)=1\) and all the operators are uniformly bounded, \[ \begin{gathered} Q_r(t)=D_r(t)D_r(0)^*\longrightarrow W(t)\\ \text{strongly}^*, \end{gathered} \] and by (6.1), \(\|E_r(t)-Q_r(t)\|\le C\gamma\). This compares \(E_r(t)\) with a convergent family; it does not assert convergence of \(E_r(t)\).

7. A completely positive limit

Proof of Theorem 1.1. Fix \(t\) and a sequence \(r\downarrow0\). The maps \(\Phi_r(x)=a_r(t)xa_r(t)^*\) are UCP from \(M\) to \(M\), and \(\Phi_r\) is the restriction of \(\operatorname{Ad}(a_r(t)b_r'(t))\) because \(b_r'(t)\) commutes with \(M\). For operators \(T_1,T_2\), the maps \(x\mapsto T_1xT_1^*\) and \(x\mapsto T_2xT_2^*\) differ in completely bounded norm by at most \((\|T_1\|+\|T_2\|)\|T_1-T_2\|\). With (5.2) and Section 6, \[ \begin{gathered} \begin{aligned} &\|\Phi_r^{(k)}(x)\\ &\qquad -Q_r(t)^{(k)}xQ_r(t)^{(k)*}\|\\ &\quad \le C\gamma\|x\| \end{aligned}\\ (x\in M_k(M)). \end{gathered} \] By Lemma 1.1 of the averaging lesson, a subnet of \((\Phi_r)\) converges pointwise in the weak operator topology to a UCP map \(\Phi:M\to M\); it need not be normal. For fixed \(x\), \(Q_r(t)xQ_r(t)^*\to W(t)xW(t)^*\) strongly, also at every matrix level, and norm balls are weakly closed, so \[ \begin{gathered} \|\Phi-\rho_t\|_{\rm cb}\le C\gamma,\\ \rho_t(x)=W(t)xW(t)^* . \end{gathered} \] The representation \(\rho_t\) of \(M\) is faithful and normal, and by Lemma 2.1 every element of \(M_1\) is within \(2\gamma\) of \(\rho_t(M)_1\). For small \(\gamma\), Proposition 4.1 of the averaging lesson, with \(Q=M\), gives a unitary \(q_t\) with \(q_t\rho_t(M)q_t^*=M\) and \(\|q_t-1\|\le C\gamma\). Then \(n_t=q_tW(t)\) satisfies \(n_tMn_t^*=M\) and \(\|n_t-W(t)\|=\|q_t-1\|\le C\gamma\). All thresholds and constants were absolute. \(\square\)

8. Exercises

Exercise 8.1. Verify \(\mathcal U_P^*T_s\mathcal U_P=T_s\otimes U_P(s)\).

Exercise 8.2. Show that, for bounded operators \(T_1,T_2\), \(\|T_1xT_1^*-T_2xT_2^*\|\)\(\le(\|T_1\|+\|T_2\|)\|T_1-T_2\|\|x\|\), at every matrix level.

Exercise 8.3. Let \(V\subseteq B(K)\) be a weak* closed subspace. Show that \(\operatorname{dist}(y,V)=\sup\{|\omega(y)|\}\) over normal functionals \(\omega\) of norm at most \(1\) vanishing on \(V\).

Exercise 8.4. Show that an invertible \(A\) with \(\|A\|\le1+\epsilon\) and \(\|A^{-1}\|\le1+\epsilon\) has polar part \(V\) with \(\|V-A\|\le\epsilon\).

Exercise 8.5. Explain why the proof does not use a continuous or measurable choice of \(t\mapsto n_t\), and where Section 2 of the next lesson removes the need for one.

9. Solutions

Solution 8.1. \((\mathcal U_P^*T_s\mathcal U_Pf)(t)\)\(=U_P(t)^*U_P(t+s)f(t+s)\)\(=U_P(s)f(t+s)\).

Solution 8.2. \(T_1xT_1^*-T_2xT_2^*\)\(=(T_1-T_2)xT_1^*+T_2x(T_1-T_2)^*\); amplification preserves norms.

Solution 8.3. \(V\) is the annihilator of its preannihilator \(V_\perp\) in the predual, by the Hahn–Banach theorem in the predual, and the quotient \(B(K)/V\) is isometrically the dual of \(V_\perp\). So the quotient norm of \(y\) is the norm of the functional \(\omega\mapsto\omega(y)\) on \(V_\perp\).

Solution 8.4. The spectrum of \(|A|\) lies in \([(1+\epsilon)^{-1},1+\epsilon]\subseteq[1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon]\), and \(V-A=V(1-|A|)\).

Solution 8.5. Each \(n_t\) is produced separately, through a cluster point of a sequence in \(r\) at that \(t\). The next lesson averages the family \(t\mapsto n_t\) with an invariant mean on \(\mathbb R_d\), which needs no measurability, and recovers continuity at the end from an intertwining relation with the modular group of \(N\).
