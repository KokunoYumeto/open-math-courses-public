Constructible duality and infinite twists

Duality maps for constructible inverse and direct images

Dualizing a sheaf twice comes with a map from the original sheaf. Whether that map is invertible determines which duality comparisons can be reversed. We will construct the comparison maps, factor the reversed maps through evaluation, and then identify the geometric hypotheses that make those evaluations invertible. An infinite locally constant coefficient requires a different argument: move it through an internal Hom, rather than evaluate it twice.

Scope. The operation, orientation and evaluation arguments are proved below. The constructible applications additionally require the geometric results identified in the prerequisite section; their full proof chains are not claimed here.

Coefficients, pairings and the operation contracts

Let kk be a commutative ring of finite global dimension. Spaces in this lesson are finite-dimensional real analytic manifolds, Hausdorff and countable at infinity, with a uniform dimension bound. Complexes are globally bounded. All tensors and internal Homs are derived. We use

ωX=aX!k,DXE=RℋomX(E,ωX),Hj(E[s])=Hj+s(E).(1) \omega_X=a_X^!k,\qquad D_XE=R\mathcal Hom_X(E,\omega_X), \qquad H^j(E[s])=H^{j+s}(E). \qquad\text{(1)}

For a dd-manifold, ωX=orX[d]\omega_X=\mathrm{or}_X[d]. Keep the orientation line in this formula unless an orientation has been chosen.

We first work formally with a map f:Y→Xf:Y\to X. The required operation contracts are ordinary adjunction f−1⊣Rf*f^{-1}\dashv Rf_*, proper-support adjunction Rf!⊣f!Rf_!\dashv f^!, tensor–Hom adjunction, the projection isomorphism

Rf!(B⊗f−1A)≃Rf!B⊗A,(2) Rf_!(B\otimes f^{-1}A)\simeq Rf_!B\otimes A, \qquad\text{(2)}

and composition f!ωX≃ωYf^!\omega_X\simeq\omega_Y, with its compatible trace Rf!ωY→ωXRf_!\omega_Y\to\omega_X. These contracts include their units, counits, naturality and boundedness. The sections below prove compact-support extension and lifting, the proper-image fibre formulas, base change and composition, the uniform manifold dimension bound, and arbitrary-coefficient projection. The adjunction section then constructs both adjunctions on bounded-below categories and fixes their trace-compatible exceptional composition. The following bounds section proves globally bounded preservation for ordinary direct image, exceptional inverse image and duality. The tensor–Hom section then constructs the remaining algebraic contract, including the actual evaluation map and its signs. The orientation section proves the constant local-support and orientation inputs. Constructible and weakly constructible geometric inputs remain prerequisites. In particular, (2) permits arbitrary bounded AA; it is not a projection theorem restricted to perfect coefficients. The formal arguments also apply outside the analytic setting whenever these same bounded operation contracts hold.

Evaluation is the pairing ev⁡E:DXE⊗E→ωX\operatorname{ev}_E:D_XE\otimes E\to\omega_X. By symmetry and currying it gives

ηE:E⟶DXDXE.(3) \eta_E:E\longrightarrow D_XD_XE. \qquad\text{(3)}

We call EE reflexive for this duality when this particular map is invertible. An abstract isomorphism between EE and DXDXED_XD_XE would not be sufficient.

All maps below use the usual symmetry on complexes. On homogeneous tensors that symmetry is v⊗w↦(−1)|v||w|w⊗vv\otimes w\mapsto(-1)^{|v||w|}w\otimes v. Currying and evaluation use the same convention, so reversing a pairing introduces no unrecorded sign.

Compact support: extension, lifting and acyclicity

The projection proof needs precise control of support when a section is extended or lifted. We supply that part of its foundation here. Throughout this section, spaces are locally compact Hausdorff and coefficients are arbitrary modules over the fixed commutative ring kk. Finite global dimension is unnecessary for these support arguments. For a compact subset K⊂XK\subset X, Γ(K;F)\Gamma(K;F) means sections of the inverse-image sheaf on KK, not sections on XX whose support lies in KK.

The human source for these results is Pierre Schapira’s freely accessible An Introduction to Sheaves on Grothendieck Topologies, 1 August 2026, §4.3. His word “soft” in that section means what we call c-soft: every section on every compact subset extends to a global section. We organise the proof around two concrete operations—extending with prescribed support and lifting through a quotient—and include the compact-neighbourhood details they require. The following sections supply the proper-image fibre formula and the uniform manifold dimension bound.

Compact subsets remember neighbourhood germs

We first justify the comparison

colim⁡K⊂V openΓ(V;F)→∼Γ(K;F).(C1) \mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_{K\subset V\text{ open}}\Gamma(V;F) \xrightarrow{\sim}\Gamma(K;F). \qquad\text{(C1)}

The elementary topological facts needed below follow from compactness and Hausdorff separation. Disjoint compact sets have disjoint open neighbourhoods: separate each pair of points, take a finite intersection on the neighbourhood of a fixed point and a finite union on the opposite compact set, then repeat over a finite cover of the first compact set. Thus a compact Hausdorff space is normal. Inside a compact neighbourhood, this separation shrinks a neighbourhood of a point so that its closure is compact and lies in any specified open neighbourhood. Applying these pointwise shrinkings and then taking a finite subcover gives compact sets subordinate to any finite open cover of a compact set.

Surjectivity of (C1) is not merely the definition of inverse image, since that definition also sheafifies. A section on KK has local representatives sis_i on ambient open sets NiN_i. Choose finitely many compact sets Ki⊂K∩NiK_i\subset K\cap N_i covering KK, and relatively compact open neighbourhoods OiO_i of KiK_i with Oi¯⊂Ni\overline{O_i}\subset N_i. For each pair i,ji,j, let EijE_{ij} be the open subset of Ni∩NjN_i\cap N_j where the germs of si,sjs_i,s_j agree. It contains Ki∩KjK_i\cap K_j. The compact set Dij=(Oi¯∩Oj¯)\EijD_{ij}=(\overline{O_i}\cap\overline{O_j})\setminus E_{ij} therefore meets KiK_i and KjK_j in disjoint closed subsets. Separate those two subsets by disjoint relatively open subsets of DijD_{ij}. Their complements in DijD_{ij} are compact, so taking their complements in XX gives open neighbourhoods of Ki,KjK_i,K_j whose intersection misses DijD_{ij}. Intersect these neighbourhoods over the finitely many pairs, also intersecting with OiO_i. The resulting opens WiW_i still contain KiK_i, and the representatives agree on every Wi∩WjW_i\cap W_j. The sheaf axiom glues them to a section on ⋃iWi\bigcup_iW_i, a neighbourhood of KK. This proves surjectivity.

For injectivity, two representatives with equal restriction to KK have equal germs at every point of KK. Their equality locus is open and contains KK. They are therefore equal after shrinking their common neighbourhood, exactly the equivalence relation in the colimit.

We also use gluing over a finite closed cover of a compact space. If sections on closed sets K1,…,KmK_1,\ldots,K_m agree on their intersections, their germs give a section on their union. To check this locally at a point, discard the finitely many closed sets not containing that point. Representatives from the remaining sets have the same germ there, so they agree on a common smaller neighbourhood. This supplies a local representative of the proposed glued section; these representatives agree wherever both are defined. Hence the sheaf axiom proves existence and uniqueness of the glued section. This argument concerns sections of restricted sheaves, so equality on an intersection includes equality of the corresponding ambient germs.

Extending a section while controlling its support

Support extension lemma. Suppose FF is c-soft, K⊂XK\subset X is compact, and s∈Γ(K;F)s\in\Gamma(K;F). If the support of ss in KK lies in an open set V⊂XV\subset X, there is t∈Γc(X;F)t\in\Gamma_c(X;F) such that

t|K=s,supp⁡(t)⊂V.(C2) t|_K=s,\qquad \operatorname{supp}(t)\subset V. \qquad\text{(C2)}

Proof. The support of a section is closed in its domain because its zero-germ locus is open. Thus A=supp⁡(s)A=\operatorname{supp}(s) is compact. Choose a compact neighbourhood LL of AA contained in VV; if AA is empty, take t=0t=0. On the compact set (K∩L)∪∂L(K\cap L)\cup\partial L, prescribe ss on K∩LK\cap L and zero on ∂L\partial L. These prescriptions agree on the intersection because A⊂int⁡LA\subset\operatorname{int}L. Finite closed gluing supplies a section there. By c-softness it extends to a global section uu of FF.

The germs of uu vanish on ∂L\partial L, so uu vanishes on an open neighbourhood of that boundary. Glue uu on int⁡L\operatorname{int}L to zero on the union of that neighbourhood with X\LX\setminus L. These opens cover XX, and the prescribed sections agree on the overlap. The resulting tt has support in L⊂VL\subset V. On K∩LK\cap L it agrees with ss; on K\LK\setminus L both vanish. This proves (C2). ▫\square

Several stability properties now have direct proofs.

  • Restriction preserves c-softness whenever the subspace in question is locally compact Hausdorff. A compact subset of that subspace is compact in XX, and iterated inverse image identifies its restricted sections with Γ(K;F)\Gamma(K;F). Extend in XX and then restrict.
  • If j:U↪Xj:U\hookrightarrow X is open and GG is c-soft on UU, then j!Gj_!G is c-soft on XX. Given a section on a compact K⊂XK\subset X, its support AA is a compact subset of K∩UK\cap U, since the stalks off UU are zero. Choose A⊂int⁡L⊂L⊂UA\subset\operatorname{int}L\subset L\subset U with LL compact. The same prescription on (K∩L)∪∂L(K\cap L)\cup\partial L, now made entirely inside UU, extends using c-softness of GG. Cut it off at ∂L\partial L as in the lemma and extend by zero to XX. It has the prescribed restriction to KK.
  • An arbitrary coproduct of c-soft sheaves is c-soft. A section of that coproduct on a compact set locally involves finitely many summands, by the sheafification definition of a coproduct. A finite cover of the compact set shows that a single finite set of indices suffices. The section factors through that finite subsum on the compact set, as one can check on stalks. Extend its finitely many components globally and then include the finite subsum in the full coproduct. No assertion about unrestricted sections commuting with infinite coproducts is used.

Finally, injective sheaves are c-soft. For opens U⊂VU\subset V, the map kU→kVk_U\to k_V is a monomorphism, as its stalks show. The identification Hom⁡(kU,I)=Γ(U;I)\operatorname{Hom}(k_U,I)=\Gamma(U;I) and injectivity of II make every restriction Γ(V;I)→Γ(U;I)\Gamma(V;I)\to\Gamma(U;I) surjective. Thus II is flabby. A section on a compact set is represented on a neighbourhood by (C1), and flabbiness extends that representative to XX. The same argument proves directly that every flabby sheaf is c-soft.

Lifting compactly supported sections

Lifting lemma. If 0→F′→F→F′′→00\to F'\to F\to F^{\prime\prime}\to0 is exact and F′F' is c-soft, then

0⟶Γc(X;F′)⟶Γc(X;F)⟶Γc(X;F′′)⟶0(C3) 0\longrightarrow\Gamma_c(X;F')\longrightarrow\Gamma_c(X;F) \longrightarrow\Gamma_c(X;F^{\prime\prime})\longrightarrow0 \qquad\text{(C3)}

is exact.

Proof. Left exactness follows from kernels and the definition of support. We prove surjectivity first when XX is compact. A section s′′s^{\prime\prime} has lifts on an open cover because the sheaf map F→F′′F\to F^{\prime\prime} is surjective on stalks. Choose a finite compact closed cover KiK_i subordinate to that cover, and use the corresponding local lifts on KiK_i.

Suppose lifts have already been glued on K1∪⋯∪Ki−1K_1\cup\cdots\cup K_{i-1}. On its intersection with KiK_i, the difference between the existing lift and the new lift is a section of F′F': restriction is exact and sections preserve kernels. The intersection is compact, so c-softness extends that difference to a global section of F′F'. Add this extension to the new lift. The adjusted lifts agree on the intersection, and finite closed gluing combines them. Finite induction gives a lift on all of XX.

For general XX, let s′′s^{\prime\prime} have compact support AA, and choose a relatively compact open UU containing AA. Write FU=j!(F|U)F_U=j_!(F|_U), and similarly for the other two sheaves. Open extension by zero is exact by its stalk formula, so these sheaves give a short exact sequence. Its first term is c-soft by the restriction and extension properties just proved. Each term has zero stalks outside the compact set U¯\overline U.

A sheaf supported on a closed set is the direct image of its restriction to that set: the natural map to that direct image is an isomorphism on every stalk. Restrict our sequence to U¯\overline U, apply the compact case there, and push the resulting section back to XX. All its support is compact. The section s′′s^{\prime\prime} belongs to Γc(X;FU′′)\Gamma_c(X;F^{\prime\prime}_U), since it vanishes near every point outside its compact support A⊂UA\subset U. The compact-case lift is therefore a compactly supported lift of the original s′′s^{\prime\prime}. This proves (C3). ▫\square

Quotient consequence. In a short exact sequence, if F′F' and FF are c-soft, then so is F′′F^{\prime\prime}. Given a section of F′′F^{\prime\prime} on a compact KK, restrict the sequence to KK. Its first term is c-soft there, so the compact case of (C3) lifts that section to Γ(K;F)\Gamma(K;F). C-softness of FF extends the lift globally. Its image is the desired extension of the original section.

The compact-cohomology criterion

We now prove, rather than assume, the equivalence

F is c-soft⟺Hcq(U;F|U)=0for every open U⊂X and q>0.(C4) F\text{ is c-soft}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H_c^q(U;F|_U)=0 \quad\text{for every open }U\subset X\text{ and }q>0. \qquad\text{(C4)}

Use the usual injective definition of the right derived functor of Γc\Gamma_c. If FF is c-soft, embed it into an injective and continue to an injective resolution. Every successive cokernel is c-soft by the quotient consequence. The lifting lemma makes each of the resulting short exact sequences exact after Γc\Gamma_c. Hence the complex of compactly supported sections of the injective resolution is exact in positive degrees. This proves Γc\Gamma_c-acyclicity. Apply the same argument to the c-soft restriction on each open UU to obtain the forward implication in (C4).

We will also use the following derived extension identity, including for a sheaf GG which is not c-soft:

Hcq(X;j!G)=Hcq(U;G),j:U↪X.(C5) H_c^q(X;j_!G)=H_c^q(U;G),\qquad j:U\hookrightarrow X. \qquad\text{(C5)}

Take an injective resolution of GG on UU. Its terms are c-soft, so their open extensions are c-soft on XX and therefore Γc\Gamma_c-acyclic by the preceding paragraph. Exactness of j!j_! makes this an acyclic resolution of j!Gj_!G. Termwise, compactly supported sections on UU and on XX are identical under extension by zero: a compact support in UU is compact in XX, and a compact support of a section of j!Gj_!G lies inside UU. The acyclic-resolution comparison, applied to these degreewise identical complexes, proves (C5).

Here is the comparison needed in that last step. For an acyclic resolution 0→M→A0→A1→⋯0\to M\to A^0\to A^1\to\cdots, put Z1=A0/MZ^1=A^0/M. The first derived-functor exact sequence identifies R1T(M)R^1T(M) with coker⁡(T(A0)→T(Z1))\operatorname{coker}(T(A^0)\to T(Z^1)), which is H1(T(A•))H^1(T(A^\bullet)) because TT preserves kernels. For q≥2q\geq2, the same exact sequence gives RqT(M)=Rq−1T(Z1)R^qT(M)=R^{q-1}T(Z^1). Repeat using the tail resolution of Z1Z^1; after finitely many repetitions this is the same degree-one calculation. Degree zero is left exactness. The connecting maps come from the given short exact sequences, so the identifications are natural. Apply this with T=ΓcT=\Gamma_c.

For the reverse implication in (C4), fix a compact K⊂XK\subset X, put U=X\KU=X\setminus K, and let i:K↪Xi:K\hookrightarrow X, j:U↪Xj:U\hookrightarrow X. Stalks give an exact sequence

0⟶j!(F|U)⟶F⟶i*(F|K)⟶0.(C6) 0\longrightarrow j_!(F|_U)\longrightarrow F \longrightarrow i_*(F|_K)\longrightarrow0. \qquad\text{(C6)}

The long exact sequence for compactly supported cohomology and (C5) show that Γc(X;F)→Γc(X;i*(F|K))\Gamma_c(X;F)\to\Gamma_c(X;i_*(F|_K)) is surjective, because its obstruction lies in Hc1(U;F|U)=0H_c^1(U;F|_U)=0. The group on the right is Γ(K;F)\Gamma(K;F), since KK is compact. Every section on KK thus has a compactly supported global extension, proving c-softness. This completes (C4).

What this proves for proper direct image

Proper support and the fibre calculation

We now supply the proper-image facts used in (C7) and in the projection argument. Let f:Y→Xf:Y\to X be continuous between locally compact Hausdorff spaces. The results in this section hold over an arbitrary fixed commutative coefficient ring kk; finite generation and finite global dimension are unnecessary. The source for the construction is Pierre Schapira’s freely accessible An Introduction to Sheaves on Grothendieck Topologies, 1 August 2026, §§4.1–4.2. We use compactly supported pieces to make the stalk calculation explicit, including its agreement with the ordinary proper-support definition.

A proper map shrinks neighbourhoods of its fibre

Suppose first that p:Z→Xp:Z\to X is proper, meaning that inverse images of compact subsets are compact. Such a map is closed. Indeed, if A⊂ZA\subset Z is closed and x∉p(A)x\notin p(A), choose a compact neighbourhood NN of xx. The set A∩p−1NA\cap p^{-1}N is compact, so its image is compact and hence closed in XX. Removing that image from int⁡N\operatorname{int}N gives a neighbourhood of xx disjoint from p(A)p(A).

Let WW be any open neighbourhood of the compact fibre Zx=p−1(x)Z_x=p^{-1}(x). Closedness of pp shows that V=X\p(Z\W)V=X\setminus p(Z\setminus W) is an open neighbourhood of xx, and p−1V⊂Wp^{-1}V\subset W. Consequently the sets p−1Vp^{-1}V, for VV ranging over neighbourhoods of xx, are cofinal among neighbourhoods of ZxZ_x. The compact-germ comparison (C1) gives

(p*H)x=colim⁡x∈VΓ(p−1V;H)≃Γ(Zx;H|Zx).(F1) (p_*H)_x =\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_{x\in V}\Gamma(p^{-1}V;H) \simeq\Gamma(Z_x;H|_{Z_x}). \qquad\text{(F1)}

This is the restriction map on germs, and is natural in HH. If the fibre is empty, the same closedness argument supplies a neighbourhood with empty inverse image, so both sides are zero. No surjectivity or local triviality of pp was assumed.

The same formula applies when pp is proper only on a closed set supporting HH: write HH as the direct image of its restriction to that closed set and apply the proper case there. In particular it applies to every sheaf supported on a fixed compact set.

Constructing the proper-support subsheaf

For an open U⊂YU\subset Y, let QUQ_U denote the restriction of QQ to UU, extended by zero. Each QU→QQ_U\to Q is monic on stalks. Relatively compact open subsets of YY form a directed family under finite unions. Define the sheaf

f!Q=colim⁡U⋐Yf*(QU),U⋐Y means U¯ is compact.(F2) f_!Q=\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_{U\Subset Y}f_*(Q_U), \qquad U\Subset Y\text{ means }\overline U\text{ is compact}. \qquad\text{(F2)}

The colimit here is a sheaf colimit. The inclusions into f*Qf_*Q identify it with a subsheaf: taking stalks gives a directed union of submodules. Thus a section of f!Qf_!Q over an open V⊂XV\subset X is locally represented by sections of QUQ_U for relatively compact UU. We prove that this is exactly the familiar condition that its support be proper over VV.

Take such a section, viewed as s∈Γ(f−1V;Q)s\in\Gamma(f^{-1}V;Q), and write S=supp⁡(s)S=\operatorname{supp}(s), a closed subset of f−1Vf^{-1}V. Locally on VV, the representative through QUQ_U implies S⊂U¯S\subset\overline U. Over a compact subset CC of that local neighbourhood, the set S∩f−1CS\cap f^{-1}C is closed in YY and contained in the compact set U¯\overline U; it is therefore compact. For a general compact C⊂VC\subset V, choose finitely many compact subsets covering CC, each subordinate to one of those local neighbourhoods. This finite shrinking is available by the compact Hausdorff argument preceding (C1). Their inverse images in SS are compact and cover the inverse image of CC. Thus S→VS\to V is proper.

Conversely, suppose S→VS\to V is proper. For x∈Vx\in V, choose a compact neighbourhood N⊂VN\subset V of xx. Then S∩f−1NS\cap f^{-1}N is compact, so it lies in some relatively compact open U⊂YU\subset Y. On f−1(int⁡N)f^{-1}(\operatorname{int}N), the section has zero germs outside UU. The stalk description of the subsheaf QU⊂QQ_U\subset Q shows that it factors through that subsheaf there. Hence ss locally belongs to a term in (F2), so it is a section of f!Qf_!Q. This proves the claimed identification with proper support.

In particular, when ff itself is proper, every section of f*Qf_*Q satisfies this condition: a closed support over an open of the target remains proper over that open. The natural inclusion is then an isomorphism f!Q=f*Qf_!Q=f_*Q.

The underived fibre formula

Fix x∈Xx\in X and put Yx=f−1(x)Y_x=f^{-1}(x). For every relatively compact open U⊂YU\subset Y, the sheaf QUQ_U is supported on the compact set U¯\overline U. Applying (F1) to that support gives

(f!Q)x≃colim⁡U⋐YΓ(Yx;(QU)|Yx)≃Γc(Yx;Q|Yx).(F3) (f_!Q)_x \simeq\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_{U\Subset Y} \Gamma(Y_x;(Q_U)|_{Y_x}) \simeq\Gamma_c(Y_x;Q|_{Y_x}). \qquad\text{(F3)}

Here are the details of the last isomorphism. By the stalk formula for open extension, (QU)|Yx(Q_U)|_{Y_x} is Q|YxQ|_{Y_x} restricted to U∩YxU\cap Y_x and extended by zero within the fibre. A global section of this sheaf has support closed in YxY_x and contained in U¯∩Yx\overline U\cap Y_x, a compact set. It therefore gives a compactly supported section of Q|YxQ|_{Y_x}. All transition maps are the inclusions of these sections into the same section module.

Conversely, if tt has compact support in YxY_x, that support is compact in YY. Choose a relatively compact open UU containing it. The section has zero germs outside U∩YxU\cap Y_x, so it belongs to Γ(Yx;(QU)|Yx)\Gamma(Y_x;(Q_U)|_{Y_x}). The directed union therefore contains exactly all compactly supported sections. This proves (F3), including injectivity, and proves the previously stated formula (C7). No commutation of unrestricted sections with a colimit was used.

Restriction to the fibre is exact, and compactly supported sections preserve kernels: a section in a kernel has the same support whether regarded in the kernel sheaf or in the containing sheaf. Testing on stalks in (F3) consequently proves that f!f_! is left exact. It also confirms that, for the map to a point, (F2) is exactly Γc\Gamma_c.

Coproducts and open extensions

Compactly supported sections commute with arbitrary coproducts. A section with compact support KK locally uses only finitely many summands; a finite cover of KK gives one finite set of indices which suffices everywhere on KK. Outside KK all its germs are zero. Stalkwise factorization therefore puts the whole section in that finite subsum, and its finitely many components have compact support. Conversely a finite family of compactly supported sections gives such a section of the coproduct. These inverse constructions prove the claim. Inverse image to a fibre preserves coproducts, so (F3) shows that the canonical map

⨁λf!Qλ→∼f!(⨁λQλ)(F4) \bigoplus_\lambda f_!Q_\lambda \xrightarrow{\sim}f_!\left(\bigoplus_\lambda Q_\lambda\right) \qquad\text{(F4)}

is an isomorphism on every stalk. This does not assert that arbitrary sections commute with coproducts.

Let V⊂XV\subset X be open, U=f−1VU=f^{-1}V, and fV:U→Vf_V:U\to V the restricted map. The proper-support description of sections immediately gives (f!Q)|V=(fV)!(Q|U)(f_!Q)|_V=(f_V)_!(Q|_U): over every open subset of VV, the section modules and proper-support conditions are identical.

There is also the extension comparison

(f!Q)V→∼f!(QU),U=f−1V.(F5) (f_!Q)_V\xrightarrow{\sim}f_!(Q_U), \qquad U=f^{-1}V. \qquad\text{(F5)}

Both sides are subsheaves of f!Qf_!Q. The right side is a subsheaf by left exactness. On a stalk inside VV, formula (F3) identifies each inclusion with the identity of Γc(Yx;Q|Yx)\Gamma_c(Y_x;Q|_{Y_x}). On a stalk outside VV, the fibre misses UU, so both sides are zero. Their images in f!Qf_!Q are thus the same subsheaf, proving (F5) canonically.

The stalk identifications Q⊗kU=QUQ\otimes k_U=Q_U and f!Q⊗kV=(f!Q)Vf_!Q\otimes k_V=(f_!Q)_V turn (F5) into the open-generator projection map (P7). It is the map which multiplies a properly supported section by the pulled-back coefficient section. Hence the isomorphism just proved is the specific map used in the projection proof, not merely an unspecified isomorphism of its endpoints.

The derived fibre formula and c-soft acyclicity

Combining (F3) with the compact-support proofs gives, for B∈D+(kY)B\in D^+(k_Y), the natural identity

(Rf!B)x≃RΓc(Yx;B|Yx).(F6) (Rf_!B)_x\simeq R\Gamma_c(Y_x;B|_{Y_x}). \qquad\text{(F6)}

To prove it, choose a bounded-below injective representative II of BB. Stalks are exact, so (Rf!B)x(Rf_!B)_x is represented by (f!I)x(f_!I)_x, which (F3) identifies termwise with Γc(Yx;I|Yx)\Gamma_c(Y_x;I|_{Y_x}). Restriction is exact, hence I|YxI|_{Y_x} represents B|YxB|_{Y_x}. Its terms are c-soft, by injective c-softness and restriction stability, and thus are Γc\Gamma_c-acyclic.

For clarity, a bounded-below complex of acyclic terms computes a right derived functor. In any fixed degree qq, cut the complex off brutally above degree q+1q+1. Swapping homogeneous coefficients gives exactly the Koszul sign. These equalities hold termwise in (P8), commute with differentials and pass to the derived category. Hence this proof supplies the unit, associativity and symmetry compatibilities needed by the pairings (5)–(11).

From compact extension to ordinary cohomology
