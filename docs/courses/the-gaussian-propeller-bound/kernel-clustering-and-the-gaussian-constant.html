Kernel clustering and the Gaussian constant

Written by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), October 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. Public domain (CC0).

Khot and Naor introduced the Gaussian partition problem of this course to determine how well kernel clustering can be approximated [KhNa]. This lesson proves the hardness half of their programme with the identity matrix as target, using the Gaussian propeller bound of Five or more cells and the propeller bound, Theorem 7.1. With \[ \begin{gathered} c_*=\frac9{8\pi},\\ \alpha_k=\frac{1-1/k}{c_*}=\frac{8\pi}9\Bigl(1-\frac1k\Bigr), \end{gathered} \] Khot and Naor's rounding algorithm achieves the loss factor \(\alpha_k\) in expectation, and Theorem 4.1 below shows that no deterministic polynomial-time algorithm achieves a fixed loss factor below \(\alpha_k\) unless \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

Besides Theorem 7.1 (and through it (HJN) and Ehrhard's inequality), the proof uses two further named statements, stated in Section 2: Khot and Naor's bound for functions with low influences (KN-INF) and the Unique Games theorem of OpenAI (UGT). The lemmas that connect them, Lemmas 3.1–3.3, are proved in full.

1. The clustering problem

Fix an integer \(k\ge3\). An input is an explicitly given rational symmetric matrix \(A=(a_{pq})_{p,q=1}^N\) that is positive semidefinite and centered, \(A\mathbf 1=0\), where \(\mathbf 1\) is the all-ones vector. For an assignment \(\sigma:[N]\to[k]\), with \([N]=\{1,\dots,N\}\), \[ \begin{gathered} \operatorname{val}_A(\sigma)=\sum_{p,q=1}^Na_{pq}\,\mathbf 1_{\{\sigma(p)=\sigma(q)\}},\\ \operatorname{Clust}(A)=\max_\sigma\operatorname{val}_A(\sigma). \end{gathered} \] The sum is over ordered pairs, diagonal included, and empty clusters are allowed. With the clusters \(S_i=\sigma^{-1}(i)\), \(\operatorname{val}_A(\sigma)=\sum_i\mathbf 1_{S_i}^{\mathsf T}A\mathbf 1_{S_i}\ge0\). An algorithm has loss factor \(\alpha\ge1\) if it returns an assignment of value at least \(\operatorname{Clust}(A)/\alpha\).

(KN-ROUND) [KhNa, Theorem 2.1, the case of the identity target]. There is a polynomial-time randomized algorithm, a semidefinite relaxation followed by rounding with two or three Gaussian vectors, whose output satisfies \(\mathbb E\operatorname{val}_A(\sigma)\ge\operatorname{Clust}(A)/\alpha_k\).

This statement is quoted to explain the threshold; it is not used in the proof of Theorem 4.1. It is an analysis with ideal semidefinite optimization and exact Gaussian sampling.

2. Fourier analysis on product spaces

Let \(\Omega=[k]\) with the uniform probability measure, and choose an orthonormal basis \(b_0=1,b_1,\dots,b_{k-1}\) of the real functions on \(\Omega\). For \(r\ge1\) and \(\nu\in\{0,\dots,k-1\}^r\) put \[ \begin{gathered} b_\nu(x)=\prod_{j=1}^rb_{\nu_j}(x_j),\\ |\nu|=\#\{j:\nu_j\ne0\}. \end{gathered} \] The \(b_\nu\) form an orthonormal basis of \(L_2(\Omega^r)\) (uniform measure), so every \(h\) has an expansion \(h=\sum_\nu\hat h(\nu)b_\nu\) with \(\sum_\nu\hat h(\nu)^2=\|h\|_2^2\). For real \(D\ge0\) the influence of coordinate \(j\) up to degree \(D\) is \[ \operatorname{Inf}_j^{\le D}(h)=\sum_{|\nu|\le D,\ \nu_j\ne0}\hat h(\nu)^2 . \] Let \(P_1\) be the orthogonal projection onto the span of the \(b_\nu\) with \(|\nu|=1\). For \(f=(f_1,\dots,f_k)\) put \(\operatorname{OBJ}(f)=\sum_i\|P_1f_i\|_2^2\), and let \(\Delta_k=\{u\in\mathbb R^k:u_i\ge0,\ \sum_iu_i\le1\}\).

Lemma 2.1. (a) The degree-one part of \(h\) is \[ \begin{aligned} &(P_1h)(x)\\ &\quad=\sum_{j=1}^r\bigl(\mathbb E[h\mid X_j=x_j]-\mathbb Eh\bigr); \end{aligned} \] as a matrix on the coordinate vector \((h(y))_y\), with \(q=k^r\), \[ (P_1)_{x,y}=\frac1q\sum_{j=1}^r\bigl(k\,\mathbf 1_{\{x_j=y_j\}}-1\bigr). \] (b) For a permutation \(\pi\) of \([r]\) let \((R_\pi x)_a=x_{\pi(a)}\). Then \(\operatorname{Inf}_j^{\le D}(h\circ R_\pi)=\operatorname{Inf}_{\pi^{-1}(j)}^{\le D}(h)\). (c) \(\sum_{a=1}^r\operatorname{Inf}_a^{\le D}(h)\le D\,\|h\|_2^2\).

Proof. (a) For \(\nu_i\ne0\), \(\mathbb Eb_{\nu_i}(X_i)=\langle b_{\nu_i},b_0\rangle=0\). Hence \(\mathbb E[b_\nu(X)\mid X_j=x_j]\) equals \(b_{\nu_j}(x_j)\) if \(\nu\) vanishes off \(j\), and \(0\) otherwise; so \(\mathbb E[h\mid X_j=x_j]-\mathbb Eh\) is the part of the expansion supported on the \(\nu\) with \(\nu_j\ne0\) and \(\nu_i=0\) for \(i\ne j\). Summing over \(j\) gives the degree-one part. In coordinates, \(\mathbb E[h\mid X_j=x_j]=\frac kq\sum_{y:y_j=x_j}h(y)\) and \(\mathbb Eh=\frac1q\sum_yh(y)\).

(b) \(b_\nu(R_\pi x)=\prod_ab_{\nu_a}(x_{\pi(a)})=b_{\nu\circ\pi^{-1}}(x)\), so \(h\circ R_\pi\) has the coefficient \(\hat h(\mu\circ\pi)\) at \(b_\mu\). The map \(\mu\mapsto\mu\circ\pi\) preserves \(|\mu|\), and \(\mu_j\ne0\) if and only if \((\mu\circ\pi)_{\pi^{-1}(j)}\ne0\).

(c) Each coefficient \(\hat h(\nu)^2\) with \(|\nu|\le D\) is counted \(|\nu|\le D\) times. \(\square\)

(KN-INF) [KhNa, Theorem 3.8]. For fixed \(k\) and every \(\eta>0\) there is \(\tau_0\in(0,\frac12)\), independent of \(r\), such that every \(r\ge1\) and every \(f:\Omega^r\to\Delta_k\) with \(\operatorname{Inf}_j^{\le\log(1/\tau_0)}(f_i)\le\tau_0\) for all \(i\in[k]\) and \(j\in[r]\) satisfy \[ \operatorname{OBJ}(f)\le C(k-1,k)+\eta . \]

Here \(C(k-1,k)\) is the supremum of \(\sum|z(A_i)|^2\) over partitions of \(\mathbb R^{k-1}\) into \(k\) cells, in the notation of Gaussian first moments of partitions. Its proof compares low-influence functions with Gaussian functions through the invariance principle of Mossel, O'Donnell and Oleszkiewicz [MOO]. Decreasing \(\tau_0\) only strengthens the hypothesis, so \(\tau_0<\frac12\) is harmless. By Theorem 7.1 and the propeller (\(k\ge3\)), \[ C(k-1,k)=c_*=\frac9{8\pi}.\tag{2.1} \]

Fix \(\eta>0\), take \(\tau_0\) from (KN-INF), choose a rational \(\tau\in(0,\tau_0)\) and put \(D=\max\{1,\lceil\log(1/\tau)\rceil\}\). Influences grow with the degree bound, so (KN-INF) yields the implication (2.2): if \(\operatorname{OBJ}(f)>c_*+\eta\), then \(\operatorname{Inf}_j^{\le D}(f_i)>\tau\) for some \(i\in[k]\) and \(j\in[r]\). Indeed, if every influence up to degree \(D\) were at most \(\tau\), every influence up to degree \(\log(1/\tau_0)\le D\) would be at most \(\tau<\tau_0\).

A Unique Games instance is a bipartite graph \(G=(V,W,E)\), an alphabet \([r]\), and for each edge \(vw\) a permutation \(\pi_{vw}\) of \([r]\). A labeling \(\ell\) of \(V\cup W\) satisfies the edge \(vw\) if \(\ell(v)=\pi_{vw}(\ell(w))\).

(UGT) [UG, Theorem 1.1]. For every fixed \(\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,\frac12)\) there are an integer \(s\ge1\) and a deterministic polynomial-time reduction from 3SAT to explicit unweighted simple bipartite Unique Games instances with alphabet \(\mathbb F_2^s\), each constraint a translation of the alphabet and the edge set nonempty, such that: if the formula is satisfiable, some labeling satisfies at least a fraction \(1-\varepsilon\) of the edges; if it is unsatisfiable, every labeling satisfies at most a fraction \(\delta\). The alphabet size and the degree and constants of the polynomial depend only on \(\varepsilon\) and \(\delta\).

Only fractions of satisfied edges are used; no regularity of the graph is needed.

3. From a game to a matrix

Let \(G=(V,W,E)\) be an instance from (UGT), with alphabet \([r]\), \(r=2^s\), constraints written in the form \(\ell(v)=\pi_{vw}(\ell(w))\), and isolated vertices deleted. Put \(M=|E|\), \(d_v=|N(v)|\) and \(\mu(v)=d_v/M\) for \(v\in V\). Choosing \(v\) with probability \(\mu(v)\) and then a uniform neighbour \(w\) chooses a uniform edge. For a table \(h:W\times\Omega^r\to\mathbb R\) define \[ \begin{gathered} (T_vh)(x)=\frac1{d_v}\sum_{w\in N(v)}h(w,R_{\pi_{vw}}x),\\ Q(h)=\sum_{v\in V}\mu(v)\,\|P_1T_vh\|_2^2 . \end{gathered} \]

Lemma 3.1. For fixed \(k\) and \(r\) one can compute in deterministic polynomial time a rational symmetric matrix \(A\), indexed by \(W\times\Omega^r\), with \[ h^{\mathsf T}Ah=Q(h),\qquad A\succeq0,\qquad A\mathbf 1=0 . \] For every \(\sigma:W\times\Omega^r\to[k]\), with \(F_i(w,x)=\mathbf 1_{\{\sigma(w,x)=i\}}\) and \(F_v=(T_vF_1,\dots,T_vF_k)\), \[ \operatorname{val}_A(\sigma)=\sum_{v\in V}\mu(v)\operatorname{OBJ}(F_v), \] each \(F_v\) maps \(\Omega^r\) to \(\Delta_k\), and every value \(\operatorname{val}_A(\sigma)\) lies in \([0,1]\).

Proof. In coordinates, \(T_v\) has the entries \((T_v)_{x,(w,y)}=\frac1{d_v}\mathbf 1_{\{w\in N(v)\}}\mathbf 1_{\{y=R_{\pi_{vw}}x\}}\). Put \[ A=\frac1q\sum_{v\in V}\mu(v)\,T_v^{\mathsf T}P_1T_v , \] with \(P_1\) the matrix of Lemma 2.1(a). It is rational and symmetric, and \(P_1^2=P_1\) gives \(h^{\mathsf T}Ah=\sum_v\mu(v)\frac1q|P_1T_vh|^2=Q(h)\ge0\); the factor \(\frac1q\) converts the Euclidean norm of the coordinate vector into the \(L_2\) norm of the uniform measure. Also \(T_v\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1\) and \(P_1\mathbf 1=0\), so \(A\mathbf 1=0\). The size is \(|W|k^r\), polynomial for fixed \(k,r\); the entries have denominators dividing \(Mq^2\prod_vd_v\), of polynomial bit length.

Since \(\mathbf 1_{\{\sigma(p)=\sigma(q)\}}=\sum_iF_i(p)F_i(q)\), \(\operatorname{val}_A(\sigma)=\sum_iF_i^{\mathsf T}AF_i\), which is \(\sum_v\mu(v)\operatorname{OBJ}(F_v)\) by the first part. Each \(F_v(x)\) is an average of standard basis vectors, so it lies in \(\Delta_k\) with coordinate sum one, and \[ \begin{aligned} 0\le\operatorname{OBJ}(F_v)&\le\sum_i\|T_vF_i\|_2^2\\ &\le\mathbb E\sum_iT_vF_i=1 , \end{aligned} \] using \(0\le T_vF_i\le1\). Averaging over \(\mu\) gives the last claim. \(\square\)

Lemma 3.2 (completeness). If some labeling satisfies at least a fraction \(1-\varepsilon\) of the edges, then \(\operatorname{Clust}(A)\ge(1-\frac1k)(1-\varepsilon)^2\).

Proof. Let \(\ell\) be such a labeling and set \(\sigma(w,x)=x_{\ell(w)}\). Then \((T_vF_i)(x)=\sum_jp_{v,j}\mathbf 1_{\{x_j=i\}}\), where \(p_{v,j}\) is the fraction of neighbours \(w\) of \(v\) with \(\pi_{vw}(\ell(w))=j\). The degree-one part of \(\mathbf 1_{\{x_j=i\}}\) is \(\mathbf 1_{\{x_j=i\}}-\frac1k\) (Lemma 2.1(a)); these parts for different \(j\) are orthogonal, and \(\sum_i\mathbb E(\mathbf 1_{\{X_j=i\}}-\frac1k)^2=1-\frac1k\). Hence \[ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{OBJ}(F_v)&=\Bigl(1-\frac1k\Bigr)\sum_jp_{v,j}^2\\ &\ge\Bigl(1-\frac1k\Bigr)p_{v,\ell(v)}^2 . \end{aligned} \] The \(\mu\)-average of \(p_{v,\ell(v)}\) is the fraction of edges satisfied by \(\ell\), at least \(1-\varepsilon\). Jensen's inequality and Lemma 3.1 give \(\operatorname{val}_A(\sigma)\ge(1-\frac1k)(1-\varepsilon)^2\). \(\square\)

Lemma 3.3 (soundness). Let \(\eta>0\) with \(c_*+2\eta<1\), and \(\tau,D\) as in (2.2). If every labeling satisfies at most a fraction \(\delta<\eta\tau^2/(4kD)\) of the edges, then \(\operatorname{Clust}(A)\le c_*+2\eta\).

Proof. Suppose \(\operatorname{val}_A(\sigma)>c_*+2\eta\), and let \(V_{\rm good}=\{v:\operatorname{OBJ}(F_v)>c_*+\eta\}\), of \(\mu\)-mass \(\beta\). Since \(\operatorname{OBJ}(F_v)\le1\), Lemma 3.1 gives \(\operatorname{val}_A(\sigma)\le\beta+(1-\beta)(c_*+\eta)\), which is at most \(c_*+\eta+\beta\), so \(\beta>\eta\).

For \(v\in V_{\rm good}\), (2.2) supplies \(i_v\in[k]\) and \(j_v\in[r]\) with \(\operatorname{Inf}_{j_v}^{\le D}(T_vF_{i_v})>\tau\). The Fourier coefficients of \(T_vF_{i_v}\) are averages of those of the functions \(F_{i_v}(w,R_{\pi_{vw}}\cdot)\); by convexity of the square and Lemma 2.1(b), \[ \begin{aligned} &\operatorname{Inf}_{j_v}^{\le D}(T_vF_{i_v})\\ &\quad\le\frac1{d_v}\sum_{w\in N(v)}\operatorname{Inf}^{\le D}_{\pi_{vw}^{-1}(j_v)}\bigl(F_{i_v}(w,\cdot)\bigr). \end{aligned} \] Each term lies in \([0,1]\), because \(0\le F_i\le1\). An average of numbers in \([0,1]\) that exceeds \(\tau\) has more than a fraction \(\tau/2\) of its terms at least \(\tau/2\); otherwise it would be at most \(\frac\tau2+\frac\tau2\).

For \(w\in W\) let \(L(w)\) be the set of \(a\in[r]\) with \(\operatorname{Inf}_a^{\le D}(F_i(w,\cdot))\ge\tau/2\) for some \(i\). By Lemma 2.1(c) and \(\|F_i(w,\cdot)\|_2^2\le1\), \[ \begin{aligned} &|L(w)|\frac\tau2\le\sum_{i=1}^k\sum_{a=1}^r\operatorname{Inf}_a^{\le D}(F_i(w,\cdot))\\ &\quad\le kD . \end{aligned} \] Label each good \(v\) by \(j_v\) and the other left vertices arbitrarily, and give each right vertex \(w\) an independent uniform label from \(L(w)\) (arbitrary if \(L(w)\) is empty). For a good \(v\), more than a fraction \(\tau/2\) of its neighbours have \(\pi_{vw}^{-1}(j_v)\in L(w)\), and on each such edge the constraint holds with probability at least \(1/|L(w)|\ge\tau/(2kD)\). The expected fraction of satisfied edges therefore exceeds \(\eta\cdot\frac\tau2\cdot\frac\tau{2kD}=\frac{\eta\tau^2}{4kD}\), and some labeling attains at least the expectation, contradicting the hypothesis. \(\square\)

4. The threshold

Theorem 4.1. Assume (KN-INF) and (UGT). For each fixed \(k\ge3\) and each fixed \(1\le\alpha<\alpha_k\), it is NP-hard, given a rational centered positive semidefinite matrix \(A\), to find \(\sigma\) with \(\operatorname{val}_A(\sigma)\ge\operatorname{Clust}(A)/\alpha\). In particular a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm with loss factor \(\alpha\) would give \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

Proof. Choose rationals \(\varepsilon\in(0,\frac12)\) and \(\eta>0\) so small that \(c_*+2\eta<1\) and \[ \alpha<\frac{(1-1/k)(1-\varepsilon)^2}{c_*+2\eta}; \] this is possible because \(\alpha<\alpha_k=(1-1/k)/c_*\). Choose \(\tau\) and \(D\) as in (2.2), depending only on \(k\) and \(\eta\), and a rational \(\delta\in(0,\frac12)\) with \(\delta<\eta\tau^2/(4kD)\). Apply (UGT) with \(\varepsilon,\delta\); this fixes \(r=2^s\), so \(k^r\) is a constant. Put \(H=(1-\frac1k)(1-\varepsilon)^2\) and \(S=c_*+2\eta\). Composing with Lemma 3.1 gives, by Lemmas 3.2 and 3.3 and (2.1): if \(\varphi\) is satisfiable, then \(\operatorname{Clust}(A_\varphi)\ge H\); if \(\varphi\) is unsatisfiable, then \(\operatorname{Clust}(A_\varphi)\le S\). By the choice of \(\varepsilon\) and \(\eta\), \(S<H/\alpha\); fix a rational \(\theta\) strictly between them. An algorithm with loss factor \(\alpha\) returns, on satisfiable formulas, an assignment of value at least \(H/\alpha>\theta\), while on unsatisfiable formulas every assignment has value at most \(S<\theta\). The returned value is an exactly computable rational, so comparing it with \(\theta\) decides 3SAT in polynomial time. \(\square\)

Remark 4.2 (randomized algorithms). A polynomial-time randomized algorithm whose expected value is at least \(\operatorname{Clust}(A)/\alpha\) for a fixed \(\alpha<\alpha_k\) would put 3SAT in RP. On the instances above its value \(X\) lies in \([0,1]\) by Lemma 3.1. In the satisfiable case \(\mathbb EX\ge H/\alpha>\theta\), so \(\Pr\{X>\theta\}\ge(H/\alpha-\theta)/(1-\theta)\), a fixed positive constant; in the unsatisfiable case \(X\le S<\theta\) always. A fixed number of repetitions gives one-sided error at most \(\frac12\).

For a general positive semidefinite target matrix in place of the identity, Khot and Naor characterized the Unique Games threshold by geometric and Gaussian partition parameters [KhNa2, Theorem 1.1]; the identity target is the case in which the Gaussian parameter is the constant \(c_*\) of this course.

5. Exercises

5.1 (easy). Show that \(\operatorname{val}_A(\sigma)=\sum_i\mathbf 1_{S_i}^{\mathsf T}A\mathbf 1_{S_i}\) and that for a positive semidefinite \(A\) the centering condition \(A\mathbf 1=0\) is equivalent to \(\sum_{p,q}a_{pq}=0\).

5.2 (medium). In the proof of Lemma 3.3, the functions \(F_i(w,\cdot)\) are indicator functions with \(\sum_iF_i(w,\cdot)=1\). Show that \(\sum_i\|F_i(w,\cdot)\|_2^2=1\), deduce \(|L(w)|\le2D/\tau\), and conclude that Lemma 3.3 holds with \(\delta<\eta\tau^2/(4D)\) in place of \(\delta<\eta\tau^2/(4kD)\).

5.3 (easy). Compute \(\operatorname{OBJ}\) for the dictator function \(f(x)=e_{x_1}\) (the \(x_1\)-th standard basis vector) and compare it with \(c_*\).

5.4 (medium). Show that \(T_v\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1\) and that \(A\mathbf 1=0\) follows.

6. Solutions

5.1. \(\mathbf 1_{\{\sigma(p)=\sigma(q)\}}=\sum_i\mathbf 1_{S_i}(p)\mathbf 1_{S_i}(q)\). If \(A\succeq0\) and \(\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}A\mathbf 1=0\), then \(A^{1/2}\mathbf 1=0\), hence \(A\mathbf 1=0\); the converse is immediate.

5.2. \(\|F_i(w,\cdot)\|_2^2=\mathbb EF_i(w,\cdot)\) for an indicator, and these expectations sum to \(1\). Lemma 2.1(c) then shows that \(|L(w)|\tau/2\) is at most \(\sum_i\sum_a\operatorname{Inf}_a^{\le D}(F_i(w,\cdot))\le D\), so \(|L(w)|\le2D/\tau\), and the probability bound per edge improves to \(\tau/(2D)\). The rest of the proof is unchanged.

5.3. \(f_i(x)=\mathbf 1_{\{x_1=i\}}\) has degree-one part \(\mathbf 1_{\{x_1=i\}}-\frac1k\), so \(\operatorname{OBJ}(f)=k\cdot\frac1k(1-\frac1k)=1-\frac1k\ge\frac23\), well above \(c_*<0.36\). This does not contradict (KN-INF): each \(f_i\) has influence \(\frac1k(1-\frac1k)\) at the first coordinate, more than any small \(\tau_0\). The completeness lemma uses averages of such dictators.

5.4. \((T_v\mathbf 1)(x)=\frac1{d_v}\sum_{w\in N(v)}1=1\). Then \(T_v\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1\) and \(P_1\mathbf 1=0\) (a constant has no degree-one part) give \(A\mathbf 1=\frac1q\sum_v\mu(v)T_v^{\mathsf T}P_1\mathbf 1=0\).
