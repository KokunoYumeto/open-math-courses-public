Strong coupling, simulations and their errors

A lattice expectation is a finite integral, but an integral with many variables can still be difficult to evaluate. Two methods are central: expand it in a parameter, or sample its probability measure. This unit does both in models where the answer and the errors can be proved.

We first solve an open two-dimensional U(1)U(1) lattice exactly. Its link variables admit an explicit change to tree and plaquette coordinates. That gives a Wilson-loop area formula, a strong-coupling expansion with a remainder, and a separate estimate for fine spacings. Periodic boundaries add a global constraint that can also be calculated exactly. An explicit embedding relates the action to a restricted four-dimensional SU(N)SU(N) configuration and identifies the resulting measure map.

We then construct a Metropolis sampler for the original compact finite lattice measure and prove its stationary law, convergence and finite sample bounds. Exact rejection sampling in the solved model makes the effects of correlated updates visible. The final calculation combines the sampling and spacing estimates for one specified observable.

Read lattice gauge theory first. The group measures, loop orientation, Wilson action and physical units are those of Unit 14. The required probability arguments—expectations, variance, covariance, total variation and finite failure bounds—are introduced through the calculations below. Familiarity with convergent series and finite geometric sums is enough for the opening example.

A Monte Carlo update index counts attempted changes to a configuration. It is different from the Euclidean time coordinate in the configuration. We retain the full action and its constants throughout, so each error bound refers to its actual probability measure, coupling, geometry and observable.

By the end, you can evaluate an exact loop expectation, identify the domain of an expansion, implement the specified sampling rule, keep its rejections, and calculate how sampling, volume and spacing affect a chosen expectation.

5. The precise action map into the four-dimensional question

Both equalities follow by the displayed diagonal entries. On a periodic four-dimensional lattice, copy a two-dimensional (0,1)-link configuration identically to every (2,3) slice through ι\iota and set every 2- and 3-link to the identity. The map intertwines two-dimensional U(1)U(1) vertex gauge maps with their similarly copied SU(N)SU(N) maps. All mixed and (2,3) plaquettes are identity. Every (0,1) plaquette is ι(P2)\iota(P_2) and occurs n2n3n_2n_3 times. The exact restricted action is therefore

This proves an actual relation preserving the original actions, defining traces, orientations, spacings and couplings. For an embedded loop, tr⁡SU(N)UC=(N−2)+UCU(1)+(UCU(1))−1\operatorname{tr}_{SU(N)}U_C=(N-2)+U_C^{U(1)}+(U_C^{U(1)})^{-1}. The divided trace would be this expression/N; it has not replaced the original trace.

The measure map is different and can be proved precisely. Pushforward of the two-dimensional Haar/product weighted measure is supported on the copied configurations, where all transverse links equal identity. Each identity singleton has Haar measure zero in SU(N)SU(N): in a positive- dimensional smooth group chart the Haar volume has a smooth positive density relative to Lebesgue measure, and a singleton has zero volume.
