Differences through the physical temporal gauge

This Unit 9 analytic chapter carries estimates for two connections through their actual anchored gauge maps. It computes every first and second spatial derivative, the inverse terms, and the receiving physical connection and curvature differences. The resulting local Lipschitz bounds preserve the original homogeneous representatives.

Read the physical gauge construction, GO.1–GO.39, and temporal-boundary differences, TD.1–TD.27. Section 5 gives an exact commuting connection example. It illustrates the gauge map without claiming to solve the evolution equation.

Human-source context: Sung-Jin Oh, Gauge choice for the Yang–Mills equations using the Yang–Mills heat flow and local well-posedness in H1, arXiv:1210.1558v2. The proofs here use the complete linked course arguments and are independent exposition. They make no novelty claim. Exact source versions and bounded reading are retained in the course provenance.

1. Two original connections and their anchored maps

Let aa and a′a' be two regular connections on the original I×ℝ3I\times\mathbb R^3, at heat boundary s=0s=0. Set b=atb=a_t and b′=at′b'=a_t'. Take the same t*∈It_*\in I and the GO solutions

∂tU=Ub,∂tU′=U′b′,U(t*)=U′(t*)=IN,Z=U−U′,δb=b−b′. \partial_t U=Ub,\qquad \partial_t U'=U'b',\qquad U(t_*)=U'(t_*)=I_N,\qquad Z=U-U',\quad \delta b=b-b'. \tag{GD.1}
(GD.1)

The original group is G⊂U(N)G\subset U(N). An undifferentiated matrix gauge has operator norm one. Every differentiated matrix and every Lie-algebra field has the Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Spatial tuples contain every ordered index; no diagonal or off-diagonal Hessian entry is dropped. All norms below are on the original ℝ3\mathbb R^3. For JtJ_t the unoriented interval between t*t_* and tt, retain GO’s actual integrals

P=∫Jt∥∂b(r)∥3dr,Q=∫Jt∥∂b(r)∥6dr,Hb=∫Jt∥∂(2)b(r)∥2dr,P′=∫Jt∥∂b′(r)∥3dr,Q′=∫Jt∥∂b′(r)∥6dr,Hb′=∫Jt∥∂(2)b′(r)∥2dr,D0=∫Jt∥δb(r)∥∞;opdr,D3=∫Jt∥∂δb(r)∥3dr,D6=∫Jt∥∂δb(r)∥6dr,D2=∫Jt∥∂(2)δb(r)∥2dr. \begin{aligned} P&=\int_{J_t}\|\partial b(r)\|_3\,dr,& Q&=\int_{J_t}\|\partial b(r)\|_6\,dr,& H_b&=\int_{J_t}\|\partial^{(2)}b(r)\|_2\,dr,\\ P'&=\int_{J_t}\|\partial b'(r)\|_3\,dr,& Q'&=\int_{J_t}\|\partial b'(r)\|_6\,dr,& H_b'&=\int_{J_t}\|\partial^{(2)}b'(r)\|_2\,dr,\\ D_0&=\int_{J_t}\|\delta b(r)\|_{\infty;\mathrm{op}}\,dr,& D_3&=\int_{J_t}\|\partial\delta b(r)\|_3\,dr,& D_6&=\int_{J_t}\|\partial\delta b(r)\|_6\,dr,\\ D_2&=\int_{J_t}\|\partial^{(2)}\delta b(r)\|_2\,dr. \end{aligned}\tag{GD.2}
(GD.2)

These are finite for each of the actual regular connections and also for the boundary domains of GO.4. Indeed the difference norms are at most the sums of the corresponding two individual norms. The exact GO.6–GO.7 Sobolev proof, applied to the difference, gives Q≤CSHbQ\le C_SH_b, Q′≤CSHb′Q'\le C_SH_b', and D6≤CSD2D_6\le C_SD_2, with CS=4/3C_S=4/\sqrt3. It is not necessary to replace the sharper actual integrals by these upper bounds. The primed objects are a second connection, not derivatives.

2. Unitary variation, including the backward time direction

Matrix differentiation gives the exact identity

∂t(U(U′)−1)=Uδb(U′)−1,U(U′)−1−IN=∫t*tU(r)δb(r)(U′(r))−1dr. \partial_t(U(U')^{-1})=U\delta b(U')^{-1},\qquad U(U')^{-1}-I_N=\int_{t_*}^{t}U(r)\delta b(r)(U'(r))^{-1}\,dr. \tag{GD.3}
(GD.3)

Both integrals are oriented. Unitary multiplication preserves the operator norm, and Z=(U(U′)−1−IN)U′Z=(U(U')^{-1}-I_N)U'. Consequently sup⁡r∈Jt∥Z(r)∥∞;op≤D0\sup_{r\in J_t}\|Z(r)\|_{\infty;\mathrm{op}}\le D_0. This estimate has no exponential in the individual sizes of bb or b′b'.

The equation for ZZ and its complete differentiated equations are

∂tZ=Zb′+Uδb,∂t∂iZ=(∂iZ)b′+Z∂ib′+(∂iU)δb+U∂iδb,∂t∂j∂iZ=(∂j∂iZ)b′+(∂iZ)∂jb′+(∂jZ)∂ib′+Z∂j∂ib′+(∂j∂iU)δb+(∂iU)∂jδb+(∂jU)∂iδb+U∂j∂iδb. \begin{aligned} \partial_tZ&=Zb'+U\delta b,\\ \partial_t\partial_iZ&=(\partial_iZ)b' +Z\partial_i b'+(\partial_iU)\delta b+U\partial_i\delta b,\\ \partial_t\partial_j\partial_iZ&=(\partial_j\partial_iZ)b' +(\partial_iZ)\partial_jb'+(\partial_jZ)\partial_i b' +Z\partial_j\partial_i b'\\ &\quad +(\partial_j\partial_iU)\delta b +(\partial_iU)\partial_j\delta b +(\partial_jU)\partial_i\delta b +U\partial_j\partial_i\delta b . \end{aligned}\tag{GD.4}
(GD.4)

No matrix factors commute in these identities. Every initial spatial derivative of ZZ is zero. For any equation Yt=Yb′+FY_t=Yb'+F with zero anchored datum, differentiation of Y(U′)−1Y(U')^{-1} gives F(U′)−1F(U')^{-1}; hence Y(t)=(∫t*tF(r)(U′(r))−1dr)U′(t)Y(t)=(\int_{t_*}^tF(r)(U'(r))^{-1}dr)U'(t). This proves the norm bound by the unoriented integral of ∥F∥\|F\| for both time directions and for every spatial tuple and indicated exponent. It justifies each following use of variation of constants without a Grönwall loss from the undifferentiated b′b' term.

Define the following explicit numerical quantities at the given tt:

z3=D3+D0(P+P′),z6=D6+D0(Q+Q′),z2=D2+D0(Hb′+Hb+PQ)+2z3Q′+2PD6. \begin{aligned} z_3&=D_3+D_0(P+P'),\\ z_6&=D_6+D_0(Q+Q'),\\ z_2&=D_2+D_0(H_b'+H_b+PQ)+2z_3Q'+2PD_6. \end{aligned}\tag{GD.5}
(GD.5)

GO.13 and GO.18–GO.22 give throughout JtJ_t ∥∂U∥3≤P\|\partial U\|_3\le P, ∥∂U∥6≤Q\|\partial U\|_6\le Q, and ∥∂(2)U∥2≤Hb+PQ\|\partial^{(2)}U\|_2\le H_b+PQ. The first derivative equation in GD.4 therefore gives z3z_3 and z6z_6, using spatial Hölder with δb\delta b in operator L∞L^\infty. For the Hessian equation, its two terms with ∂Z\partial Z and ∂b′\partial b' have full tuple norm at most 2∥∂Z∥3∥∂b′∥62\|\partial Z\|_3\|\partial b'\|_6. This follows pointwise from ∑i,j|XiYj|2≤|X|2|Y|2\sum_{i,j}|X_iY_j|^2\le |X|^2|Y|^2 and the triangle inequality for the two distinct placements. Its two terms with ∂U\partial U and ∂δb\partial\delta b have bound 2P∥∂δb∥62P\|\partial\delta b\|_6. The other three forcing contributions are exactly D2D_2, D0Hb′D_0H_b', and D0(Hb+PQ)D_0(H_b+PQ) after integration. Thus

supr∈Jt∥Z(r)∥∞;op≤D0,supr∈Jt∥∂Z(r)∥3≤z3,supr∈Jt∥∂Z(r)∥6≤z6,supr∈Jt∥∂(2)Z(r)∥2≤z2. \begin{aligned} \sup_{r\in J_t}\|Z(r)\|_{\infty;\mathrm{op}}&\le D_0,\\ \sup_{r\in J_t}\|\partial Z(r)\|_3&\le z_3,\qquad \sup_{r\in J_t}\|\partial Z(r)\|_6\le z_6,\\ \sup_{r\in J_t}\|\partial^{(2)}Z(r)\|_2&\le z_2. \end{aligned}\tag{GD.6}
(GD.6)

The same exact identities hold with the two connections exchanged. Every resulting bound therefore holds independently. Also unitarity and the triangle inequality give the upper bound two for the operator norm of their difference, so its upper bound can be reduced to the minimum of two and the integral bound. Section 8 derives the stronger linear dependence needed in the receiving physical map.

All coefficients on the right are evaluated on the larger JtJ_t, so each also bounds the intermediate subinterval needed inside an integral. This supplies the suprema used in the Hessian argument.

3. The logarithmic derivative and its full derivative

(GD.7)

4. The receiving physical connection and curvature

Write δa=a−a′\delta a=a-a' for the spatial connections at heat boundary. At any rr in JtJ_t, set Bi=UaiU−1B_i=Ua_iU^{-1}, Bi′=U′ai′(U′)−1B_i'=U'a_i'(U')^{-1} and Ai=Bi−KiA_i=B_i-K_i, Ai′=Bi′−Ki′A_i'=B_i'-K_i'. (GD.9)

For clarity the second identity is obtained by first differentiating BiB_i to get U(∂jai)U−1+[Kj,Bi]U(\partial_j a_i)U^{-1}+[K_j,B_i], and then subtracting the primed identity. It contains all derivatives of both inverse matrices. The bracket bound is the original coefficient two in Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Therefore, at every such rr,

∥A−A′∥6≤∥δa∥6+2D0∥a′∥6+k6,∥∂(A−A′)∥2≤∥∂δa∥2+2D0∥∂a′∥2+2k3∥a∥6+2P′(∥δa∥6+2D0∥a′∥6)+k2. \begin{aligned} \|A-A'\|_6 &\le\|\delta a\|_6+2D_0\|a'\|_6+k_6,\\ \|\partial(A-A')\|_2 &\le\|\partial\delta a\|_2+2D_0\|\partial a'\|_2 +2k_3\|a\|_6 +2P'(\|\delta a\|_6+2D_0\|a'\|_6)+k_2. \end{aligned}\tag{GD.10}
(GD.10)

The full gradient and vector tuple norms use the same contraction proof as GD.4, with no factor depending on the number of entries. All spatial connection norms here are evaluated at rr; D0D_0 and the numerical coefficients are evaluated on JtJ_t. Taking their suprema preserves the bound. Finally let Ei=Ftia(r,0)E_i=F^a_{ti}(r,0) and Ei′=Ftia′(r,0)E_i'=F^{a'}_{ti}(r,0), and retain the three-entry magnetic tuple M=(F23a,F31a,F12a)M=(F^a_{23},F^a_{31},F^a_{12}), with M′M' defined in the same order. GO.35 and covariance give

(GD.11)

Dividing only the displayed electric norm inequality by its original physical speed cc gives the corresponding c−1c^{-1}-weighted energy comparison. The electric field itself is unchanged. (GD.12)

Here the fixed D0D_0 for JtJ_t bounds each intermediate subinterval. Thus the inhomogeneous difference domain is preserved when it is present; GD.10 itself requires no undifferentiated L2L^2 connection.

5. An exact commuting example of the map and its difference

Fix an original spatial length ℓ>0\ell>0 and put f(x)=exp⁡(−|x|2/ℓ2)f(x)=\exp(-|x|^2/\ell^2), T=diag⁡(i,−i)T=\operatorname{diag}(i,-i). Let λ\lambda and λ′\lambda' be real integrable functions of the original physical time, with unit inverse time. For the two connections set ai=ai′=0a_i=a_i'=0, b=λfTb=\lambda fT and b′=λ′fTb'=\lambda'fT. Write L(t)=∫t*tλ(r)drL(t)=\int_{t_*}^t\lambda(r)dr and L′(t)L'(t) for its primed counterpart. No spatial or time coordinate has been changed. All matrices in either time series commute in this example, and direct differentiation gives

(GD.13)

In particular ∂tA=E\partial_tA=E, and all spatial curvatures vanish because the mixed derivatives of ff commute and the brackets of TT with itself are zero. These are connection examples for the proved gauge map; they are not asserted to solve the Yang–Mills evolution equations. 6. What this calculation gives the next argument

Every right side is explicit in the two original boundary connections, the electric and magnetic fields, and the actual difference of bb and its derivatives. On a family with bounded individual GO norms, D0,D3,D6,D2D_0,D_3,D_6,D_2 tending to zero makes z3,z6,z2z_3,z_6,z_2 and k3,k6,k2k_3,k_6,k_2 tend to zero. Equations GD.10–GD.12 then carry convergence of the displayed caloric fields into convergence of the physical fields. This is continuity of the proved gauge map, not yet convergence of the nonlinear caloric solutions. The corresponding differences of TB.1–TB.8 have now been proved in TD.1–TD.27 and are evaluated in Section 7 below. The remaining nonlinear wave and initial-data difference estimates must still be derived from the two original evolution equations.

7. Evaluation with the complete temporal-boundary difference

8. The stronger linear dependence and its evaluated receiving bound

The same exact maps have a stronger linear bound. We derive it here and then insert the complete temporal-boundary estimates. On the actual JtJ_t define the finite individual maxima

9. Exercises with full solutions

Exercise 1. The difference of the two inverse matrices

Prove U−1−(U′)−1=−U−1Z(U′)−1U^{-1}-(U')^{-1}=-U^{-1}Z(U')^{-1}.

Solution. Substitute Z=U−U′Z=U-U' on the right. The result is −(U′)−1+U−1-(U')^{-1}+U^{-1}, in the displayed order. Multiplication on either side by a unitary matrix preserves the operator norm, so the inverse difference has exactly the same norm as ZZ. No commutation of the original matrices is used.

Exercise 2. The backward time interval

Derive the variation-of-constants bound when t<t*t<t_*.

Solution. Differentiating Y(U′)−1Y(U')^{-1} gives F(U′)−1F(U')^{-1}, so the anchored solution is Y(t)=(∫t*tF(r)(U′(r))−1dr)U′(t)Y(t)=(\int_{t_*}^tF(r)(U'(r))^{-1}dr)U'(t). Reversing the integration limits changes the sign, while taking the norm gives ∥Y(t)∥≤∫tt*∥F(r)∥dr\|Y(t)\|\le\int_t^{t_*}\|F(r)\|dr. This is precisely the integral on JtJ_t. It leaves the original ODE and the orientation of its exact solution unchanged.

Exercise 3. Keep the logarithmic derivative order

Differentiate Ki=(∂iU)U−1K_i=(\partial_iU)U^{-1} in xjx_j.

Solution. The inverse derivative is ∂jU−1=−U−1(∂jU)U−1\partial_jU^{-1}=-U^{-1}(\partial_jU)U^{-1}. The product rule gives ∂jKi=(∂j∂iU)U−1−KiKj\partial_jK_i=(\partial_j\partial_iU)U^{-1}-K_iK_j. The final product is KiKjK_iK_j; exchanging it would generally change a noncommuting matrix. Subtracting the primed identity gives the last line of GD.7.

Exercise 4. Why both Hessian placements contribute

Bound the two terms (∂iZ)∂jb′+(∂jZ)∂ib′(\partial_iZ)\partial_jb'+(\partial_jZ)\partial_i b' in the full L2L^2 Hessian tuple.

Solution. For the first placement, the squared pointwise tuple norm is at most ∑i,j|∂iZ|2|∂jb′|2=|∂Z|2|∂b′|2\sum_{i,j}|\partial_iZ|^2|\partial_jb'|^2 =|\partial Z|^2|\partial b'|^2. Hölder gives its Lx3L^3_x times Lx6L^6_x bound. The other placement has the same bound and remains a separate term. Their sum is at most 2∥∂Z∥3∥∂b′∥62\|\partial Z\|_3\|\partial b'\|_6, the coefficient used in GD.5.

Exercise 5. The exact unitary chord

Evaluate the example at phases π/6\pi/6 and 5π/65\pi/6.

Solution. Their first diagonal entries differ by 3\sqrt3, since both imaginary parts are 1/21/2, while the real parts are 3/2\sqrt3/2 and −3/2-\sqrt3/2. The second entries are conjugates and give the same modulus. Hence the operator norm is 3\sqrt3. The integral coefficient bound is the original phase difference 2π/32\pi/3. The inequality also follows from 2|sin⁡(δ/2)|≤|δ|2|\sin(\delta/2)|\le|\delta|.

Exercise 6. The upper bound two and the two reference fields

Derive the minimum in the curvature estimate GD.21.

Solution. Unitarity and the triangle inequality give ∥U−U′∥op≤2\|U-U'\|_{\rm op}\le2; the temporal estimate gives ∥U−U′∥op≤ldb\|U-U'\|_{\rm op}\le\sqrt l\,d_b. Exchanging the connections gives the same first term and 2dcap∥F∥22d_{\rm cap}\|F\|_2. Both bounds hold, so their minimum is the complete expression in GD.21, with its original electric speed factor.

Exercise 7. Recover the coefficient eight

Find both contributions to 8P*V*D08P_*V_*D_0 in GD.19.

Solution. In GD.9 the bracket with δK\delta K contributes 2V*(D3+2P*D0)2V_*(D_3+2P_*D_0), including 4P*V*D04P_*V_*D_0. Exercise 8. Preserve the original anchored difference

Evaluate the connection difference and every gauge-error coefficient at t=t*t=t_*.
