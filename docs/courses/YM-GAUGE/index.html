Geometry and states in Yang–Mills theory

Begin with fields, electromagnetism and symmetry. Build the geometry and classical dynamics, then the quantum foundations needed to understand Yang–Mills theory and its literature.

The course has eighteen units, with mathematical preparation introduced in stages. The eighteen full lessons are complete. Sources are selected for their relevance, reliability and teaching value. No research project determines the course’s answer or receives a reserved place.

Start reading

1. Fields, coordinates and physical quantities — scalar and vector fields, units, derivatives, coordinate changes, initial data, an exactly solved transport equation, and eight exercises with full solutions.

2. Electromagnetism and gauge freedom — fields and sources, Maxwell equations, potentials, local and global gauge questions, waves, energy flux, and eight exercises with full solutions.

3. Symmetry through matrices — complex phases, matrix arithmetic, unitary actions, changing frames, electromagnetic covariance, global gauge classes, and eight exercises with full solutions.

4. Lie groups and Lie algebras — convergent matrix series, local coordinates, tangent generators, SU(2) and SU(3), rotation coverings, invariant inner products, and eight exercises with full solutions.

5. Covariant derivatives and curvature — local frames, complete curvature formulas, differential forms, Bianchi, electromagnetic conventions, nonabelian examples, and eight exercises with full solutions.

6. Bundles, parallel transport and holonomy — overlapping frames, exact transport, Wilson loops, curvature variations, the Hopf bundle, and eight exercises with full solutions.

7. The Yang–Mills action and field equations — metrics, full variations and boundary terms, Maxwell comparison, scalar matter and current, and eight exercises with full solutions.

8. Constraints, initial data and energy — Gauss constraint, actual temporal gauge, full stress and energy derivations, matter exchange, boundary flux, and eight exercises with full solutions.

9. Local and global classical evolution — Fourier and Sobolev preparation, local construction, constraints, finite propagation, the complete heat and wave analysis, regular continuation, global finite-energy evolution in its stated class, and eight main exercises with full solutions. Thirty-six linked analysis chapters supply the full proof and 289 additional solutions.

10. Euclidean Yang–Mills and instantons — Hodge star, self-duality, the exact action bound, explicit instanton, smooth sphere bundle, charge, gauge classes, eight main solutions and a complete operator companion with four additional solutions.

11. Oscillators and free quantum fields — states, probabilities, exact oscillator solution, finite Fourier modes, Fock space, vacuum energies, zero modes, domains, spectra and eight complete solutions.

12. Quantum gauge symmetry — physical states, exact finite constraints, compact-group averaging, gauge slices, ghosts, BRST, finite Ward identities and eight complete solutions.

13. Renormalization and asymptotic freedom — exact gauge and ghost coefficient, heat-kernel proof, subtraction, scale maps and eight complete solutions.

14. Yang–Mills theory on a lattice — original Wilson action, quantified spacing error, finite Haar measure, positive transfer, spectral logarithm, reflection and eight complete solutions.

15. Strong coupling, simulations and their errors — exact Wilson loops, controlled strong coupling, finite-volume and spacing bounds, correct sampling, autocorrelation and eight complete solutions.

16. Spectra, mass gaps and confinement — exact spectral measures, vacuum subtraction, physical units, charged-source maps, screening, regulator limits and eight complete solutions.

17. Continuum limits and reconstruction — actual Gaussian measure, reflection quotient, fields and domains, state maps, exact infrared limits and eight complete solutions.

18. The established research landscape and open problems — methods, original-source comparisons, full two-dimensional and finite constructions, and eight complete solutions.

The route through the subject

  1. Starting points: Fields, coordinates and physical quantities; Electromagnetism and gauge freedom; Symmetry through matrices.
  2. Gauge geometry: Lie groups and Lie algebras; Covariant derivatives and curvature; Bundles, parallel transport and holonomy.
  3. Classical dynamics: The Yang-Mills action and field equations; Constraints, initial data and energy.
  4. Classical analysis and topology: Local and global classical evolution; Euclidean Yang-Mills and instantons.
  5. Quantum foundations: From oscillators to quantum fields; Gauge symmetry in the quantum theory.
  6. Quantum scales: Renormalization and asymptotic freedom.
  7. Lattice theory and observables: Yang-Mills theory on a lattice; Strong coupling, simulations and their errors; Spectra, mass gaps and confinement.
  8. Continuum theory and the literature: Continuum limits and reconstruction; The established research landscape and open problems.
