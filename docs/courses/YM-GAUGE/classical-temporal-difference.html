Differences at the temporal heat boundary

This Unit 9 analytic chapter compares the temporal components of two actual connections. It subtracts the full covariant equations, proves two energy estimates, and integrates the original heat equation to the physical boundary. The resulting four norms are exactly those needed to compare the physical temporal gauges.

Read the one-connection boundary proof, TB.1–TB.11, and the physical gauge construction, GO.1–GO.39. The order of the physical and heat norms matters throughout. A difference of fields is used inside every norm.

Human-source context: Sung-Jin Oh, Gauge choice for the Yang–Mills equations using the Yang–Mills heat flow and local well-posedness in H1, arXiv:1210.1558v2. The proofs here use the complete linked course arguments and are independent exposition. They make no novelty claim. Exact source versions and bounded reading are retained in the course provenance.

1. Both original connections and every difference input

Write the two connections as aa and a′a'; a prime labels the second connection and never a derivative. Their original speed is the same c>0c>0, and both have as=as′=0a_s=a_s'=0 and at(S)=at′(S)=0a_t(S)=a_t'(S)=0. Define

DjX=∂jX+[aj,X],Dj′X=∂jX+[aj′,X],Ej=Ftja,Ej′=Ftja′,Gj=Fsja,Gj′=Fsja′,W=Fsta,W′=Fsta′,ηj=aj−aj′,V=W−W′,δE=E−E′,δG=G−G′,ϑ(s)=at(s)−at′(s). \begin{aligned} D_jX&=\partial_jX+[a_j,X],& D_j'X&=\partial_jX+[a_j',X],\\ E_j&=F_{tj}^{a},&E_j'&=F_{tj}^{a'},\\ G_j&=F_{sj}^{a},&G_j'&=F_{sj}^{a'},\\ W&=F_{st}^{a},&W'&=F_{st}^{a'},\\ \eta_j&=a_j-a_j',&V&=W-W',\\ \delta E&=E-E',&\delta G&=G-G',\\ \vartheta(s)&=a_t(s)-a_t'(s). \end{aligned}\tag{TD.1}
(TD.1)

All these fields remain in the original matrix representation. Every derivative tuple contains all ordered spatial indices and every output component. The matrix norm is Hilbert–Schmidt, with |[X,Y]|≤2|X||Y||[X,Y]|\le2|X||Y|. Both spatial connections are anti-Hermitian, so DjD_j and Dj′D_j' satisfy covariant integration by parts in the real Hilbert–Schmidt pairing. The physical measure is dtd3xdt\,d^3x; the heat measure is ds/sds/s only where stated.

For r=0,1r=0,1, retain the actual weighted spatial coefficient norms

Ar=sup0<s≤Ssr/2+1/4∥∂x(r)ax(s)∥Lt,x∞,Ar′=sup0<s≤Ssr/2+1/4∥∂x(r)ax′(s)∥Lt,x∞,δAr=sup0<s≤Ssr/2+1/4∥∂x(r)η(s)∥Lt,x∞. \begin{aligned} A_r&=\sup_{0<s\le S}s^{r/2+1/4} \|\partial_x^{(r)}a_x(s)\|_{L^\infty_{t,x}},\\ A_r'&=\sup_{0<s\le S}s^{r/2+1/4} \|\partial_x^{(r)}a_x'(s)\|_{L^\infty_{t,x}},\\ \delta A_r&=\sup_{0<s\le S}s^{r/2+1/4} \|\partial_x^{(r)}\eta(s)\|_{L^\infty_{t,x}}. \end{aligned}\tag{TD.2}
(TD.2)

The already proved UrU_r for each individual connection bound its ArA_r. The difference norms in TD.2 are actual norms of the difference; they are not declared small or bounded by an initial-data distance.

The four individual and two difference heat-space-time inputs are

e=∥s1/4∥E(s)∥Lt,x4∥L2(ds/s),e′=∥s1/4∥E′(s)∥Lt,x4∥L2(ds/s),g=∥s3/4∥G(s)∥Lt,x4∥L2(ds/s),g′=∥s3/4∥G′(s)∥Lt,x4∥L2(ds/s),δe=∥s1/4∥δE(s)∥Lt,x4∥L2(ds/s),δg=∥s3/4∥δG(s)∥Lt,x4∥L2(ds/s). \begin{aligned} e&=\|s^{1/4}\|E(s)\|_{L^4_{t,x}}\|_{L^2(ds/s)},& e'&=\|s^{1/4}\|E'(s)\|_{L^4_{t,x}}\|_{L^2(ds/s)},\\ g&=\|s^{3/4}\|G(s)\|_{L^4_{t,x}}\|_{L^2(ds/s)},& g'&=\|s^{3/4}\|G'(s)\|_{L^4_{t,x}}\|_{L^2(ds/s)},\\ \delta e&=\|s^{1/4}\|\delta E(s)\|_{L^4_{t,x}}\|_{L^2(ds/s)},& \delta g&=\|s^{3/4}\|\delta G(s)\|_{L^4_{t,x}}\|_{L^2(ds/s)}. \end{aligned}\tag{TD.3}
(TD.3)

They are finite for the current two regular connections; individual upper bounds were already proved in ST, CF, HS and FC. The two difference norms are also finite by their triangle inequalities. Finiteness is not being substituted for a quantitative difference estimate from the original data.

TB.1–TB.3 for the second connection gives the explicit numbers

2. The exact two-connection equation

The two original equations and their actual Gauss data are

(∂s−∑jDjDj)W=2∑j[Ej,Gj],W(0)=0,(∂s−∑jDj′Dj′)W′=2∑j[Ej′,Gj′],W′(0)=0. \begin{aligned} (\partial_s-\sum_jD_jD_j)W&=2\sum_j[E_j,G_j],&W(0)&=0,\\ (\partial_s-\sum_jD_j'D_j')W'&=2\sum_j[E_j',G_j'],&W'(0)&=0. \end{aligned}\tag{TD.5}
(TD.5)

In particular V(0)=0V(0)=0, regardless of whether the original spatial and electric data of the two connections agree. This is the common zero Gauss heat datum, not an assumed equality of the two solutions.

For any matrix field X the exact operator difference is

∑j(DjDj−Dj′Dj′)X=2∑j[ηj,∂jX]+[∑j∂jηj,X]+∑j[ηj,[aj,X]]+∑j[aj′,[ηj,X]]. \begin{aligned} \sum_j(D_jD_j-D_j'D_j')X ={}&2\sum_j[\eta_j,\partial_jX] +[\sum_j\partial_j\eta_j,X]\\ &+\sum_j[\eta_j,[a_j,X]] +\sum_j[a_j',[\eta_j,X]]. \end{aligned}\tag{TD.6}
(TD.6)

To verify this, expand each DjDjXD_jD_jX as

∂j2X+2[aj,∂jX]+[∂jaj,X]+[aj,[aj,X]], \partial_j^2X+2[a_j,\partial_jX] +[\partial_ja_j,X]+[a_j,[a_j,X]],

then subtract the primed expansion. The nested difference is exactly

[aj,[aj,X]]−[aj′,[aj′,X]]=[ηj,[aj,X]]+[aj′,[ηj,X]]. [a_j,[a_j,X]]-[a_j',[a_j',X]] =[\eta_j,[a_j,X]]+[a_j',[\eta_j,X]].

Expanding aj=aj′+ηja_j=a_j'+\eta_j also displays its three terms

[ηj,[aj′,X]]+[aj′,[ηj,X]]+[ηj,[ηj,X]]; [\eta_j,[a_j',X]]+[a_j',[\eta_j,X]] +[\eta_j,[\eta_j,X]];

the quadratic difference term has not been removed by using the actual first connection in TD.6.

Set

Qδ=2∑j([δEj,Gj]+[Ej′,δGj]),Hη=2∑j[ηj,∂jW′]+[∑j∂jηj,W′]+∑j[ηj,[aj,W′]]+∑j[aj′,[ηj,W′]],H=Hη+Qδ. \begin{aligned} Q_\delta&=2\sum_j([\delta E_j,G_j]+[E_j',\delta G_j]),\\ H_\eta&=2\sum_j[\eta_j,\partial_jW'] +[\sum_j\partial_j\eta_j,W']\\ &\quad+\sum_j[\eta_j,[a_j,W']] +\sum_j[a_j',[\eta_j,W']],\\ H&=H_\eta+Q_\delta. \end{aligned}\tag{TD.7}
(TD.7)

The electric difference is exact because

[Ej,Gj]−[Ej′,Gj′]=[δEj,Gj]+[Ej′,δGj]. [E_j,G_j]-[E_j',G_j']=[\delta E_j,G_j]+[E_j',\delta G_j].

It equivalently has all three terms [δEj,Gj′]+[Ej′,δGj]+[δEj,δGj][\delta E_j,G_j']+[E_j',\delta G_j]+[\delta E_j,\delta G_j]. No difference-product term or contracted index is absent. Subtraction of TD.5 now proves the full equation

(∂s−∑jDjDj)V=H,V(0)=0. (\partial_s-\sum_jD_jD_j)V=H,\qquad V(0)=0. \tag{TD.8}
(TD.8)

The original cc does not occur explicitly in TD.5–TD.8, just as in TB.1. The electric field remains FtjF_{tj}, rather than c−1Ftjc^{-1}F_{tj}; all its original speed dependence remains in the actual e inputs and their proved providers. No physical coordinate or field has been rescaled.

3. Every new forcing term is integrable with its original weight

Physical Hölder, full-index Cauchy–Schwarz, and the exact weight s=s1/4s3/4s=s^{1/4}s^{3/4} give

∫0S∥Qδ(s)∥Lt,x2ds≤LQ:=4(δeg+e′δg). \int_0^S\|Q_\delta(s)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}ds \le L_Q:=4(\delta e\,g+e'\delta g). \tag{TD.9}
(TD.9)

Each original coefficient two is multiplied by the bracket factor two, while Cauchy–Schwarz over all three spatial components costs no further factor. Heat Cauchy–Schwarz applies to the two factors in ds/sds/s, after the original dsds integral is written with its factor s.

At each heat time the four coefficient-difference contributions obey

∥Hη(s)∥Lt,x2≤4δA0s−1/4∥∂xW′(s)∥Lt,x2+23δA1s−3/4∥W′(s)∥Lt,x2+4δA0A0s−1/2∥W′(s)∥Lt,x2+4A0′δA0s−1/2∥W′(s)∥Lt,x2. \begin{aligned} \|H_\eta(s)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}\le{}& 4\delta A_0s^{-1/4}\|\partial_xW'(s)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}\\ &+2\sqrt3\delta A_1s^{-3/4}\|W'(s)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}\\ &+4\delta A_0A_0s^{-1/2}\|W'(s)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}\\ &+4A_0'\delta A_0s^{-1/2}\|W'(s)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}. \end{aligned}\tag{TD.10}
(TD.10)

The divergence keeps all three diagonal terms and uses |∑j∂jηj|≤3|∂xη||\sum_j\partial_j\eta_j|\le\sqrt3|\partial_x\eta|. For example

|∑j[ηj,[aj,W′]]|≤4(∑j|ηj|2)1/2(∑j|aj|2)1/2|W′|. |\sum_j[\eta_j,[a_j,W']]|\le 4(\sum_j|\eta_j|^2)^{1/2}(\sum_j|a_j|^2)^{1/2}|W'|.

The last line has the same bound with its original bracket order.

The exact scalar heat integrals are ∫0Ss−1/2ds=2S\int_0^S s^{-1/2}ds=2\sqrt S and ∫0Ss−3/4ds=4S1/4\int_0^S s^{-3/4}ds=4S^{1/4}. Cauchy–Schwarz in dsds treats the first line of TD.10, using the square root 2S1/4\sqrt2S^{1/4} of the first integral. Consequently define the finite numbers

Lη=42δA0S1/4B′+83δA1S1/4L′+8δA0A0SL′+8A0′δA0SL′,LH=Lη+LQ,∫0S∥Hη(s)∥Lt,x2ds≤Lη,∫0S∥H(s)∥Lt,x2ds≤LH. \begin{aligned} L_\eta={}&4\sqrt2\delta A_0S^{1/4}B' +8\sqrt3\delta A_1S^{1/4}L'\\ &+8\delta A_0A_0\sqrt S\,L' +8A_0'\delta A_0\sqrt S\,L',\\ L_H&=L_\eta+L_Q,\\ \int_0^S\|H_\eta(s)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}ds&\le L_\eta, \qquad \int_0^S\|H(s)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}ds\le L_H. \end{aligned}\tag{TD.11}
(TD.11)

All four coefficient terms of TD.7 remain separately visible in LηL_\eta. In particular the weights on δA0\delta A_0 and δA1\delta A_1 are the original s1/4s^{1/4} and s3/4s^{3/4}; no stronger zero-heat supremum of a coefficient is assumed.

4. Exact covariant energy and two complete estimates

Use the real Hilbert–Schmidt pairing, summed over every matrix entry and integrated over I×ℝ3I\times\mathbb R^3. Spatial covariant integration by parts in TD.8 gives the exact identity

12dds∥V(s)∥Lt,x22+∥DxV(s)∥Lt,x22=Re⁡⟨V(s),H(s)⟩Lt,x2. \frac12\frac{d}{ds}\|V(s)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}^2 +\|D_xV(s)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}^2 =\operatorname{Re}\langle V(s),H(s)\rangle_{L^2_{t,x}}. \tag{TD.12}
(TD.12)

There is no physical-time integration by parts and no missing physical endpoint term. The common s=0s=0 energy datum is zero. Regularizing ∥V∥2\|V\|_2 by (∥V∥22+ϵ2)1/2(\|V\|_2^2+\epsilon^2)^{1/2}, dropping its nonnegative spatial energy term, and integrating proves ∥V(s)∥2≤∫0s∥H(r)∥2dr\|V(s)\|_2\le\int_0^s\|H(r)\|_2dr as ϵ↓0\epsilon\downarrow0. Integrating TD.12 then gives

sups∥V(s)∥2≤LH,(∫0S∥DxV(s)∥22ds)1/2≤LH. \sup_s\|V(s)\|_2\le L_H,\qquad \left(\int_0^S\|D_xV(s)\|_2^2ds\right)^{1/2}\le L_H. \tag{TD.13}
(TD.13)

The following further estimate shows that the energy itself can be bounded without δA1\delta A_1. Retain the exact operator factorization, in its actual noncommuting order,

DjDj−Dj′Dj′=Dj(Dj−Dj′)+(Dj−Dj′)Dj′. D_jD_j-D_j'D_j' =D_j(D_j-D_j')+(D_j-D_j')D_j'. \tag{TD.14}
(TD.14)

Since (Dj−Dj′)X=[ηj,X](D_j-D_j')X=[\eta_j,X], define

Fj=[ηj,W′],R=∑j[ηj,Dj′W′]+Qδ,H=∑jDjFj+R. \begin{aligned} F_j&=[\eta_j,W'],\\ R&=\sum_j[\eta_j,D_j'W']+Q_\delta,\\ H&=\sum_jD_jF_j+R. \end{aligned}\tag{TD.15}
(TD.15)

This is the same H as TD.7, by the exact factorization TD.14; no coefficient term is discarded. Its two useful norms are

F*=22δA0S1/4L′,R*=22δA0S1/4BD′+LQ,(∫0S∥F(s)∥Lt,x22ds)1/2≤F*,∫0S∥R(s)∥Lt,x2ds≤R*. \begin{aligned} F_*&=2\sqrt2\delta A_0S^{1/4}L',\\ R_*&=2\sqrt2\delta A_0S^{1/4}B_D'+L_Q,\\ \left(\int_0^S\|F(s)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}^2ds\right)^{1/2} &\le F_*,\qquad \int_0^S\|R(s)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}ds\le R_*. \end{aligned}\tag{TD.16}
(TD.16)

The first uses the full vector bound |F|≤2|η||W′||F|\le2|\eta||W'| and ∫s−1/2ds=2S\int s^{-1/2}ds=2\sqrt S. For R the single bracket has coefficient two; Cauchy–Schwarz pairs s−1/4s^{-1/4} with ∥D′W′∥2\|D'W'\|_2 in dsds. This proves the factor 222\sqrt2, including the original covariant gradient of the primed field.

The complete energy identity TD.12 is therefore also

12(∥V∥22)′+∥DxV∥22=−Re⁡⟨DxV,F⟩+Re⁡⟨V,R⟩. \frac12(\|V\|_2^2)'+\|D_xV\|_2^2 =-\operatorname{Re}\langle D_xV,F\rangle +\operatorname{Re}\langle V,R\rangle. \tag{TD.17}
(TD.17)

For Ys=sup⁡0≤r≤s∥V(r)∥2Y_s=\sup_{0\le r\le s}\|V(r)\|_2, Young’s inequality with coefficients 1/2,1/21/2,1/2, integrated from zero to any σ≤s\sigma\le s, gives

∥V(σ)∥22+∫0σ∥DxV(r)∥22dr≤F*2+2YsR*. \|V(\sigma)\|_2^2+\int_0^\sigma\|D_xV(r)\|_2^2dr \le F_*^2+2Y_sR_*. \tag{TD.18}
(TD.18)

Taking the supremum of the first term gives Ys2≤F*2+2YsR*Y_s^2\le F_*^2+2Y_sR_*, whose nonnegative root is Ys≤R*+R*2+F*2Y_s\le R_*+\sqrt{R_*^2+F_*^2}. At s=Ss=S, the same inequality for the gradient, together with

(R*+R*2+F*2)2=F*2+2R*(R*+R*2+F*2), (R_*+\sqrt{R_*^2+F_*^2})^2 =F_*^2+2R_*(R_*+\sqrt{R_*^2+F_*^2}),

proves the same upper bound for its heat L2L^2 norm.

Both estimates are valid, so put

LV=min⁡{LH,R*+R*2+F*2},BV=(1+22A0S1/4)LV,sups∥V(s)∥Lt,x2≤LV,(∫0S∥DxV(s)∥Lt,x22ds)1/2≤LV,(∫0S∥∂xV(s)∥Lt,x22ds)1/2≤BV. \begin{aligned} L_V&=\min\{L_H,\ R_*+\sqrt{R_*^2+F_*^2}\},\\ B_V&=(1+2\sqrt2 A_0S^{1/4})L_V,\\ \sup_s\|V(s)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}&\le L_V,\\ \left(\int_0^S\|D_xV(s)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}^2ds\right)^{1/2} &\le L_V,\\ \left(\int_0^S\|\partial_xV(s)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}^2ds\right)^{1/2} &\le B_V. \end{aligned}\tag{TD.19}
(TD.19)

For the last line use the full identity ∂jV=DjV−[aj,V]\partial_jV=D_jV-[a_j,V], whose bracket contributes 2A0s−1/4LV2A_0s^{-1/4}L_V, and integrate its square with the same 2S2\sqrt S scalar integral. 5. The entire ordinary heat forcing of V

Expand the unprimed covariant operator in TD.8, preserving H:

NV:=(∂s−Δ)V=2∑j[aj,∂jV]+[∑j∂jaj,V]+∑j[aj,[aj,V]]+H. N_V:=(\partial_s-\Delta)V =2\sum_j[a_j,\partial_jV] +[\sum_j\partial_ja_j,V] +\sum_j[a_j,[a_j,V]]+H. \tag{TD.20}
(TD.20)

The first three groups are the original ordinary coefficient terms acting on the actual difference field. H is exactly the four coefficient-difference terms and both electric-difference terms in TD.7. The estimates in TD.19 and the original scalar heat integrals now prove

LNδ=42A0S1/4BV+83A1S1/4LV+8A02SLV+LH,∫0S∥NV(s)∥Lt,x2ds≤LNδ. \begin{aligned} L_N^\delta={}&4\sqrt2 A_0S^{1/4}B_V +8\sqrt3 A_1S^{1/4}L_V +8A_0^2\sqrt S\,L_V+L_H,\\ \int_0^S\|N_V(s)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}ds&\le L_N^\delta. \end{aligned}\tag{TD.21}
(TD.21)

The coefficient four in the first line of TD.20 comes from its two and the bracket’s two; Cauchy–Schwarz with the ordinary gradient gives 42A0S1/4BV4\sqrt2 A_0S^{1/4}B_V. The divergence coefficient is 232\sqrt3, multiplied by 4S1/44S^{1/4}. The double bracket has coefficient four, multiplied by 2S2\sqrt S. 6. The exact finite-endpoint identity and its zero-heat limit

The two endpoint temporal gauge conditions give the full difference identity

ϑ(s)=−∫sSV(r)dr. \vartheta(s)=-\int_s^S V(r)dr. \tag{TD.22}
(TD.22)

Thus, without changing either endpoint,

∥ϑ(s)∥Lt,x2≤(S−s)LV,∥∂xϑ(s)∥Lt,x2≤S−sBV. \begin{aligned} \|\vartheta(s)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}&\le(S-s)L_V,\\ \|\partial_x\vartheta(s)\|_{L^2_{t,x}} &\le\sqrt{S-s}\,B_V. \end{aligned}\tag{TD.23}
(TD.23)

The first heat integral is absolutely convergent in Lt,x2L^2_{t,x}. The derivative integral converges there by Cauchy–Schwarz in drdr; distributional differentiation identifies it with the gradient of the original ϑ\vartheta. Both therefore have their actual limits as s↓0s\downarrow0.

For every positive ss, integrate ΔV=∂sV−NV\Delta V=\partial_sV-N_V before estimating it. This gives the exact signed identity

Δϑ(s)=−∫sSΔV(r)dr=−V(S)+V(s)+∫sSNV(r)dr. \begin{aligned} \Delta\vartheta(s) &=-\int_s^S\Delta V(r)dr\\ &=-V(S)+V(s)+\int_s^S N_V(r)dr. \end{aligned}\tag{TD.24}
(TD.24)

The first endpoint is negative, the lower endpoint is positive, and the ordinary forcing integral is positive. At s=Ss=S, all three terms cancel to zero, as required by ϑ(S)=0\vartheta(S)=0. Through the actual regular zero-heat boundary, V(s)→V(0)=0V(s)\to V(0)=0 in Lt,x2L^2_{t,x}, and TD.21 gives convergence of the final integral. Together with the TD.23 limit, this proves in distributions and in its resulting L2L^2 Laplacian domain

Δϑ(0)=−V(S)+∫0SNV(r)dr,∥ϑ(0)∥Lt,x2≤SLV,∥∂xϑ(0)∥Lt,x2≤Dδ:=SBV,∥∂x(2)ϑ(0)∥Lt,x2=∥Δϑ(0)∥Lt,x2≤Hδ:=LV+LNδ. \begin{aligned} \Delta\vartheta(0)&=-V(S)+\int_0^S N_V(r)dr,\\ \|\vartheta(0)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}&\le SL_V,\\ \|\partial_x\vartheta(0)\|_{L^2_{t,x}} &\le D_\delta:=\sqrt S\,B_V,\\ \|\partial_x^{(2)}\vartheta(0)\|_{L^2_{t,x}} &=\|\Delta\vartheta(0)\|_{L^2_{t,x}} \le H_\delta:=L_V+L_N^\delta. \end{aligned}\tag{TD.25}
(TD.25)

For the full Hessian equality, spatial Fourier transformation and the original Plancherel measure give ∑i,j|ξiξj|2=(∑iξi2)2\sum_{i,j}|\xi_i\xi_j|^2=(\sum_i\xi_i^2)^2. The identity includes every mixed and diagonal entry. Since ϑ(0)\vartheta(0) and its distributional Laplacian are in Lt,x2L^2_{t,x}, this proves its Lt2Hx2L^2_tH^2_x domain and the equality of the displayed two norms. No absolute integral of ∥ΔV(r)∥2\|\Delta V(r)\|_2 was asserted finite.

For completeness, at any positive heat slice TD.24 gives the uniform bound ∥∂x(2)ϑ(s)∥Lt,x2≤2LV+LNδ\|\partial_x^{(2)}\vartheta(s)\|_{L^2_{t,x}} \le2L_V+L_N^\delta. 7. All temporal-boundary inputs required by the gauge difference

Put δb=ϑ(0)=at(0)−at′(0)\delta b=\vartheta(0)=a_t(0)-a_t'(0), exactly the difference used in GD.1. Its Lt2Hx2L^2_tH^2_x domain just proved permits the original Morrey–Sobolev and Sobolev inequalities at almost every physical time. Squaring the first and using time Cauchy–Schwarz gives

Exercise 1. Subtract noncommuting squares

Solution. Expand Dj(Dj−Dj′)+(Dj−Dj′)Dj′D_j(D_j-D'_j)+(D_j-D'_j)D'_j. Its four products are DjDj−DjDj′+DjDj′−Dj′Dj′D_jD_j-D_jD'_j+D_jD'_j-D'_jD'_j. The middle two cancel in their unchanged order. Exercise 2. Keep the quadratic difference

Exercise 6. Why the whole Hessian has the same norm
