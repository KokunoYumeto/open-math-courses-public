GP.6. Dyadic projections and their complete consistency

The map πm:Xm+1→Xm\pi_m:X_{m+1}\to X_m multiplies the two fine links along each old edge in traversal order, and discards other fine links. It is continuous and surjective: prescribe the first half as identity and the second as the old link, and set unused links arbitrarily. Associativity gives the composed projections πm,k\pi_{m,k} for k>mk>m.

A full refinement can be executed using GP.5 only. Subdivide every old edge into halves. In each old face insert the vertical chord between its horizontal-side midpoints, dividing it into equal-area rectangles. Subdivide that chord at the face centre. Insert the horizontal chord in each half rectangle. These give its four dyadic children. The unchanged faces are unaffected; every new face parameter is proportional to its physical area, so the sums in (G8) are exactly the old face parameters. Applying the edge and chord integrations in reverse order proves

(πm)*μm+1=μm. (\pi_m)_*\mu_{m+1}=\mu_m. \tag{G10}
(G10)

The constructed geometric order also specifies a finite list of elementary conditional samplers for each refinement. Internal gauge factors cancel along every subdivided old edge, so the projections intertwine the fine gauge action with its restriction to old vertices.

GP.8. A countable supply of random choices from one probability space

We now prove existence of compatible configurations rather than appealing to an unproved extension theorem. Start from Ω=[0,1]\Omega=[0,1] with Lebesgue probability. For non-dyadic ω\omega, let bk(ω)∈{0,1}b_k(\omega)\in\{0,1\} be its binary digits. Give dyadic points the expansion ending in zeros; this countable exceptional set has measure zero. Any specification of rr distinct digits has probability 2−r2^{-r}: partition into intervals of length 2−M2^{-M}, where MM is the largest specified place, and count the 2M−r2^{M-r} allowed intervals.

Partition the positive integers into Ij={2j(2k−1):k≥1}I_j=\{2^j(2k-1):k\geq1\}, j≥0j\geq0, and put Zj=∑k≥12−kb2j(2k−1)Z_j=\sum_{k\geq1}2^{-k}b_{2^j(2k-1)}. They are Borel maps. The digit calculation proves that every finite tuple of the ZjZ_j has independent uniform distributions on dyadic intervals. Approximating interval endpoints from above and below by dyadic rationals proves the same for all interval boxes. It extends to Borel sets by the following elementary uniqueness argument, also used below: for a class of sets closed under intersections, equality of two finite measures on that class extends to its generated sigma algebra. To prove it, the equality class for complements and disjoint countable unions is a lambda system; first hold one intersected set fixed, then vary the other, to show the smallest such lambda system containing an intersection-closed generating class is closed under intersections, hence is a sigma algebra. Applying this argument coordinate by coordinate proves the asserted finite product laws. A fixed enumeration of ℕ0×ℕ0\mathbb N_0\times\mathbb N_0 partitions the indices of the ZjZ_j into countably many disjoint infinite blocks. Each block can supply all proposals for one sampler, and different blocks are independent in every finite collection.

GP.11. The continuum probability space and its uniqueness

Define

X∞={(U0,U1,…)∈∏m≥0Xm:πm(Um+1)=Um for every m}. X_\infty=\{(U^0,U^1,\ldots)\in\prod_{m\geq0}X_m: \pi_m(U^{m+1})=U^m\text{ for every }m\}.

Choose bounded compatible metrics on the finite products and weight them by 2−m−12^{-m-1}. This gives a metric on the product; convergence is exactly coordinatewise convergence. Each XmX_m is compact. A diagonal subsequence chosen successively in its coordinates converges in this weighted metric, since its tail weights tend to zero. Thus the product is compact; the displayed equalities are closed, so X∞X_\infty is compact as well. The recursive map GP.10 is Borel into it: each coordinate is Borel, and a countable product of second-countable metric spaces has a countable cylinder basis, so its Borel sigma algebra is generated by coordinate inverse images. Define μ∞\mu_\infty as the pushforward of Lebesgue probability by that actual map. This proves existence.

The marginal on XmX_m is μm\mu_m. These marginals determine the probability uniquely: any finite collection of coordinates of a compatible sequence is a continuous function of the coordinate with largest index. Thus all finite-coordinate cylinder probabilities are determined. Cylinders are closed under intersections and generate the Borel sigma algebra; the elementary measure uniqueness argument in GP.8 proves uniqueness. This proof has not imported an unproved uncountable projective-limit theorem.

GP.12. Precisely which continuum paths receive holonomy

Let V∞=⋃mVmV_\infty=\bigcup_m V_m. Take all finite oriented edge words in all Γm\Gamma_m, with endpoints in this set, and identify a coarse edge with its two fine subedges; also identify insertion/removal of immediately reversed pairs and identity words. The resulting countable groupoid 𝒫dyad(D)\mathcal P_{\rm dyad}(D) has objects V∞V_\infty. Composition is traversal followed by traversal when endpoints match; after passage to a common refinement all products are finite words, and associativity is literal word associativity. The relations are compatible with composition and reversal.

For a compatible configuration define HγH_\gamma using any containing grid and (G2). Projection compatibility and group cancellation prove independence of its representative. Every Hγ:X∞→GH_\gamma:X_\infty\to G is a continuous map depending on a finite level. The identities Hγδ=HδHγH_{\gamma\delta}=H_\delta H_\gamma, Hγ−1=Hγ−1H_{\gamma^{-1}}=H_\gamma^{-1}, and Hid=IH_{\rm id}=I hold for every configuration, including the null-set replacement in GP.10. Every finite joint distribution is the one from any common containing grid. This is the proved continuum object: holonomies on this specified countable family, with all dyadic refinements already present and no residual mesh parameter in the probability law.

It does not assert holonomy on every smooth curve, continuity in a metric on arbitrary paths, or a random differentiable connection. Nor are geometrically different paths identified by arbitrary homotopies: a contractible rectangle has the nontrivial law (G12), so that additional quotient would be false.

GP.13. Gauge invariance at the constructed limit

GP.14. Boundary gluing as an actual probability operation

Equation (G9) is also the precise conditional probability for a face decomposition with retained boundary data. GP.16. What finite-area consistency establishes

For every fixed dyadic rectangle and every finite collection of the specified paths, their probability distribution is exactly unchanged by every further dyadic refinement. The physical lengths and g2g_2 are held fixed; face times shrink because physical face areas shrink.
