Heat smoothing and the caloric gauge

The construction chapter produced a DeTurck solution for every spatial connection in Ḣ1(ℝ3)\dot H^1(\mathbb R^3). This chapter constructs its caloric representative, proves quantitative dependence on the original connection, and identifies exactly the solution map extended from regular data.

Keep the same x1,x2,x3x^1,x^2,x^3, heat parameter ss, closed matrix group G⊂U(N)G\subset U(N), trace inner product and reference length ℓ>0\ell>0. No spatial coordinate or heat endpoint is changed. The heat parameter has units of length squared. On the Lie algebra the matrix norm is the norm of κ(X,Y)=−tr⁡(XY)\kappa(X,Y)=-\operatorname{tr}(XY); on general matrices use the Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Undifferentiated gauge matrices in L∞L^\infty are measured in operator norm. A unitary matrix has operator norm one, and multiplication by it preserves the Hilbert–Schmidt norm.

The source comparison is Sung-Jin Oh’s Gauge choice for the Yang–Mills equations using the Yang–Mills heat flow and local well-posedness in H1H^1, arXiv:1210.1558v2, Section 5 and its gauge appendix, together with the heat-flow discussion in Finite energy global well-posedness of the Yang–Mills equations on ℝ1+3\mathbb R^{1+3}, arXiv:1210.1557v2. The constants, proofs, examples and teaching organization here are independent course exposition.
