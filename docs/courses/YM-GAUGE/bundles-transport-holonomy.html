Bundles, parallel transport and holonomy

Lesson YM-F06 · Geometry and states in Yang–Mills theory

A field need not have one set of coordinates valid everywhere. It may have one description in each region, with a specified invertible map on overlaps. A bundle is the space obtained by putting those descriptions together. A connection differentiates its sections. Parallel transport solves that differentiation rule along a path. Holonomy records the resulting map when the path returns to its starting point.

This lesson constructs those objects and their maps. It proves the transport equation by a convergent matrix series, retains the order of every factor around a loop, and works through a bundle on the sphere that has no global frame. Preparation is Lesson 5 and its earlier matrix and calculus lessons. No classification theorem for bundles or general existence theorem for differential equations is needed for the constructions below.

1. Coordinate regions and the spaces they describe

A topological space specifies which subsets are open: arbitrary unions and finite intersections of open sets are open, and the empty set and the whole space are open. A continuous map has open inverse images of open sets. A homeomorphism is a continuous bijection with continuous inverse. The space is Hausdorff if distinct points have disjoint open neighbourhoods. It is second countable if a countable family of open sets is a basis: every open neighbourhood of a point contains a member of that family containing the point.

A smooth manifold MM of dimension mm is a Hausdorff, second-countable space with coordinate charts xi:Ui→Vi⊂ℝmx_i:U_i\to V_i\subset\mathbb R^m that are homeomorphisms onto open sets. On an overlap both coordinate changes are smooth. Maps and functions on MM are smooth when their coordinate expressions are smooth. The chain rule proves that this definition is independent of the chosen charts.

A tangent vector at pp is a coordinate velocity, with the exact identification

vj=D(xj∘xi−1)xi(p)vi. v_j=D(x_j\circ x_i^{-1})_{x_i(p)}v_i. \tag{F6.1}
(F6.1)

The derivative of the inverse chart makes this map invertible; the chain rule makes three successive identifications agree. Vector fields and differential forms therefore have compatible coordinate expressions. For forms the map is precisely the pullback proved in Lesson 5, (F5.30)–(F5.34), retaining all derivatives of the coordinate maps. Exterior differentiation agrees on overlaps by (F5.31), so it is defined on MM.

Only elementary consequences of these definitions will be used. A second-countable space has a countable subcover of any open cover: for each basis member contained in a cover member choose one such member. Every point belongs to a basis member of this kind. The chosen countable collection therefore covers the space. A compact set means one for which every open cover has a finite subcover. Closed bounded coordinate balls and closed intervals are compact by the finite-dimensional compactness argument of Lesson 4. Continuous images and finite unions of compact sets are compact: pull an open cover back for the first assertion and take the union of finitely many finite subcovers for the second.

A compact subset KK of a Hausdorff space is closed. For p∉Kp\notin K, separate pp from each point of KK. Finitely many of the neighbourhoods in KK cover it. The intersection of their corresponding neighbourhoods of pp misses KK; hence its complement is open. These facts justify the gluing and finite path subdivisions used below.

2. Gluing vector coordinates

Fix a positive integer NN and an open cover (Ui)(U_i) of MM. For every overlap choose a smooth map hij:Ui∩Uj→GL⁡N(ℂ)h_{ij}:U_i\cap U_j\to\operatorname{GL}_N(\mathbb C) with

Theorem 2.1. The gluing really is a smooth vector bundle

Each map ei(x)v=[i,x,v]e_i(x)v=[i,x,v] is a smooth product chart over UiU_i. The total space is Hausdorff and second countable. Its fibres are vector spaces, and their linear operations are smooth in these charts. Conversely local frames of a vector bundle produce (F6.2) and recover the original bundle by (F6.4).

Proof. Reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity of the relation are exactly the identity, inverse and triple identities in (F6.2). Every class over x∈Uix\in U_i has exactly one ii-representative. Thus the proposed product map is a bijection onto π−1(Ui)\pi^{-1}(U_i).

Give EE the quotient topology: a subset is open exactly when its inverse image in the disjoint union is open. The quotient map is open. Indeed the saturation of an open set in its ii-component is the union of its other components’ images under (x,v)↦(x,hij(x)v)(x,v)\mapsto(x,h_{ij}(x)v) on overlaps. Each such map is a homeomorphism, with smooth inverse using hjih_{ji}. Its image is open. Hence our product bijections and their inverses are continuous and their images are open.

Points over different base points can be separated by inverse images of disjoint base neighbourhoods. Two different points over the same base point lie in one open product chart and can be separated there. This proves Hausdorffness. Choose a countable subcover of (Ui)(U_i). Each product has a countable basis, from a base basis and rational balls in ℝ2N\mathbb R^{2N}. Their countable union, carried into the open products, is a countable basis of EE.

The chart changes are exactly (x,v)↦(x,hij(x)v)(x,v)\mapsto(x,h_{ij}(x)v), combined with the base coordinate changes where necessary. They and their inverses are smooth, giving a smooth atlas. Addition and scalar multiplication agree between charts because every hij(x)h_{ij}(x) is linear. Their coordinate formulas prove smoothness. Finally, in an existing bundle, [i,x,v]↦ei(x)v[i,x,v]\mapsto e_i(x)v is well defined by (F6.3). It and its inverse are the identity in each fibre chart, proving the recovery assertion. ▫\square

A section is a smooth s:M→Es:M\to E with πs(x)=x\pi s(x)=x. The exact section data are smooth columns ψi\psi_i satisfying (F6.3). Agreement on overlaps makes the local sections glue; the local product charts prove the converse and smoothness.

Equation (F6.5) proves it is well defined; its inverse uses ki−1k_i^{-1}. Thus this is an actual bundle isomorphism.

For a smooth f:L→Mf:L\to M, the pullback bundle has fibres (f*E)y=Ef(y)(f^*E)_y=E_{f(y)}, represented as pairs (y,v)(y,v) with π(v)=f(y)\pi(v)=f(y). 3. Principal frames and associated fields

Let GG be one of the matrix Lie groups already constructed in Lesson 4, and let every hijh_{ij} take values in GG. Replace the column vv in (F6.4) by a group element aa:

P={[i,x,a]:a∈G},[j,x,a]=[i,x,hij(x)a],[i,x,a]b=[i,x,ab]. P=\{[i,x,a]:a\in G\},\qquad [j,x,a]=[i,x,h_{ij}(x)a],\qquad [i,x,a]b=[i,x,ab]. \tag{F6.6}
(F6.6)

The same open-chart, separation and countability proof applies with GG in place of ℂN\mathbb C^N. Its smooth group operations give the smooth chart changes and right action. On each fibre the action is free and transitive, since it is right multiplication in a product chart. This is a principal right GG-bundle. The sections si(x)=[i,x,I]s_i(x)=[i,x,I] satisfy sj=sihijs_j=s_i h_{ij}.

There is a smooth division map on pairs in the same fibre:

Freeness gives uniqueness, and the local expression proves smoothness and independence of the chart. In particular δ(pa,qb)=a−1δ(p,q)b\delta(pa,qb)=a^{-1}\delta(p,q)b. In a local frame every such p=ei(x)ap=e_i(x)a, uniquely. These charts give the principal GL⁡N(ℂ)\operatorname{GL}_N(\mathbb C)-bundle Fr⁡(E)\operatorname{Fr}(E). If the specified transitions belong to GG, the frames ei(x)ae_i(x)a, a∈Ga\in G, form the corresponding principal GG-bundle inside it. The actual transition law verifies agreement of this subset on every overlap.

Its coordinate map before quotienting is (x,a,v)↦(x,ρ(a)v)(x,a,v)\mapsto(x,\rho(a)v); it is continuous, and the inverse product chart is its restriction to a=Ia=I. 4. Connections agree across the overlaps

Let Γi\Gamma_i be matrix-valued one-forms on UiU_i. All equations between these forms on an overlap use the actual base pullback when the two coordinate lists differ. Existence of connection data

Every bundle just constructed over the stated manifold admits a connection. For an empty base the assertion is the unique empty construction. Assume the base nonempty below. We supply the smooth weights needed for the proof.

Choose relatively compact coordinate balls about every base point. The countable-subcover argument gives open balls OnO_n covering MM, with compact closures CnC_n contained in their charts. Take increasing finite unions KjK_j of these closures so that Kj⊂int⁡Kj+1K_j\subset\operatorname{int}K_{j+1} and ⋃jKj=M\bigcup_jK_j=M. Recursively, after choosing KjK_j, finitely many OnO_n cover it; include their closures and the next unused indexed closure in Kj+1K_{j+1}. The open union of those balls contains KjK_j, proving the interior inclusion and exhaustion.

Put K−1=K−2=⌀K_{-1}=K_{-2}=\varnothing. Each compact layer Kj\int⁡Kj−1K_j\setminus\operatorname{int}K_{j-1} lies in the open shell int⁡Kj+1\Kj−2\operatorname{int}K_{j+1}\setminus K_{j-2}. Cover that layer by finitely many smaller coordinate balls whose larger closed balls lie in the shell and a bundle chart. Such choices exist by openness in a coordinate chart and compactness of the layer. Over all layers these smaller balls cover MM. The larger balls are locally finite: a neighbourhood inside int⁡Kk\operatorname{int}K_k misses every shell with j≥k+2j\geq k+2; only finitely many balls from the preceding layers remain.

5. Global gauge transformations

A gauge transformation of PP is a smooth map Φ:P→P\Phi:P\to P over the identity of MM that commutes with the right action. Write Φ(si)=siki\Phi(s_i)=s_i k_i. Equivariance forces

Φ(si(x)a)=si(x)ki(x)a,kj=hij−1kihij. \Phi(s_i(x)a)=s_i(x)k_i(x)a,\qquad k_j=h_{ij}^{-1}k_i h_{ij}. \tag{F6.20}
