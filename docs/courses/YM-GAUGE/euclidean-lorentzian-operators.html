The Euclidean and Lorentzian linearized Yang–Mills equations

Companion to Lesson YM-F10. Starting from the action and field equation of Lesson 7, we calculate the complete linearized equation. A principal symbol records its highest derivative terms at a point; the calculation below defines that operation and uses it to compare Euclidean geometry with physical-time propagation. Sections 7–8 give exact solutions and their action and energy. Section 10 includes four exercises with full solutions.

1. Background, metric, and the exact variation

Let UU be an open coordinate domain in a smooth four-manifold, with a fixed smooth nondegenerate metric gμνg_{\mu\nu}, of either positive signature or signature (−+++)(-+++). Indices run over 0,1,2,30,1,2,3; every repeated pair is a full ordered sum. Set w=|det⁡g|w=\sqrt{|\det g|}. Let G⊂U(N)G\subset U(N), let 𝔤\mathfrak g be its real skew-Hermitian Lie algebra, and retain κ(X,Y)=−tr⁡(XY)\kappa(X,Y)=-\operatorname{tr}(XY), gYM≠0g_{\mathrm{YM}}\ne0, and ℏ>0\hbar>0.

Fix an arbitrary smooth connection A=AμdxμA=A_\mu dx^\mu, not assumed to satisfy the field equation. It is the connection called Γ\Gamma in F07; no coupling is absorbed into a new variable. Write

DμX=∂μX+[Aμ,X],Fμν=∂μAν−∂νAμ+[Aμ,Aν], D_\mu X=\partial_\mu X+[A_\mu,X],\qquad F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu+[A_\mu,A_\nu], \tag{E.1}
(E.1)
ℰν(A)=1wDμ(wFμν),Fμν=gμρgνσFρσ. \mathcal E^\nu(A)=\frac1wD_\mu(wF^{\mu\nu}),\qquad F^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}. \tag{E.2}
(E.2)

This is precisely F7.14. The notation DμD_\mu in density expressions acts on the displayed matrix coefficient by partial derivative and commutator; the density factor and all inverse-metric factors remain inside its partial derivative. This convention avoids silently treating coordinate indices as constant on a varying metric.

For a smooth adjoint-valued one-form aa, introduce a dimensionless real parameter ε\varepsilon. Expanding the original curvature gives

Fμν(A+εa)=Fμν+εBμν+ε2[aμ,aν],Bμν=Dμaν−Dνaμ. F_{\mu\nu}(A+\varepsilon a) =F_{\mu\nu}+\varepsilon B_{\mu\nu} +\varepsilon^2[a_\mu,a_\nu],\qquad B_{\mu\nu}=D_\mu a_\nu-D_\nu a_\mu. \tag{E.3}
(E.3)

The exact linearization LAa=(d/dε)0ℰ(A+εa)L_Aa=(d/d\varepsilon)_0\mathcal E(A+\varepsilon a) is

(LAa)ν=1wDμ(wgμρgνσ(Dρaσ−Dσaρ))+[aμ,Fμν]. \boxed{(L_Aa)^\nu= \frac1wD_\mu\left(wg^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma} (D_\rho a_\sigma-D_\sigma a_\rho)\right) +[a_\mu,F^{\mu\nu}].} \tag{E.4}
(E.4)

The second term comes from varying the connection in the outer covariant derivative; it is present at every background with noncommuting curvature. This proof follows directly by differentiating the three terms in (E.3) and the two terms in DμD_\mu, with the metric fixed.

Here is an expansion which displays every coefficient of every derivative of aa. Define

Kμρνσ=gμρgνσ−gμσgνρ,Hρνσ=1w∂μ(wKμρνσ). K^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}=g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}-g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho}, \qquad H^{\rho\nu\sigma}=\frac1w\partial_\mu(wK^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}). \tag{E.5}
(E.5)

Then (E.4) is exactly

(LAa)ν=Kμρνσ∂μ∂ρaσ+Hρνσ∂ρaσ+Kμρνσ([Aρ,∂μaσ]+[Aμ,∂ρaσ])+Hρνσ[Aρ,aσ]+Kμρνσ[∂μAρ,aσ]+Kμρνσ[Aμ,[Aρ,aσ]]+[aμ,Fμν]. \begin{aligned} (L_Aa)^\nu={}&K^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}\partial_\mu\partial_\rho a_\sigma +H^{\rho\nu\sigma}\partial_\rho a_\sigma\\ &+K^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma} \bigl([A_\rho,\partial_\mu a_\sigma] +[A_\mu,\partial_\rho a_\sigma]\bigr)\\ &+H^{\rho\nu\sigma}[A_\rho,a_\sigma] +K^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}[\partial_\mu A_\rho,a_\sigma]\\ &+K^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}[A_\mu,[A_\rho,a_\sigma]] +[a_\mu,F^{\mu\nu}]. \end{aligned}\tag{E.6}
(E.6)

To verify it, antisymmetry rewrites the raised BB in (E.4) as Kμρνσ(∂ρaσ+[Aρ,aσ])K^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}(\partial_\rho a_\sigma+[A_\rho,a_\sigma]). The outer partial derivative gives the first, second, fourth, fifth and first commutator-derivative terms; the outer commutator gives the remaining derivative term and nested commutator. Thus (E.6) retains the first derivatives of the metric, the first derivatives of the background, and all zero-order curvature terms.

2. The actual infinitesimal gauge map and both Bianchi identities

For a smooth dimensionless χ:U→𝔤\chi:U\to\mathfrak g, set uε=exp⁡(εχ)u_\varepsilon=\exp(\varepsilon\chi). The original gauge transformation is

Auε=uεAuε−1−(duε)uε−1. A^{u_\varepsilon}=u_\varepsilon A u_\varepsilon^{-1} -(du_\varepsilon)u_\varepsilon^{-1}.

Its derivative at zero is the precise one-form

IAχ:=ddε|0Auε=[χ,A]−dχ=−DAχ. I_A\chi:=\left.\frac d{d\varepsilon}\right|_0 A^{u_\varepsilon} =[\chi,A]-d\chi=-D_A\chi. \tag{E.7}
(E.7)

Gauge covariance of the curvature follows by inserting the gauge expression in dA+A∧AdA+A\wedge A, using d(u−1)=−u−1(du)u−1d(u^{-1})=-u^{-1}(du)u^{-1}; its value is Fu=uFu−1F^u=uFu^{-1}. For every adjoint-valued coefficient XX, the same product rule gives Dμu(uXu−1)=u(DμX)u−1D_\mu^u(uXu^{-1})=u(D_\mu X)u^{-1}. The scalar metric and density commute with uu. Applying this to (E.2) gives ℰν(Au)=uℰν(A)u−1\mathcal E^\nu(A^u)=u\mathcal E^\nu(A)u^{-1}. Differentiation now proves the full background-dependent identity

LA(−DAχ)ν=[χ,ℰν(A)]. \boxed{L_A(-D_A\chi)^\nu=[\chi,\mathcal E^\nu(A)].} \tag{E.8}
(E.8)

At a solution ℰ(A)=0\mathcal E(A)=0, all infinitesimal gauge directions are in ker⁡LA\ker L_A. At an arbitrary background their image is the right side of (E.8), rather than an assumed zero. At the curvature level, expanding the derivative commutator gives

[Dμ,Dν]χ=[Fμν,χ],Bμν(−Dχ)=−[Fμν,χ]=[χ,Fμν], [D_\mu,D_\nu]\chi=[F_{\mu\nu},\chi],\qquad B_{\mu\nu}(-D\chi)=-[F_{\mu\nu},\chi]=[\chi,F_{\mu\nu}], \tag{E.9}
(E.9)

in agreement with the derivative of the actual curvature transformation. The first equality follows by expanding two second derivatives: mixed partials cancel, the remaining derivatives of AA give their two curvature terms, and Jacobi turns the nested commutators into [[Aμ,Aν],χ][[A_\mu,A_\nu],\chi].

The differential-form Bianchi identity is

DAF=dF+A∧F−F∧A=0. D_AF=dF+A\wedge F-F\wedge A=0. \tag{E.10}
(E.10)

Indeed dF=dA∧A−A∧dAdF=dA\wedge A-A\wedge dA, while A∧F−F∧A=A∧dA−dA∧AA\wedge F-F\wedge A=A\wedge dA-dA\wedge A. Differentiating this identity for A+εaA+\varepsilon a and using (E.3) proves its exact linearized version

DAB+a∧F−F∧a=0,B=DAa=da+A∧a+a∧A. D_AB+a\wedge F-F\wedge a=0,\qquad B=D_Aa=da+A\wedge a+a\wedge A. \tag{E.11}
(E.11)

Equivalently DA2a=F∧a−a∧FD_A^2a=F\wedge a-a\wedge F; expanding the four products in the left side gives this equality with the same signs. In components (E.11) retains the six terms

DλBμν+DμBνλ+DνBλμ+[aλ,Fμν]+[aμ,Fνλ]+[aν,Fλμ]=0. D_\lambda B_{\mu\nu}+D_\mu B_{\nu\lambda}+D_\nu B_{\lambda\mu} +[a_\lambda,F_{\mu\nu}]+[a_\mu,F_{\nu\lambda}]+[a_\nu,F_{\lambda\mu}]=0. \tag{E.12}
(E.12)

There is also an exact divergence identity for the field equation. Write Jμν=wFμνJ^{\mu\nu}=wF^{\mu\nu}, an antisymmetric matrix coefficient. Swapping the summed indices in half the expression gives

Dν(wℰν)=DνDμJμν=12[Dν,Dμ]Jμν=w2[Fνμ,Fμν]=0. D_\nu(w\mathcal E^\nu)=D_\nu D_\mu J^{\mu\nu} =\tfrac12[D_\nu,D_\mu]J^{\mu\nu} =\tfrac w2[F_{\nu\mu},F^{\mu\nu}]=0. \tag{E.13}
(E.13)

The last zero is an algebraic contraction, not a commutativity assumption about the curvature: it is −wgμρgνσ[Fμν,Fρσ]/2-w g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}[F_{\mu\nu},F_{\rho\sigma}]/2, and interchange of the two pairs negates the bracket while preserving its coefficient. No derivatives of ww have been dropped: they were included in JJ before the derivative commutator was taken. Differentiating (E.13) proves

1wDν(w(LAa)ν)+[aν,ℰν(A)]=0. \frac1wD_\nu\bigl(w(L_Aa)^\nu\bigr) +[a_\nu,\mathcal E^\nu(A)]=0. \tag{E.14}
(E.14)

Thus at a solution there are two actual consecutive-zero maps: χ↦−DAχ↦LA(−DAχ)=0\chi\mapsto-D_A\chi\mapsto L_A(-D_A\chi)=0, and a↦LAa↦w−1Dν(w(LAa)ν)=0a\mapsto L_Aa\mapsto w^{-1}D_\nu(w(L_Aa)^\nu)=0. Equations (E.8), (E.11) and (E.14) give their complete counterparts at an arbitrary background.

3. The principal symbol and its exact gauge kernel

Fix a point pp, a nonzero real cotangent vector ξ=ξμdxμ\xi=\xi_\mu dx^\mu, and define qg(ξ)=gμν(p)ξμξνq_g(\xi)=g^{\mu\nu}(p)\xi_\mu\xi_\nu, ξμ=gμν(p)ξν\xi^\mu=g^{\mu\nu}(p)\xi_\nu. Our symbol convention replaces each occurrence of ∂μ\partial_\mu in the highest-order part by iξμi\xi_\mu. It can be derived by applying the operator to eiλϕae^{i\lambda\phi}a, with dϕ(p)=ξd\phi(p)=\xi, and extracting the coefficient of λ2\lambda^2 at pp; derivatives of the coefficient functions or amplitude contribute lower powers of λ\lambda. Complex exponentials here merely define the complexified symbol; the original connection and variation remain real elements of the skew-Hermitian Lie algebra.

Lower the output index with the original metric. Equation (E.6) gives

(σ2(LA)(ξ)a)ν=−qg(ξ)aν+ξνξσaσ. \boxed{\bigl(\sigma_2(L_A)(\xi)a\bigr)_\nu =-q_g(\xi)a_\nu+\xi_\nu\xi^\sigma a_\sigma.} \tag{E.15}
(E.15)

The gauge-map and divergence-map symbols are respectively σ1(IA)(ξ)χ=−iξχ\sigma_1(I_A)(\xi)\chi=-i\xi\chi and a↦iξμaμa\mapsto i\xi^\mu a_\mu. Both adjacent compositions with (E.15) vanish, since ξμξμ=qg(ξ)\xi^\mu\xi_\mu=q_g(\xi). This is the highest-order part of the exact identities in §2.

If qg(ξ)≠0q_g(\xi)\ne0, then σ2(LA)(ξ)a=0\sigma_2(L_A)(\xi)a=0 implies aν=ξν(ξσaσ)/qg(ξ)a_\nu=\xi_\nu(\xi^\sigma a_\sigma)/q_g(\xi). Conversely every a=ξχa=\xi\chi is in the kernel. Thus the entire kernel is exactly the gauge direction ξ⊗𝔤\xi\otimes\mathfrak g, and the symbol acts as −qg(ξ)-q_g(\xi) on the transverse subspace ξσaσ=0\xi^\sigma a_\sigma=0. These two spaces give a direct sum when qg(ξ)≠0q_g(\xi)\ne0.

If qg(ξ)=0q_g(\xi)=0, (E.15) instead has kernel exactly {a:ξσaσ=0}\{a:\xi^\sigma a_\sigma=0\}, and image ξ⊗𝔤\xi\otimes\mathfrak g. The contraction functional is nonzero because the metric is nondegenerate and ξ≠0\xi\ne0, so it is surjective on each Lie-algebra coefficient. If d=dim⁡ℝ𝔤d=\dim_\mathbb R\mathfrak g, the kernel has dimension 3d3d, the image dimension dd, and the gauge subspace has dimension dd inside that kernel. The quotient kernel by gauge has dimension 2d2d. This is an exact symbol calculation, without identifying it with a classification of nonlinear fields or quantum states.

Even in Euclidean signature the ungauged operator is not elliptic as an operator on all one-forms: its symbol has the nonzero gauge kernel at every ξ≠0\xi\ne0. The next section removes that degeneracy at the operator level while retaining its gauge equation.

4. Background divergence gauge, all lower terms, and the action factor

Define the background divergence and scalar covariant operator by

GA(a)=1wDμ(wgμσaσ),ΔAχ=1wDμ(wgμσDσχ). G_A(a)=\frac1wD_\mu(wg^{\mu\sigma}a_\sigma),\qquad \Delta_A\chi=\frac1wD_\mu(wg^{\mu\sigma}D_\sigma\chi). \tag{E.16}
(E.16)

All matrix entries of GA(a)G_A(a) are scalars in coordinate indices. Put Dν=gνλDλD^\nu=g^{\nu\lambda}D_\lambda on such adjoint scalars, and define the gauge-fixed linearized operator

(PAa)ν=(LAa)ν+DνGA(a). \boxed{(P_Aa)^\nu=(L_Aa)^\nu+D^\nu G_A(a).} \tag{E.17}
(E.17)

The plus sign cancels the negative gradient-of-divergence in the leading part of (E.4). The equation consisting of PAa=0P_Aa=0 together with GA(a)=0G_A(a)=0 is equivalent to LAa=0L_Aa=0 together with that same gauge condition, directly by (E.17).

To display every additional coefficient, set bσ=w−1∂μ(wgμσ)b^\sigma=w^{-1}\partial_\mu(wg^{\mu\sigma}). Then GA(a)=gρσ∂ρaσ+bσaσ+gρσ[Aρ,aσ]G_A(a)=g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_\rho a_\sigma+b^\sigma a_\sigma+g^{\rho\sigma}[A_\rho,a_\sigma], and

DνGA(a)=gνλ{gρσ∂λ∂ρaσ+(∂λgρσ)∂ρaσ+bσ∂λaσ+gρσ[Aρ,∂λaσ]+gρσ[Aλ,∂ρaσ]+(∂λbσ)aσ+(∂λgρσ)[Aρ,aσ]+gρσ[∂λAρ,aσ]+bσ[Aλ,aσ]+gρσ[Aλ,[Aρ,aσ]]}. \begin{aligned} D^\nu G_A(a)=g^{\nu\lambda}\bigl\{& g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_\lambda\partial_\rho a_\sigma +(\partial_\lambda g^{\rho\sigma})\partial_\rho a_\sigma +b^\sigma\partial_\lambda a_\sigma\\ &+g^{\rho\sigma}[A_\rho,\partial_\lambda a_\sigma] +g^{\rho\sigma}[A_\lambda,\partial_\rho a_\sigma]\\ &+(\partial_\lambda b^\sigma)a_\sigma +(\partial_\lambda g^{\rho\sigma})[A_\rho,a_\sigma] +g^{\rho\sigma}[\partial_\lambda A_\rho,a_\sigma]\\ &+b^\sigma[A_\lambda,a_\sigma] +g^{\rho\sigma}[A_\lambda,[A_\rho,a_\sigma]]\bigr\}. \end{aligned}\tag{E.18}
(E.18)

Equations (E.6) and (E.18), added without omission, are the full second-, first-, and zero-order operator for an arbitrary smooth metric and arbitrary background. They include second derivatives of the metric in ∂λbσ\partial_\lambda b^\sigma. No constant-metric approximation is used.

These are operators on the actual adjoint bundle, not only on a selected matrix chart. Under a fixed finite gauge transformation uu, the connection path transforms exactly as (A+εa)u=Au+εuau−1(A+\varepsilon a)^u=A^u+\varepsilon uau^{-1}. Differentiating the already proved covariance of ℰ\mathcal E therefore gives LAu(uau−1)=u(LAa)u−1L_{A^u}(uau^{-1})=u(L_Aa)u^{-1}. The derivative product rule before (E.8) similarly gives GAu(uau−1)=uGA(a)u−1G_{A^u}(uau^{-1})=uG_A(a)u^{-1}, and then the same equality for PP follows from (E.17). These formulas prove the overlap maps for all three operators. Under an actual coordinate change x=f(y)x=f(y), with Jνb=∂xν/∂ybJ^\nu{}_b=\partial x^\nu/\partial y^b and inverse KbνK^b{}_\nu, the variation is ãb=Jνbaν∘f\widetilde a_b=J^\nu{}_b a_\nu\circ f. Differentiating F7.19 with its fixed coordinate change gives (L̃ã)b=Kbν(LAa)ν∘f(\widetilde L\widetilde a)^b=K^b{}_\nu(L_Aa)^\nu\circ f; (E.25) makes GG an adjoint scalar and DνGD^\nu G a vector, proving the same vector transformation for PP. Thus lowering the output in the symbol calculation means applying the original metric tensor, not changing the operator’s domain.

The infinitesimal gauge equation is also exact:

GA(a−DAχ)=GA(a)−ΔAχ,PA(−DAχ)ν=[χ,ℰν(A)]−DνΔAχ. G_A(a-D_A\chi)=G_A(a)-\Delta_A\chi, \qquad P_A(-D_A\chi)^\nu=[\chi,\mathcal E^\nu(A)]-D^\nu\Delta_A\chi. \tag{E.19}
(E.19)

The first equation proves which scalar equation is associated to choosing this gauge, and shows that residual transformations obey ΔAχ=0\Delta_A\chi=0. It does not assert that an arbitrary global gauge problem with unspecified boundary conditions has a solution. The second follows by (E.8) and (E.17). Similarly (E.14) gives

1wDν(w(PAa)ν)=ΔAGA(a)−[aν,ℰν(A)]. \frac1wD_\nu\bigl(w(P_Aa)^\nu\bigr) =\Delta_AG_A(a)-[a_\nu,\mathcal E^\nu(A)]. \tag{E.20}
(E.20)

In particular at a background solution, PAa=0P_Aa=0 implies the scalar equation ΔAGA(a)=0\Delta_AG_A(a)=0. Additional data conditions are needed to deduce GA(a)=0G_A(a)=0; no such deduction is made without them.

The coefficient of the gauge term agrees exactly with the original action. For compactly supported variations, F7.15 gives

D2SA(a,a)=−2σℏgYM2∫wκ(aν,(LAa)ν)d4x. D^2S_A(a,a)=-\frac{2\sigma\hbar}{g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2} \int w\,\kappa(a_\nu,(L_Aa)^\nu)\,d^4x. \tag{E.21}
(E.21)

Here σ=+1\sigma=+1 in Euclidean signature and −1-1 in Lorentzian signature. This follows by differentiating F7.15 at a fixed test one-form; it does not require ℰ(A)=0\mathcal E(A)=0. Metric factors stay fixed. Trace invariance and integration by parts give ∫wκ(aν,DνGA(a))=−∫wκ(GA(a),GA(a))\int w\kappa(a_\nu,D^\nu G_A(a))=-\int w\kappa(G_A(a),G_A(a)). Hence adding

Sgf[a]=σℏgYM2∫wκ(GA(a),GA(a))d4x S_{\rm gf}[a]=\frac{\sigma\hbar}{g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2} \int w\kappa(G_A(a),G_A(a))\,d^4x \tag{E.22}
(E.22)

to the action as a quadratic function of the perturbation contributes twice its coefficient to the second variation and changes (E.21) to the same expression with PAP_A. In Euclidean signature this added quadratic term is nonnegative. It does not erase the background-curvature contribution to the original Hessian.

5. Intrinsic form and the retained Ricci and curvature terms

An equivalent formula identifies the usual notation DμFμνD^\mu F_{\mu\nu} precisely. Define the Levi-Civita coefficients

Cμνλ=12gλτ(∂μgτν+∂νgτμ−∂τgμν). C^\lambda_{\mu\nu}=\tfrac12g^{\lambda\tau} (\partial_\mu g_{\tau\nu}+\partial_\nu g_{\tau\mu} -\partial_\tau g_{\mu\nu}). \tag{E.23}
(E.23)

They are symmetric in μ,ν\mu,\nu, and substitution verifies ∂λgμν=Cλμρgρν+Cλνρgμρ\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu}=C^\rho_{\lambda\mu}g_{\rho\nu}+C^\rho_{\lambda\nu}g_{\mu\rho}. Define the combined covariant derivative on a one-form by

∇μAaν=∂μaν+[Aμ,aν]−Cμνλaλ. \nabla^A_\mu a_\nu=\partial_\mu a_\nu+[A_\mu,a_\nu]-C^\lambda_{\mu\nu}a_\lambda. \tag{E.24}
(E.24)

On each additional covariant index subtract its corresponding CC term, and on each contravariant index add it. The symmetry of CC makes the antisymmetric derivative in (E.24) exactly BB of (E.3).

Differentiating the determinant by its cofactor formula gives ∂μlog⁡w=12gρσ∂μgρσ=Cλμλ\partial_\mu\log w=\tfrac12g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_\mu g_{\rho\sigma}=C^\lambda_{\lambda\mu}. Since CμλνFμλ=0C^\nu_{\mu\lambda}F^{\mu\lambda}=0 by symmetry, the coordinate formula for ∇μAFμν\nabla^A_\mu F^{\mu\nu} is w−1Dμ(wFμν)w^{-1}D_\mu(wF^{\mu\nu}). Metric compatibility therefore proves

gνλℰλ(A)=(∇A)μFμν,GA(a)=(∇A)μaμ. g_{\nu\lambda}\mathcal E^\lambda(A)=(\nabla^A)^\mu F_{\mu\nu}, \qquad G_A(a)=(\nabla^A)^\mu a_\mu. \tag{E.25}
(E.25)

Fix the curvature convention

Rρσμν=∂μCνσρ−∂νCμσρ+CμλρCνσλ−CνλρCμσλ,Ric⁡σν=Rλσλν. R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} =\partial_\mu C^\rho_{\nu\sigma}-\partial_\nu C^\rho_{\mu\sigma} +C^\rho_{\mu\lambda}C^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} -C^\rho_{\nu\lambda}C^\lambda_{\mu\sigma},\qquad \operatorname{Ric}_{\sigma\nu}=R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\lambda\nu}. \tag{E.26}
(E.26)

Expansion of two derivatives (E.24) gives

[∇λA,∇νA]aμ=[Fλν,aμ]−Rσμλνaσ. [\nabla^A_\lambda,\nabla^A_\nu]a_\mu =[F_{\lambda\nu},a_\mu]-R^\sigma{}_{\mu\lambda\nu}a_\sigma. \tag{E.27}
(E.27)

All second partial derivatives of aa cancel; the gauge terms combine as in (E.9); the remaining four coefficient terms are precisely (E.26) with its minus sign on a covariant index. Contracting λ,μ\lambda,\mu, and using metric compatibility and antisymmetry of the first two indices of the lowered Riemann tensor, gives

(∇A)μ∇νAaμ=∇νA(∇A)μaμ+[Fμν,aμ]+Ric⁡νσaσ. (\nabla^A)^\mu\nabla^A_\nu a_\mu =\nabla^A_\nu(\nabla^A)^\mu a_\mu +[F_{\mu\nu},a^\mu]+\operatorname{Ric}_{\nu}{}^\sigma a_\sigma. \tag{E.28}
(E.28)

For the sign, gμλRσμλν=−Ric⁡σνg^{\mu\lambda}R^\sigma{}_{\mu\lambda\nu}=-\operatorname{Ric}^{\sigma}{}_{\nu}. The Riemann antisymmetry used here follows by applying [∇λ,∇ν][\nabla_\lambda,\nabla_\nu] to the metric-compatibility identity ∇g=0\nabla g=0: its action on the two covariant indices gives Rαβλν+Rβαλν=0R_{\alpha\beta\lambda\nu}+R_{\beta\alpha\lambda\nu}=0. The symmetry of Ricci for (E.23) follows by substituting (E.26): the derivative difference is the derivative of Cλσλ=∂σlog⁡wC^\lambda_{\lambda\sigma}=\partial_\sigma\log w, and the remaining contracted products are symmetric after renaming indices. Thus its two placements in (E.28) agree.

Substituting (E.28) into (E.4), and retaining its additional [aμ,Fμν][a^\mu,F_{\mu\nu}] term, gives

(LAa)ν=(∇A)μ∇μAaν−∇νAGA(a)−Ric⁡νσaσ−2[Fμν,aμ],(PAa)ν=(∇A)μ∇μAaν−Ric⁡νσaσ−2[Fμν,aμ]. \begin{aligned} (L_Aa)_\nu={}&(\nabla^A)^\mu\nabla^A_\mu a_\nu -\nabla^A_\nu G_A(a)-\operatorname{Ric}_{\nu}{}^\sigma a_\sigma -2[F_{\mu\nu},a^\mu],\\ (P_Aa)_\nu={}&(\nabla^A)^\mu\nabla^A_\mu a_\nu -\operatorname{Ric}_{\nu}{}^\sigma a_\sigma -2[F_{\mu\nu},a^\mu]. \end{aligned}\tag{E.29}
(E.29)

The second covariant derivative includes the connection on its derivative index. Explicitly it is

gλμ{∂λ(∇μAaν)+[Aλ,∇μAaν]−Cλμs∇sAaν−Cλνs∇μAas} g^{\lambda\mu}\{\partial_\lambda(\nabla^A_\mu a_\nu)+[A_\lambda,\nabla^A_\mu a_\nu]-C^s_{\lambda\mu}\nabla^A_s a_\nu-C^s_{\lambda\nu}\nabla^A_\mu a_s\}

, with (E.24) substituted for each first derivative. Consequently (E.29) retains all terms in (E.6) and (E.18), including metric derivatives; it is not a replacement by an ordinary scalar Laplacian. In particular the curvature term has its full factor two and its displayed sign.

6. Ellipticity and the Lorentzian characteristic covectors

The cancellation in (E.17) now gives

σ2(PA)(ξ)a=−qg(ξ)a,σ2(ΔA)(ξ)χ=−qg(ξ)χ. \boxed{\sigma_2(P_A)(\xi)a=-q_g(\xi)a,\qquad \sigma_2(\Delta_A)(\xi)\chi=-q_g(\xi)\chi.} \tag{E.30}
(E.30)

An order-two square operator is called elliptic here when this symbol is invertible for every nonzero real covector. For positive gg, also g−1g^{-1} is positive, so qg(ξ)>0q_g(\xi)>0 for every ξ≠0\xi\ne0. The inverse of the symbol is exactly −qg(ξ)−1-q_g(\xi)^{-1} times the identity, proving ellipticity of both operators. With the positive one-form metric and κ\kappa, the symbol of −PA-P_A has pairing qg(ξ)|a|2q_g(\xi)|a|^2; on any compact coordinate region where the metric is positive definite this also supplies a positive uniform lower bound in fixed coordinate norms, since the smallest metric eigenvalue has a positive minimum. This pointwise and compact-region assertion does not imply positivity of the complete operator with its zero-order terms or solve an arbitrary boundary problem.

For Lorentzian gg, the exact characteristic covectors of PAP_A and ΔA\Delta_A are the nonzero real ξ\xi with qg(ξ)=0q_g(\xi)=0, since the symbol is a scalar multiple of the identity. In the course’s physical coordinates (t,x1,x2,x3)(t,x^1,x^2,x^3), retain

(E.31)

At the zero background this gives (P0a)ν=(−c−2∂t2+∑j∂j2)aν(P_0a)_\nu=(-c^{-2}\partial_t^2+\sum_j\partial_j^2)a_\nu. For the phase kjxj−ωtk_jx^j-\omega t, its covector is (−ω,k1,k2,k3)(-\omega,k_1,k_2,k_3) and its characteristic equation is

ω2=c2∑jkj2. \omega^2=c^2\sum_jk_j^2. \tag{E.32}
(E.32)

The wave numbers have units length−1^{-1}, ω\omega has units time−1^{-1}, and cc has units length/time, so all terms in (E.31) have units length−2^{-2}. Also ata_t has units time−1^{-1} and aja_j length−1^{-1}; their divergence terms −c−2∂tat-c^{-2}\partial_ta_t and ∂jaj\partial_ja_j both have units length−2^{-2}. The exact real change of coordinate x0=ctx^0=ct makes the Lorentzian metric −(dx0)2+∑j(dxj)2-(dx^0)^2+\sum_j(dx^j)^2, with at=ca0a_t=c a_0 and Ftj=cF0jF_{tj}=cF_{0j}. 7. An exact commuting field on two explicitly defined domains

Fix lengths H,L1,L2,L3>0H,L_1,L_2,L_3>0, a positive speed cc, and an integer n≥1n\geq1. Let 𝕋3\mathbb T^3 have coordinates xjx^j modulo LjL_j, and write V=L1L2L3V=L_1L_2L_3, k=2πn/L1>0k=2\pi n/L_1>0, ω=ck\omega=ck. The following are new domains used for an elementary example, not substitutions for the instanton’s ℝ4\mathbb R^4:

  • ME=[0,H]×𝕋3M_E=[0,H]\times\mathbb T^3, with length coordinate ss and metric ds2+∑j(dxj)2ds^2+\sum_j(dx^j)^2.
  • ML=ℝt×𝕋3M_L=\mathbb R_t\times\mathbb T^3, with physical time tt and metric −c2dt2+∑j(dxj)2-c^2dt^2+\sum_j(dx^j)^2.

Use the trivial SU(2)SU(2) bundle and the fixed matrix T=T3=diag⁡(−i/2,i/2)T=T_3=\operatorname{diag}(-i/2,i/2), so κ(T,T)=1/2\kappa(T,T)=1/2. Define the actual connection by

AE=TfE(s)cos⁡(kx1)dx2,AL=TfL(t)cos⁡(kx1)dx2. A_E=T f_E(s)\cos(kx^1)\,dx^2,\qquad A_L=T f_L(t)\cos(kx^1)\,dx^2. \tag{E.33}
(E.33)

The amplitudes ff have units length−1^{-1}. In F7.20’s component parameterization this means A23=fcos⁡(kx1)/gYMA_2^3=f\cos(kx^1)/g_{\mathrm{YM}}; the matrices in (E.33) remain the original connection, not those component variables. Both divergences G0(A)G_0(A) vanish, because only the 2-component is present and it is independent of x2x^2. The only independent curvature components are

(FE)s2=TfE′(s)cos⁡(kx1),(FE)12=−TkfE(s)sin⁡(kx1),(FL)t2=TfL′(t)cos⁡(kx1),(FL)12=−TkfL(t)sin⁡(kx1). \begin{array}{lll} (F_E)_{s2}=T f_E'(s)\cos(kx^1),&& (F_E)_{12}=-Tk f_E(s)\sin(kx^1),\\ (F_L)_{t2}=T f_L'(t)\cos(kx^1),&& (F_L)_{12}=-Tk f_L(t)\sin(kx^1). \end{array}\tag{E.34}
(E.34)

Their reverse components are their negatives and all other components vanish. For ν≠2\nu\ne2, (E.2) is zero because the only possible derivative is ∂2\partial_2. For ν=2\nu=2, its Euclidean value is T(fE″−k2fE)cos⁡(kx1)T(f_E''-k^2f_E)\cos(kx^1), while its Lorentzian value is T(−c−2fL″−k2fL)cos⁡(kx1)T(-c^{-2}f_L''-k^2f_L)\cos(kx^1). (E.35)

respectively. Their divergence gauge, curvature Bianchi identity, and Gauss equation have all been checked, rather than inferred from only the 2-component.

For Euclidean boundary values fE(0)=U0f_E(0)=U_0 and fE(H)=UHf_E(H)=U_H, where both specified real amplitudes have units length−1^{-1}, the unique solution is

fE(s)=U0sinh⁡(k(H−s))sinh⁡(kH)+UHsinh⁡(ks)sinh⁡(kH). f_E(s)=U_0\frac{\sinh(k(H-s))}{\sinh(kH)} +U_H\frac{\sinh(ks)}{\sinh(kH)}. \tag{E.36}
(E.36)

Both terms solve the first equation in (E.35), the denominator is positive, and direct endpoint evaluation gives the prescribed data. For uniqueness, a difference vv with zero endpoints obeys v″−k2v=0v''-k^2v=0; multiply by vv, integrate, and integrate by parts to get −∫0H[(v′)2+k2v2]ds=0-\int_0^H[(v')^2+k^2v^2]\,ds=0. Thus v=0v=0. The nonnegative weights in (E.36) have sum at most one: their sum equals cosh⁡(k(s−H/2))/cosh⁡(kH/2)\cosh(k(s-H/2))/\cosh(kH/2), at most one on [0,H][0,H]. Consequently |fE(s)|≤max⁡(|U0|,|UH|)|f_E(s)|\leq\max(|U_0|,|U_H|) throughout the slab. This is a direct bounded boundary-value calculation.

It does not make the same Euclidean equation a stable forward evolution problem. Fix a nonzero amplitude UU with units length−1^{-1} and, on the same fixed cylinder, set km=2πm/L1k_m=2\pi m/L_1 and

um(s,x1)=Ue−kmH/2sinh⁡(kms)cos⁡(kmx1),m=1,2,…. u_m(s,x^1)=Ue^{-k_mH/2}\sinh(k_ms)\cos(k_mx^1),\quad m=1,2,\ldots. \tag{E.37}
(E.37)

∥∂1p∂sum(0,⋅)∥∞=|U|kmp+1e−kmH/2→0,∥um(H,⋅)∥∞=|U|2(ekmH/2−e−3kmH/2)→∞. \|\partial_1^p\partial_su_m(0,\cdot)\|_\infty =|U|k_m^{p+1}e^{-k_mH/2}\longrightarrow0, \qquad \|u_m(H,\cdot)\|_\infty =\frac{|U|}{2}(e^{k_mH/2}-e^{-3k_mH/2})\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{E.38}
(E.38)

Derivatives in the other spatial coordinates vanish. The first convergence follows because an exponential dominates each fixed polynomial; for example the exponential power series bounds ekmH/4e^{k_mH/4} below by any chosen power for large mm. Thus the Cauchy data converge to zero in every fixed spatial derivative norm while the later values diverge. This proves the failure of continuous forward Cauchy dependence for this explicit class and these stated norms. It does not claim that every Euclidean Cauchy datum has a solution.

For Lorentzian initial data fL(0)=U0f_L(0)=U_0 and fL′(0)=V0f_L'(0)=V_0, with V0V_0 of units length−1^{-1}time−1^{-1}, the unique solution for every real tt is

For uniqueness, the difference satisfies (v′2+ω2v2)′=2v′(v″+ω2v)=0(v'^2+\omega^2v^2)'=2v'(v''+\omega^2v)=0; zero initial value and derivative give zero energy and hence v=0v=0. 8. The full action and energy in the example

The ordered curvature contraction in the Euclidean example is 2κ(T,T)[(fE′)2cos⁡2(kx1)+k2fE2sin⁡2(kx1)]2\kappa(T,T)[(f_E')^2\cos^2(kx^1)+k^2f_E^2\sin^2(kx^1)]. Both spatial trigonometric squares have integral V/2V/2, since n≥1n\geq1. Inserting κ(T,T)=1/2\kappa(T,T)=1/2 and the original F7.5 coefficient gives

SE[AE]=ℏV4gYM2∫0H[(fE′)2+k2fE2]ds. S_E[A_E]=\frac{\hbar V}{4g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2} \int_0^H[(f_E')^2+k^2f_E^2]\,ds. \tag{E.40}
(E.40)

For the solution (E.36), (fEfE′)′=(fE′)2+k2fE2(f_E f_E')'=(f_E')^2+k^2f_E^2, so its exact boundary action is

SE[AE]=ℏVk4gYM2sinh⁡(kH)[(U02+UH2)cosh⁡(kH)−2U0UH]. S_E[A_E]=\frac{\hbar V k}{4g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2\sinh(kH)} [(U_0^2+U_H^2)\cosh(kH)-2U_0U_H]. \tag{E.41}
(E.41)

If one perturbs fEf_E by a smooth real vv vanishing at both endpoints, the cross integral ∫(fE′v′+k2fEv)\int(f_E'v'+k^2f_Ev) vanishes by integration by parts and (E.35). Thus the action increases by exactly ℏV/(4gYM2)∫[(v′)2+k2v2]\hbar V/(4g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2)\int[(v')^2+k^2v^2], positive unless v=0v=0. This proves minimization in this specified family.

On any finite time interval [t0,t1][t_0,t_1], the Lorentzian determinant is w=cw=c, the temporal inverse metric is −c−2-c^{-2}, and σ=−1\sigma=-1. Substitution in the same original action gives

SL[AL]=ℏV4gYM2∫t0t1[c−1(fL′)2−ck2fL2]dt. S_L[A_L]=\frac{\hbar V}{4g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2} \int_{t_0}^{t_1}[c^{-1}(f_L')^2-ck^2f_L^2]\,dt. \tag{E.42}
(E.42)

The Legendre momentum of this one-mode Lagrangian is p=ℏVfL′/(2gYM2c)p=\hbar V f_L'/(2g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2c). Its energy is therefore

ℋL=pfL′−ℒL=ℏV4gYM2[c−1(fL′)2+ck2fL2]=ℏV4gYM2[c−1V02+ck2U02]. \mathcal H_L=p f_L'-\mathcal L_L =\frac{\hbar V}{4g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2} [c^{-1}(f_L')^2+ck^2f_L^2] =\frac{\hbar V}{4g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2} [c^{-1}V_0^2+ck^2U_0^2]. \tag{E.43}
(E.43)

Its time derivative vanishes by (E.35). It has units action/time; (E.40) has units action. These quantities follow from the exact trace and full ordered sums, not from a scalar action whose coefficient was chosen separately. The bounded Euclidean boundary solution, its unstable Cauchy sequence, and the Lorentzian conserved-energy evolution provide an explicit analytical distinction linked by the common connection ansatz and the exact change in the stated metric. They establish no identification of Euclidean and Lorentzian quantum theories.

Exercise 1. A gauge direction at a background that is not a solution

Use the original gauge rule and derive the infinitesimal one-form produced by uε=exp⁡(εχ)u_\varepsilon=\exp(\varepsilon\chi). Determine its exact image under LAL_A. Under what condition is it necessarily in the kernel?

Solution. Differentiating the two products in uεAuε−1−(duε)uε−1u_\varepsilon A u_\varepsilon^{-1}-(du_\varepsilon)u_\varepsilon^{-1} at zero gives χA−Aχ−dχ=−DAχ\chi A-A\chi-d\chi=-D_A\chi. Curvature and field-equation covariance give ℰ(Auε)=uεℰ(A)uε−1\mathcal E(A^{u_\varepsilon})=u_\varepsilon\mathcal E(A)u_\varepsilon^{-1}. The chain rule on the left and product rule on the right yield LA(−DAχ)=[χ,ℰ(A)]L_A(-D_A\chi)=[\chi,\mathcal E(A)]. At a solution the right side vanishes for every χ\chi. For a specific χ\chi at another background it vanishes exactly when that χ\chi commutes with every component of ℰ(A)\mathcal E(A). There is no additional zero term available to discard.

Exercise 2. Null covectors and the quotient of the symbol kernel

Let d=dim⁡ℝ𝔤d=\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathfrak g. At a nonzero Lorentzian null covector ξ\xi, calculate the kernel and image dimensions of the ungauged symbol and the dimension remaining in the kernel after quotienting by its gauge directions.

Solution. Because qg(ξ)=0q_g(\xi)=0, (E.15) is the map a↦ξ(ξσaσ)a\mapsto \xi(\xi^\sigma a_\sigma). Nondegeneracy of gg makes the scalar contraction a nonzero linear functional. For each of the dd matrix directions it maps four coefficients onto one coefficient, giving kernel dimension 3d3d, image dimension dd. The gauge vectors ξχ\xi\chi form a dd-dimensional subspace of the kernel since their contraction is qg(ξ)χ=0q_g(\xi)\chi=0. The quotient consequently has dimension 3d−d=2d3d-d=2d. This is a quotient of finite-dimensional symbol spaces at the specified covector; no quantum state count is needed.

Solution. Now k=0k=0, cos⁡(kx1)=1\cos(kx^1)=1, and the magnetic curvature vanishes. The equations for the only connection coefficient are fE″=0f_E''=0 and fL″=0f_L''=0, with solutions
