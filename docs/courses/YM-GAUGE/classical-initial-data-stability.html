Stability with respect to the original initial data

Two nearby initial data sets should give nearby solutions for a common positive time. The word nearby here has an exact meaning: the spatial gradient of the potential is measured in L2L^2, and the electric field is measured in c−1L2c^{-1}L^2. This chapter proves that estimate for existing regular solutions, evaluates a positive interval, and proves uniform spatial translation continuity and persistence of one extra derivative.

The proof combines the finite individual wave bound, fixed heat comparison, temporal differences, spatial tension differences, and original-gauge reconstruction. The last chapter uses the prefix CR. In particular CR.12 defines the ordered quadratic and cubic maps, CR.28–CR.30 give the full potential receiver, and CR.33–CR.57 supply the boundary and physical reconstruction. In these cross-references the present UjU_j coefficient for the individual potential is written AjA_j in CR; the underlying bound is the same weighted derivative norm, with each solution’s values retained.

CP.48–CP.51 prove the displayed implications. Existence on a longer interval requires the continuation argument; the estimate here holds on the original common interval of the two regular solutions.

For an arbitrary actual spatial displacement h define (𝖳hf)(x)=f(x+h)(\mathsf T_hf)(x)=f(x+h). This is the original translation, not a change of scale. Translation commutes with every spatial derivative, the original heat and wave kernels, all bracket products, and physical time differentiation. Apply it to the original solution. The translated connection is again a regular solution, with translated original data. Its initial heat ODE has the same constant identity anchor; translating that ODE and using uniqueness proves V0,h(t,s,x)=V0(t,s,x+h)V_{0,h}(t,s,x)=V_0(t,s,x+h). The backward endpoint heat gauge and the original t* temporal gauge satisfy the identical statement. Consequently the full map CP.43 is translation equivariant, including its two affine derivative terms and both anchors.

All individual norms and all numerical providers are translation invariant. A common choice of the constants CP.46–CP.49 therefore works for every h, and CP.51 gives

supt∈J∥∂(𝖳hA0−A0)(t)∥2+c−1supt∈J∥(𝖳hE0−E0)(t)∥2≤Cρ{∥𝖳h∂A0(t*)−∂A0(t*)∥2+c−1∥𝖳hE0(t*)−E0(t*)∥2}. \begin{aligned} \sup_{t\in J}\|\partial(\mathsf T_hA^0-A^0)(t)\|_2 +c^{-1}\sup_{t\in J}\|(\mathsf T_hE^0-E^0)(t)\|_2 \le C_\rho\{\|\mathsf T_h\partial A^0(t_*)-\partial A^0(t_*)\|_2 +c^{-1}\|\mathsf T_hE^0(t_*)-E^0(t_*)\|_2\}. \tag{CP.52} \end{aligned}
(CP.52)

Strong translation continuity in L2L^2 follows directly from the original Fourier identity

∥𝖳hf−f∥22=(2π)−3∫|eiξ⋅h−1|2|f̂(ξ)|2d3ξ \|\mathsf T_hf-f\|_2^2=(2\pi)^{-3}\int |e^{i\xi\cdot h}-1|^2|\widehat f(\xi)|^2d^3\xi

: its integrand tends to zero and is bounded by 4|f̂|24|\widehat f|^2. Dominated convergence proves that the right side of CP.52 tends to zero. Thus the actual spatial translation modulus persists uniformly in physical time. For a family of initial data with a common modulus and common bounded individual data radius, the same inequality proves a common modulus on J. This is stronger than pointwise continuity of each fixed regular solution.

If the original potential also has an L2L^2 difference, retain its exact temporal-gauge identity

δA0(t)=δA0(t*)+∫t*tδE0(r)dr. \delta A^0(t)=\delta A^0(t_*)+\int_{t_*}^t\delta E^0(r)dr. \tag{CP.53}
(CP.53)

Therefore

ℓ−1supJ∥δA0∥2+ρ≤ℓ−1∥δA0(t*)∥2+(1+cT/ℓ)Cρδ*. \begin{aligned} \ell^{-1}\sup_J\|\delta A^0\|_2+\rho &\le\ell^{-1}\|\delta A^0(t_*)\|_2 +(1+cT/\ell)C_\rho\delta_*. \tag{CP.54} \end{aligned}
(CP.54)

This supplies the original inhomogeneous energy-data topology with all length factors; it does not impose an L2L^2 condition in the homogeneous statement. Apply CP.53 to spatial translations and the same Fourier argument to the initial potential to obtain uniform inhomogeneous translation persistence as well.

There is a further exact derivative consequence when the original initial data possess one additional derivative. For h=r e_j, the fundamental theorem in L2L^2 gives ∥𝖳hf−f∥2≤|r|∥∂jf∥2\|\mathsf T_hf-f\|_2\le |r|\|\partial_jf\|_2. Divide CP.52 by |r|. Weak compactness in L2L^2 and distributional convergence of these actual difference quotients identify ∂j∂A0(t)\partial_j\partial A^0(t) and ∂jE0(t)\partial_jE^0(t), with the bound

To justify the sum of suprema, first fix one physical time for each summand and use the bound for both difference quotients at once; weak lower semicontinuity gives their sum, and taking the independent suprema gives CP.55. For the original regular pair the derivatives already exist, so this also follows by strong difference-quotient convergence at each pair of times. This establishes the stated one derivative persistence without a physical-Hessian coefficient in the constant. No further differentiability of the nonlinear solution map is inferred solely from its Lipschitz estimate.

12. Worked example with commuting electric data

Fix a skew-Hermitian matrix T0T_0, choose a real ψ∈Cc∞(ℝ3)\psi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3), and define

13. Exercises with full solutions

Exercise 1. Ordered cubic differences

For a trilinear map CC, expand the difference C(a,a,a)−C(a′,a′,a′)C(a,a,a)-C(a',a',a') without commuting factors.

Solution. Insert and subtract C(a′,a,a)C(a',a,a) and C(a′,a′,a)C(a',a',a). With η=a−a′\eta=a-a', trilinearity gives

C(a,a,a)−C(a′,a′,a′)=C(η,a,a)+C(a′,η,a)+C(a′,a′,η). C(a,a,a)-C(a',a',a') =C(\eta,a,a)+C(a',\eta,a)+C(a',a',\eta).

Each insertion cancels exactly once. This argument applies in particular to the nested bracket map CR.12 and uses no symmetry of its slots.

Exercise 2. The heat weight

Compute the two norms of sαs^\alpha in CP.3 and find the admissible range for the square norm.

Solution. For α>0\alpha>0,

∫0Ss2αdss=S2α2α,sup0<s≤Ssα=Sα. \int_0^S s^{2\alpha}\frac{ds}{s}=\frac{S^{2\alpha}}{2\alpha}, \qquad \sup_{0<s\le S}s^\alpha=S^\alpha.

Exercise 3. Homogeneous numerical bounds at zero

Exercise 5. Inverting inequalities without changing their signs

Solution. Move the unknown terms to the left:

Exercise 6. What uniqueness gives for the actual potential

Suppose δ*=0\delta_*=0. Prove equality of the original potentials, not only of their spatial gradients.

Solution. CP.51 gives ρ=0\rho=0; hence ∂δA0(t)=0\partial\delta A^0(t)=0 and δE0(t)=0\delta E^0(t)=0. A distribution with zero spatial gradient on connected ℝ3\mathbb R^3 is spatially constant: convolution with an approximate identity is constant on every ball, and passage to distributions gives the assertion. The actual potential difference belongs to L6L^6. A nonzero constant on ℝ3\mathbb R^3 has infinite L6L^6 norm, so that constant is zero. Thus both original fields agree.

Exercise 7. Uniform translation continuity

Explain why CP.52 gives a modulus uniform in physical time.

Solution. Apply the Fourier identity in Section 11 to ∂A0(t*)\partial A^0(t_*) and E0(t*)E^0(t_*), both in L2L^2. Dominated convergence makes their translation differences tend to zero. The constant CρC_\rho is independent of the displacement because the original kernels, norms, brackets and gauge anchors commute with translation. CP.52 places the supremum over the entire interval on the left, while its right side contains only those initial translation differences. Therefore one displacement bound works at every time in the interval. No supremum is passed through a pointwise limit.
