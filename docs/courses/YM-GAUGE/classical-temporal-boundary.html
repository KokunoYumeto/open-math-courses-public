Temporal boundary estimates and the endpoint identity

The gauge transformation at the physical heat boundary needs time-integrated bounds on at(0)a_t(0) and its spatial derivatives. The direct absolute integral of its second derivative is too weak at the critical heat weight. We instead integrate the complete heat equation first and prove the resulting endpoint identity, including its sign and domain.

Use the actual regular caloric-temporal solution and the original physical coordinate tt, speed cc, interval II and heat interval [0,S][0,S]. The prerequisites are the backward heat bounds and space-time tension estimates. The temporal curvature is W=FstW=F_{st}, with its actual Gauss datum W(0)=0W(0)=0.

2. Integrate the heat equation before estimating the Laplacian

The endpoint gauge gives the actual identity at(s)=−∫sSW(r)dra_t(s)=-\int_s^S W(r)dr. Therefore

∥at(0)∥Lt,x2≤SLW,∥∂xat(0)∥Lt,x2≤SBW. \|a_t(0)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}\le S L_W,\qquad \|\partial_xa_t(0)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}\le\sqrt S B_W. \tag{TB.6}
(TB.6)

The first integral is absolutely convergent in Lt,x2L^2_{t,x}. The derivative integral converges by Cauchy–Schwarz and TB.3, and agrees with its distributional derivative.

For every positive ϵ\epsilon, the full heat equation gives

Δat(ϵ)=−∫ϵSΔW(r)dr=−W(S)+W(ϵ)+∫ϵSNW(r)dr. \begin{aligned} \Delta a_t(\epsilon) &=-\int_\epsilon^S\Delta W(r)dr\\ &=-W(S)+W(\epsilon)+\int_\epsilon^S N_W(r)dr . \end{aligned}\tag{TB.7}
(TB.7)

Regularity through zero and the actual datum W(0)=0W(0)=0 give W(ϵ)→0W(\epsilon)\to0 in Lt,x2L^2_{t,x}. The last integral converges by TB.5. Together with the L2L^2 convergence of at(ϵ)a_t(\epsilon), this proves the exact boundary identity in distributions and in its resulting L2L^2 Laplacian domain:

Δat(0)=−W(S)+∫0SNW(r)dr,∥∂x(2)at(0)∥Lt,x2=∥Δat(0)∥Lt,x2≤LW+LN. \Delta a_t(0)=-W(S)+\int_0^S N_W(r)dr,\qquad \|\partial_x^{(2)}a_t(0)\|_{L^2_{t,x}} =\|\Delta a_t(0)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}\le L_W+L_N . \tag{TB.8}
(TB.8)

The equality of full Hessian and Laplacian norms follows by Plancherel exactly as in ST.20. Since at(0)a_t(0) and its Laplacian are in L2L^2, this also proves the required second-derivative domain. It avoids an unjustified absolute integral of ∥ΔW(r)∥2\|\Delta W(r)\|_2.

Exercise 8. The time ODE made possible by the boundary bound

On the other side of t*t_*, the same iterated Volterra integrals retain their oriented endpoints; their absolute values have the same factorial bound.

For two bounded solutions, iteration of their difference integral equation bounds that difference by its uniform norm times Bn/n!B^n/n!. Letting nn increase proves uniqueness.
