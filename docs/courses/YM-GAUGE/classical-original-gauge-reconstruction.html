Reconstructing the original gauge and its initial anchors

Heat flow makes the spatial connection easier to estimate. To compare the physical solutions, we must carry those estimates through the actual gauge transformations. This chapter proves the two maps back to the original temporal gauge, retains their initial anchors, and bounds the resulting differences. It also derives a potential-increment estimate whose remaining curve coefficient tends to zero with the physical interval.

Read the fixed heat comparison, potential wave comparison, temporal boundary estimates, and gauge comparison first. The temporal difference chapter and spatial tension chapter supply the electric and boundary inputs. These are arguments for existing regular solutions on the stated intervals.

The arrows are the maps in CR.46–CR.48. Their order matters. The lower identity compares two physical times using the same static gauge.

13. Exercises with full solutions

Exercise 1. The divergence constant

Prove CR.17 for a matrix-valued vector field with square-integrable gradient. Characterize equality in its Fourier estimate.

Solution. For each matrix entry and almost every frequency, Cauchy–Schwarz gives

|∑jξjv̂j|2≤|ξ|2∑j|v̂j|2. \left|\sum_j\xi_j\widehat v_j\right|^2 \le |\xi|^2\sum_j|\widehat v_j|^2.

Sum over matrix entries and integrate with the factor (2π)−3(2\pi)^{-3}. Parseval gives CR.17. Equality holds precisely when each vector of Fourier coefficients is parallel to ξ\xi, for almost every nonzero frequency. In particular gradients attain equality. This is an integrated Fourier bound; the pointwise bound on a general matrix of derivatives still has the dimension factor 3\sqrt3.

Exercise 2. A logarithmic heat integral

Evaluate ∫0Ss−plog⁡(S/s)ds\int_0^S s^{-p}\log(S/s)\,ds, specifying its domain.

Solution. For p<1p<1, put s=Se−us=Se^{-u}, retaining the Jacobian ds=−Se−ududs=-Se^{-u}du. The integral is

S1−p∫0∞ue−(1−p)udu=S1−p(1−p)2. S^{1-p}\int_0^\infty u e^{-(1-p)u}du =\frac{S^{1-p}}{(1-p)^2}.

Integration by parts gives the last equality; both boundary terms vanish. At p=1p=1 the integrand in the new variable is uu, and for p>1p>1 it grows exponentially, so both integrals diverge.

Exercise 3. The backward heat kernel

Tf(Se−x)=∫0xe−(x−y)/2F(y)dy. Tf(Se^{-x})=\int_0^x e^{-(x-y)/2}F(y)dy.
