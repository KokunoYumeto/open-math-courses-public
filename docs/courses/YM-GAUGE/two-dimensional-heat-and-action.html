HK.1. The group and the exact metric

Let

σ1=(0110),σ2=(0−ii0),σ3=(100−1). \sigma_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad \sigma_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix},\quad \sigma_3=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

(H1)

Then Q(q)*=Q(q0,−𝒒)Q(q)^*=Q(q_0,-\boldsymbol q), Q(q)*Q(q)=IQ(q)^*Q(q)=I, and det⁡Q(q)=|q|2=1\det Q(q)=|q|^2=1. Conversely every SU(2) matrix has the form (zw−w‾z‾)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}z&w\\-\bar w&\bar z\end{smallmatrix}\right), |z|2+|w|2=1|z|^2+|w|^2=1, and uniquely has form (H1). Matrix multiplication gives

The minus sign on the cross product follows from the chosen +iσa+i\sigma_a basis. Inverse is (a,−𝒗)(a,-\boldsymbol v). Both left and right multiplication are real linear norm-preserving maps of ℝ4\mathbb R^4, by Q*Q=|q|2IQ^*Q=|q|^2I.

The course metric is κ(X,Y)=−tr⁡(XY)\kappa(X,Y)=-\operatorname{tr}(XY), using the two-dimensional defining trace. At the identity, X=i𝒙⋅𝝈X=i\boldsymbol x\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma, Y=i𝒚⋅𝝈Y=i\boldsymbol y\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma, so

(H2)

Thus the resulting bi-invariant Riemannian metric is twice the unit-sphere metric on S3S^3. In particular its radius is 2\sqrt2, its total volume is 42π24\sqrt2\pi^2, and its Laplace operator is Δκ=12ΔS3\Delta_\kappa=\tfrac12\Delta_{S^3}. These factors are retained throughout.

The two omitted poles have mass zero. The usual change of variables for orthogonal maps in ℝ4\mathbb R^4, or its restriction to sphere charts, proves invariance under every orthogonal map. HK.3. Polynomial completeness, with its approximation proof

Real coordinate polynomials restricted to S3S^3 are uniformly dense in real continuous functions. Here are the details needed below. For continuous ff let |f|≤M|f|\leq M. Given ϵ>0\epsilon>0, choose δ>0\delta>0 such that |f(x)−f(y)|<ϵ|f(x)-f(y)|<\epsilon when |x−y|<δ|x-y|<\delta. If M=0M=0 there is nothing to prove. Set L=2M/δL=2M/\delta, and on a containing cube define

F(z)=infx∈S3{f(x)+L|z−x|}. F(z)=\inf_{x\in S^3}\{f(x)+L|z-x|\}.

The infimum is attained by compactness. The triangle inequality proves that FF is LL-Lipschitz. For z∈S3z\in S^3, the choice x=zx=z gives F(z)≤f(z)F(z)\leq f(z). Points with |x−z|<δ|x-z|<\delta give f(x)+L|x−z|≥f(z)−ϵf(x)+L|x-z|\geq f(z)-\epsilon; the other points give at least −M+Lδ=M≥f(z)-M+L\delta=M\geq f(z). Thus |F−f|≤ϵ|F-f|\leq\epsilon on the sphere.

After an explicit affine change from the containing cube to [0,1]4[0,1]^4, approximate FF by its multivariate Bernstein polynomial BmF(x)=𝔼F(K1/m,…,K4/m)B_mF(x)=\mathbb E F(K_1/m,\ldots,K_4/m), where independent KjK_j have binomial parameters (m,xj)(m,x_j). This expectation is a coordinate polynomial. Cauchy–Schwarz and Var⁡(Kj/m)=xj(1−xj)/m\operatorname{Var}(K_j/m)=x_j(1-x_j)/m bound the error by the rescaled Lipschitz constant times 4/(4m)\sqrt{4/(4m)}. It tends to zero uniformly. Taking real and imaginary parts proves complex polynomial density.

These polynomials are also dense in L2(S3,μ)L^2(S^3,\mu). To justify the intervening continuous-density statement, cover the sphere by finitely many coordinate patches whose compact subpatch interiors cover it. In each patch the measure has a smooth bounded density on smaller compact subsets. Bounded measurable functions there are approximable in L2L^2 by finite step functions on boxes: subdivide boxes and use the defining simple-function approximation for Lebesgue integration; measurable sets admit finite unions of boxes up to arbitrarily small measure. Replace box indicators by continuous ramps supported in arbitrarily thin boundary strips. The strips have measure tending to zero, since their Euclidean volumes do and the density is bounded. A continuous partition of unity for this finite cover can be built by distances to the patch complements, divided by their positive sum. It combines the approximations. First truncate an arbitrary L2L^2 function, and then remove small chart-edge subsets; their L2L^2 contribution tends to zero by integrability. This proves the assertion with no representation-completeness assumption.
