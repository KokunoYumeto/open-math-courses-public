Results on multi-marginal transport, supporting potentials, local branches and Borel transport, with their assumptions

The course proves the statements below; each link leads to the statement with its assumptions and its proof. The lessons are self-checked by the writing AI; independent mathematical review is not complete.

  • Supporting potentials — Proposition 2.1. If c >= u(x1)+u(x2)+u(x3) on U^3 with mu(U) = 1, u bounded, and some coupling is carried by the equality set, then the Kantorovich value is 3 times the integral of u, and every optimal coupling is carried by the equality set.
  • Mass imbalance (OpenAI, after Gerolin, Kausamo and Rajala) — Lemma 3.1. For mu = nu/3 + (1/6) sum of four branch images with disjoint supports, no measure-preserving Borel map selects a branch at almost every central point.
  • No Monge optimizer — Corollary 4.1. A supporting potential whose contact set offers only the four branches above each central point excludes every Monge optimizer.
  • Smooth implicit and inverse functions — Lemma 1.1. Implicit functions and local inverses of smooth maps are smooth.
  • Jets of the Riesz cost — Lemma 1.2. Hessian of |b|^-s with eigenvalues s(s+1)|b|^(-s-2) and -s|b|^(-s-2); at (0, a_k, -a_k) the cost is 2 + 2^-s, the x-gradient vanishes and the z-gradient is s(1 + 2^(-s-1)) a_k.
  • A local branch (OpenAI) — Lemma 2.1. Near each triple (0, a_k, -a_k) there are diffeomorphisms X_k, Z_k and a smooth w_k with c >= u_0(x) + w_k(y) + v_k(z), equality exactly at x = X_k(y), z = Z_k(y).
  • A potential on five balls (OpenAI) — Proposition 3.1. A bounded continuous potential on five small balls supports the Riesz cost with contact set exactly the permutations of the two branch families.
  • The common ball — Lemma 3.2. On a ball B around 0 the maps Y_k = X_k^-1 and W_k = Z_k Y_k are diffeomorphisms onto disjoint open sets.
  • The density — Lemma 4.1. mu = nu/3 + (1/6) sum of the four images of nu has a smooth compactly supported density with smooth square root.
  • No Monge optimizer for Riesz costs (OpenAI) — Theorem 4.2. For every d >= 2 and s > 0, in particular for the Coulomb cost on R^3, the three-marginal minimum for mu is finite and attained, but every pair of measure-preserving Borel maps costs strictly more.
  • Uniform variables — Lemma 1.1. If R has a continuous distribution function F, then F(R) is uniform on [0,1].
  • Quantiles — Lemma 1.2. For a continuous distribution function, the rational quantile q satisfies q(u) <= t iff u <= F(t), so q(U) has distribution function F.
  • Rosenblatt's transform — Lemma 2.1. Successive conditional distribution functions send a density on R^d to the uniform measure on the cube.
  • Borel transport — Corollary 2.3. Any two Borel probability measures with densities on R^d are related by a Borel map Q with Q#alpha = beta.
  • Splitting into prescribed masses — Lemma 3.1. A Borel set is a disjoint union of slabs of prescribed masses for a measure with a density.
  • Monge plans approach the minimum (OpenAI) — Theorem 4.1. For the density of the second lesson, measure-preserving Borel maps come within every epsilon > 0 of the Kantorovich minimum without attaining it.
