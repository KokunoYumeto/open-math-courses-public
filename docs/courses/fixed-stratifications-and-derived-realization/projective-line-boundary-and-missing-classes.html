Fixed stratifications and derived realization

The projective line: a boundary class and a missing object

Both strata of the projective line’s partition into an affine line and infinity are contractible, yet its fixed constructible heart loses a degree-two ambient class and the cone object it determines. We prove this by classifying the ordinary heart, resolving its diagrams and calculating the link at infinity.

Work over any field. Standard sheaf adjunctions and truncation triangles are prerequisites. Equations 14–19 retain their locators in the parent lesson. The example concerns this fixed partition; the separate all-refinement K3 argument in the parent course is a stronger result and is not needed here.

A fixed projective-line stratification already loses a class

The parent course gives a K3 argument permitting every complex refinement. Here we prove a simpler failure for one fixed stratification, distinguished from that stronger assertion. Let X=ℙ1(ℂ)X=\mathbb P^1(\mathbb C), let j:ℂ↪Xj:\mathbb C\hookrightarrow X, and let i:{∞}↪Xi:\{\infty\}\hookrightarrow X. Work over any field kk. Put ℬ=Cons⁡k(X,{ℂ,∞})\mathcal B=\operatorname{Cons}_k(X,\{\mathbb C,\infty\}), allowing arbitrary stalk vector spaces. Its finite-stalk subcategory is ℬf\mathcal B_f. Both strata are contractible.

The ordinary heart and its projectives

The open restriction is a constant sheaf MℂM_{\mathbb C}, since ℂ\mathbb C is simply connected. A punctured neighborhood of infinity is connected, so j*Mℂ=MXj_*M_{\mathbb C}=M_X. The following stalkwise pullback construction identifies the heart with the category of one-arrow diagrams

A→uM.(14) A\xrightarrow{\,u\,}M. \qquad\text{(14)}

The stalk at infinity is AA, and the map is its ordinary specialization to the nearby constant module. Conversely take the pullback of MX→i*MM_X\to i_*M and i*A→i*ui*Mi_*A\xrightarrow{i_*u}i_*M. Its restriction to the open stratum is MℂM_{\mathbb C}, its closed stalk is AA, and its specialization is uu. The natural maps from a sheaf to this pullback are isomorphisms at every stalk. This proves the classification for finite and infinite vector spaces.

Write P∞(V)=(V→idV)P_\infty(V)=(V\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}}V) and Po(W)=(0→W)P_o(W)=(0\to W). A map from the first to (14) is determined by V→AV\to A, and one from the second by W→MW\to M. Since vector spaces are projective, both diagrams are projective. Every diagram has the explicit resolution

0→Po(A)→a↦(a,−u(a))P∞(A)⊕Po(M)→(A→uM)→0.(15) 0\longrightarrow P_o(A) \xrightarrow{\ a\mapsto(a,-u(a))\ } P_\infty(A)\oplus P_o(M) \longrightarrow (A\xrightarrow{u}M)\longrightarrow0. \qquad\text{(15)}

The last map is identity at the closed vertex and (a,m)↦u(a)+m(a,m)\mapsto u(a)+m at the open vertex. Its kernel at the open vertex is {(a,−u(a))}\{(a,-u(a))\}, and its closed kernel is zero. This verifies exactness. All terms remain finite when A,MA,M are finite, so both hearts have no Yoneda extensions of degree at least two.

The constant sheaf kXk_X is P∞(k)P_\infty(k), already projective. Consequently

Hom⁡Db(ℬ)(kX,kX[q])=0(q>0),Hom⁡Db(X;k)(kX,kX[2])=H2(X;k)=k.(16) \operatorname{Hom}_{D^b(\mathcal B)}(k_X,k_X[q])=0 \quad(q>0),\qquad \operatorname{Hom}_{D^b(X;k)}(k_X,k_X[2]) =H^2(X;k)=k. \qquad\text{(16)}

The same source vanishing holds in Db(ℬf)D^b(\mathcal B_f). For the ambient equality, Hom⁡(kX,−)=Γ(X,−)\operatorname{Hom}(k_X,-)=\Gamma(X,-), and its derived functor is sheaf cohomology. The projective line is the two-sphere: its cell decomposition has a zero-cell and a two-cell, with zero differential. Thus its second cohomology over kk is kk. This calculates the missing morphism directly.

The missing object has the same constant cohomology

Choose 0≠u:kX→kX[2]0\ne u:k_X\to k_X[2] in the ambient category and set E=Cone⁡(−u)[−1]E=\operatorname{Cone}(-u)[-1]. Rotation gives

kX[1]→E→kX→ukX[2].(17) k_X[1]\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow k_X \xrightarrow{\,u\,}k_X[2]. \qquad\text{(17)}

The cohomology sequence gives H−1(E)=kXH^{-1}(E)=k_X, H0(E)=kXH^0(E)=k_X, and zero otherwise. The displayed triangle is its canonical truncation triangle, so its connecting class is exactly uu, including the cone sign. Thus EE is constructible for the fixed two-stratum partition, with finite cohomology stalks.

If it were realized by a bounded complex from either heart, its cohomology there would be the same two constant sheaves. Its source truncation triangle would have connecting map in Ext⁡2(kX,kX)=0\operatorname{Ext}^2(k_X,k_X)=0, hence would split. Realization preserves the canonical truncation maps, forcing the connecting map in (17) to vanish. This contradicts the choice of uu. Therefore realization from either fixed heart is neither full nor essentially surjective.

The link explains why contractible strata do not suffice

The fixed-stratification criterion tests not only the strata but the comparison between direct images derived internally and in all sheaves. The constant local system kℂk_{\mathbb C} is injective in Loc⁡k(ℂ)\operatorname{Loc}_k(\mathbb C), because that category is vector spaces. Internally its derived direct image is therefore j*kℂ=kXj_*k_{\mathbb C}=k_X in degree zero. Ambient direct image has the boundary stalk

(Rqj*kℂ)∞=Hq(S1;k)={k,q=0,1,0,q≥2.(18) (R^qj_*k_{\mathbb C})_\infty =H^q(S^1;k) = \begin{cases} k,&q=0,1,\\ 0,&q\geq2 . \end{cases} \qquad\text{(18)}

A punctured disk retracts to the circle, and arbitrarily small punctured disks give the same restriction on this cohomology. These are the actual stalks of the derived direct image. Its degree-one boundary term proves that the canonical comparison σj\sigma_j is not an isomorphism. The link’s fundamental-group map is

π1(S1)=ℤ→π1(ℂ)=0.(19) \pi_1(S^1)=\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\pi_1(\mathbb C)=0. \qquad\text{(19)}

It is not injective. Universal-cover acyclicity holds for both strata, yet the independent boundary condition fails.

Removing also zero from the open stratum changes the answer. For the three torus-orbit strata ℂ*,0,∞\mathbb C^*,0,\infty, each link circle maps isomorphically onto π1(ℂ*)=ℤ\pi_1(\mathbb C^*)=\mathbb Z, after choosing a generator. The full comparison proof in the linked lesson establishes bounded and bounded-below realization for the unrestricted three-stratum heart, for every unital coefficient ring. Thus this fixed projective-line example does not assert failure after every complex refinement. The parent course’s K3 argument supplies precisely that stronger phenomenon.

Lunts and Schnürer’s Categories of constructible sheaves, Theorem 5.25 and Remark 6.12 supplies the fixed-stratification criterion and the projective-line failure example. The full one-arrow calculation and cone argument here make the failure of both morphism and object realization explicit.

Further exercises on the boundary comparison

An injective open coefficient can have an ambient higher direct image

Difficulty: Intermediate.

On the two-stratum projective line, compute the boundary stalks of direct image derived within the constructible categories and of ambient derived direct image for kℂk_{\mathbb C}. Explain why applying the ambient functor to an internally injective object need not give a degree-zero complex.

Solution. The open local-system heart is vector spaces, so kℂk_{\mathbb C} is injective there. Its internal resolution has one term, and its internal derived image has boundary stalk kk in degree zero and zero in positive degree. In all sheaves, shrinking punctured disks computes H*(S1;k)H^*(S^1;k), with kk in degrees zero and one. The coefficient is injective in the local-system heart, not asserted injective in all sheaves on ℂ\mathbb C. Its nonzero boundary R1j*R^1j_* is exactly the obstruction to using that internal resolution for the ambient image.

A finite constant coefficient is not the injective test on a torus stratum

Difficulty: Advanced.

Let A=k[t,t−1]A=k[t,t^{-1}], the monodromy ring of ℂ*\mathbb C^*. For any injective AA-module II, prove that multiplication by t−1t-1 is surjective and hence H1(S1;I)=0H^1(S^1;I)=0. Compare with the constant finite module k=A/(t−1)k=A/(t-1). Explain why H1(S1;k)=kH^1(S^1;k)=k does not disprove the three-stratum comparison.

Solution. For x∈Ix\in I, define an AA-map from the ideal (t−1)A(t-1)A by (t−1)a↦ax(t-1)a\mapsto ax. The ring is a domain, so the representation is unique. Injectivity extends this map to A→IA\to I. Its value yy at one satisfies (t−1)y=x(t-1)y=x, proving surjectivity. The free resolution 0→A→t−1A→k→00\to A\xrightarrow{t-1}A\to k\to0 computes group cohomology as H0=ker⁡(t−1)H^0=\ker(t-1), H1=coker⁡(t−1)H^1=\operatorname{coker}(t-1), and no higher groups. The injective coefficient therefore has no positive link cohomology. On kk the map t−1t-1 is zero, and H1=kH^1=k. That finite constant module is not injective over AA; the internal derived direct image uses an injective resolution of it and recovers the same degree-one boundary term as the ambient image. A comparison isomorphism does not require every ordinary local system to have acyclic links.
