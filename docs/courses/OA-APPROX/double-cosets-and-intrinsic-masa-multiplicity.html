Double cosets and intrinsic MASA multiplicity

Written by GPT-6.1 Sol (OpenAI), Ultra, September 2026. New original text: public domain (CC0).

A MASA acts on the trace Hilbert space from the left and from the right. For a malnormal abelian subgroup, every nontrivial double coset carries the same regular representation of a product group. The number of those copies becomes a type I multiplicity. Separating the copy coming from the subgroup itself requires an additional argument: a reducing subspace alone need not give a central summand.

We use the group trace and subgroup criterion and its regular-group operator foundations. G00 proves the regular commutants; H03–H04 supply separable predual and the type II1\mathrm{II}_1II1​ conclusion. T03 proves uniqueness of the normal normalized trace for an ICC group algebra. T04 proves trace preservation for an abstract isomorphism of the group factors, using positive-supremum preservation and the bounded trace-square continuity proved in T04c; T05–T06 prove the intrinsic finite or countably infinite size of the particular matrix blocks below. These give the precise trace and multiplicity inputs without a general projection-comparison or type-classification argument.

The freely readable comparison is Sinclair–Smith, The Pukánszky invariant for masas in group von Neumann factors, §2, manuscript p. 4, for the intrinsic complement invariant; Theorem 3.2, pp. 8–10, for double-coset intertwiners; and Theorem 4.1 with its proof, p. 11, for the stabilizer multiplicity calculation. Under (1), every off-subgroup stabilizer is trivial, so all nontrivial double cosets belong to a single class and the Pukánszky invariant is the singleton {n}\{n\}{n}. The explicit regular-product model and full matrix commutant are proved below. Anantharaman–Popa, An introduction to II1 factors, Theorem 1.3.6, Proposition 4.1.3 and Lemma 12.1.9, supply freely readable proofs of regular commutation, factor-trace uniqueness and the central subgroup projection.

1. The left/right algebra and its canonical projection

Let GGG be a countable discrete ICC group and H⊂GH\subset GH⊂G an infinite abelian subgroup such that

hgk−1=g,g∉H, h,k∈H⟹h=k=e.(1)h g k^{-1}=g,\quad g\notin H,\ h,k\in H \quad\Longrightarrow\quad h=k=e. \tag{1}hgk−1=g,g∈/H, h,k∈H⟹h=k=e.(1)

On ℓ2(G)=L2(M,τ)\ell^2(G)=L^2(M,\tau)ℓ2(G)=L2(M,τ), use

For each positive integer mmm, the inverse (1+mS)−1(1+mS)^{-1}(1+mS)−1 belongs to B\mathcal BB and has norm at most 111, by H02. The role of infinitude is precise. If HHH is finite, conjugacy orbits outside HHH can support nonzero constant square-summable vectors. The infinite-subgroup hypothesis supplies the missing separation in the exercise's intended MASA setting.

2. Every nontrivial double coset is a regular product orbit

Let Γ=H\G/H\Gamma=H\backslash G/HΓ=H\G/H, Γ0=Γ∖{H}\Gamma_0=\Gamma\setminus\{H\}Γ0​=Γ∖{H}, and select a representative gγg_\gammagγ​ for each γ∈Γ0\gamma\in\Gamma_0γ∈Γ0​. The disjoint basis decomposition is

ℓ2(G)=ℓ2(H)⊕⨁γ∈Γ0ℓ2(HgγH).(4)\ell^2(G)=\ell^2(H)\oplus \bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma_0}\ell^2(Hg_\gamma H). \tag{4}ℓ2(G)=ℓ2(H)⊕γ∈Γ0​⨁​ℓ2(Hgγ​H).(4)

Every summand reduces both λ(H)\lambda(H)λ(H) and ρ(H)\rho(H)ρ(H): left and right subgroup multiplication preserve each double coset, as do their inverses.

Proof. The underlying map (a,b)↦agγb(a,b)\mapsto ag_\gamma b(a,b)↦agγ​b is onto. If agγb=a′gγb′ag_\gamma b=a'g_\gamma b'agγ​b=a′gγ​b′, then (a′−1a)gγ(bb′−1)=gγ(a'^{-1}a)g_\gamma(bb'^{-1})=g_\gamma(a′−1a)gγ​(bb′−1)=gγ​. 3. Computing the commutant, including inter-copy operators

Let AH=λH(H)′′A_H=\lambda_H(H)''AH​=λH​(H)′′ on HH\mathcal H_HHH​. The direct regular-group commutant proof, G00, applied to HHH, gives

AH′=ρH(H)′′=AH,(9)A_H'=\rho_H(H)''=A_H, \tag{9}AH′​=ρH​(H)′′=AH​,(9)

where the last equality uses ρH(h)=λH(h−1)\rho_H(h)=\lambda_H(h^{-1})ρH​(h)=λH​(h−1) for abelian HHH. Thus AHA_HAH​ is maximal abelian in its regular Hilbert-space representation. Restricting AAA to ℓ2(H)\ell^2(H)ℓ2(H) identifies it faithfully and normally with AHA_HAH​: its trace vector is separating, so the restriction has zero kernel.

On HH⊗HH\mathcal H_H\otimes\mathcal H_HHH​⊗HH​, put

D=AH ⊗ˉ AH.(10)D=A_H\,\bar\otimes\,A_H. \tag{10}D=AH​⊗ˉ​AH​.(10)

Here the spatial von Neumann tensor product means the bicommutant generated by its coordinate algebraic tensor operators, so it is strongly closed by H01. The unitaries in (6) generate DDD. To verify that their bicommutant contains the full coordinate algebras, write an operator commuting with λH(H)⊗1\lambda_H(H)\otimes1λH​(H)⊗1 in matrix entries over the second coordinate. Every entry commutes with λH(H)\lambda_H(H)λH​(H), hence with AH=λH(H)′′A_H=\lambda_H(H)''AH​=λH​(H)′′. Thus AH⊗1A_H\otimes1AH​⊗1 belongs to the indicated bicommutant; the other coordinate follows in the same way. The reverse inclusion holds because the generating unitaries already belong to the spatial tensor product. This algebra is itself maximal abelian: the representation is the regular representation of the abelian group H×HH\times HH×H, with inversion in the second group coordinate. The same trace commutation argument proves D′=DD'=DD′=D.

Let n=∣Γ0∣n=|\Gamma_0|n=∣Γ0​∣, a positive integer or countable infinity. Lemma 1.1 separates the two blocks centrally, so no intertwiner in C\mathcal CC connects ℓ2(H)\ell^2(H)ℓ2(H) with its orthogonal complement. On the first block its commutant is AHA_HAH​. On the second, its represented algebra is D⊗1KD\otimes1_{\mathcal K}D⊗1K​.

Theorem 3.1. With the hypotheses of Section 1,

C≅AH ⊕ (D ⊗ˉ B(K)).(11)\mathcal C\cong A_H\ \oplus\ \bigl(D\,\bar\otimes\,B(\mathcal K)\bigr). \tag{11}C≅AH​ ⊕ (D⊗ˉ​B(K)).(11)

The second summand is homogeneous of type In\mathrm I_nIn​.

Proof. Only the second-block commutant remains to compute. Write an operator on (HH⊗HH)⊗K(\mathcal H_H\otimes\mathcal H_H)\otimes\mathcal K(HH​⊗HH​)⊗K in matrix entries TγηT_{\gamma\eta}Tγη​. It commutes with every d⊗1d\otimes1d⊗1, d∈Dd\in Dd∈D, exactly when every entry commutes with DDD, hence belongs to DDD.

Conversely, operators of D⊗ˉB(K)D\bar\otimes B(\mathcal K)D⊗ˉ​B(K) commute with D⊗1D\otimes1D⊗1. If a bounded matrix has every entry in DDD, each finite-coordinate compression belongs to D⊗MmD\otimes M_mD⊗Mm​. These compressions converge strongly to the operator, so it belongs to the spatial von Neumann tensor product. This proves (11), including all operators that interchange equivalent copies.

Commutation with every constant multiplicity matrix unit forces a central operator to have zero off-diagonal entries and one identical diagonal entry d∈Dd\in Dd∈D. Conversely d⊗1d\otimes1d⊗1 is central because DDD is abelian. Thus the center is D⊗1D\otimes1D⊗1, in the finite and countably infinite cases alike. The diagonal rank-one projections in B(K)B(\mathcal K)B(K) give mutually equivalent abelian projections 1D⊗eγγ1_D\otimes e_{\gamma\gamma}1D​⊗eγγ​: each corner is DDD. Their central support is the second summand's identity, since a central projection z⊗1z\otimes1z⊗1 dominating one of them has z=1z=1z=1. Their matrix units identify that summand as matrices of size nnn over the abelian center DDD, or countably infinite matrices if n=∞n=\inftyn=∞. This is precisely homogeneous type In\mathrm I_nIn​. □\square□

The individual double-coset projections are generally not central in C\mathcal CC. All nontrivial double-coset representations are equivalent, so their commutant contains off-diagonal matrix units. The central separation is between the subgroup block and the entire multiplicity block.

The decomposition proof also works for arbitrary discrete GGG, infinite abelian HHH satisfying (1), and an arbitrary double-coset index set. In Lemma 1.1 one may choose a countably infinite subgroup H0⊂HH_0\subset HH0​⊂H: select countably many distinct elements of HHH and take the subgroup they generate; the finite words in these generators and their inverses form a countable set. Then its conjugation action is still free outside HHH, and its fixed vectors are exactly ℓ2(H)\ell^2(H)ℓ2(H). Finite-coordinate compressions in Theorem 3.1 then form a net rather than a sequence. Formula (11) remains valid with K=ℓ2(Γ0)\mathcal K=\ell^2(\Gamma_0)K=ℓ2(Γ0​); countability is used here only for the countable multiplicity and separable-factor formulation of the source exercise.

4. Why the multiplicity is intrinsic to the MASA

For a MASA in a finite factor, define intrinsically

CA=(A∨JAJ)′,CA0=(1−eA)CA(1−eA),(12)\mathcal C_A=(A\vee JAJ)',\qquad \mathcal C_A^0=(1-e_A)\mathcal C_A(1-e_A), \tag{12}CA​=(A∨JAJ)′,CA0​=(1−eA​)CA​(1−eA​),(12)

in its canonical trace representation. The distinguished projection eAe_AeA​ comes from L2(A)L^2(A)L2(A), not from a chosen group presentation. For the present group MASAs it is central, and Theorem 3.1 says that CA0\mathcal C_A^0CA0​ is homogeneous of type In\mathrm I_nIn​.

Theorem 4.1. If an isomorphism between finite factors carries one of these group MASAs onto another, their numbers nnn are equal.

Proof. Let θ:M→N\theta:M\to Nθ:M→N carry AAA onto A1A_1A1​. The abstract ∗*∗-isomorphism θ\thetaθ preserves increasing positive suprema. Therefore τNθ\tau_N\thetaτN​θ is a normalized order-normal trace on the ICC group algebra MMM. T04 proves its equality with τM\tau_MτM​: T04c supplies continuity along the bounded trace-square-convergent conjugation averages of T03. Thus τNθ=τM\tau_N\theta=\tau_MτN​θ=τM​, under the stated abstract-isomorphism hypothesis. Hence

Uθ(xΩM)=θ(x)ΩN(13)U_\theta(x\Omega_M)=\theta(x)\Omega_N \tag{13}Uθ​(xΩM​)=θ(x)ΩN​(13)

extends to a unitary of the trace Hilbert spaces. It intertwines left multiplication, takes AΩM‾\overline{A\Omega_M}AΩM​​ onto A1ΩN‾\overline{A_1\Omega_N}A1​ΩN​​, and satisfies

UθJM=JNUθ,(14)U_\theta J_M=J_NU_\theta, \tag{14}Uθ​JM​=JN​Uθ​,(14)

because both sides send xΩMx\Omega_MxΩM​ to θ(x)∗ΩN\theta(x)^*\Omega_Nθ(x)∗ΩN​. It therefore carries eAe_AeA​ to eA1e_{A_1}eA1​​ and unitarily conjugates the two algebras in (12). The two complementary blocks have the explicit matrix models D⊗ˉB(ℓ2(n))D\bar\otimes B(\ell^2(n))D⊗ˉ​B(ℓ2(n)) and D1⊗ˉB(ℓ2(m))D_1\bar\otimes B(\ell^2(m))D1​⊗ˉ​B(ℓ2(m)). T05–T06 prove their sizes intrinsic: a finite block has exactly its matrix size in any orthogonal full-support abelian projection decomposition of the unit, while a countably infinite block has a proper shift isometry and a finite block has none. The induced isomorphism therefore forces n=mn=mn=m. □\square□

This distinguishes MASAs as embedded pairs (M,A)(M,A)(M,A). It does not prove that their ambient factors are nonisomorphic. It also distinguishes the n=1n=1n=1 case correctly: even when both summands of (11) are abelian, the canonical projection eAe_AeA​ still singles out the complement in (12).

5. Exercises with complete solutions

Exercise 1. Verify the identity JλhJ=ρhJ\lambda_hJ=\rho_hJλh​J=ρh​, including an arbitrary complex coefficient.

Solution. On cδtc\delta_tcδt​, the first JJJ gives c‾δt−1\overline c\delta_{t^{-1}}cδt−1​, left translation gives c‾δht−1\overline c\delta_{ht^{-1}}cδht−1​, and the second JJJ gives cδth−1c\delta_{th^{-1}}cδth−1​. Thus the composite is linear and equals ρh\rho_hρh​. Omitting either complex conjugation would make JJJ fail to be the trace conjugation.

Exercise 2. Show why reducing each summand in (4) does not itself imply that their projections are central in C\mathcal CC.

Solution. A reducing projection commutes with B\mathcal BB, so it belongs to C\mathcal CC. Centrality additionally requires commutation with every element of C\mathcal CC. Two equivalent nontrivial orbit representations have an intertwiner between them, which belongs to C\mathcal CC and fails to commute with their separate projections. Lemma 1.1 proves the stronger fact eA∈Be_A\in\mathcal BeA​∈B, which gives centrality for the subgroup projection.

Exercise 3. Prove directly that the kernel of (3) is the common fixed-vector space.

Solution. If Sξ=0S\xi=0Sξ=0, positivity gives 0=⟨ξ,Sξ⟩=∑j2−j∥(Uj−1)ξ∥20=\langle\xi,S\xi\rangle=\sum_j2^{-j}\|(U_j-1)\xi\|^20=⟨ξ,Sξ⟩=∑j​2−j∥(Uj​−1)ξ∥2. Every summand is nonnegative, so every term vanishes. Conversely a common fixed vector is killed by every summand and hence by the norm-convergent sum. A vector with zero positive quadratic form belongs to the kernel, since ⟨ξ,Sξ⟩=∥S1/2ξ∥2\langle\xi,S\xi\rangle=\|S^{1/2}\xi\|^2⟨ξ,Sξ⟩=∥S1/2ξ∥2.

Exercise 4. For H={e}⊂F2H=\{e\}\subset\mathbb F_2H={e}⊂F2​, compute B\mathcal BB, C\mathcal CC and the centrality of eAe_AeA​.

Solution. Both subgroup representations are the identity, so B=C1\mathcal B=\mathbb C1B=C1 and C=B(ℓ2(F2))\mathcal C=B(\ell^2(\mathbb F_2))C=B(ℓ2(F2​)). The projection eAe_AeA​ has rank one, onto δe\delta_eδe​. An operator carrying δe\delta_eδe​ to δa\delta_aδa​, for a free generator aaa, does not commute with it. Thus it is not central. This is why malnormality with a finite subgroup is insufficient for the intended nonzero central abelian summand.

Exercise 5. Derive the injectivity of the basis map (5), and identify exactly where malnormality enters.

Solution. Equality agγb=a′gγb′ag_\gamma b=a'g_\gamma b'agγ​b=a′gγ​b′ gives (a′−1a)gγ(bb′−1)=gγ(a'^{-1}a)g_\gamma(bb'^{-1})=g_\gamma(a′−1a)gγ​(bb′−1)=gγ​. Both outer factors belong to HHH, and gγ∉Hg_\gamma\notin Hgγ​∈/H. Apply (1), with h=a′−1ah=a'^{-1}ah=a′−1a and k−1=bb′−1k^{-1}=bb'^{-1}k−1=bb′−1, to obtain a=a′a=a'a=a′, b=b′b=b'b=b′. Without this conclusion the map would quotient a nontrivial stabilizer and need not be a regular product representation.

Exercise 6. Why does (6) generate AH⊗ˉAHA_H\bar\otimes A_HAH​⊗ˉ​AH​, even though it uses the right regular representation in its second coordinate?

Solution. Set k=ek=ek=e to obtain λH(h)⊗1\lambda_H(h)\otimes1λH​(h)⊗1, and set h=eh=eh=e to obtain 1⊗ρH(k)1\otimes\rho_H(k)1⊗ρH​(k). Their joint von Neumann algebra is the spatial tensor product in (10).

Exercise 7. In the n=2n=2n=2 case, exhibit an operator in C\mathcal CC that interchanges the two nontrivial double-coset copies.

Solution. Under (8), take 1D⊗(e12+e21)1_D\otimes(e_{12}+e_{21})1D​⊗(e12​+e21​) on the second block and zero on the subgroup block. It commutes with D⊗1D\otimes1D⊗1, so belongs to C\mathcal CC. It swaps the two multiplicity coordinates and does not commute with the projection 1D⊗e111_D\otimes e_{11}1D​⊗e11​. The latter projection is therefore not central.

Exercise 8. Prove the strong convergence of finite-coordinate compressions used in Theorem 3.1.

Solution. Let PFP_FPF​ project onto the coordinates of a finite subset F⊂Γ0F\subset\Gamma_0F⊂Γ0​. These projections tend strongly to 111 along inclusion. For a bounded operator TTT, PFTPFξ−Tξ=PFT(PFξ−ξ)+(PF−1)TξP_FTP_F\xi-T\xi=P_FT(P_F\xi-\xi)+(P_F-1)T\xiPF​TPF​ξ−Tξ=PF​T(PF​ξ−ξ)+(PF​−1)Tξ, whose norm tends to zero. Thus the finite matrices recover every bounded operator with entries in DDD. This works as a net for an uncountable index set as well.

Exercise 9. What happens to the invariant when there are exactly two total double cosets?

Solution. There is one nontrivial double coset, so n=1n=1n=1 and CA0≅D\mathcal C_A^0\cong DCA0​≅D is abelian, of type I1\mathrm I_1I1​. The full algebra is AH⊕DA_H\oplus DAH​⊕D, also abelian. The canonical subgroup projection eAe_AeA​ is nevertheless preserved under pair isomorphisms, so the complement still has a well-defined multiplicity 111; it cannot be confused with an absent complement or an arbitrary abelian summand.

Exercise 10. Explain why the unitary (13) preserves the right action and not just the left action.

Solution. The trace conjugation sends xΩx\OmegaxΩ to x∗Ωx^*\Omegax∗Ω, and (14) follows on a dense set. Right multiplication by aaa is Ja∗JJ a^*JJa∗J when aaa denotes left multiplication. Therefore intertwining the left action and JJJ also intertwines the right action. Since θ(A)=A1\theta(A)=A_1θ(A)=A1​, the whole left/right generated algebra and its commutant are conjugated, along with eAe_AeA​.
