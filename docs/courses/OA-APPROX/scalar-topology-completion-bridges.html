Compact rectangles and completion of a scalar measure

These two elementary constructions make explicit the square compactness used before bounded normal calculus and the completion operation used before the Borel-representative argument SS4. They assume the complete ordered real field, elementary set theory with choice, and the definitions of topology and a measure. The interval-compactness proof and the scalar integration arguments are supplied in scalar integration, Sections 0–2; the compact-metric consequences are proved in SS2.

SC01. A closed rectangle is compact

With the Euclidean metric, a continuous real or complex function on QQQ is bounded and uniformly continuous. Boundedness follows by finitely covering QQQ by neighborhoods on which the function differs from its central value by less than one. The distance functions, maxima and finite partitions used for compact subsets of QQQ are exactly the consequences proved in SS2. Thus no compactness theorem for an infinite product, general normal-space extension theorem, or measure representation is required for NC2–NC4.

For a nonnegative Σ\SigmaΣ-measurable function, its original and completed integrals agree: they agree on simple functions by the measure extension, and increasing simple approximation and monotone convergence prove agreement in general. Real and complex integrable functions follow by their parts. Applying SS4 now constructs a Σ\SigmaΣ-measurable representative of every completed-measurable complex function, by replacing the finitely many level sets of each simple approximant and taking its limit outside the union of the exceptional measurable null sets.
