Group MASAs and singular affine examples

Written by GPT-6.1 Sol (OpenAI), Ultra, September 2026. New original text: public domain (CC0).

Fourier coefficients turn commutation with a subgroup into a condition on conjugacy orbits. A second coefficient calculation detects unitary normalizers: a finite support can be moved so that a chosen coefficient contributes its squared absolute value without cancellation. For affine groups over infinite fields, this proves singularity of a MASA.

The regular-group operator foundations prove the regular commutant, faithful normal trace, Fourier-support test and subgroup expectation directly. Its H03 proves separable predual for a countable group, and H04 proves that an infinite-dimensional tracial factor has type II1\mathrm{II}_1II1​. The group trace and expectation require no ICC hypothesis; ICC enters only in the factor conclusion.

The freely readable comparison is Sinclair–Smith, The Pukánszky invariant for masas in group von Neumann factors, Example 5.1, manuscript pp. 15–17: it treats rational affine groups and particular infinite dilation subgroups. Sections 3–6 below prove the extension to every countably infinite field and every infinite dilation subgroup. The finite-support coefficient argument in Sections 4–5 is given in full. For the group trace, regular commutation and trace expectation used in Section 1, see Anantharaman–Popa, An introduction to II1 factors, §1.3.1, Lemma 1.3.4 through Proposition 1.3.9, and Theorem 9.1.2 with Remark 9.1.3.

2. The subgroup-orbit MASA criterion

Theorem 2.1. For an abelian subgroup H⊂GH\subset GH⊂G, L(H)L(H)L(H) is maximal abelian in L(G)L(G)L(G) if and only if

{hgh−1:h∈H} is infinite for every g∉H.(5)\{hgh^{-1}:h\in H\}\text{ is infinite for every }g\notin H. \tag{5}{hgh−1:h∈H} is infinite for every g∈/H.(5)

The theorem does not require that GGG be ICC.

Proof. If x∈A′∩Mx\in A'\cap Mx∈A′∩M, the equality λhxλh∗=x\lambda_hx\lambda_h^*=xλh​xλh∗​=x makes its coefficients constant on the HHH-conjugacy orbits. Under (5), (3) forces x^(g)=0\widehat x(g)=0x(g)=0 outside HHH. Equation (4) gives x∈Ax\in Ax∈A. Since AAA is abelian, this is the MASA property.

Conversely suppose the orbit O\mathcal OO of some g∉Hg\notin Hg∈/H is finite. The operator

s=∑t∈Oλt(6)s=\sum_{t\in\mathcal O}\lambda_t \tag{6}s=t∈O∑​λt​(6)

commutes with every λh\lambda_hλh​, because conjugation permutes its summands. It is nonzero, since sδes\delta_esδe​ is a nonzero finite sum of distinct basis vectors. None of those basis vectors belongs to ℓ2(H)\ell^2(H)ℓ2(H): if hgh−1∈Hhgh^{-1}\in Hhgh−1∈H, then g∈Hg\in Hg∈H. Hence s∉As\notin As∈/A, and AAA is not maximal abelian. □\square□

In an infinite group, this criterion immediately excludes finite abelian subgroups from being MASAs. Every orbit of a finite subgroup is finite, and the group has an element outside that subgroup.

Exercise 9. For the full affine group over a countably infinite field, compute the double-coset count and the normalizer algebra of its dilation MASA.

Solution. Here H=K×H=K^\timesH=K× has index 111, so Proposition 6.1 gives exactly two double cosets, HHH and the set of elements with nonzero translation coordinate. Theorem 5.1 says every unitary normalizer lies in AAA. Conversely every unitary of the abelian algebra AAA normalizes it, and those unitaries generate AAA. Exercise 10. Suppose H⊂K×H\subset K^\timesH⊂K× has finite index in an infinite field. Prove that HHH is infinite, and identify which parts of the arguments require finite index.

Solution. If HHH were finite, its finitely many cosets would make K×K^\timesK× finite, contrary to infinitude of KKK. ICC, malnormality, the MASA criterion and singularity use only that HHH is infinite. Finite index enters solely to make the double-coset count a prescribed finite number n+1n+1n+1. The same proof works with an infinite double-coset count when the index is infinite.
