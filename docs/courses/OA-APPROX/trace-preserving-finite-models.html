Trace-preserving finite models

Written by GPT-6.1 Sol (OpenAI), Ultra, October 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. New original text: public domain (CC0).

A useful finite record has two jobs: reproduce the ambient trace exactly and represent the chosen unitary observations accurately. We start with the second job and the error allowed when a matrix contraction is replaced by a unitary. We then design a rational record by mixing reconstruction with state preparation, construct the two trace-preserving maps, and check that their composite recovers the observations.

Throughout, MMM has a faithful normal tracial state τ\tauτ, and τm=Tr⁡/m\tau_m=\operatorname{Tr}/mτm​=Tr/m. No factor or separability assumption is needed. Such an algebra is finite: if v∗v=1v^*v=1v∗v=1, traciality gives τ(1−vv∗)=0\tau(1-vv^*)=0τ(1−vv∗)=0, and faithfulness forces vv∗=1vv^*=1vv∗=1. The incoming matrix models are therefore supplied by the direct faithful-trace construction, using its trace realization, trace-retaining finite densities, scalar spatial bound and explicit matrix maps. Its stronger center-valued statements and the continuous-core construction are unnecessary here.

The complete tracial-adjoint lesson supplies finite diagonalization and matrix densities (P03), all CP and Schwarz rules (P04), the trace Hilbert completion (P05), ordinary normal trace adjoints (Lemma 1.1), the weighted Hilbert bound (Theorem 2.1), and the rational-density channel (Theorem 3.1). Their scalar and coefficient-predual inputs are the retained Sections 1–10 and CP01–06. General bounded polar decomposition is proved by the positive-inverse argument T04a; Exercise 9 below proves the finite complement extension. Its closed-range projections and positive inverses are the Hilbert projection and continuous-calculus arguments of retained scalar Sections 8 and 6. For its bounded strong limits, the coefficient-series proof is direct: choose a finite prefix with small summable tail; strong convergence handles the prefix, while the uniform operator bound controls the tail. Thus a bounded strong limit is ultraweak and stays in the given ultraweakly closed algebra. These same precise elementary inputs apply to the direct faithful-trace construction. No trace-preserving conditional expectation or general Radon–Nikodym theorem is required. Unitary budgets for Theorems 2.1 and 3.1 may always be chosen below one. The normal unital model construction below supplies the topology conversion and exact unit correction used when we assemble the trace-preserving maps.

The unitary test uses the state actually induced by reconstruction. The state-preparation construction makes the record faithful and rational. The trace reconstruction and its ordinary adjoint then give both exact trace identities. The worked diagnostics include a density with a zero eigenvalue and a reconstruction that does not preserve matrix trace.

Exercise 9. Explain how to extend the polar partial isometry of a square matrix to a unitary.

References and proof scope

The rational perturbation and trace-preserving factorization were developed by Uffe Haagerup in A new proof of the equivalence of injectivity and hyperfiniteness for factors on a separable Hilbert space, Journal of Functional Analysis 62 (1985), 160–201. A freely accessible method comparison is Xiaoyan Zhou and Junsheng Fang, A note on relative amenability of finite von Neumann algebras, Journal of Operator Theory 81:1 (2019), 107–132: Lemmas 3.5–3.6 and the scalar specialization of Claim 1 in the proof of Theorem 3.2, printed pp.120–127. The source's later relative-algebra hypotheses are not imported. The finite injectivity input is the direct local faithful-trace proof, and the exact complex estimate (14) supplies the needed real part without inferring its sign from an absolute-value lower bound.

The state-preparation proof gives the primary rational perturbation with bound 2α2\alpha2α. Every faithfully tracial injective algebra is retained; factoriality and separable predual enter the later subalgebra conclusions. These finite maps prove no AFD conclusion by themselves. The new normalization argument (N1)–(N3) proves the incoming topology and unit correction with its own explicit trace budget.

For optional reading on trace-preserving expectations, Matthew Daws's Conditional Expectations, Section 4, Theorem 4.1 (source label thm:main), Proof 1, gives an accessible Hilbert-space compression proof: the trace on the ambient algebra is normal, semifinite and faithful, and its restriction to the subalgebra must also be semifinite.
