Properly infinite injective algebras and dyadic approximation

Written by GPT-6.1 Sol (OpenAI), Ultra, September 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. New original text: public domain (CC0).

Finite completely positive models record operators in a matrix algebra. In a properly infinite algebra, their reconstruction map can be implemented by one isometry. Approximating that isometry by unitaries then moves the matrix algebra itself into a position that approximates the original operators.

We use the equivalence of injectivity and semidiscreteness proved in Averaging, crossed products, and injectivity, the Choi matrix criterion in Completely positive finite models, and standard form from OA-MOD. The projection facts are the existing foundations inputs: halving a properly infinite projection, projection comparison and projection Schroeder–Bernstein. The central finite-part criterion for projections and finite absorption are used in (8): a projection with no nonzero finite central summand in its central support is properly infinite. These facts imply a partition of the properly infinite identity into countably many projections equivalent to it. No general weight construction enters this lesson.

5. Problems with complete solutions

Exercise 1. For the unilateral shift vvv on ℓ2(N)\ell^2(\mathbb N)ℓ2(N), describe the unitaries (2) and verify (3) directly.

Solution. The defect is the first coordinate, and eje_jej​ is the jjj-th coordinate projection. The unitary cycles coordinates 1,…,n+11,\ldots,n+11,…,n+1, sends the last back to the first, and fixes all later coordinates. It agrees with the shift on the first nnn coordinates. Thus its difference from the shift has norm at most two and annihilates that initial span, giving exactly the tail bound (3).

Exercise 2. Explain why the same unitaries do not converge strong* to the shift.

Solution. The shift adjoint annihilates the first basis vector, whereas each cycling unitary's adjoint sends it to the (n+1)(n+1)(n+1)-st basis vector, of norm one. Strong convergence of the adjoints fails. Alternatively a strong* limit would preserve the identity wnwn∗=1w_nw_n^*=1wn​wn∗​=1, while the shift has a one-dimensional range defect.
