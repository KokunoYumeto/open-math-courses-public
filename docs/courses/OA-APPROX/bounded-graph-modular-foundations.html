Modular normalization and fullification from bounded graph projections

This proof concerns an arbitrary complex Hilbert space. No cyclic vector, faithful state, countability or finiteness assumption is made. Inner products are linear in the first variable. The new exposition is dedicated under CC0-1.0 to the extent of rights held. The freely readable human proof method is credited to Marc A. Rieffel and Alfons Van Daele, A bounded operator approach to Tomita–Takesaki theory, Pacific Journal of Mathematics 69 (1977), 187–221, especially Proposition 2.2, Proposition 3.1, Lemmas 5.4–5.12, Theorems 5.13–5.15 and the appendix: https://msp.org/pjm/1977/69-1/pjm-v69-n1-p17-s.pdf. The article and its images retain their human authors' rights; this dedication does not apply to them. A correction to the existence argument in Lemma 5.6 is included below.

The prerequisites are orthogonal projections and full bounded/unbounded spectral calculus, bicommutant and positive-contraction density in the original algebra, and vectorwise integration, the scalar hyperbolic kernel and continuous Fourier uniqueness. The latter uses the explicit scalar measure and integration construction, elementary Banach, Hilbert and continuous calculus, real-Borel interchange and Gaussian Fourier construction, and rectangle Cauchy proof. All Hilbert spaces and left Hilbert algebras retain their full stated generality.

The following diagram records the bounded objects used in BG1–6. The graph projection E is a projection in H ⊕ H; its entries p and b are operators on H. The spectral inversions in BG4 are performed on finite bands of R. The conclusion in BG6 identifies complete closed domains.

BG1. The real subspace determines bounded modular data

Let A be a dense complex algebra in H with conjugate-linear involution #, with A² dense, bounded left multipliers L_a, the identity ⟨ab,c⟩=⟨b,a#c⟩, and closable #. Write M=L(A)''. The representation is nondegenerate since A² is dense, and L_{a#}=L_a*. Its common kernel is zero. It is faithful: L_a=0 gives b#a#=0 for all b, hence a#=0 by nondegeneracy.

Let K be the closed real span of a#a, and let Q be the real orthogonal projection onto iK; P is the real projection onto K. Polarization says K+iK contains A² and is dense. Every vector in K lies in the graph closure of # with value itself: approximate it by real finite sums of a#a. Every vector in iK has value its negative. Closability therefore gives K∩iK=0. Put

R=P+Q,T=[R(2−R)]1/2,C=P−Q.(BG1) R=P+Q,\qquad T=[R(2-R)]^{1/2},\qquad C=P-Q. \tag{BG1}R=P+Q,T=[R(2−R)]1/2,C=P−Q.(BG1)

Both R and 2−R are injective: their kernels are respectively K⊥∩(iK)⊥ and K∩iK. They are positive real operators with 0≤R≤2. The identities C²=R(2−R), C P=(1−Q)C and C Q=(1−P)C follow by multiplying the projections. Also C² commutes with P and Q. Consequently T does too. Define J on the dense range of T by J(Tu)=Cu. The equality ||Cu||=||Tu|| makes it an isometry; its range is dense because C is self-adjoint and injective. Thus it extends to a self-adjoint real orthogonal operator, with J²=1, C=JT=TJ, and

JP=(1−Q)J,JQ=(1−P)J,JR=(2−R)J.(BG2) JP=(1-Q)J,\quad JQ=(1-P)J,\quad JR=(2-R)J. \tag{BG2}JP=(1−Q)J,JQ=(1−P)J,JR=(2−R)J.(BG2)

Here cancellation of T is valid by its injectivity, rather than by a bounded inverse. Multiplication by i interchanges P and Q, so R and T are complex linear and C and J are conjugate-linear. J is an antiunitary. In particular J K=(iK)⊥. Set V=(iK)⊥ and define, by bounded Borel calculus,

Ut=(2−R)itR−it(t∈R).(BG3) U_t=(2-R)^{it}R^{-it}\quad(t\in\mathbb R). \tag{BG3}Ut​=(2−R)itR−it(t∈R).(BG3)

The endpoint spectral masses vanish by injectivity. Scalar dominated convergence proves strong continuity and the group law. Anti-linearity in (BG2) gives J U_t J=U_t, not U_{−t}. The group preserves K, iK and V: it commutes with R and C=TJ, hence with P=(R+C)/2 and Q=(R−C)/2 as real operators. No unbounded product is being taken in (BG3).

BG2. A graph projection supplies a bounded dual vector

Define A' to consist of η∈H for which bounded B_η and a vector ηᵇ satisfy

Bηa=Laη,Bη∗a=Laη♭(a∈A).(BG4) B_\eta a=L_a\eta,\qquad B_\eta^*a=L_a\eta^\flat \quad(a\in A). \tag{BG4}Bη​a=La​η,Bη∗​a=La​η♭(a∈A).(BG4)

Both are unique by density and nondegeneracy. Associativity on A shows B_η∈M'. One has (ηᵇ)ᵇ=η. A' is an algebra with product η∘ζ=B_ηζ, B_{η∘ζ}=B_ηB_ζ, and (η∘ζ)ᵇ=ζᵇ∘ηᵇ. To verify membership, test on a∈A, move B_η and B_ζ past L_a, and use the same identities with B_ζB_η. This also proves ⟨η∘ζ,θ⟩=⟨ζ,ηᵇ∘θ⟩. Self-adjoint elements of A' lie in V: for ηᵇ=η, ⟨η,a#a⟩=⟨B_ηa,a⟩ is real, and then use real span and closure.

For any η∈H take the closure N of {(a,L_aη):a∈A} in H⊕H. This is a closed subspace, whether or not the initial multiplier map has yet been proved closable. It reduces the diagonal left representation: invariance follows from associativity, and invariance under adjoints from L_a*=L_{a#}. Write its orthogonal projection

If η∈V, the quadratic form ⟨L_aη,a⟩ is real. Polarization makes ⟨L_aη,d⟩=⟨a,L_dη⟩. Thus (L_aη,−a) is orthogonal to N, and E kills it. Its two components give

The first diagonal block p is injective: pu=0 implies bu=0 by bb=p(1−p); E(u,0)=0 then implies ⟨u,a⟩=0 for every a∈A. This is a statement about a graph projection, with no assumed multiplier adjoint core.

We next solve a real equation. For λ with Re λ>0, define on the real Hilbert space V the bounded real operator D_λ=pr_V(λ·)|_V. It has ⟨D_λu,u⟩_real=(Re λ)||u||², hence ||D_λu||≥(Re λ)||u||. Its range is closed. Its adjoint has the same coercivity, so has zero kernel; the range is dense and therefore all of V. In particular D_λ is invertible. Equivalently D_λ=(Re λ)I+(Im λ)pr_V(i·)|_V and its second term is skew-adjoint. It need not be a scalar-product operator. This supplies the missing justification in the article's Lemma 5.6.

For ξ∈K there is consequently a unique η∈V with

BG5. Recover individual vectors and the full commutant

Take ξ=a#a. Since B_ηd=L_dη for every d∈A, subtract the two formulas in (BG14) after applying them to d. For all −π<φ<π,

The bracket is continuous and bounded in H. Pair it with any vector to get a bounded continuous scalar g. BG6. Every algebra vector, fullification, and full closed graphs

For self-adjoint ξ∈A, the range support f of L_ξ satisfies fξ=ξ. This time test with every B_η from A'; their representation is nondegenerate, and B_η(fξ−ξ)=(f−1)L_ξη=0.
