The central sequence algebra is finite, but need not be a factor. We now relate its size and commutativity to ordinary sequences. The crucial result is stronger than a single nonzero commutator: if this algebra is noncommutative, every nonzero corner is noncommutative. This gives type II1\mathrm{II}_1II1​, with its center retained. For the hyperfinite factor we can go further and prove that the central sequence algebra is itself a factor.

3. Hypercentrality is also independent of the ultrafilter

  1. C(M)=H(M)C(M)=H(M)C(M)=H(M).

Being commutative is a weaker conclusion than being scalar. Theorem 2.1 and Theorem 3.1 deliberately keep these two tests separate.
