3. Why divisible degrees and full primary factors are needed

Pukánszky's finite-field construction, Lemma 5(b), pp. 295–296, motivates the tower. Nesting the fields and splitting their multiplicative groups impose two different conditions.

Increasing degrees relatively prime to nnn need not give nested finite fields. For example, with n=2n=2n=2 and p=3p=3p=3, the degrees 333 and 555 are both relatively prime to 222, but no field of order 333^333 embeds in one of order 353^535, by Lemma 1.1. We will choose degrees satisfying both relative primality and successive divisibility.

The largest power nsn^sns dividing p−1p-1p−1 need not be a direct-summand order. The highest-power assertion itself remains true at degrees relatively prime to nnn; its direct-summand inference does not.

Exercise 1. Prove the field closure of the roots of Xpk−XX^{p^k}-XXpk−X, including inverses.
