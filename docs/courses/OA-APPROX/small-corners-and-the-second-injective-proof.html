Small corners and the second injective proof

Written by GPT-6.1 Sol (OpenAI), Ultra, October 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. New original text: public domain (CC0).

A small corner can carry a good matrix model without occupying most of the algebra. We first choose one such corner which also almost commutes with the prescribed operators. A maximality argument then fills the complement with further good corners. Orthogonality makes the squared errors add, giving approximation on the whole identity.

We use finite-rank models and repaired internal matrix systems, together with the normal trace-preserving expectation: it is the L2L^2L2 orthogonal projection and is contractive in operator norm. Its exact prerequisite is Modular-invariant subalgebras and conditional expectations, ME-01, ME-04 and ME-08, specialized to a faithful finite trace.

Sections 1–2 concern a separable injective II1\mathrm{II}_1II1​ factor MMM, with normalized trace τ\tauτ. Section 3 proves a local-to-global implication for every finite algebra with a faithful normal tracial state. The final sequential conclusion retains separable predual. The scalar pinching input uses only the earlier finite expected-subfactor and contained-AFD constructions, so the second proof has its own route to the finite injective conclusion.

4. The second finite-injective conclusion

Theorem 4.1. Every injective II1\mathrm{II}_1II1​ factor with separable predual is generated by an increasing sequence of finite-dimensional unital algebras, and is isomorphic to the tracial matrix product RRR.

Proof. Proposition 2.2 gives small corners in every nonzero corner. Theorem 3.1 makes MMM locally AFD. The earlier exact containment and dyadic construction then supplies an increasing generating dyadic sequence and its trace-GNS identification with RRR. □\square□

The first proof reaches internal matrix approximation through balanced Kraus families and unitary couplings. This proof reaches it through external finite-rank projections, scalar pinching, partial-isometry repair and maximality. Haagerup's matrix-factorization method and Popa's small-corner method give two approaches to Connes' injectivity and AFD theorem. The general nonfactor finite converse is proved in Central disintegration and measurable matrix assembly and Injective algebras and separable tracial envelopes. Its central argument retains the distinction from the factor proof above.
