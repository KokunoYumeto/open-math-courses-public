Ultraproduct expectations and semi-lift ambiguity

The multiplier construction gives a von Neumann algebra Mω=Nω/IωM^\omega=N_\omega/I_\omegaMω=Nω​/Iω​ with a normal constant copy of MMM. Taking the ultraweak limit of a representative recovers an element of MMM. We construct this map, prove its normality directly, and use it to distinguish the information in a convergent automorphism family from the information in its limit.

Throughout Sections 1–2, MMM has a faithful normal state φ\varphiφ, and ω\omegaω is free. Section 3 assumes separable predual when using Mω=Cω/IωM_\omega=C_\omega/I_\omegaMω​=Cω​/Iω​. Section 4 uses the tracial hyperfinite II1\mathrm{II}_1II1​ factor RRR. Inputs are the preceding quotient and normal embedding proofs, ultraweak compactness of bounded balls, and the already proved tensor model for RRR. We construct the expectation directly from the quotient.

1. The canonical ultraweak-limit map

Theorem 1.1. The formula

Eω(π(xn))=uw-lim⁡n→ωxn(1)E_\omega(\pi(x_n))=\operatorname*{uw-lim}_{n\to\omega}x_n \tag{1}Eω​(π(xn​))=n→ωuw-lim​xn​(1)

φ∘Eω=φω.(2)\varphi\circ E_\omega=\varphi^\omega. \tag{2}φ∘Eω​=φω.(2)

Proof. A bounded sequence has a unique ultraweak ultralimit, since a bounded closed ball of MMM is compact Hausdorff for that topology. A sequence in IωI_\omegaIω​ converges strong* to zero along ω\omegaω, hence ultraweakly to zero. Thus (1) is independent of the representative.

It is linear, unital and fixes constants. For a,b∈Ma,b\in Ma,b∈M, their constant sequences are multipliers, and separate ultraweak continuity of multiplication gives

Eω(aXb)=aEω(X)b.(3)E_\omega(aXb)=aE_\omega(X)b. \tag{3}Eω​(aXb)=aEω​(X)b.(3)

Complete positivity can be checked at every finite matrix level. A positive element of Mk(Mω)M_k(M^\omega)Mk​(Mω) has a positive lift in Mk(Nω)M_k(N_\omega)Mk​(Nω​): lift its positive square root and multiply the lift by its adjoint. Every coordinate matrix is positive. Its entrywise ultraweak limit is positive because the positive cone of Mk(M)M_k(M)Mk​(M) is ultraweakly closed. These entries are exactly the values of the matrix amplification of (1).

Equation (2) follows from the definition of the quotient state. To prove normality, let 0≤Xi↑X0\le X_i\uparrow X0≤Xi​↑X be an arbitrary bounded increasing net in MωM^\omegaMω, and put Y=sup⁡iEω(Xi)Y=\sup_i E_\omega(X_i)Y=supi​Eω​(Xi​) in MMM. Positivity gives Y≤Eω(X)Y\le E_\omega(X)Y≤Eω​(X). Normality of the two states and (2) give

φ(Eω(X)−Y)=φω(X)−lim⁡iφω(Xi)=0.(4)\varphi(E_\omega(X)-Y) =\varphi^\omega(X)-\lim_i\varphi^\omega(X_i)=0. \tag{4}φ(Eω​(X)−Y)=φω(X)−ilim​φω(Xi​)=0.(4)

The map depends on the ultrafilter but not on the state used to describe the strong*-null ideal. The state identity (2) is a convenient proof of normality, rather than an extra definition of the map.

3. A trace with values in the original center

Assume now that MMM has separable predual, without imposing a factor hypothesis. The preceding lesson embeds the finite von Neumann algebra MωM_\omegaMω​ normally in MωM^\omegaMω.

5. Two limits on a fast reindexing theorem

The same example prevents fast reindexing with unrestricted semi-lift equivariance.

Proposition 5.1. There are separable von Neumann subalgebras P,Q⊂RωP,Q\subset R^\omegaP,Q⊂Rω and a countable group GGG of semi-liftable automorphisms preserving PPP, for which no injective *-homomorphism can satisfy simultaneously

Φ(P∩Rω)⊂Q′∩Rω,γΦ=Φγ(γ∈G).(17)\Phi(P\cap R_\omega)\subset Q'\cap R_\omega, \qquad \gamma\Phi=\Phi\gamma\quad(\gamma\in G). \tag{17}Φ(P∩Rω​)⊂Q′∩Rω​,γΦ=Φγ(γ∈G).(17)

Both projections have trace 1/21/21/2, so the representation is faithful. In particular PPP has separable predual and lies in RωR_\omegaRω​. 6. Exercises with complete solutions

Exercise 1. Why does a positive matrix in the quotient have a positive matrix lift for the complete positivity argument?

Solution. The quotient map Mk(Nω)→Mk(Mω)M_k(N_\omega)\to M_k(M^\omega)Mk​(Nω​)→Mk​(Mω) is surjective. Lift the positive square root of the given matrix to BBB, and use B∗BB^*BB∗B. Its image is the original positive matrix. Every coordinate of B∗BB^*BB∗B is positive, so taking ultraweak limits proves positivity at that matrix level.

Exercise 2. Prove normality of EωE_\omegaEω​ without interchanging an increasing supremum with an ultralimit.

Solution. Form the supremum YYY of the increasing images in the already known von Neumann algebra MMM. Positivity gives Y≤Eω(X)Y\le E_\omega(X)Y≤Eω​(X). The normal state identity (2) gives zero value on their positive difference, as in (4), and faithfulness makes the difference zero. No interchange of two limits is used.

Exercise 3. What changes if MMM has a nontrivial center?

Solution. The ultraweak limit of a centralizing representative need only be central, rather than scalar. Equations (10)–(11) still prove a normal Z(M)Z(M)Z(M)-valued trace. A scalar trace on MωM_\omegaMω​ is then obtained by composing with a normal state on the original center; different center states can give different scalar traces.

Exercise 4. Check the off-diagonal matrix units in the counterexample.

Solution. Put e=(1+Z)/2e=(1+Z)/2e=(1+Z)/2, f=(1−Z)/2f=(1-Z)/2f=(1−Z)/2, v=eXfv=eXfv=eXf. Anticommutation gives Xf=eXXf=eXXf=eX and Xe=fXXe=fXXe=fX. Hence vv∗=evv^*=evv∗=e, v∗v=fv^*v=fv∗v=f, v2=0v^2=0v2=0, and e+f=1e+f=1e+f=1. Together with e,f,v,v∗e,f,v,v^*e,f,v,v∗, these are a unital M2M_2M2​ system. Since Eω(Z)=0E_\omega(Z)=0Eω​(Z)=0, both diagonal projections have trace 1/21/21/2.

Exercise 5. Compute the size of the ambiguity between the two semi-lifts on XXX.

Solution. One sends XXX to −X-X−X, and the other sends it to XXX. The difference is −2X-2X−2X, with squared trace 222-norm 4τω(X∗X)=44\tau_\omega(X^*X)=44τω​(X∗X)=4. In particular it is neither zero nor a strong*-null quotient element.

Exercise 6. Explain why covariance with EωE_\omegaEω​ does not detect this ambiguity.

Solution. The element XXX has ultraweak limit zero, so Eω(X)=Eω(−X)=0E_\omega(X)=E_\omega(-X)=0Eω​(X)=Eω​(−X)=0. Both families have identity limit and therefore satisfy (6) with that limit. The faithful expectation detects positive elements faithfully, but it is not an injective linear map on all elements.

Exercise 7. Identify the incompatible requirements in Proposition 5.1.

Solution. Relative commutation with QQQ, which contains ZZZ, makes the image of XXX fixed by Ad⁡(Z)\operatorname{Ad}(Z)Ad(Z). Equivariance makes that same image negated because XXX is negated in the domain. Over C\mathbb CC these equations force zero. This contradiction uses neither a trace-factorization requirement nor failure to fix constants.

Exercise 8. Recover the scalar centralizing version of (19) when MMM is a factor.

Solution. If x∈P∩Mωx\in P\cap M_\omegax∈P∩Mω​, Theorem 3.1 gives Eω(x)=τω(x)1E_\omega(x)=\tau_\omega(x)1Eω​(x)=τω​(x)1. Equation (19) becomes Eω(aΦ(x))=τω(x)Eω(a)E_\omega(a\Phi(x))=\tau_\omega(x)E_\omega(a)Eω​(aΦ(x))=τω​(x)Eω​(a). Applying any normal state φ\varphiφ gives φω(aΦ(x))=τω(x)φω(a)\varphi^\omega(a\Phi(x))=\tau_\omega(x)\varphi^\omega(a)φω(aΦ(x))=τω​(x)φω(a).
