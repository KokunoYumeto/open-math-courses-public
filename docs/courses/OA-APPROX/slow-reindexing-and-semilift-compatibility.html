6. Exercises with complete solutions

Exercise 1. Why is βγ\beta_\gammaβγ​ unique even though a convergent limit does not determine its semi-lift?

Solution. The chosen family acts as its limit on every constant a∈Ma\in Ma∈M. Hence βγ(a)=γ(a)\beta_\gamma(a)=\gamma(a)βγ​(a)=γ(a). An actual γ\gammaγ fixes that restriction, so every family representing it has the same limit. The reverse implication fails: different semi-lifts can have that same restriction.

Exercise 2. What purpose does the hypothesis bγ∈Hb_\gamma\in Hbγ​∈H serve?

Solution. It ensures bγ(P)=Pb_\gamma(P)=Pbγ​(P)=P, so Φ(bγ(x))\Phi(b_\gamma(x))Φ(bγ​(x)) is defined. It also allows a countable algebra invariant under all those constant lifts and their finite equivariance tests. Invariance under γ\gammaγ alone would not imply invariance under its different constant lift.

Exercise 3. Explain why the first weak-limit test uses Eω(a)E_\omega(a)Eω​(a) rather than va(k)v_a(k)va​(k).

Solution. The first selection varies the inner index while fixing nnn. After p(n)p(n)p(n) is fixed, the second selection varies the outer coordinate and replaces va(k)v_a(k)va​(k) by that expectation, yielding the second error in (9).
