5. Normal extension and an explicit example

The faithful-state extension lemma applies: norm closure first, then bounded Kaplansky approximation and the exact symmetric state isometry give a unique normal injective *-homomorphism on PPP. Constants are fixed by normal density. The image of P∩MωP\cap M_\omegaP∩Mω​ stays in the normal subalgebra MωM_\omegaMω​. Here are the extension steps for each clause. Complete A\mathcal AA in norm first; rational complex linearity extends to complex linearity by the norm bound. For every bounded strong* approximating net from that completion, the state isometry makes its image a strong*-Cauchy net in the full target von Neumann algebra. The full proof of Lemma 5.1 establishes its limit using the cyclic separating vector, its dense commutant orbit and the common operator bound, proves independence of the approximating net, and passes bounded products and adjoints to the limit. Faithfulness gives injectivity. On increasing positive nets, preservation of ρ\rhoρ forces the image supremum to equal the image of the original supremum; the complete order-normal-functional/predual theorem then gives normality against every normal functional. 6. Exercises with complete solutions

Exercise 1. Why is βγ\beta_\gammaβγ​ unique even though a convergent limit does not determine its semi-lift?

Solution. The chosen family acts as its limit on every constant a∈Ma\in Ma∈M. Hence βγ(a)=γ(a)\beta_\gamma(a)=\gamma(a)βγ​(a)=γ(a). An actual γ\gammaγ fixes that restriction, so every family representing it has the same limit. The reverse implication fails: different semi-lifts can have that same restriction.

Exercise 2. What purpose does the hypothesis bγ∈Hb_\gamma\in Hbγ​∈H serve?

Solution. It ensures bγ(P)=Pb_\gamma(P)=Pbγ​(P)=P, so Φ(bγ(x))\Phi(b_\gamma(x))Φ(bγ​(x)) is defined. It also allows a countable algebra invariant under all those constant lifts and their finite equivariance tests. Invariance under γ\gammaγ alone would not imply invariance under its different constant lift.

Exercise 3. Explain why the first weak-limit test uses Eω(a)E_\omega(a)Eω​(a) rather than va(k)v_a(k)va​(k).

Solution. The first selection varies the inner index while fixing nnn. The coefficient Eω(a)E_\omega(a)Eω​(a) is an operator of MMM independent of that index, so ultraweak convergence of uxu_xux​ gives (4). After p(n)p(n)p(n) is fixed, the second selection varies the outer coordinate and replaces va(k)v_a(k)va​(k) by that expectation, yielding the second error in (9).

Exercise 4. Why does the second commutator test apply to every xxx, rather than only centralizing xxx?
