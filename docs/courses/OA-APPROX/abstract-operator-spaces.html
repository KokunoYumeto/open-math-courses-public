From matrix norms to operators

Written by GPT-6.1 Sol (OpenAI), Ultra, September 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. New original text: public domain (CC0).

A normed vector space remembers the size of a single vector. A subspace of bounded operators also remembers the size of every matrix of its vectors. These matrix norms interact with direct sums and scalar matrix multiplication. Ruan's theorem says that those two interactions are enough to recover an operator realization.

We will prove the theorem by constructing enough completely contractive maps to detect every matrix norm. The construction uses ordinary Hahn–Banach and finite-dimensional states. It does not assume that the vector space is complete or separable.

The retained scalar foundations prove scalar compactness, Hahn–Banach, Hilbert representation, operator adjoints, the C*-identity and Hilbert completion. MG01 proves scalar Cauchy–Schwarz and norming states, including for a positive scalar matrix. We use the exact retained scalar inputs for that proof. A linear map is completely contractive when its entrywise map on matrices is contractive at every size; a complete isometry preserves every such norm. No extension or factorization theorem for completely bounded maps is required here. Inner products are linear in the second variable.

The construction has one concrete goal: given a matrix YYY, find a coefficient operator whose norm at that size detects ∥Y∥\|Y\|∥Y∥, while its norms at every other size stay below the prescribed norms. The finite-state lemma provides those simultaneous bounds. Rectangular coefficient operators then combine into one Hilbert-space direct sum.

The scalar matrix norms here are operator norms between Euclidean spaces. Products axbaxbaxb use linear combinations of the entries of xxx, so require no multiplication on VVV.

If V⊂B(H)V\subset B(H)V⊂B(H), its inherited matrix norms satisfy both axioms. The first follows from the norm of a block diagonal operator, and the second from composition with the operators a⊗1Ha\otimes1_Ha⊗1H​ and b⊗1Hb\otimes1_Hb⊗1H​.

3. Turn the bound into a coefficient operator

For the states in Lemma 2.1, make Hilbert spaces from the scalar matrices. Let HpH_pHp​ be MnM_nMn​ modulo the null space of

∥a∥p2=p(aa∗),\|a\|_p^2=p(aa^*),∥a∥p2​=p(aa∗),

and let HqH_qHq​ be the analogous quotient for ∥b∥q2=q(b∗b)\|b\|_q^2=q(b^*b)∥b∥q2​=q(b∗b). The inner products are ⟨a,c⟩p=p(ca∗)\langle a,c\rangle_p=p(ca^*)⟨a,c⟩p​=p(ca∗) and ⟨b,d⟩q=q(b∗d)\langle b,d\rangle_q=q(b^*d)⟨b,d⟩q​=q(b∗d). Positivity of the states makes these forms positive semidefinite. Scalar Cauchy–Schwarz shows that a null vector pairs to zero with every vector, so the null sets are linear subspaces, and the forms descend to positive definite forms on the quotients. A finite-dimensional inner-product space is complete: choose an orthonormal basis by the finite Gram–Schmidt procedure and take the coordinate limits of any Cauchy sequence. These spaces are therefore finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. Write Hp‾\overline{H_p}Hp​​ for the conjugate Hilbert space; the correspondence a↦aˉa\mapsto\bar aa↦aˉ is conjugate linear.

4. Detect every matrix norm

Theorem 4.1 (Ruan representation). Every abstract operator space VVV has a linear complete isometry into B(H)B(H)B(H) for some Hilbert space HHH. If VVV is complete, its image is a closed subspace.

Proof. Fix nonzero Y=[vij]∈Mn(V)Y=[v_{ij}]\in M_n(V)Y=[vij​]∈Mn​(V). Hahn–Banach gives a functional fff on Mn(V)M_n(V)Mn​(V) with ∥f∥=1\|f\|=1∥f∥=1 and f(Y)=∥Y∥nf(Y)=\|Y\|_nf(Y)=∥Y∥n​. Use Lemmas 2.1 and 3.1 to construct θf\theta_fθf​.

∑ip(aiai∗)=p(1n)=1,∑jq(bj∗bj)=q(1n)=1.\sum_i p(a_i a_i^*)=p(1_n)=1, \qquad \sum_j q(b_j^*b_j)=q(1_n)=1.i∑​p(ai​ai∗​)=p(1n​)=1,j∑​q(bj∗​bj​)=q(1n​)=1.

Moreover,

∑i,jaiDn(vij)bj=Y.\sum_{i,j}a_iD_n(v_{ij})b_j=Y.i,j∑​ai​Dn​(vij​)bj​=Y.

Equation (3) shows that a coefficient of θf(n)(Y)\theta_f^{(n)}(Y)θf(n)​(Y), between these unit tuples, equals f(Y)=∥Y∥nf(Y)=\|Y\|_nf(Y)=∥Y∥n​. Complete contractivity gives the other inequality. Hence ∥θf(n)(Y)∥=∥Y∥n\|\theta_f^{(n)}(Y)\|=\|Y\|_n∥θf(n)​(Y)∥=∥Y∥n​.

Embed the rectangular operator θf(v):Hq→Hp‾\theta_f(v):H_q\to\overline{H_p}θf​(v):Hq​→Hp​​ as the upper-right corner in B(Hp‾⊕Hq)B(\overline{H_p}\oplus H_q)B(Hp​​⊕Hq​), with all other corners zero. For every size mmm and X∈Mm(V)X\in M_m(V)X∈Mm​(V), this corner sends a tuple u⊕wu\oplus wu⊕w to θf(m)(X)w⊕0\theta_f^{(m)}(X)w\oplus0θf(m)​(X)w⊕0. The upper bound is its operator norm, and testing tuples 0⊕w0\oplus w0⊕w gives the reverse bound. Thus corner insertion preserves the norm at every size. For each nonzero matrix YYY at each size, choose such a map that detects its norm. Form the Hilbert direct sum of their spaces and the block diagonal map

Θ(v)=⨁YθY(v).\Theta(v)=\bigoplus_Y\theta_Y(v).Θ(v)=Y⨁​θY​(v).

The direct sum is the space of families (ζY)(\zeta_Y)(ζY​) for which the supremum of the finite sums ∑Y∈F∥ζY∥2\sum_{Y\in F}\|\zeta_Y\|^2∑Y∈F​∥ζY​∥2 is finite. Finite-support vectors are dense by that supremum; coordinatewise limits of a Cauchy sequence, and passage of its Cauchy bound to each finite sum, prove completeness. Each coordinate is a complete contraction. Summing the squared coordinate bounds first on finite-support vectors defines Θ(v)\Theta(v)Θ(v) with norm at most ∥v∥\|v\|∥v∥, and then extends it by continuity. At a fixed matrix size, interchanging the finite tuple with the Hilbert direct sum is an isometry, since its squared norm is the same sum in either order. The corresponding entrywise operator acts coordinatewise, so the identical bound proves contractivity at every matrix size. For any YYY, its own coordinate gives the reverse inequality at that size. Thus Θ\ThetaΘ is a complete isometry.

The zero vector space has the trivial realization. In the nonzero case the index family is a set: it is contained in the union of the sets Mn(V)M_n(V)Mn​(V), n≥1n\ge1n≥1. There is no separability assertion about the resulting Hilbert space. Finally an isometry from a complete normed space has closed image. □\square□

The operator realization need not be an algebra, self-adjoint, or unital. The theorem realizes the specified linear space and all of its matrix norms. It introduces none of those additional structures.

5. An elementary construction of matrix norms

Let EEE be any normed space. Set

∥[xij]∥nmin⁡=sup⁡g∈E∗∥g∥≤1∥[g(xij)]∥Mn.\|[x_{ij}]\|_n^{\min} =\sup_{\substack{g\in E^*\\\|g\|\le1}} \|[g(x_{ij})]\|_{M_n}.∥[xij​]∥nmin​=g∈E∗∥g∥≤1​sup​∥[g(xij​)]∥Mn​​.

This is a norm. If an entry is nonzero, Hahn–Banach gives a functional detecting it. The direct-sum and scalar-multiplication axioms follow from their scalar matrix versions and taking suprema. At scalar size the norm is the original norm of EEE.

There is a direct operator realization here: map xxx to the bounded scalar function g↦g(x)g\mapsto g(x)g↦g(x) on the dual unit ball, and let that function act by multiplication on the Hilbert space ℓ2\ell^2ℓ2 of that set. At every size the operator norm is exactly the displayed supremum. This example illustrates that the same scalar norm can be extended to matrix norms in a way that reflects a chosen operator structure. The representation theorem applies to every structure satisfying the axioms, not only to this one.
