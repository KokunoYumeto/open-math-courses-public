Nuclear biduals and extensions

Written by GPT-6.1 Sol (OpenAI), Ultra, September 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. New original text: public domain (CC0).

There are two different approximation questions here. A finite model of a C*-algebra must approximate each chosen element in norm. A finite model of its bidual is tested by normal functionals. We will connect those questions after constructing the central projection that separates an ideal from its quotient.

The central projection is useful even before approximation enters the argument: it explains how the quotient map acts on the bidual, and why the original algebra need not split. The other construction is a completely positive retraction onto a represented commutant. We build it on a dilation space, including the extra step needed when the coefficient algebra has no unit. These two constructions meet in the nuclearity and extension theorems below.

Prerequisites are Tensor positivity and nuclearity, Finite models of a von Neumann algebra, and Averaging, crossed products, and injectivity. The state representation and bidual construction identifies A∗∗A^{**}A∗∗ with its universal represented closure and its predual with A∗A^*A∗. The normal-extension theorem gives central kernels and normal isomorphisms onto essential represented closures. Its second-adjoint proof treats inclusions and quotients. The underlying central-ideal result is Theorem 8.3(4).

All algebras may be nonunital, and all Hilbert spaces and index sets may be arbitrary. Zero algebras have the unique zero interpretation. Inner products are linear in the second variable.

Convert the two approximation questions

Theorem 3.1. For any C*-algebra AAA,

A is nuclear⟺A∗∗ is injective.A\text{ is nuclear} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A^{**}\text{ is injective}.A is nuclear⟺A∗∗ is injective.

Proof. Starting with nuclear AAA, Proposition 2.1 constructs a retraction onto every state GNS commutant. Commutant permanence gives injectivity of the corresponding represented von Neumann algebras; the finite-cover construction of Lemma 1.1 then gives injectivity of A∗∗A^{**}A∗∗.

In the reverse direction, let M=A∗∗M=A^{**}M=A∗∗ be injective. The injective/semidiscrete equivalence in the averaging lesson produces cpc predual factorizations

M∗⟶Mn∗⟶M∗M_*\longrightarrow M_n^*\longrightarrow M_*M∗​⟶Mn∗​⟶M∗​

approximating the identity at each predual element in norm. The identification M∗=A∗M_*=A^*M∗​=A∗ preserves both the norm and the dual matrix orders. These factorizations therefore satisfy the pointwise weak* hypothesis of Corollary 4.2 in the tensor-positivity lesson, which proves nuclearity of AAA. The norm on Mn∗M_n^*Mn∗​ is the trace norm. Thus the passage from normal tests back to norm approximation uses predual approximation and the proved tensor corollary. □\square□

Corollary 3.2. If AAA is nuclear, the von Neumann algebra generated by every nondegenerate representation of AAA is injective and semidiscrete. Conversely, this property for the universal representation, or injectivity for every state GNS representation, implies nuclearity.

Proof. A nondegenerate represented closure is normally isomorphic to a central summand of A∗∗A^{**}A∗∗. Central corners preserve injectivity, and injectivity gives semidiscreteness. The universal representation realizes the whole bidual. The last assertion follows from Lemma 1.1 and Theorem 3.1. □\square□

For a degenerate representation, the represented closure is understood on its essential Hilbert space. Taking the bicommutant in an ambient space with a zero representation summand adds an unrelated identity there; the normal extension theorem is applied to the essential space.
