Finite outer actions and Bernoulli shifts

Written by GPT-6.1 Sol (OpenAI), Ultra, September 2026. Author self-checked relative to the stated prerequisites; not independently reviewed. New original text: public domain (CC0).

A finite group can act on the hyperfinite finite factor while every nonidentity element acts outerly. Averaging then produces a fixed algebra with finite-dimensional approximants. To see that this algebra is again a factor, we compute a relative commutant using finite Fourier coefficients.

We use the tracial infinite product and the finite AFD uniqueness theorem. The foundational inputs are the finite trace GNS representation, JMJ=M′JMJ=M'JMJ=M′, the bicommutant theorem and the fact that an ultraweakly closed ideal in a von Neumann algebra is a central summand. No classification theorem is used.

An action satisfies αgαh=αgh\alpha_g\alpha_h=\alpha_{gh}αg​αh​=αgh​. Throughout Sections 1–3, MMM is a factor of type II1\mathrm{II}_1II1​, τ\tauτ is its normalized trace and GGG is finite. An automorphism is outer if it has no unitary implementer in MMM.

Lemma 1.3. Under the same outerness hypothesis, any faithful normal covariant representation (ρ,V)(\rho,V)(ρ,V) gives a normal isomorphism

Proof. Covariance makes (8) a unital star homomorphism. The finite coefficient bound gives

∥∑gρ(xg)Vg∥≤∑g∥xg∥≤∣G∣∥X∥,\left\|\sum_g\rho(x_g)V_g\right\| \le\sum_g\|x_g\|\le |G|\|X\|,​g∑​ρ(xg​)Vg​​≤g∑​∥xg​∥≤∣G∣∥X∥,

so this homomorphism is bounded, hence contractive by the C∗C^*C∗-identity. It is normal because there are finitely many normal coefficient maps. Its kernel is a central ideal in the factor PPP, and it cannot be all of PPP because the map is unital. It is therefore zero. A faithful normal representation of a von Neumann algebra has ultraweakly closed range: its bounded balls are ultraweakly compact and their image is compact, and the inverse is normal. Thus the range is precisely the indicated generated algebra. □\square□

Finiteness is used in both the coefficient expansion and the norm bound. This proof does not supply an integrated isomorphism for arbitrary infinite groups.

4. Countable Bernoulli actions and the inverse index

Let Γ\GammaΓ be an infinite countable discrete group and use the tracial product RΓR_\GammaRΓ​ indexed by Γ\GammaΓ. The left Bernoulli action is

βh ⁣(⨂g∈Γxg)=⨂g∈Γxh−1g,(17)\beta_h\!\left(\bigotimes_{g\in\Gamma}x_g\right) =\bigotimes_{g\in\Gamma}x_{h^{-1}g}, \tag{17}βh​​g∈Γ⨂​xg​​=g∈Γ⨂​xh−1g​,(17)

where the entry on the right is placed at site ggg. Thus a local element at site ggg moves to site hghghg.

Proposition 4.1. Formula (17) extends to a trace-preserving normal action, and βh\beta_hβh​ is outer for every h≠eh\ne eh=e.

Proof. Coordinate permutation is a star automorphism of the algebraic local union, preserves its trace and has inverse βh−1\beta_{h^{-1}}βh−1​. Its trace GNS unitary extends it normally to the generated von Neumann algebra. On local tensors direct substitution gives βhβk=βhk\beta_h\beta_k=\beta_{hk}βh​βk​=βhk​, and normality extends the equality.

Fix h≠eh\ne eh=e. Choose sites gng_ngn​ so that both gng_ngn​ and hgnhg_nhgn​ eventually avoid every fixed finite subset. This is possible by avoiding F∪h−1FF\cup h^{-1}FF∪h−1F at each step of an exhaustion. The ZZZ-operators at gng_ngn​ are central by the estimate (14), and their images are the ZZZ-operators at the distinct sites hgnhg_nhgn​. Their 222-distance is 2\sqrt22​. An inner automorphism would move them a distance tending to zero. □\square□

Placing xhgx_{hg}xhg​ at site ggg instead defines

γh=βh−1,γhγk=γkh.(18)\gamma_h=\beta_{h^{-1}},\qquad \gamma_h\gamma_k=\gamma_{kh}. \tag{18}γh​=βh−1​,γh​γk​=γkh​.(18)

Each map is an automorphism and is outer away from the identity, but the maps form an action of the opposite group. They give an action of Γ\GammaΓ itself only when the relevant group elements commute. Formula (17) supplies the inverse needed for a left action.

5. Exercises with complete solutions

Exercise 1. In the regular representation, locate the coefficient xgx_gxg​ in a matrix entry, and locate the coefficient of the identity in X∗XX^*XX∗X.

Exercise 2. Suppose a nonzero aaa satisfies aθ(y)=yaa\theta(y)=yaaθ(y)=ya for every yyy in a factor. Explain why a partial-isometry implementer cannot have a proper nonzero support.

Solution. Taking adjoints of the relation with y∗y^*y∗ gives θ(y)a∗=a∗y\theta(y)a^*=a^*yθ(y)a∗=a∗y. Hence a∗a∈θ(M)′∩M=C1a^*a\in\theta(M)'\cap M=\mathbb C1a∗a∈θ(M)′∩M=C1 and aa∗∈M′∩M=C1aa^*\in M'\cap M=\mathbb C1aa∗∈M′∩M=C1. Their support projections are nonzero central projections, hence 111. The polar partial isometry is therefore unitary, and the intertwining relation implements θ\thetaθ by its adjoint.

Exercise 3. Find a nonfactor on which a nonidentity automorphism is outer, although its fixed algebra has a nontrivial relative commutant.

Solution. Take M=C⊕C2M=\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C^2M=C⊕C2 and let the generator of Z/2\mathbb Z/2Z/2 exchange the last two coordinates and fix the first. Every inner automorphism of an abelian algebra is the identity, so this nonidentity automorphism is outer. Its fixed algebra is C⊕{(z,z):z∈C}\mathbb C\oplus\{(z,z):z\in\mathbb C\}C⊕{(z,z):z∈C}. Since MMM is abelian, its relative commutant in MMM is all of MMM. It also acts trivially on a nonzero central summand. Thus ordinary outerness is insufficient for the factor conclusion off factors.

Exercise 4. For the swap action on M2⊗M2M_2\otimes M_2M2​⊗M2​, determine the fixed algebra and explain why it need not be a full matrix algebra.

Solution. The swap of the two tensor legs is implemented by the flip unitary on C2⊗C2\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^2C2⊗C2. Its eigenspaces are the symmetric space of dimension 333 and the antisymmetric space of dimension 111. The fixed algebra is its commutant, M3⊕CM_3\oplus\mathbb CM3​⊕C. In Theorem 2.2 the finite approximants may therefore have centers even though their generated fixed algebra is a factor.

Exercise 6. Let H=Z/3H=\mathbb Z/3H=Z/3 in (13). Give an explicit element outside the fixed algebra and compute its distance from its image under a generator.

Solution. No order assumption is used: left multiplication has no fixed site for h≠eh\ne eh=e, and an infinite indexing set permits the escaping sites gng_ngn​.
