Semiregular MASAs and scalar pinching

Written by GPT-6.1 Sol (OpenAI), Ultra. Course exposition: CC0-1.0 to the extent of rights held. Earlier programme and human-source notices retain their own terms.

A semiregular MASA has enough normalizers to match any two of its projections of equal trace. Compatible matches build finite matrix algebras whose diagonals approximate the entire MASA. Their closure is an irreducible AFD subfactor. A coupled finite Fourier basis makes each chosen matrix stage scalar on a conjugate MASA; finite partitions in that MASA give scalar pinching for an ambient finite list.

We use the relative pinching preparation, and the expected-subfactor construction. Finite trace expectations, TL3, constructs the normal, ucp, bimodular expectation onto every unital subalgebra and identifies it with the trace-GNS orthogonal projection. TL4 proves the intersection assertion for decreasing trace spaces by bounded operator clusters. These are finite tracial arguments. Factor and separability hypotheses are stated separately for each result.

Sorin Popa, On a problem of R. V. Kadison on maximal abelian *-subalgebras in factors, INCREST preprint 41/1981, May 1981, second version. Theorem 1 and its compatible matrix construction, printed pp.10–13 (PDF pp.14–17), build a particular regular MASA in an irreducible AFD subfactor. Theorem 2.2 here instead starts with a given semiregular MASA and transports its chosen first-corner partitions by its own partial normalizers.

The scalar-pinching construction uses the standard finite Fourier basis on two equal matrix factors. Its full orthogonality and compression calculations appear above: every matrix in the first factor has constant diagonal in that coupled basis. Conjugating the ambient MASA by this finite unitary and passing to one of its finite partitions gives the stated approximation. Finite trace expectations and bounded decreasing-range limits are supplied by TL3–TL4; MX1 supplies the finite-stage projection limit and scalar center. The last four exercises retain the decreasing-expectation, relative-commutant, refinement and normalized-corner estimates as independent applications of the same trace calculus.
