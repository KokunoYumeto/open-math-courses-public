Completely bounded extension and factorization

Written by GPT-6.1 Sol (OpenAI), Ultra, September 2026. Author self-checked relative to the stated prerequisites; not independently reviewed. New original text: public domain (CC0).

Complete positivity requires a map to preserve every matrix positive cone. Complete boundedness asks a different question: how much can the map enlarge a matrix norm? A completely bounded map can reverse the sign of a positive element. Its extension theorem must therefore control matrix norms without imposing positivity on the extension.

The connection between the two notions comes from a two-by-two block. We place the map in an off-diagonal corner and use scalar diagonal corners to turn a norm estimate into positivity. Arveson's theorem extends the resulting positive map, and Stinespring's theorem recovers the original map as a coefficient of a representation.

Prerequisites are Completely positive finite models, including Arveson's extension theorem, and Completely positive maps, including Stinespring dilation and the multiplicative domain. We also use the elementary decomposition of a representation of a matrix algebra. The free extension and block-method sources are identified in the references below. No separability hypothesis occurs in this lesson.

1. Matrix norms on a subspace

Let VVV be a linear subspace of a C*-algebra AAA. The norm on Mn(V)M_n(V)Mn​(V) is its norm as a subspace of Mn(A)M_n(A)Mn​(A). For θ:V→B(H)\theta:V\to B(H)θ:V→B(H), write

θ(n)([vij])=[θ(vij)],∥θ∥cb=sup⁡n≥1∥θ(n)∥.\theta^{(n)}([v_{ij}])=[\theta(v_{ij})],\qquad \|\theta\|_{\rm cb}=\sup_{n\ge1}\|\theta^{(n)}\|.θ(n)([vij​])=[θ(vij​)],∥θ∥cb​=n≥1sup​∥θ(n)∥.

The map is completely bounded if this supremum is finite, and a complete contraction if it is at most one. A complete isometry preserves the norm at every matrix size.

The map z↦−zz\mapsto-zz↦−z on C\mathbb CC has completely bounded norm one. It is not positive, since its value at 111 is −1-1−1. Thus a completely bounded extension theorem cannot promise a completely positive extension for an arbitrary original map.

We will repeatedly use the following block criterion.

Lemma 1.1. For an operator X:L→KX:L\to KX:L→K,

(1KXX∗1L)≥0⟺∥X∥≤1.\begin{pmatrix}1_K&X\\X^*&1_L\end{pmatrix}\ge0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \|X\|\le1.(1K​X∗​X1L​​)≥0⟺∥X∥≤1.

2. The scalar-diagonal operator system

Assume temporarily that AAA is unital. Define

S(V)={(λ1Avw∗μ1A):λ,μ∈C, v,w∈V}⊂M2(A).\mathcal S(V)= \left\{ \begin{pmatrix}\lambda1_A&v\\w^*&\mu1_A\end{pmatrix}: \lambda,\mu\in\mathbb C,\ v,w\in V \right\}\subset M_2(A).S(V)={(λ1A​w∗​vμ1A​​):λ,μ∈C, v,w∈V}⊂M2​(A).

4. Extension with the same completely bounded norm

Theorem 4.1 (Completely bounded extension and factorization). Let V⊂AV\subset AV⊂A be a linear subspace of a C*-algebra and let θ:V→B(H)\theta:V\to B(H)θ:V→B(H) be completely bounded. There are a representation π:A→B(K)\pi:A\to B(K)π:A→B(K) and operators S,T:H→KS,T:H\to KS,T:H→K such that

5. Surjective two-isometries are rigid

A linear isometry of C*-algebras can preserve the scalar norm while reversing multiplication. Preserving the norm at matrix size two rules out that possibility when the map is surjective.

Lemma 5.1. A linear functional fff on a unital C*-algebra with ∥f∥=f(1)=1\|f\|=f(1)=1∥f∥=f(1)=1 is positive. Consequently a unital contractive map between unital C*-algebras is positive.

Proof. For self-adjoint hhh and real ttt,

∣1+itf(h)∣2≤∥1+ith∥2≤1+t2∥h∥2.|1+itf(h)|^2\le\|1+ith\|^2\le1+t^2\|h\|^2.∣1+itf(h)∣2≤∥1+ith∥2≤1+t2∥h∥2.

The linear term in ttt forces Im⁡f(h)=0\operatorname{Im}f(h)=0Imf(h)=0. For 0≤h≤10\le h\le10≤h≤1, we then have ∣1−f(h)∣=∣f(1−h)∣≤1|1-f(h)|=|f(1-h)|\le1∣1−f(h)∣=∣f(1−h)∣≤1, so f(h)≥0f(h)\ge0f(h)≥0. Rescaling proves positivity on every positive element.

For a unital contraction ρ\rhoρ, compose with any state of the target. The resulting functional has norm at most one and value one at the unit, so is a state by the first assertion. States detect positivity in a C*-algebra, hence ρ\rhoρ is positive. □\square□

They show that T(2)T^{(2)}T(2) is an isometry as well. Both bidual algebras are unital.

Put u=T(1)u=T(1)u=T(1), so ∥u∥=1\|u\|=1∥u∥=1. 6. A matrix norm detects transposition

Transposition on MnM_nMn​ is an isometry at scalar size. Its second amplification can already enlarge norms.

Example 6.1. Let t:M2→M2t:M_2\to M_2t:M2​→M2​ be transposition and set

7. Exercises with solutions

Exercise 1 (A sign can be completely bounded; introductory). Factor θ(z)=−z\theta(z)=-zθ(z)=−z on C\mathbb CC in the form of Theorem 4.1 with product of operator norms one. Explain why no positive extension exists.

Solution. Take π(z)=z\pi(z)=zπ(z)=z, S=−1S=-1S=−1, T=1T=1T=1 on C\mathbb CC. Then S∗π(z)T=−zS^*\pi(z)T=-zS∗π(z)T=−z and ∥S∥∥T∥=1\|S\|\|T\|=1∥S∥∥T∥=1. Any extension on the same algebra must still send 111 to −1-1−1, preventing positivity.

Exercise 2 (The regularization step; intermediate). In Theorem 2.1, why may we add ε1\varepsilon1ε1 to the diagonal blocks, and why must the matrices multiplying XXX have scalar entries?

Solution. Adding a positive diagonal matrix preserves positivity and makes both diagonal blocks invertible. Scalar matrix multiplication takes finite linear combinations of entries of XXX, so stays in Mn(V)M_n(V)Mn​(V) and commutes with θ(n)\theta^{(n)}θ(n). Multiplication by arbitrary elements of AAA need not preserve VVV, and θ\thetaθ need not respect it.

Exercise 3 (Enlarging the diagonals fails; intermediate). Let A=B=C2A=B=\mathbb C^2A=B=C2, let e=(1,0)e=(1,0)e=(1,0), and let θ\thetaθ exchange the two coordinates. Show that θ\thetaθ is a complete isometry, but the rule that keeps the diagonal entries and applies θ\thetaθ only to the off-diagonal entries need not preserve positivity in M2(A)M_2(A)M2​(A).
