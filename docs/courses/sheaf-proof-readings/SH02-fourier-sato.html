Sheaf proof readings

Fourier kernels as radial averaging

The proofs below establish the displayed comparison, cone, equivalence, and section formulas relative to the explicit sheaf-operation prerequisites. The duality supplement supplies conditional proofs of the two duality identities. The separately proved normalization theorem, SH02-NDF-SOURCE-MAPS, constructs the unique opposite comparison giving the prescribed paired inverse identities and computes its exact relation to the literal comparison.

The Fourier–Sato definitions and theorem statements are compared with Kashiwara and Schapira, Microlocal Study of Sheaves, Astérisque 128 (1985), §2.1, pp. 39–40. That section explicitly omits the proofs. The arguments below supply the halfspace comparison and the full vector-bundle inversion through a radial averaging kernel, including the zero section. The source comparison at the end identifies the additional open proof passages and their scope. The examples and problems are independently written; local identifiers belong to this course.

SH02-FS-SETUP — The two integration rules

Let kk be a commutative ring with identity and finite global dimension. Let BB be a locally compact Hausdorff space and τ:E→B\tau:E\to B a real vector bundle of constant finite rank nn; write π:E*→B\pi:E^*\to B for its dual. Everything is local on the base, so a locally constant finite rank can instead be treated on its rank components, with the usual boundedness conditions on the operations. In this unit the rank is fixed. No assumption of a field, noetherian coefficients, constructibility, finite rank of coefficient sheaves, compact base, or proper bundle projection is imposed.

Write OEO_E for the relative orientation local system on BB. Its pullback to EE is the orientation sheaf of the fibers; the relative dualizing complex is τ−1OE[n]\tau^{-1}O_E[n]. Dual bases give a specified positive dual-orientation identification OE≃OE*O_E\simeq O_{E^*}, and the sign representation gives OE⊗kOE≃kBO_E\otimes_k O_E\simeq k_B. These local systems are locally free of rank one. Tensoring with them needs no derived correction. All other tensor products of complexes below are derived unless one factor is explicitly a flat constant extension sheaf.

An object of Dℝ>0+(E;k)D^+_{\mathbb R_{>0}}(E;k) has bounded-below cohomology sheaves locally constant on every positive scalar orbit. We use the equivalent natural scalar-invariance isomorphism. The zero section is a separate fixed orbit in each fiber; a condition on punctured fibers alone does not determine an object’s behavior there.

Set X=E×BE*X=E\times_B E^*, with projections p:X→Ep:X\to E, q:X→E*q:X\to E^*, and closed subsets

C={(x,ξ):⟨x,ξ⟩≥0},N={(x,ξ):⟨x,ξ⟩≤0}. C=\{(x,\xi):\langle x,\xi\rangle\geq0\},\qquad N=\{(x,\xi):\langle x,\xi\rangle\leq0\}.

For a locally closed set AA, kAk_A means the constant sheaf on AA, extended by zero using the open embedding followed by the closed embedding. We set HA=H⊗kAH_A=H\otimes k_A and RΓAH=Rℋom(kA,H)R\Gamma_AH=R\mathcal Hom(k_A,H). These are different operations, including when AA is closed.

Define

TEF=Rq!((p−1F)N),SEG=Rp*RΓN(q!G).(FS1) T_EF=Rq_!((p^{-1}F)_N),\qquad S_EG=Rp_*R\Gamma_N(q^!G). \qquad\text{(FS1)}

Here q!G=q−1G⊗ρ−1OE[n]q^!G=q^{-1}G\otimes \rho^{-1}O_E[n], where ρ:X→B\rho:X\to B. In particular the orientation in this formula is that of the fiber of qq, namely EE. The negative pairing is used in both definitions. TET_E is left adjoint to SES_E: tensor–Hom adjunction, q!⊣q!q_!\dashv q^!, and p−1⊣Rp*p^{-1}\dashv Rp_* give, in order,

RHom⁡(TEF,G)≃RHom⁡((p−1F)N,q!G)≃RHom⁡(F,SEG).(FS2) R\operatorname{Hom}(T_EF,G) \simeq R\operatorname{Hom}((p^{-1}F)_N,q^!G) \simeq R\operatorname{Hom}(F,S_EG). \qquad\text{(FS2)}

The notation is derived kk-module Hom, so this includes all degree shifts and identifies the actual adjunction. The usual finite-dimensional fiber bounds and the bounded flat kernels ensure that these operations preserve the indicated bounded-below categories. Scalar equivariance preserves conicity. The proper-support symbol !! in FS1 is essential: pp and qq usually are not proper.

The imported operation package consists of the open–closed localization triangles, proper-support base change and its stalk formula, projection formula, composition of proper-support images, tensor–Hom adjunction, exceptional inverse image for a real vector-bundle projection, and the relative orientation trace. We also use conic contraction: for a vector-bundle projection rr, zero section ii, and conic HH, the natural maps identify Rr*HRr_*H with i−1Hi^{-1}H and Rr!HRr_!H with i!Hi^!H.

SH02-FS-HALFSPACE — Compactly supported cohomology that detects a boundary

The following elementary facts are used to calculate kernels, rather than to infer a global sheaf from an unconstructed collection of stalk isomorphisms.

Lemma. For a nonempty open convex subset UU of an oriented real nn-space, the orientation trace identifies RΓc(U;k)R\Gamma_c(U;k) with k[−n]k[-n]. The extension map induced by an inclusion of two such open sets is the identity under their traces. A closed halfspace in a positive-dimensional vector space has zero compactly supported cohomology. More generally a nonzero closed convex cone containing no line has zero compactly supported cohomology. Finally, if UU is open convex and ℓ\ell is linear, then

RΓc(U∩{ℓ≤0};k)=0if U∩{ℓ>0}≠⌀.(FS3) R\Gamma_c(U\cap\{\ell\leq0\};k)=0 \quad\text{if }U\cap\{\ell>0\}\ne\varnothing. \qquad\text{(FS3)}

Proof. An open convex set is contractible and is a manifold with the inherited orientation. Poincaré duality for this manifold identifies its compactly supported cohomology with the dual orientation generator in degree nn; no dualization of a coefficient module is involved. Naturality of the orientation trace for an open inclusion identifies the map on this generator. One can equivalently use an orientation-preserving radial homeomorphism with ℝn\mathbb R^n: after selecting an interior point, the Minkowski gauge of the translated open convex set gives a continuous radial reparametrization, including the directions in which the radial endpoint is infinite.

The localization triangle for the closed halfline in ℝ\mathbb R reduces its compactly supported cohomology to the extension map from an open halfline to ℝ\mathbb R. Both are oriented one-manifolds and that map is an isomorphism by the preceding paragraph. A closed halfspace is a product of a closed halfline with a Euclidean space, so the proper-support projection formula gives the asserted vanishing.

For the cone assertion, finite-dimensional separation supplies a linear functional strictly positive on every nonzero point of the cone. Its level-one slice AA is nonempty compact convex. The cone is the quotient of A×[0,∞)A\times[0,\infty) that identifies A×{0}A\times\{0\}. Its one-point compactification is the suspension of AA: the other endpoint is the point at infinity. Since AA is contractible, this suspension is contractible. The identification of compactly supported sheaf cohomology with reduced cohomology of this compactification therefore gives zero. These spaces are locally contractible metrizable spaces, so the comparison with singular cohomology applies. This argument explains why the prohibition on lines matters: a vector subspace has nonzero top compactly supported cohomology.

For FS3 apply compactly supported cohomology to kU∩{ℓ>0}→kU→kU∩{ℓ≤0}→k_{U\cap\{\ell>0\}}\to k_U\to k_{U\cap\{\ell\leq0\}}\to. When the first open set is nonempty it, too, is open convex of dimension nn. Its map to the middle term is an isomorphism by trace compatibility, so the third term vanishes. This proof covers both a genuine cut and the case where the intersection with the closed halfspace is empty. Rank zero is handled directly: the only nonempty fiber is a point. ▫\square

SH02-FS-COMPARE — Why ordinary image and proper-support image agree in the transform

Theorem. There are natural isomorphisms

TEF≃Rq*RΓC(p−1F),SEG≃Rp!((q!G)C).(FS4) T_EF\simeq Rq_*R\Gamma_C(p^{-1}F),\qquad S_EG\simeq Rp_!((q^!G)_C). \qquad\text{(FS4)}

Proof of the first isomorphism. Put L=p−1FL=p^{-1}F and H=RΓCLH=R\Gamma_CL. First we construct the local-support interchange

RΓC(LN)≃HN.(FS5) R\Gamma_C(L_N)\simeq H_N. \qquad\text{(FS5)}

The open complement W=X\NW=X\setminus N lies in the interior of CC, and its closure lies in CC. Thus RΓC(LW)=LWR\Gamma_C(L_W)=L_W. Apply RΓCR\Gamma_C to the localization triangle LW→L→LN→L_W\to L\to L_N\to. The first two terms identify with the first two terms of HW→H→HN→H_W\to H\to H_N\to, because H|W=L|WH|_W=L|_W. The functorial localization construction identifies their third terms, proving FS5. This is a special consequence of the two complementary halfspace inequalities, not an assertion that restriction to an arbitrary closed set commutes with arbitrary local cohomology.

Next HNH_N is supported on {0}×BE*\{0\}\times_BE^*. Indeed, near a point with x≠0x\ne0, choose a bundle coordinate in the ξ\xi-variable equal to t=⟨x,ξ⟩t=\langle x,\xi\rangle. Complete it by coordinates in the kernel of that nonzero linear functional. This is a local change of fiber coordinates over the xx-space. In these coordinates LL is pulled back from the remaining variables and C={t≥0}C=\{t\geq0\}, N={t≤0}N=\{t\leq0\}. The triangle defining RΓt≥0R\Gamma_{t\geq0} shows that its stalk at t=0t=0 is zero: the restriction from an interval to its negative half-interval induces the identity on the pulled-back coefficient complex. This last assertion follows by taking product neighborhoods and the contractible-interval projection formula, and then the filtered colimit over those neighborhoods. Its restriction to t<0t<0 is zero as well. Hence its restriction to NN vanishes. This works with an arbitrary bounded-below coefficient complex on the remaining locally compact base.

All four objects L,LN,H,HNL,L_N,H,H_N are conic in the xx-fiber. Let i:E*→Xi:E^*\to X be its zero section. The morphism RΓC(LN)→LNR\Gamma_C(L_N)\to L_N becomes an isomorphism after Rq!Rq_!: conic contraction identifies this with applying i!i^!, and i!RΓC=i!i^!R\Gamma_C=i^! because the zero section lies in CC. The map H→HNH\to H_N becomes an isomorphism after Rq*Rq_*: conic contraction identifies this with applying i−1i^{-1}, which sees no change on restricting to NN. Finally, the natural map Rq!(HN)→Rq*(HN)Rq_!(H_N)\to Rq_*(H_N) is an isomorphism because HNH_N is supported on the zero section, where qq is the identity and is proper. Therefore the following chain, with its displayed arrows, constructs FS4:

Rq!(LN)←Rq!RΓC(LN)≃Rq!(HN)⟶Rq*(HN)←Rq*H.(FS6) Rq_!(L_N)\ \longleftarrow\ Rq_!R\Gamma_C(L_N) \ \simeq\ Rq_!(H_N)\ \longrightarrow\ Rq_*(H_N) \ \longleftarrow\ Rq_*H. \qquad\text{(FS6)}

Each arrow is now proved invertible. No assertion that qq itself is proper has been used. Interchange the vector bundle and its dual, reverse the two inequalities, and include the base-pulled orientation complex in the coefficient object. The same argument gives the second isomorphism of FS4. ▫\square

SH02-FS-CONE — Two cone formulas, with their boundary conventions

For a subset A⊂EA\subset E, define

A∘={ξ∈E*:π(ξ)∈τ(A),⟨x,ξ⟩≥0 for every x∈Aπ(ξ)}. A^\circ=\{\xi\in E^*: \pi(\xi)\in\tau(A),\ \langle x,\xi\rangle\geq0\text{ for every }x\in A_{\pi(\xi)}\}.

The requirement π(ξ)∈τ(A)\pi(\xi)\in\tau(A) is part of this definition. An empty fiber does not acquire the whole dual fiber through a vacuous inequality. Let aa denote fiberwise negation. Convexity and absence of lines below are fiberwise conditions; openness and closedness are in the total bundle topology.

Theorem. If γ⊂E\gamma\subset E is a closed convex cone containing the entire zero section and each fiber contains no line, then

TEkγ≃kInt⁡(γ∘).(FS7) T_Ek_\gamma\simeq k_{\operatorname{Int}(\gamma^\circ)}. \qquad\text{(FS7)}

Here Int\operatorname{Int} is total-space interior. If U⊂EU\subset E is an open fiberwise convex cone, possibly with empty fibers, then

TEkU≃k(U∘)a⊗π−1OE[−n].(FS8) T_Ek_U\simeq k_{(U^\circ)^a}\otimes\pi^{-1}O_E[-n]. \qquad\text{(FS8)}

Proof. By proper-support base change, the stalk in FS7 is the compactly supported cohomology of γb∩{x:⟨x,ξ⟩≤0}\gamma_b\cap\{x:\langle x,\xi\rangle\leq0\}. This is a closed cone containing no line. It is {0}\{0\} exactly when the pairing is strictly positive on every nonzero point of γb\gamma_b; otherwise it is a nonzero cone and the lemma gives zero.

The strict-positivity locus just described is precisely Int⁡(γ∘)\operatorname{Int}(\gamma^\circ). To verify the statement in the total topology, trivialize near bb and intersect γ\gamma with the unit-sphere bundle. Its projection is closed locally, because the sphere is compact. Strict positivity on the compact fiber slice consequently persists on a neighborhood of (b,ξ)(b,\xi). If the slice is empty, the same compactness argument shows that it remains empty on a base neighborhood. Conversely a nonzero x∈γbx\in\gamma_b with ⟨x,ξ⟩≤0\langle x,\xi\rangle\leq0 prevents interior: a small perturbation of ξ\xi makes the pairing negative if necessary. This also covers γb={0}\gamma_b=\{0\}; it must not be replaced by an argument assuming a full-dimensional cone.

To produce the sheaf isomorphism, use the restriction morphism kγ→k0Bk_\gamma\to k_{0_B}. Its transform maps TEkγT_Ek_\gamma to kE*k_{E^*}. On the open strict-positivity locus, its fiber is the identity of the point {0}\{0\}, so restriction gives a sheaf isomorphism there. Off that open set the transform has zero stalks. The open–closed localization triangle then identifies it with extension by zero of that constant restriction. This constructs FS7 rather than merely listing its stalks.

For FS8 put B0=τ(U)B_0=\tau(U), an open subset of BB, and first work over B0B_0. There every UbU_b is nonempty open convex of dimension nn. Set D=(U∘)aD=(U^\circ)^a. It is closed in E*|B0E^*|_{B_0}: its complement is the projection of the open set where x∈Ux\in U and ⟨x,ξ⟩>0\langle x,\xi\rangle>0, and that projection is open. For ξ∉D\xi\notin D, FS3 makes the transform stalk zero. On DD, the negative halfspace contains the whole of UbU_b. Restriction and proper-support base change therefore identify the transform with the proper-support image of the open subset U×B0DU\times_{B_0}D of the rank-nn bundle over DD. Its relative orientation trace is an isomorphism, since every fiber is nonempty open convex. This gives π−1OE[−n]\pi^{-1}O_E[-n] on DD. Closed extension in E*|B0E^*|_{B_0} constructs the claimed object there. Finally, base-change compatibility with extension by zero from the open base B0B_0 proves FS8 on E*E^*. Thus DD is allowed to be only locally closed in the full total space. ▫\square

SH02-FS-KERNEL — A kernel identity that remembers gluing at the zero section

The next calculation is the main inversion argument. Matching only the stalks of its final two objects would leave their extension across the zero section undetermined.

Let Y=E×BEY=E\times_BE, with points denoted (x,y)(x,y). Over YY, integrate ξ∈Eb*\xi\in E^*_b. Write rr for that projection and put u=⟨x,ξ⟩u=\langle x,\xi\rangle, v=⟨y,ξ⟩v=\langle y,\xi\rangle. Define

K=Rr!k{u≤0,v≥0}⊗OE*[n],h(y,s)=(sy,y),s≥0,h+=h|s>0. K=Rr_!k_{\{u\leq0,\ v\geq0\}}\otimes O_{E^*}[n], \quad h(y,s)=(sy,y),\quad s\geq0, \quad h_+=h|_{s>0}.

Orientation sheaves in this paragraph are pulled back from BB.

Kernel lemma. There is a natural isomorphism

K≃R(h+)!kE×(0,∞)[1].(FS9) K\simeq R(h_+)_!k_{E\times(0,\infty)}[1]. \qquad\text{(FS9)}

Proof. Cut the inequality u≤0u\leq0 by v<0v<0 and its complement. This gives a specified triangle

J⟶D⟶K⟶J[1],J=Rr!k{u≤0,v<0}⊗OE*[n],D=Rr!k{u≤0}⊗OE*[n].(FS10) J\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow J[1], \quad J=Rr_!k_{\{u\leq0,v<0\}}\otimes O_{E^*}[n], \quad D=Rr_!k_{\{u\leq0\}}\otimes O_{E^*}[n]. \qquad\text{(FS10)}

Put Z={x=0}Z=\{x=0\}, V={y≠0}V=\{y\ne0\}, and A={(x,y)∈V:x=sy for some s≥0}A=\{(x,y)\in V:x=sy\text{ for some }s\geq0\}. The latter is closed in VV. This can be checked in a local trivialization by choosing a linear functional nonzero on yy; on a smaller neighborhood the scalar ss, when it exists, is the continuous quotient of that functional on xx and yy. Write i:A↪Vi:A\hookrightarrow V and j:V↪Yj:V\hookrightarrow Y.

The restriction from the full vector bundle to u≤0u\leq0, together with orientation trace for the full bundle, constructs a morphism kY→Dk_Y\to D. At x=0x=0 it is the identity trace. At x≠0x\ne0 the fiber is a closed halfspace and its compactly supported cohomology is zero. Restricting the constructed morphism to ZZ, and then using closed extension, gives D≃kZD\simeq k_Z.

On VV, integration of the open halfspace v<0v<0, with the same orientation twist, is canonically kVk_V. Restriction to u≤0u\leq0 constructs a map from this sheaf to J|VJ|_V. If x=syx=sy with s≥0s\geq0, that restriction does nothing to the fiber. If xx is a negative multiple of yy, the fiber is empty. If x,yx,y are independent, the fiber is a product of a closed halfline, an open halfline, and ℝn−2\mathbb R^{n-2}, whose compactly supported cohomology is zero. At y=0y=0 the strict inequality v<0v<0 is impossible. These cases, and restriction of the constructed map to AA, give J≃j!i*kAJ\simeq j_!i_*k_A. In particular JJ and DD are actual sheaves in degree zero.

Under these identifications the arrow J→DJ\to D in FS10 is restriction to A∩ZA\cap Z, followed by extension by zero from Z∩VZ\cap V into ZZ. At a point (0,y)(0,y) with y≠0y\ne0, the map is the compact-support extension from an open halfspace to the full fiber, and the orientation traces make it the identity. At every other stalk either the source or the target is zero. Equality here is equality of already constructed maps between degree-zero sheaves, so checking their stalk maps proves equality of the maps themselves.

Now apply Rh!Rh_! to the open–closed triangle in the scalar parameter. Rotating once gives

Rh!kE×[0,∞)⟶kZ⟶R(h+)!kE×(0,∞)[1]⟶.(FS11) Rh_!k_{E\times[0,\infty)}\longrightarrow k_Z \longrightarrow R(h_+)_!k_{E\times(0,\infty)}[1]\longrightarrow. \qquad\text{(FS11)}

Over VV, hh is a homeomorphism onto the closed set AA. Over the zero pair its fiber is the closed halfline, with vanishing compactly supported cohomology. Hence restriction over VV, followed by extension by zero, identifies the first term with j!i*kAj_!i_*k_A. Its arrow to kZk_Z is exactly the restriction morphism just identified in FS10: it is the identity at (0,y)(0,y), y≠0y\ne0, and all other relevant stalk maps are zero.

Thus FS10 and FS11 are cofibers of the same identified sheaf morphism. Use the functorial cofiber in the usual derived enhancement, or the explicit cone of this degree-zero map; this gives FS9 naturally. An arbitrary nonfunctorial choice of a triangulated-category cone is unnecessary. All constructions use restriction, extension, and orientation trace, so they glue over the base. Rank zero is included: J=0J=0, D=kBD=k_B, and the positive halfline shifted by one has compactly supported cohomology kk. ▫\square

SH02-FS-INVERSION — Inversion and the same-sign square

Theorem. The functors TET_E and SES_E are inverse equivalences between Dℝ>0+(E;k)D^+_{\mathbb R_{>0}}(E;k) and Dℝ>0+(E*;k)D^+_{\mathbb R_{>0}}(E^*;k). In particular the unit and counit of the adjunction FS2 are isomorphisms. If TE*T_{E^*} uses the same nonpositive pairing convention as TET_E, then

TE*TEF≃a−1F⊗τ−1OE[−n].(FS12) T_{E^*}T_EF\simeq a^{-1}F\otimes\tau^{-1}O_E[-n]. \qquad\text{(FS12)}

Proof. Use the second description of SES_E in FS4. Composition of proper-support kernel functors, base change, and projection formula identify SETES_ET_E with the kernel KK of FS9. More explicitly, in the triple product the two restrictions are precisely ⟨x,ξ⟩≤0\langle x,\xi\rangle\leq0 and ⟨y,ξ⟩≥0\langle y,\xi\rangle\geq0. The exceptional inverse image in SES_E contributes OE[n]O_E[n]; to integrate the intermediate fiber E*E^*, identify this with OE*[n]O_{E^*}[n] by the specified positive dual-orientation identification. The intersection kernel is the tensor product of the two flat constant-extension kernels. No properness is imposed on their common support; it is proper-support integration that composes.

After FS9, projection formula for h+h_+ turns this kernel operation into

Rpr⁡!μ−1F[1],pr⁡(y,s)=y,μ(y,s)=sy,s>0. R\operatorname{pr}_!\mu^{-1}F[1],\qquad \operatorname{pr}(y,s)=y,\quad \mu(y,s)=sy,\quad s>0.

Conicity identifies μ−1F\mu^{-1}F with pr⁡−1F\operatorname{pr}^{-1}F, naturally also over the fixed zero section. Proper-support projection formula and the oriented positive halfline give Rpr⁡!pr⁡−1F[1]≃FR\operatorname{pr}_!\operatorname{pr}^{-1}F[1]\simeq F. This constructs a natural isomorphism SETE≃idS_ET_E\simeq\mathrm{id}.

For TESET_ES_E, the intermediate fiber is EE, its orientation twist is already OE[n]O_E[n], and its two inequalities are ⟨x,ξ⟩≥0\langle x,\xi\rangle\geq0, ⟨x,η⟩≤0\langle x,\eta\rangle\leq0. Apply the same kernel proof with the perfect pairing replaced by its negative. It gives the radial relation ξ=sη\xi=s\eta, s>0s>0, and the same averaging argument gives TESE≃idT_ES_E\simeq\mathrm{id}. These two natural isomorphisms establish equivalence. A right adjoint to an equivalence has invertible adjunction unit and counit, so the actual unit and counit of FS2 are isomorphisms as well. This last categorical step asserts invertibility; it does not identify a separately constructed radial map with a particular normalized adjunction map.

Finally the positive-kernel expression for SES_E directly gives SEG≃a−1(TE*G)⊗τ−1OE[n]. S_EG\simeq a^{-1}(T_{E^*}G)\otimes\tau^{-1}O_E[n]. Insert G=TEFG=T_EF, apply the just-proved inversion, and cancel the orientation line using OE⊗2≃kBO_E^{\otimes2}\simeq k_B. Negation is an involution and fixes every base-pulled local system. This yields FS12, including both its antipode and its shift. ▫\square

SH02-FS-NORMALIZATION — What is and is not normalized

FS2 names a specific adjunction and FS6 names a specific comparison. The equivalence proof establishes invertibility without claiming that all isomorphisms arising from these constructions are literally equal.

There are two concrete signs to retain. In the scalar triangle used in FS11, with the increasing coordinate ss as orientation, the connecting map from the endpoint class to Hc1((0,∞);k)H_c^1((0,\infty);k) is minus the integration generator. For example, a cutoff equal to one near zero and zero near infinity has differential of integral −1-1. This calculation may be read over ℝ\mathbb R to fix the integral sign convention; the sign itself is the same cellular boundary sign over every kk. Changing to t=1/st=1/s reverses orientation. Independently, the orientation identification induced by a negative-definite identification E→E*E\to E^* differs from positive dual orientation by (−1)n(-1)^n. Indeed, in a positive orthonormal basis its matrix is −In-I_n, of determinant sign (−1)n(-1)^n; the calculation is local and does not require a globally chosen metric.

SH02-FS-SECTIONS — Testing a transform on regions

Derived sections on an open set VV mean RHom⁡(kV,−)R\operatorname{Hom}(k_V,-). For a locally closed set AA, define RΓA(Y;F)=RHom⁡(kA,F)R\Gamma_A(Y;F)=R\operatorname{Hom}(k_A,F); this convention makes sense even when AA is closed only over an open part of the base.

Corollary. For an open fiberwise convex cone V⊂E*V\subset E^*,

RΓ(V;TEF)≃RΓV∘(E;F).(FS13) R\Gamma(V;T_EF)\simeq R\Gamma_{V^\circ}(E;F). \qquad\text{(FS13)}

For a closed fiberwise convex cone δ⊂E*\delta\subset E^*, containing the zero section and containing no line in any fiber,

RΓδ(E*;TEF)≃RΓ(Int⁡((δ∘)a);F⊗τ−1OE)[−n].(FS14) R\Gamma_\delta(E^*;T_EF) \simeq R\Gamma(\operatorname{Int}((\delta^\circ)^a); F\otimes\tau^{-1}O_E)[-n]. \qquad\text{(FS14)}

Proof. Apply the fully faithful inverse equivalence to both arguments of derived Hom. FS8 and the positive-kernel expression for SES_E give SEkV≃kV∘S_Ek_V\simeq k_{V^\circ}: the two shifts cancel and the two dual orientation lines contract. Consequently RHom⁡(kV,TEF)≃RHom⁡(kV∘,F)R\operatorname{Hom}(k_V,T_EF)\simeq R\operatorname{Hom}(k_{V^\circ},F), proving FS13. Empty base fibers are governed by the definition of polar and locally closed extension, exactly as in FS8.

Likewise FS7 gives SEkδ≃kInt⁡((δ∘)a)⊗τ−1OE[n]S_Ek_\delta\simeq k_{\operatorname{Int}((\delta^\circ)^a)}\otimes\tau^{-1}O_E[n]. Move the locally free orientation line and the shift across derived Hom, and use the characterization of derived sections on an open set. This proves FS14. The orientation factor stays inside derived global sections: on a nontrivial bundle it is not a fixed coefficient module that can be moved outside. ▫\square

  1. A nonsymmetric wedge. Work over a point with k=ℤk=\mathbb Z and E=ℝ2E=\mathbb R^2. Let γ={(u,v):u≥v≥0}\gamma=\{(u,v):u\geq v\geq0\}, generated by (1,0)(1,0) and (1,1)(1,1).   2. A coefficient sheaf with torsion on a nonorientable bundle.   3. A cone whose base behavior matters. In E=ℝb×ℝxE=\mathbb R_b\times\mathbb R_x, let γ\gamma be the union of the zero section and {b=0,x≥0}\{b=0,x\geq0\}. SH02-FS-PROBLEMS — Problems with solutions

Problem 1. Let M∈D+(B;k)M\in D^+(B;k) be arbitrary. Compute the transforms of i*Mi_*M and τ−1M\tau^{-1}M, where i:B↪Ei:B\hookrightarrow E is the zero section. Explain why MM need not have finite-rank cohomology.

Solution. On the zero section the negative-pairing restriction is automatic and qq restricts to the identity of E*E^*, so TEi*M=π−1MT_Ei_*M=\pi^{-1}M. FS8 for U=EU=E, followed by projection formula, gives TEτ−1M=i*′(M⊗OE)[−n]T_E\tau^{-1}M=i'_* (M\otimes O_E)[-n]. Only tensoring with flat constant-extension kernels and a locally free orientation line occurs. The proper-support projection formula used here has no finite-rank requirement on MM; it is part of the stated bounded derived operation package.

Problem 2. Over a point and with E=ℝE=\mathbb R oriented, compute TEk[0,∞)T_Ek_{[0,\infty)}, then apply the same-sign transform again. Check FS12 including its antipode.

Solution. FS7 gives k(0,∞)k_{(0,\infty)}. FS8 gives k(−∞,0][−1]k_{(-\infty,0]}[-1] for its next transform. This is a−1k[0,∞)[−1]a^{-1}k_{[0,\infty)}[-1]. The change from a closed ray to an open ray on the first step and back to a closed ray on the second is essential; discarding the endpoint on the last step would contradict inversion.

Problem 3. In the proof of FS9, why does the fiber calculation alone not establish the kernel isomorphism? Give the missing data and explain where they were supplied.

Solution. Stalk modules with the same dimensions and degrees can have different specialization maps and extension classes across a closed stratum. Here the positive radial locus meets the zero pair. The proof constructs JJ and DD as degree-zero sheaves using restriction maps and traces, identifies the actual sheaf morphism J→DJ\to D with the radial boundary restriction, and takes its functorial cofiber. Equality of the two already constructed sheaf maps is checked stalkwise. This supplies the gluing data that a bare stalk inventory would lack.

Problem 4. Explain why a closed linear subspace cannot replace the cone hypothesis in FS7, and compute the transform of a linear subspace L⊂EL\subset E over a point.

Solution. Let d=dim⁡Ld=\dim L. If ξ∉L⟂\xi\notin L^\perp, the negative-pairing slice in LL is a closed halfspace and has zero compactly supported cohomology. If ξ∈L⟂\xi\in L^\perp, it is all of LL, with compactly supported cohomology or⁡(L)[−d]\operatorname{or}(L)[-d]. Restriction to the closed annihilator, followed by its relative orientation trace, constructs TEkL=kL⟂⊗or⁡(L)[−d]T_Ek_L=k_{L^\perp}\otimes\operatorname{or}(L)[-d]. For d>0d>0, the interior of L⟂L^\perp in E*E^* is empty, so FS7 would falsely predict zero. A cone containing a line fails exactly the vanishing lemma used there. This calculation is an additional example, not an unauthorized weakening of that lemma’s assumptions.
