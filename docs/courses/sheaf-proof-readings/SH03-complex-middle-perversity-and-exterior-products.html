Complex middle perversity and exterior products

On a complex stratum, the middle perversity puts a locally constant coefficient in ordinary degree minus its complex dimension. Its point costalk lies in the opposite positive degree: the point orientation shift uses twice that dimension. These measurements determine the cuts. We construct their truncations inside the complex constructible category, then prove functor, tensor and Hom bounds with their coefficient hypotheses.

Complex microlocal stratifications and constructibility supplies compatible complex μ-strata and their closed total conormal. Holomorphic operations and complex Fourier symmetries supplies globally bounded complex membership for the operations used below.

Let kk be commutative of finite global dimension. Manifolds and maps are complex analytic, Hausdorff and countable at infinity, with uniform finite dimension bounds. Every DbD^b object has one global cohomology interval. Weak complex constructibility permits arbitrary modules; strong constructibility requires perfect stalks. The strong perverse t-structure retains Noetherianity. Field hypotheses below are separate.

Write dim⁡ℂ\dim_{\mathbb C} for complex dimension; on analytic sets real dimension is twice it. Supports mean nonzero-stalk loci, with dimension unchanged by closure. Empty loci have dimension −∞-\infty. Locally closed analytic pieces and complex μ-strata have the precise closure, frontier and local finiteness conditions of the stratification lesson.

The even values determine the complex cuts

Choose a real perversity pp with

p(2a)=−a(a∈ℤ).(1) p(2a)=-a\qquad(a\in\mathbb Z). \qquad\text{(1)}

Both −⌊r/2⌋-\lfloor r/2\rfloor and −⌈r/2⌉-\lceil r/2\rceil satisfy this. Define the middle cuts by intersecting the real pp-cuts with Dwℂcb(X;k)D^b_{\mathrm w\mathbb C\mathrm c}(X;k), and define their degree translates by the usual shifts. We write midD≤r{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\le r} and midD≥r{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\ge r}.

For an adapted complex μ-stratification X=⨆αSαX=\bigsqcup_\alpha S_\alpha, put aα=dim⁡ℂSαa_\alpha=\dim_{\mathbb C}S_\alpha. The full real stratum criterion gives

F∈midD≤0⟺iα−1F∈D≤−aα,F∈midD≥0⟺iα!F∈D≥−aα,for every α.(2) \begin{aligned} F\in{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\le0} &\Longleftrightarrow i_\alpha^{-1}F\in D^{\le-a_\alpha},\\ F\in{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\ge0} &\Longleftrightarrow i_\alpha^!F\in D^{\ge-a_\alpha}, \end{aligned} \quad\text{for every }\alpha . \qquad\text{(2)}

The chosen exceptional restrictions have locally constant cohomology by the earlier full limiting-conormal proof. All strata have real dimension 2aα2a_\alpha, so (2) uses only (1). It proves independence from every odd value of pp. No complex dimension is being assigned to arbitrary real subsets.

Support, cosupport and analytic-subset tests

The intrinsic criteria are

F∈midD≤0⟺dim⁡ℂsupp⁡HjF≤−j,F∈midD≥0⟺dim⁡ℂcosupp⁡jF≤j,for every j,(4) \begin{aligned} F\in{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\le0} &\Longleftrightarrow \dim_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{supp}H^jF\le-j,\\ F\in{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\ge0} &\Longleftrightarrow \dim_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{cosupp}^jF\le j, \end{aligned} \quad\text{for every }j , \qquad\text{(4)}

where cosupp⁡jF={x:Hj(ix!F)≠0}\operatorname{cosupp}^jF=\{x:H^j(i_x^!F)\ne0\}.

For the first line, a nonzero ordinary restriction in degree jj on an aa-stratum contributes dimension aa. The support inequalities therefore say j≤−aj\le-a whenever that restriction is nonzero, exactly the upper condition (2). Locally finite unions preserve the supremum of these dimensions, including infinitely many components.

For the second, (3) identifies point costalk degree jj with exceptional stratum degree j−2aj-2a. The lower stratum condition says j−2a≥−aj-2a\ge-a, equivalently a≤ja\le j. This proves both directions of the cosupport criterion. Negative point-costalk degrees consequently vanish in the lower cut; this is not an ordinary-stalk assertion.

Another equivalent formulation is

F∈midD≥0⟺iT!F∈D≥−dim⁡ℂT(T)for every locally closed complex analytic T⊂X.(5) F\in{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\ge0} \Longleftrightarrow i_T^!F\in D^{\ge-\dim_{\mathbb C}T}(T) \quad\text{for every locally closed complex analytic }T\subset X. \qquad\text{(5)}

Allow singular and nonpure TT, using maximal dimension. For the forward implication intersect adapted complex strata with TT, refine analytically, and use the actual finite closed support filtration. On an aa-stratum the exceptional coefficient starts in degree −a-a. For an intersection piece of complex dimension t≤at\le a, the full arbitrary-coefficient singular-subset estimate gives −a+2a−2t=a−2t≥−t≥−dim⁡ℂT. -a+2a-2t=a-2t\ge-t\ge-\dim_{\mathbb C}T. That estimate uses the actual flat cellular dualizing model. The finite closed-layer reconstruction and ordinary left t-exactness of closed direct image give the bound on all of TT. Conversely, take TT to be each analytic stratum in (5); the result is (2). This does not presuppose a perverse t-structure on singular TT.

Truncation triangles remain complex constructible

Massey’s Notes on Perverse Sheaves and Vanishing Cycles, §2 and §5, provides the complex support and cosupport normalization for comparison. Beilinson–Bernstein–Deligne’s Faisceaux pervers, Theorem 1.4.10, provides the formal open/closed gluing mechanism. To apply it here, the closed-layer correction must remain complex constructible; the real t-structure by itself does not establish that fact. The construction below checks this membership before invoking the gluing triangle, retaining the actual exceptional restriction and its adjunction arrow.

Theorem. The middle cuts form a bounded t-structure on Dwℂcb(X;k)D^b_{\mathrm w\mathbb C\mathrm c}(X;k). Over a Noetherian kk, they form a bounded t-structure on Dℂcb(X;k)D^b_{\mathbb C\mathrm c}(X;k).

Proof. Shift stability and orthogonality follow from the real theorem because the complex category is full. We must construct triangles in that smaller category.

Choose adapted complex μ-strata and their closed dimension filtration ⌀=Z−1⊂Z0⊂⋯⊂Zn=X\varnothing=Z_{-1}\subset Z_0\subset\cdots\subset Z_n=X. Their analytic closures are locally finite, so those unions are closed analytic. The dimension-filtration theorem retains the full approaching-layer μ-condition. The corresponding real filtration has empty odd layers.

Four bounds for holomorphic maps

Suppose f:Y→Xf:Y\to X is holomorphic with dim⁡ℂf−1(x)≤d\dim_{\mathbb C}f^{-1}(x)\le d, d≥0d\ge0. Then

f−1:midD≤0(X)⟶midD≤d(Y),f!:midD≥0(X)⟶midD≥−d(Y).(9) \begin{aligned} f^{-1}:{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\le0}(X) &\longrightarrow{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\le d}(Y),\\ f^!:{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\ge0}(X) &\longrightarrow{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\ge-d}(Y). \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(9)}

For globally bounded weakly complex constructible GG on YY,

G∈midD≤0,Rf!G∈Dwℂcb(X)⟹Rf!G∈midD≤d(X),G∈midD≥0,Rf*G∈Dwℂcb(X)⟹Rf*G∈midD≥−d(X).(10) \begin{aligned} G\in{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\le0},\ Rf_!G\in D^b_{\mathrm w\mathbb C\mathrm c}(X) &\Longrightarrow Rf_!G\in{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\le d}(X),\\ G\in{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\ge0},\ Rf_*G\in D^b_{\mathrm w\mathbb C\mathrm c}(X) &\Longrightarrow Rf_*G\in{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\ge-d}(X). \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(10)}

The real fibre bound is 2d2d, and p[−2d](2a)=d−ap[-2d](2a)=d-a, p[2d](2a)=−d−ap[2d](2a)=-d-a. On even dimensions these are constant perversity changes +d,−d+d,-d. The real inverse upper target becomes upper dd. The exceptional lower target has the additional degree −2d-2d, hence becomes lower d−2d=−dd-2d=-d. The compact direct upper target has degree 2d2d and constant −d-d, hence upper dd. The ordinary direct lower target has constant −d-d, hence lower −d-d. These are (9)–(10).

The holomorphic operation theorem supplies complex membership for the inverse operations. Direct membership in (10) remains a separate hypothesis; properness on the actual closed support supplies it. A zero-dimensional analytic map with accumulating images still disproves automatic nonproper constructibility, as in the preceding lesson.

Exterior tensor and its evaluated dual pairing

The product calculation has two different inputs. Massey’s notes, §1, records the proper-support Künneth comparison and the adjoint product-Hom identity in its finite constructible setting. The upper tensor bound below comes instead from the direction of derived tensor degrees and permits weak coefficients. To obtain the lower bound we use the explicitly constructed evaluation pairing, finite local cochains and field duality. The order of the factors and the complex orientation identify the actual morphism; an abstract object isomorphism would not check that pairing. The integral Tor example shows why this second argument cannot simply inherit the first argument’s coefficient range.

Put F⊠LG=q1−1F⊗kLq2−1GF\boxtimes^LG=q_1^{-1}F\otimes_k^Lq_2^{-1}G. The weak operation theorem gives its globally bounded complex membership. For the full coefficient ring,

F∈midD≤0(X),G∈midD≤0(Y)⟹F⊠LG∈midD≤0(X×Y).(12) F\in{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\le0}(X),\ G\in{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\le0}(Y) \Longrightarrow F\boxtimes^LG\in{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\le0}(X\times Y). \qquad\text{(12)}

On a product of strata of complex dimensions a,ba,b, ordinary restriction is the tensor of the restrictions, upper bounded by −a,−b-a,-b. Locally take bounded-above flat coefficient resolutions with those upper term bounds. Their total tensor has no terms above −a−b-a-b, the required upper stratum bound. Negative Tor can lower the degree but cannot raise it.

For strong inputs there is an actual comparison

DXF⊠LDYG→∼DX×Y(F⊠LG).(13) D_XF\boxtimes^LD_YG \xrightarrow{\sim}D_{X\times Y}(F\boxtimes^LG). \qquad\text{(13)}

Define it by the tensor of the evaluation pairings, Koszul interchange and the ordered orientation identification ωX⊠LωY=ωX×Y\omega_X\boxtimes^L\omega_Y=\omega_{X\times Y}, then curry. Here is a local verification of that arrow.

The actual small-ball compact-section comparisons represent ix!Fi_x^!F and iy!Gi_y^!G. Compact-support cross product gives ix!F⊗kLiy!G⟶i(x,y)!(F⊠LG). i_x^!F\otimes_k^Li_y^!G\longrightarrow i_{(x,y)}^!(F\boxtimes^LG). It is an isomorphism: choose finite compatible real triangulations on compact ball closures, including the boundaries and coefficient strata. Delete the boundaries to obtain the open balls. The finite closed simplex filtrations build coefficients by localization triangles from open simplex coefficients, which are locally derived constant on each contractible simplex. For real open simplices of dimensions r,sr,s, compact sections are P⊗orr[−r]P\otimes\mathrm{or}_r[-r] and Q⊗ors[−s]Q\otimes\mathrm{or}_s[-s]. On their product they are (P⊗LQ)⊗(orr⊗ors)[−r−s](P\otimes^LQ)\otimes(\mathrm{or}_r\otimes\mathrm{or}_s)[-r-s]. The cross product is this identification with the ordered product orientation and cohomological tensor sign. It is an isomorphism on the coefficient cells. The small-ball transition maps identify this arrow with the displayed point costalk map. Finite triangulation and local derived-constant prerequisites remain explicit; no unrestricted inverse-limit claim is used.

The costalks are perfect. For perfect coefficients the evaluation map P∨⊗LQ∨→(P⊗LQ)∨P^\vee\otimes^LQ^\vee\to(P\otimes^LQ)^\vee is an isomorphism: check finite projectives, then bounded total complexes with their Koszul signs. Thus (13) is an isomorphism on every stalk, with its actual evaluation normalization.

Over a field, strong lower-cut objects have upper-cut duals by (11). Apply (12), (13), and evaluated biduality:

F∈midDℂc≥0(X),G∈midDℂc≥0(Y)⟹F⊠LG∈midDℂc≥0(X×Y).(14) F\in{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\ge0}_{\mathbb C\mathrm c}(X),\ G\in{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\ge0}_{\mathbb C\mathrm c}(Y) \Longrightarrow F\boxtimes^LG \in{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\ge0}_{\mathbb C\mathrm c}(X\times Y). \qquad\text{(14)}

Tensor of perfect stalks remains perfect. Thus the exterior product of strong perverse sheaves over a field is perverse. The field condition enters the cut exchange, separately from the product pairing.

Exceptional exterior Hom has the lower bound

For weak complex constructible inputs,

F∈midD≤0(X),G∈midD≥0(Y)⟹Rℋom(q1−1F,q2!G)∈midD≥0(X×Y).(15) F\in{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\le0}(X),\ G\in{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\ge0}(Y) \Longrightarrow R\mathcal Hom(q_1^{-1}F,q_2^!G) \in{}^{\mathrm{mid}}D^{\ge0}(X\times Y). \qquad\text{(15)}

Use the bounded-first-argument exceptional Hom isomorphism in the current prerequisite draft Exceptional operations, SH02-EX-HOM, and the actual submersion comparison in Manifold duality, SH02-MD-SUBMERSION. Their exact coefficient, amplitude and evaluation contracts remain prerequisites.

On i:Sα×Tβ↪X×Yi:S_\alpha\times T_\beta\hookrightarrow X\times Y, write r1,r2r_1,r_2 for its projections, with complex dimensions a,ba,b. Exceptional Hom and exceptional composition give

i!Rℋom(q1−1F,q2!G)≃Rℋom(r1−1iα−1F,r2!iβ!G),r2!iβ!G≃ωSα⊠Liβ!G=kSα[2a]⊠Liβ!G.(16) \begin{aligned} i^!R\mathcal Hom(q_1^{-1}F,q_2^!G) &\simeq R\mathcal Hom (r_1^{-1}i_\alpha^{-1}F,r_2^!i_\beta^!G),\\ r_2^!i_\beta^!G &\simeq\omega_{S_\alpha}\boxtimes^Li_\beta^!G =k_{S_\alpha}[2a]\boxtimes^Li_\beta^!G . \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(16)}

The second line is the trace-normalized comparison for the real oriented 2a2a-dimensional projection, valid for arbitrary bounded-below coefficients. This proves (15) without field or perfectness assumptions, or a tensor-with-infinite-dual substitution.

Exercises with complete solutions

Odd values disappear only on complex strata

Difficulty: Introductory.

Compare p−(r)=−⌊r/2⌋p_-(r)=-\lfloor r/2\rfloor, p+(r)=−⌈r/2⌉p_+(r)=-\lceil r/2\rceil. Prove equality of their complex cuts and inequality of their real line hearts.

Solution. Their consecutive steps are zero or minus one, so both are real perversities. Both have value −a-a at 2a2a, hence (2) identifies all complex cuts. On the real line their values at its stratum dimension one are zero and minus one. The real smooth-stratum criterion puts a nonzero locally constant coefficient in their hearts only in ordinary degrees zero and minus one respectively. Thus kℝk_{\mathbb R} belongs to the first heart and not the second; kℝ[1]k_{\mathbb R}[1] belongs to the second. Even-dimensional stratification was essential.

A closed curve and its two restrictions

Difficulty: Intermediate.

For a closed coordinate line i:ℂ↪ℂ2i:\mathbb C\hookrightarrow\mathbb C^2, show i*k[1]i_*k[1] is perverse and compare i−1kℂ2[2]i^{-1}k_{\mathbb C^2}[2], i!kℂ2[2]i^!k_{\mathbb C^2}[2] with the curve heart.

Solution. The supported object’s ordinary degree is minus one with dimension-one support; its curve-point costalk is k[−2][1]=k[−1]k[-2][1]=k[-1], in degree one, and zero off the curve. Both tests (4) hold. Ordinary restriction of the ambient heart object is kℂ[2]k_{\mathbb C}[2], degree minus two: it meets the upper curve bound minus one but fails the lower. Exceptional restriction includes the real normal shift minus two, giving kℂk_{\mathbb C}, which meets the lower bound but fails the upper. Shifting the former by minus one or the latter by plus one gives kℂ[1]k_{\mathbb C}[1]. Complex codimension and real normal degree are different.

A finite branched map retains monodromy

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Over a field let f:ℂ→ℂf:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C, z↦z2z\mapsto z^2. Prove Rf*k[1]Rf_*k[1] is perverse and compute its stalks, point costalks and punctured monodromy.

Solution. The map is proper with zero-dimensional fibres, so the output is strong bounded complex constructible, Rf*=Rf!Rf_*=Rf_!, and both bounds (10) put it in the heart. Its stalks are k2[1]k^2[1] at nonzero points and k[1]k[1] at zero by the proper fibre calculation. A punctured loop exchanges the two sheets, giving the transposition matrix.

At a nonzero point the costalk is k2[−1]k^2[-1]. At zero, preimages of a sufficiently small disk and its puncture are a disk and its puncture. The unshifted fibre of their section restriction is k[−2]k[-2], so the shifted point costalk is k[−1]k[-1]. These have degree one and satisfy (4). In characteristic two the transposition minus the identity is nonzero nilpotent, so the monodromy is not diagonalizable. Perverse membership does not imply semisimplicity.

A node gives a nonsplit perverse sequence

Difficulty: Advanced.

Let Z={xy=0}⊂ℂ2Z=\{xy=0\}\subset\mathbb C^2, i:Z↪ℂ2i:Z\hookrightarrow\mathbb C^2, and ν:ℂ⊔ℂ→Z\nu:\mathbb C\sqcup\mathbb C\to Z its normalization. Over a field set F=i*kZ[1]F=i_*k_Z[1], G=i*ν*k[1]G=i_*\nu_*k[1]. Prove perversity and find their exact sequence.

For GG, its two normalized disks give k2[−1]k^2[-1], in degree one. Both meet the node’s lower bound zero; their only ordinary degree minus one has dimension-one support, giving upper membership. They are strong perverse.

The ordinary sheaf sequence 0→kZ→ν*k→k{0}→00\to k_Z\to\nu_*k\to k_{\{0\}}\to0 is stalkwise exact, with diagonal first map at the node. The perverse kernel appears in point costalk degree zero despite ordinary sheaf injectivity.

Solution. Complex orientation gives ωℂ=ℤ[2]\omega_{\mathbb C}=\mathbb Z[2]. Dualizing the finite free resolution of ℤ/2\mathbb Z/2 leaves ℤ/2\mathbb Z/2 in degree one, so its coefficient dual is (ℤ/2)[−1](\mathbb Z/2)[-1]. Therefore DℂF=(ℤ/2)[−1][2−1]=(ℤ/2)ℂD_{\mathbb C}F=(\mathbb Z/2)[-1][2-1]=(\mathbb Z/2)_{\mathbb C}. Its ordinary degree zero on a dimension-one stratum satisfies the lower bound minus one and violates the upper. The original was a strong heart object. Perfectness and canonical orientation hold; field-exact coefficient dualization is what fails.
