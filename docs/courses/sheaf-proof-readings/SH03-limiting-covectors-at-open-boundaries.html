Limiting covectors at open boundaries

Extending a sheaf across an open boundary can create singular directions. The new direction need not be the sum of two convergent covectors: their bases may approach one another while their lengths tend to infinity. We first isolate the error estimate that makes such cancellation meaningful. We then move the boundary into the region where the sheaf is already defined, pass to the actual extension functors, and finally treat the trace across a missing submanifold.

Let kk be a commutative ring and let the manifolds be finite dimensional, Hausdorff and countable at infinity. All complexes in the extension and trace statements belong to D+(kX)D^+(k_X) or the indicated open subspace; they are bounded below, and their stalk modules need not be finite or perfect. Smooth charts suffice, and the coordinate estimates below are invariant under C2C^2 changes. We use the noncharacteristic extension bounds and uniform directional localization, the two extension-continuity calculations, and the proper-image and closed-embedding support tests. Each has its proof in the linked reading.

Cancellation with a controlled base error

For two conic sets A,B⊂T*XA,B\subset T^*X, define their limiting sum in a coordinate chart by

(x;ξ)∈A+̂B⇔{(xn;αn)∈A,(yn;βn)∈B,xn,yn⟶x,αn+βn⟶ξ,|xn−yn||αn|⟶0.(L1) (x;\xi)\in A\widehat+ B \iff \begin{cases} (x_n;\alpha_n)\in A,\quad(y_n;\beta_n)\in B,\\ x_n,y_n\longrightarrow x,\quad\alpha_n+\beta_n\longrightarrow\xi,\\ |x_n-y_n|\,|\alpha_n|\longrightarrow0. \end{cases} \qquad\text{(L1)}

The sets need not be closed in the whole cotangent bundle. In particular AA may be a microsupport defined only over an open subset. Since the sums in (L1) are bounded, the inequality |βn|≤|αn|+|αn+βn||\beta_n|\leq|\alpha_n|+|\alpha_n+\beta_n| shows that the last condition is equivalent to the one with βn\beta_n. Interchanging the inputs proves symmetry. Constant witnesses give the ordinary same-base sum as a subset. Multiplying both input covectors by a positive constant proves conicity.

The resulting set is closed. Indeed, given points of the limiting sum converging to (x;ξ)(x;\xi), select from a witness for the mm-th point one pair with each base error, sum error and weighted product less than 1/m1/m. Those selected pairs witness membership at the limit. This argument also handles zero output covectors. Local equivalence of norms makes the definition independent of the particular norms used.

Here is the coordinate check, including the large terms. If hh changes base coordinates, put Q(x)=Dhx−TQ(x)=Dh_x^{-T}. On a smaller relatively compact chart, h,Qh,Q are Lipschitz and the matrices are bounded. The transformed sum equals

Q(xn)αn+Q(yn)βn=Q(yn)(αn+βn)+(Q(xn)−Q(yn))αn.(L2) Q(x_n)\alpha_n+Q(y_n)\beta_n =Q(y_n)(\alpha_n+\beta_n) +(Q(x_n)-Q(y_n))\alpha_n. \qquad\text{(L2)}

The last summand has norm at most C|xn−yn||αn|C|x_n-y_n||\alpha_n|, hence tends to zero. The transformed weighted product is bounded by a constant times the original one. Thus (L1) transforms by the cotangent map at xx; applying the same argument to h−1h^{-1} proves the converse. A locally Lipschitz derivative is the precise regularity used here; C2C^2 charts provide it. Mere convergence of the two bases would not control the error in (L2).

Inward directions and the two signs

The full open-extension estimates

For an arbitrary open inclusion j:Ω↪Xj:\Omega\hookrightarrow X and F∈D+(kΩ)F\in D^+(k_\Omega), put A=SS⁡(F)A=\operatorname{SS}(F), viewed only over Ω\Omega. Then

SS⁡(Rj*F)⊂A+̂N*(Ω),SS⁡(j!F)⊂A+̂N*(Ω)a.(L14) \operatorname{SS}(Rj_*F)\subset A\widehat+N^*(\Omega), \qquad \operatorname{SS}(j_!F)\subset A\widehat+N^*(\Omega)^a. \qquad\text{(L14)}

Adding a full conormal

Let MM be a closed smooth submanifold, with adapted coordinates (u,z)(u,z) and M={u=0}M=\{u=0\}. Write cotangent coordinates as (u,z;a,b)(u,z;a,b), where bb is tangential to MM. For conic AA, define ZM(A)⊂T*MZ_M(A)\subset T^*M by

(z0;b0)∈ZM(A)⇔{(un,zn;an,bn)∈A,un→0,zn→z0,bn→b0,|un||an|→0.(L20) (z_0;b_0)\in Z_M(A) \iff \begin{cases} (u_n,z_n;a_n,b_n)\in A,\\ u_n\to0,\quad z_n\to z_0,\quad b_n\to b_0,\\ |u_n||a_n|\to0. \end{cases} \qquad\text{(L20)}

If ρ:T*X|M→T*M\rho:T^*X|_M\to T^*M is restriction of covectors, then

A+̂TM*X=ρ−1ZM(A).(L21) A\widehat+T_M^*X=\rho^{-1}Z_M(A). \qquad\text{(L21)}

For necessity, a conormal input has base (0,wn)(0,w_n) and covector (cn,0)(c_n,0). The limiting sum forces bn→b0b_n\to b_0, while the base separation is at least |un||u_n|. Thus its weighted condition implies (L20). Conversely, for any prescribed final normal component a0a_0, pair the witness in (L20) with (0,zn;a0−an,0)(0,z_n;a_0-a_n,0). The sum is (a0,bn)(a_0,b_n), and the required product is bounded by |un||an|+|un||bn|→0|u_n||a_n|+|u_n||b_n|\to0. This proves equality for every a0a_0; one must not demand that the normal input sequence be bounded.

The tangential condition is intrinsic. For completeness, write an inverse adapted coordinate change as (u,z)=(P(u′,z′),Q(u′,z′))(u,z)=(P(u',z'),Q(u',z')), with P(0,z′)=0P(0,z')=0. Covectors transform by

The normal coordinates have comparable size near MM. Moreover Dz′P(0,z′)=0D_{z'}P(0,z')=0, so |Dz′P(u′,z′)|≤C|u′||D_{z'}P(u',z')|\leq C|u'|. Bounded derivatives and the same estimate for QQ give

|u′||a′|≤C|u|(|a|+|b|),b′−Dz′Q(0,z′)tb=O(|u′|(|a|+|b|)).(L23) |u'||a'|\leq C|u|(|a|+|b|), \qquad b'-D_{z'}Q(0,z')^t b=O\bigl(|u'|(|a|+|b|)\bigr). \qquad\text{(L23)}

Both errors tend to zero for a sequence in (L20). The limiting tangential covector therefore transforms by exactly the cotangent change on MM. Applying the inverse change proves equivalence.

The trace across a missing submanifold

Let U=X\MU=X\setminus M, with inclusions j:U↪Xj:U\hookrightarrow X and i:M↪Xi:M\hookrightarrow X. For F∈D+(kU)F\in D^+(k_U) and A=SS⁡(F)A=\operatorname{SS}(F),

Products, restriction, and the limiting sum

We now turn the boundary trace into an estimate for tensor products. The key is to realize ordinary restriction as a trace from a punctured cylinder. First we prove the external-product estimate, keeping the actual compact restriction maps. Then the cylinder argument and a diagonal coordinate calculation supply the limiting sum.

In this section, assume that the commutative ring kk has finite global dimension dd, and take bounded complexes with arbitrary stalk modules. No constructibility, finite generation, perfection, or noncharacteristic condition is imposed. These hypotheses ensure that every derived tensor used here is bounded. The preceding extension and trace arguments retain their stated bounded-below scope over an arbitrary commutative ring.

Coefficients on a compact cap

For manifolds X,YX,Y, with projections qX,qYq_X,q_Y, set

F⊠LG=qX−1F⊗kLqY−1G,F∈D[a,b](kX),G∈D[c,e](kY).(Q1) F\boxtimes^L G=q_X^{-1}F\otimes_k^L q_Y^{-1}G, \qquad F\in D^{[a,b]}(k_X),\quad G\in D^{[c,e]}(k_Y). \qquad\text{(Q1)}

Four tensor and restriction checks with solutions

A tilted cut changes the cap height

Take first and second coordinates x,y∈ℝx,y\in\mathbb R, the cone C=(−∞,0]C=(-\infty,0], and a target covector (1,2)(1,2) at the origin. Compare the cap cut at −h-h by this covector with the cap cut by (1,0)(1,0), at vertex (z,w)(z,w).

Both covectors are strictly negative on C×{0}C\times\{0\}, so (Q9) and the cone-to-test proof still exclude the target direction.

Why the half-line is closed

In the cylinder construction, take X=M={*}X=M=\{*\} and F=k≠0F=k\ne0. Compare the units for the closed and open positive half-line sheaves at zero.

Solution. In both cases the restriction to ℝ\{0}\mathbb R\setminus\{0\} is constant on the positive component and zero on the negative one. Its ordinary derived extension has stalk kk at zero: sections on a small positive interval are kk, with no higher cohomology. The closed half-line sheaf also has stalk kk, and its unit is the identity under constant sections. The open extension-by-zero sheaf has zero stalk, so its unit at zero is 0→k0\to k, not an isomorphism. Replacing the closed half-line in (Q11) by the open one would break (Q14).

H−1(F⊗LG)=VX,H0(F⊗LG)=VX,Hj(F⊗LG)=0(j≠−1,0).(Q20) H^{-1}(F\otimes^L G)=V_X, \qquad H^0(F\otimes^L G)=V_X, \qquad H^j(F\otimes^L G)=0\quad(j\ne-1,0). \qquad\text{(Q20)}

The exhibited complex is constant, so the zero-section criterion bounds its microsupport by the zero section; the limiting sum of the two input zero sections is the same zero section. The nonzero degree −1-1 shows why the derived tensor cannot be replaced by the ordinary tensor. The infinite module is not subject to any finite-generation or perfect-stalk requirement in this proof.

Four checks with solutions

An unbounded sequence contributes a finite tangent direction

In X=ℝu×ℝzX=\mathbb R_u\times\mathbb R_z, let M={u=0}M=\{u=0\}. Let AA consist of all positive multiples of (a,b)=(n,1)(a,b)=(n,1) over (u,z)=(n−2,0)(u,z)=(n^{-2},0), for integers n≥1n\geq1. Show that every (0,0;a0,1)(0,0;a_0,1) belongs to A+̂TM*XA\widehat+T_M^*X, although the ordinary same-base sum is empty.

Solution. The displayed sequence has bn=1b_n=1 and |un||an|=1/n→0|u_n||a_n|=1/n\to0. Pair it with (0,0;a0−n,0)(0,0;a_0-n,0). The sums converge to the specified covector and the weighted base error tends to zero. There are no bases shared by AA and TM*XT_M^*X, so the ordinary sum is empty. Any sequence in this example whose tangential coefficient tends to one and whose base tends to MM has scaling factor tending to one and n→∞n\to\infty; its normal coefficient must therefore diverge. Bounded inputs would miss this direction.

Why base convergence alone is insufficient

Replace the bases in the preceding example by (n−1,0)(n^{-1},0). Can a limiting sum have final tangential coefficient one? Check the effect of the adapted change u′=ezuu'=e^z u, z′=zz'=z.

Solution. Write the positive scale as cnc_n. Tangential convergence to one forces cn→1c_n\to1, whereas |un||an|=cn→1|u_n||a_n|=c_n\to1. Condition (L20) fails, so no final normal component can repair the defect. In the new coordinates, a′=e−z′aa'=e^{-z'}a and b′=b−uab'=b-ua. The unscaled sequence has b′=0b'=0 although b=1b=1. This is the uncontrolled error in (L2) and (L23). In the first example the corresponding error is ua=1/n→0ua=1/n\to0, so the tangent limit is unchanged.

The two sides in normal rank one

Let X=ℝX=\mathbb R, M={0}M=\{0\}, and let FF be the constant sheaf with a nonzero, possibly infinitely generated, module VV on U=ℝ\{0}U=\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}. Compute its trace i−1Rj*Fi^{-1}Rj_*F and compare the ambient boundary directions of the two extensions.

Solution. Each sufficiently small punctured interval is the disjoint union of two contractible intervals, so its derived sections are V⊕VV\oplus V in degree zero. The restriction maps identify the two summands by their sides, giving i−1Rj*F=V⊕Vi^{-1}Rj_*F=V\oplus V. The rank-one blowup has two points in its sphere fibre, exactly encoding those two components. Ordinary extension is locally the sum of the constant sheaves on the two closed half-lines; extension by zero is the corresponding sum on the open half-lines. The endpoint support tests in the directional reading give opposite half-rays for the two sides, whose union is the full cotangent fibre at zero in either case. The trace is a complex on a point and has only the zero covector there. No finite-generation assumption entered any calculation.

Radial control does not replace sphere control

In normal rank two, take θn=(1,0)\theta_n=(1,0), tn→0+t_n\to0^+, and an=tn−1(0,1)a_n=t_n^{-1}(0,1). Does tn|τn|→0t_n|\tau_n|\to0 alone imply tn|an|→0t_n|a_n|\to0?

Solution. Here τn=⟨θn,an⟩=0\tau_n=\langle\theta_n,a_n\rangle=0, but the sphere covector is λn=(0,1)|T(1,0)S1\lambda_n=(0,1)|_{T_{(1,0)}S^1}, of norm one. Thus tn|an|=1t_n|a_n|=1. The missing condition is precisely λn→0\lambda_n\to0, which comes from the zero sphere component of the proper-image lift (L30). Equation (L31) combines both controls and makes clear why neither may be omitted.

Sources and scope
