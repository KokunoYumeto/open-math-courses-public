Constructible costalks and Verdier duality

A constructible complex has perfect stalks. Its costalks are perfect too, but this needs a local cohomology argument: the costalk is the fibre of restriction from a small ball to its punctured ball. Verdier duality exchanges these two measurements, with their degrees intact. The resulting biduality comes from the actual evaluation map.

Use the three small-ball comparisons with their proper-support transport and compact chart cutoff for the local restriction and support maps. The compact-set perfection lemma applies to the small sphere. Weak duality uses internal-Hom stability together with the uniform bounded-Hom estimate.

The exceptional-operation proofs supply trace-normalized dual sections and the exceptional internal-Hom comparison. The cohomological-biduality lesson specifies formal neighborhood systems, their canonical stalk and costalk comparisons and the evaluation-compatible dual pairings. We apply these contracts to the small-ball systems below.

The two local measurements

Let kk be commutative of finite global dimension. Let XX be a finite-dimensional real analytic manifold, Hausdorff and countable at infinity, with a uniform dimension bound. All complexes are globally bounded. Write

Ax(F)=Fx,Cx(F)=ix!F=RΓ{x}(X;F),DXF=Rℋom(F,ωX).(1) A_x(F)=F_x,\qquad C_x(F)=i_x^!F=R\Gamma_{\{x\}}(X;F), \qquad D_XF=R\mathcal Hom(F,\omega_X). \qquad\text{(1)}

Here ixi_x is the point inclusion and ωX\omega_X the manifold dualizing complex. A perfect coefficient complex has a bounded finite-projective representative. For coefficients put P∨=RHom⁡k(P,k)P^\vee=R\operatorname{Hom}_k(P,k). This notation does not assert perfection or biduality of PP.

Suppose first that FF is only weakly ℝ\mathbb R-constructible. In a coordinate chart centered at xx, the small-ball theorem gives, for sufficiently small radii, the actual compatible isomorphisms

RΓ(Bϵ;F)⟶Ax(F),Cx(F)⟶RΓc(Bϵ;F).(2) R\Gamma(B_\epsilon;F)\longrightarrow A_x(F),\qquad C_x(F)\longrightarrow R\Gamma_c(B_\epsilon;F). \qquad\text{(2)}

All shrinking ordinary restrictions and compact-support extension maps commute with these identifications. The theorem also gives ordinary sections on closed balls and on the punctured ball, with the latter identified by restriction with sections on any sufficiently small interior sphere. A compact chart cutoff justifies the local application when the chart is not globally proper.

The balls are cofinal among neighborhoods of xx. Therefore the formal ind-system of ordinary sections is represented by Ax(F)A_x(F), and the formal pro-system of compact sections by Cx(F)C_x(F). Their representing maps are the canonical ones in (2). This statement retains the transition maps. It is stronger than stabilization of the dimensions of cohomology groups. Neither representative is yet claimed perfect.

The existing definition calls FF cohomologically constructible when these two formal neighborhood systems are represented, their canonical representatives are the stalk and costalk, and both representatives are perfect. Within the weakly constructible class, (2) has already supplied everything except the two perfection conditions.

Perfect stalks give perfect costalks

Proposition. If F∈Dℝ-cb(kX)F\in D^b_{\mathbb R\text{-}c}(k_X), then Cx(F)C_x(F) is perfect for every xx.

Proof. In positive dimension choose a small coordinate ball and an interior sphere SδS_\delta, with 0<δ<ϵ0<\delta<\epsilon, within the stabilized range. Support localization and the actual small-ball restrictions give

Cx(F)⟶Ax(F)→uxRΓ(Sδ;F|Sδ)→+1.(3) C_x(F)\longrightarrow A_x(F) \xrightarrow{u_x}R\Gamma(S_\delta;F|_{S_\delta})\xrightarrow{+1}. \qquad\text{(3)}

The map uxu_x is restriction through the punctured ball. The stalk term is perfect by definition. The sphere is compact and subanalytic, so its section complex is perfect by the compact-set lemma. That lemma applies to the whole ordinary restriction of FF; it does not require a globally constant coefficient on the sphere. Finite cone closure makes the fibre in (3) perfect. In dimension zero the point is open locally and its costalk equals its stalk. ▫\square

Consequently, for bounded weakly constructible FF,

F is ℝ-constructible⟺F is cohomologically constructible.(4) F\text{ is }\mathbb R\text{-constructible} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F\text{ is cohomologically constructible}. \qquad\text{(4)}

The forward implication uses (2) and (3). The reverse implication uses the perfect stalk clause of the definition together with the given weak constructibility. The same definition directly makes every costalk perfect. No Noetherian hypothesis enters (3) or (4).

Duality exchanges the measurements before biduality

The dual-sections prerequisite identifies, naturally and with the evaluation pairings,

RΓ(U;DXF)≃RHom⁡k(RΓc(U;F),k).(5) R\Gamma(U;D_XF)\simeq R\operatorname{Hom}_k(R\Gamma_c(U;F),k). \qquad\text{(5)}

For bounded weakly constructible FF, its compact-section system in (2) is already constant as a formal system with representative Cx(F)C_x(F). Applying the contravariant derived coefficient dual gives a represented ordinary-section system for DXFD_XF. Exact filtered stalk colimits then give

(DXF)x≃Cx(F)∨.(6) (D_XF)_x\simeq C_x(F)^\vee. \qquad\text{(6)}

This application uses a functor on a represented formal system. It requires no assertion that derived Hom commutes with an arbitrary inverse limit.

The other measurement follows directly from exceptional duality, without any perfection assumption. The normalized formula ix!DXF≃D{x}(ix−1F)i_x^!D_XF\simeq D_{\{x\}}(i_x^{-1}F), obtained from internal exceptional adjunction and ix!ωX=ω{x}=ki_x^!\omega_X=\omega_{\{x\}}=k, gives

Cx(DXF)≃Ax(F)∨.(7) C_x(D_XF)\simeq A_x(F)^\vee. \qquad\text{(7)}

Both are canonical local dual pairings. Boundedness of DXFD_XF follows from the manifold bounded-Hom theorem and the bounded orientation complex. Weak constructibility follows from the weak internal-Hom theorem, since ωX\omega_X is a locally constant shifted orientation line. Thus the small-ball stabilization also applies to DXFD_XF.

If all Cx(F)C_x(F) are perfect, then their coefficient duals are perfect, so (6) makes DXFD_XF ℝ\mathbb R-constructible. We have proved the general-ring implications

F is ℝ-constructible⟺F is cohomologically constructible,F is cohomologically constructible⟹(∀x,Cx(F) perfect)⟹DXF is ℝ-constructible.(8) \begin{gathered} F\text{ is }\mathbb R\text{-constructible} \ \Longleftrightarrow\ F\text{ is cohomologically constructible},\\ F\text{ is cohomologically constructible} \ \Longrightarrow\ (\forall x,\ C_x(F)\text{ perfect}) \ \Longrightarrow\ D_XF\text{ is }\mathbb R\text{-constructible}. \end{gathered} \qquad\text{(8)}

These are assertions about a given bounded weakly constructible input. That standing assumption supplies the geometric part of the output and the local stabilization used in (6).

The evaluation map is biduality

If PP is perfect, then P∨P^\vee is perfect and the canonical evaluation ηP:P→P∨∨\eta_P:P\to P^{\vee\vee} is an isomorphism. Indeed, for a finite projective module evaluation is the usual coordinate isomorphism for a finite free module, restricted to a direct summand. A bounded finite-projective complex gives the same chain isomorphism, with the standard cohomological dual signs.

For constructible FF, (8) makes DXFD_XF constructible. Apply (6) to its dual and use (7):

(DXDXF)x≃Cx(DXF)∨≃Ax(F)∨∨.(9) (D_XD_XF)_x\simeq C_x(D_XF)^\vee \simeq A_x(F)^{\vee\vee}. \qquad\text{(9)}

Under these identifications the stalk of the sheaf evaluation map F→DXDXFF\to D_XD_XF is ηAx(F)\eta_{A_x(F)}. This is where the natural maps matter: (5)–(7) come from the adjunction evaluation and trace, and restriction and support extension preserve those pairings. They identify the actual evaluation, rather than just two abstractly isomorphic objects. Perfectness makes it an isomorphism at every stalk; stalkwise detection gives

F→∼DXDXF.(10) F\xrightarrow{\sim}D_XD_XF. \qquad\text{(10)}

Thus Verdier duality is a contravariant equivalence on the bounded ℝ\mathbb R-constructible category over the full coefficient ring in use. On a map F→GF\to G its direction is DXG→DXFD_XG\to D_XF. Orientations have remained inside ωX\omega_X; no global orientation was chosen.

Detecting perfection over a field or the integers

To reverse the last implication in (8), we need an additional algebraic detection result. We prove it for the two coefficient rings treated here.

Detection lemma. Let kk be a field or ℤ\mathbb Z, and M∈Db(k)M\in D^b(k). If M∨M^\vee is perfect, then MM is perfect.

We first prove that the coefficient dual detects zero on bounded complexes. Over a field, dualization is exact and a nonzero vector space has a nonzero linear functional. Its derived dual therefore detects each nonzero cohomology group.

For ℤ\mathbb Z, start with an abelian group TT such that

Hom⁡(T,ℤ)=0,Ext⁡1(T,ℤ)=0.(11) \operatorname{Hom}(T,\mathbb Z)=0, \qquad\operatorname{Ext}^1(T,\mathbb Z)=0. \qquad\text{(11)}

The integral coefficient dual has no Ext degrees above one. One concrete reason is the injective resolution 0→ℤ→ℚ→ℚ/ℤ→00\to\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Q\to\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z\to0. Divisible groups are injective: to extend a homomorphism from a subgroup across one new element, divide its prescribed multiple if that element has finite order modulo the subgroup, and choose an arbitrary value if it has infinite order there. The least positive multiple in the subgroup makes the first extension well-defined. Zorn’s lemma then extends across the whole group. This proves injectivity of both displayed divisible groups.

If TT contained a subgroup ℤ/n\mathbb Z/n, with n>1n>1, its inclusion and the long Ext sequence would give a surjection Ext⁡1(T,ℤ)→Ext⁡1(ℤ/n,ℤ)=ℤ/n\operatorname{Ext}^1(T,\mathbb Z)\to\operatorname{Ext}^1(\mathbb Z/n,\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/n, because the next Ext degree of the quotient vanishes. Hence (11) makes TT torsion free.

For each prime pp, apply Hom⁡(T,−)\operatorname{Hom}(T,-) to 0→ℤ→pℤ→ℤ/p→00\to\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{p}\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/p\to0. Equation (11) gives Hom⁡(T,ℤ/p)=0\operatorname{Hom}(T,\mathbb Z/p)=0. If T/pTT/pT were nonzero, its vector-space structure over 𝔽p\mathbb F_p would give a nonzero such functional. Thus pT=TpT=T. Factoring integers into primes makes TT divisible. A torsion-free divisible group is a ℚ\mathbb Q-vector space, with division by each nonzero integer uniquely determined.

We also need Ext⁡1(ℚ,ℤ)≠0\operatorname{Ext}^1(\mathbb Q,\mathbb Z)\ne0. Here is a proof. Put B=ℤ[1/2]B=\mathbb Z[1/2]. Every compatible family an∈ℤ/2na_n\in\mathbb Z/2^n, with an+1≡an(mod⁡2n)a_{n+1}\equiv a_n\pmod{2^n}, defines

ha:B→ℚ/ℤ,ha(2−n)=an/2n(mod⁡ℤ).(12) h_a:B\to\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z,\qquad h_a(2^{-n})=a_n/2^n\pmod{\mathbb Z}. \qquad\text{(12)}

These homomorphisms vanish on ℤ\mathbb Z, and arbitrary infinite binary sequences give uncountably many distinct compatible families. Injectivity of ℚ/ℤ\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z extends each to ℚ\mathbb Q; distinct restrictions give distinct extensions. In contrast, every map ℚ→ℚ\mathbb Q\to\mathbb Q is multiplication by one rational number, so there are only countably many maps which can lift to ℚ\mathbb Q. The injective resolution identifies

Ext⁡1(ℚ,ℤ)≃Hom⁡(ℚ,ℚ/ℤ)/im⁡Hom⁡(ℚ,ℚ).(13) \operatorname{Ext}^1(\mathbb Q,\mathbb Z) \simeq\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Q,\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z) \big/\operatorname{im}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Q,\mathbb Q). \qquad\text{(13)}

This quotient is nonzero, and in fact uncountable. If T≠0T\ne0, a vector-space complement gives T≃ℚ⊕LT\simeq\mathbb Q\oplus L. Its Ext group then has the nonzero group in (13) as a summand, contradicting (11). Thus T=0T=0.

For bounded MM, the integral hyper-Ext spectral sequence has only columns zero and one. Its short exact sequences are

0⟶Ext⁡1(Hq+1(M),ℤ)⟶H−q(M∨)⟶Hom⁡(Hq(M),ℤ)⟶0.(14) 0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^1(H^{q+1}(M),\mathbb Z) \longrightarrow H^{-q}(M^\vee) \longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(H^q(M),\mathbb Z) \longrightarrow0. \qquad\text{(14)}

Finite cohomological bounds justify convergence, and no splitting of these sequences is assumed. If M∨=0M^\vee=0, (14) and the group argument make every Hq(M)H^q(M) zero. This proves zero detection over ℤ\mathbb Z.

Now put N=M∨N=M^\vee, assumed perfect. Its evaluation ηN:N→N∨∨\eta_N:N\to N^{\vee\vee} is an isomorphism. The evaluation identity

(ηM)∨∘ηM∨=id⁡M∨(15) (\eta_M)^\vee\circ\eta_{M^\vee} =\operatorname{id}_{M^\vee} \qquad\text{(15)}

makes (ηM)∨(\eta_M)^\vee its inverse, hence an isomorphism. Dualize the cone of ηM\eta_M. It is zero; zero detection makes that cone zero. Therefore M≃M∨∨=N∨M\simeq M^{\vee\vee}=N^\vee, which is perfect. This proves the lemma. It does not presuppose biduality of MM.

Finally suppose DXFD_XF is constructible. Its costalks are perfect by (3); (7) says that these are Fx∨F_x^\vee. The detection lemma makes every FxF_x perfect. The given weak constructibility then makes FF constructible. Thus all four conditions in (8) are equivalent over a field or ℤ\mathbb Z. For the general coefficient ring we retain precisely the implications already proved in (8).

Examples and exercises with solutions

A line costalk carries the integration degree

Difficulty: Introductory.

Let PP be a perfect coefficient complex, constant on the oriented real line. Calculate its stalk, costalk and Verdier-dual stalk at zero, including the map used for the costalk.

Solution. The stalk is PP. A punctured small interval has two contractible components, so its coefficient complex is P⊕PP\oplus P. The restriction map is the diagonal P→P⊕PP\to P\oplus P. Its fibre is P[−1]P[-1], using the increasing orientation to identify the difference quotient by (a,b)↦b−a(a,b)\mapsto b-a. Equation (6) gives the dual stalk (P[−1])∨=P∨[1](P[-1])^\vee=P^\vee[1]. This agrees with ωℝ=kℝ[1]\omega_{\mathbb R}=k_{\mathbb R}[1] and DℝPℝ=(P∨)ℝ[1]D_{\mathbb R}P_{\mathbb R}=(P^\vee)_{\mathbb R}[1]. For P=kP=k, the costalk is in degree one and the dual stalk in degree minus one. These opposite degrees come from fibre and dual operations.

Integral torsion at a point survives duality

Difficulty: Intermediate.

In X=ℝ2X=\mathbb R^2, let i:{0}↪Xi:\{0\}\hookrightarrow X and F=i*(ℤ/n)F=i_*(\mathbb Z/n), n>1n>1. Compute both local measurements, DXFD_XF, and its second dual.

Solution. Sections on a small ball are the point coefficient, and all their support is at that point. Thus A0(F)=C0(F)=ℤ/nA_0(F)=C_0(F)=\mathbb Z/n, while both measurements vanish elsewhere. Resolve the coefficient by [ℤ→nℤ][\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{n}\mathbb Z] in degrees minus one and zero. Its derived integral dual has zero degree-zero cohomology and ℤ/n\mathbb Z/n in degree one, hence is (ℤ/n)[−1](\mathbb Z/n)[-1]. Exceptional duality for the closed point gives DXF=i*((ℤ/n)[−1])D_XF=i_*((\mathbb Z/n)[-1]): the dualizing complex of the point is ℤ\mathbb Z, so no ambient two-dimensional shift is inserted in this point calculation. Dualizing again yields (ℤ/n[−1])∨=(ℤ/n)∨[1]=ℤ/n(\mathbb Z/n[-1])^\vee=(\mathbb Z/n)^\vee[1]=\mathbb Z/n. The actual finite-projective evaluation gives F→DXDXFF\to D_XD_XF as an isomorphism. Perfection includes this torsion, and ordinary Hom⁡(ℤ/n,ℤ)=0\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z/n,\mathbb Z)=0 alone would miss its dual.

A closed half-line has zero boundary costalk

Difficulty: Intermediate.

On the increasing oriented real line take F=k[0,∞)F=k_{[0,\infty)}. Compute C0(F)C_0(F), the stalks of DℝFD_{\mathbb R}F, and the resulting dual sheaf complex.

Solution. At zero the ordinary stalk is kk. The left punctured interval contributes zero and the right contributes kk; the restriction map is k→idkk\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}}k. Its fibre is zero, so C0(F)=0C_0(F)=0. At a positive point the costalk is k[−1]k[-1], and at a negative point it is zero. Thus (6) gives dual stalks k[1]k[1] on the positive open half-line and zero at and to the left of zero. On that open half-line the dual is its constant shifted coefficient by the local orientation calculation. Localization, with zero ordinary restriction to the closed complement, identifies the whole object with its open extension: DℝF=k(0,∞)[1]D_{\mathbb R}F=k_{(0,\infty)}[1]. All these local measurements are perfect, including the zero boundary costalk. The theorem then returns FF on a second dualization. A nonzero stalk need not give a nonzero costalk at the same point.

Rank of the attachment map determines two dual degrees

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Work over a field. Near a cut on the line, let the point stalk be A=kaA=k^a, the sum of its two neighboring interval coefficients be B=kbB=k^b, and the actual restriction u:A→Bu:A\to B have rank rr. Compute the costalk and dual stalk cohomology at the cut.

Solution. The costalk is the two-term complex [A→uB][A\xrightarrow{u}B] in degrees zero and one. Its cohomology is ker⁡(u)\ker(u) in degree zero and coker⁡(u)\operatorname{coker}(u) in degree one, of dimensions a−ra-r and b−rb-r. Exact field dualization reverses the degrees. Thus the dual stalk has coker⁡(u)*\operatorname{coker}(u)^* in degree minus one and ker⁡(u)*\ker(u)^* in degree zero, and no other cohomology. Its costalk is A*A^* in degree zero by (7). In particular the list of coefficient dimensions a,ba,b does not determine the dual stalk: the attachment rank changes both groups. Every displayed complex is perfect.

Stabilized infinite coefficients still fail biduality

Difficulty: Advanced.

Over a field let V=⨁m≥1kV=\bigoplus_{m\geq1}k and F=i*VF=i_*V at one point of a manifold. Its neighborhood systems stabilize. Decide whether it is cohomologically constructible, whether its dual is ℝ\mathbb R-constructible, and whether evaluation into the second dual is an isomorphism.

Solution. Both local measurements at the point are VV, with identity transitions; elsewhere they are zero. Thus representability and the canonical comparisons hold. The point coefficient is infinite dimensional, so it is not perfect and the full cohomological-constructibility condition fails. Its dual is i*V*i_*V^*, with V*≃kℕV^*\simeq k^{\mathbb N}. The coordinate vectors already give infinitely many independent elements of this product, so the dual is also not constructible.

The evaluation V→V**V\to V^{**} is injective but is not onto. The quotient kℕ/k(ℕ)k^{\mathbb N}/k^{(\mathbb N)} is nonzero, as seen from the all-ones sequence. Choose a linear functional on the quotient nonzero on that class, and compose it with the quotient map. The resulting functional on V*=kℕV^*=k^{\mathbb N} vanishes on all finitely supported sequences but is nonzero. Evaluation by a vector v∈Vv\in V is a finite linear combination of coordinate evaluations. If it vanished on every finitely supported sequence, all coefficients of vv would be zero. Therefore the chosen functional is outside the evaluation image. This establishes the failure directly. The weak small-ball stabilization never asserted finite coefficients or weak biduality.

The rational point coefficient is detected in Ext

Difficulty: Advanced.

Over ℤ\mathbb Z, let F=i*ℚF=i_*\mathbb Q. Show that its coefficient dual is nonzero although its ordinary integral Hom is zero. Give a specific homomorphism which represents a nonzero Ext class, and decide whether the dual is perfect.

Solution. A homomorphism ℚ→ℤ\mathbb Q\to\mathbb Z sends 11 to an integer divisible by every positive integer, hence to zero; unique rational division then makes the entire map zero. To obtain an Ext class, set an=∑m!<n,m≥22m!(mod⁡2n)a_n=\sum_{m!<n,\ m\geq2}2^{m!}\pmod{2^n}. These residues are compatible. Equation (12) defines a homomorphism on ℤ[1/2]\mathbb Z[1/2], zero on ℤ\mathbb Z. Extend it to ℚ\mathbb Q by injectivity of ℚ/ℤ\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z.

It cannot lift to a map ℚ→ℚ\mathbb Q\to\mathbb Q. Such a lift would be multiplication by a rational qq; its value at 11, reduced modulo ℤ\mathbb Z, is zero, so qq would be an integer. Its residues modulo 2n2^n would then have to be every ana_n. A nonnegative integer has binary digits eventually zero, and a negative integer has its compatible binary digits eventually one. The chosen digits have infinitely many isolated ones at factorial positions and arbitrarily long zero gaps, so neither is possible. Equation (13) makes this a nonzero element of E=Ext⁡1(ℚ,ℤ)E=\operatorname{Ext}^1(\mathbb Q,\mathbb Z).

The coefficient dual is E[−1]E[-1]. The proof of (13) also gives uncountably many distinct classes, since an uncountable set is being divided by a countable subgroup. Thus EE is not finitely generated over ℤ\mathbb Z, and E[−1]E[-1] is not perfect. Accordingly DXF=i*E[−1]D_XF=i_*E[-1] is weakly constructible but not ℝ\mathbb R-constructible. This example explains why derived dual detection over the integers must include its Ext degree.

Accessible duality comparison

Andreas Hohl and Pierre Schapira, Unusual functorialities for weakly constructible sheaves, version 2, 7 January 2025, §3, state the stalk–costalk duality proposition for weakly cohomologically constructible sheaves. The author source labels it pro:dual and explains that perfection is unused in the earlier proof, which it does not repeat. The natural small-ball systems and evaluation proofs in this lesson retain that weak-coefficient assertion; perfection is required when evaluation is inverted. The modern inverse/direct comparison extensions follow the constructible evaluation criterion and infinite-twist comparison proofs, without inverting evaluation of an infinite locally constant twist.

Readable source and dependency account

The small-ball comparison maps identify the formal neighbourhood systems before dualizing them. Perfection of a costalk is proved from a compact supported-cohomology fibre; the actual evaluation map then becomes the perfect-complex evaluation under those identifications. The field and integer detection arguments, including the rational Ext calculation, are independent arguments written here. They do not assert the converse for every ring. The free notes leave a biduality proof as an exercise, so that exercise is not cited as a completed proof of the larger coefficient scope.
