When a kernel quantizes a contact transformation

A cotangent correspondence can be a graph even when the corresponding sheaf operator loses information. The missing condition is an identity condition on the kernel’s directional endomorphisms. In this lesson we prove that condition sufficient for an equivalence, and explain how the equivalence transports the entire sheaf of directional morphisms.

Use Dual kernels and an unchanged parameter and Directional morphisms through a sheaf kernel. The additional local inputs are the conormal coefficient model, the submanifold formula, the microlocal identity and composition maps, and the worked microlocal-duality proof. That duality uses the constructible biduality theorem and its external-Hom evaluation comparison. We state exactly what is needed; the named proofs and their further hypotheses remain prerequisites.

The correspondence and the identity condition

The coefficient ring kk is commutative with identity and finite global dimension. Manifolds are smooth, Hausdorff, finite dimensional and countable at infinity. Objects are globally bounded complexes of sheaves of kk-modules. The selected cotangent regions are open. Cohomological constructibility has the formal-system and perfect stalk/costalk meaning stated in the dual-kernel lesson; mere boundedness does not imply it.

Write P=X×YP=X\times Y, p1(x,y;ξ,η)=(x;ξ)p_1(x,y;\xi,\eta)=(x;\xi), and p2a(x,y;ξ,η)=(y;−η)p_2^a(x,y;\xi,\eta)=(y;-\eta). Suppose Λ⊂ΩX×ΩYa\Lambda\subset\Omega_X\times\Omega_Y^a is a relatively closed conic correspondence whose two projections

p1:Λ→∼ΩX,p2a:Λ→∼ΩY(1) p_1:\Lambda\xrightarrow{\sim}\Omega_X, \qquad p_2^a:\Lambda\xrightarrow{\sim}\Omega_Y \qquad\text{(1)}

are homeomorphisms. Set χ=p1∘(p2a|Λ)−1\chi=p_1\circ(p_2^a|_\Lambda)^{-1}. For now only the topology of this graph matters. When Λ\Lambda is smooth Lagrangian and the two projections are diffeomorphisms, χ\chi is a homogeneous symplectic, or contact, transformation.

Let K∈Db(kP)K\in D^b(k_P) satisfy the following three conditions:

  1. KK is cohomologically constructible.

  2. In either selected cotangent region, its microsupport is contained in the same graph:

    SS⁡(K)∩(p1−1ΩX∪(p2a)−1ΩY)⊂Λ.(2) \operatorname{SS}(K)\cap \bigl(p_1^{-1}\Omega_X\cup(p_2^a)^{-1}\Omega_Y\bigr) \subset\Lambda. \qquad\text{(2)}

  3. The identity-induced morphism is an isomorphism:

    eK:kΛ→∼μhom⁡P(K,K)|Λ.(3) e_K:k_\Lambda\xrightarrow{\sim} \mu\operatorname{hom}_P(K,K)|_\Lambda. \qquad\text{(3)}

The map in (3) is obtained from id⁡K\operatorname{id}_K, using the microlocal unit. An abstract isomorphism with a constant complex is not the stated condition. In particular, the normalization of its identity section is retained.

Theorem 1. Under (1)–(3), the localized operators

ΦK:𝒟Y(ΩY)⟶𝒟X(ΩX),ΨK:𝒟X(ΩX)⟶𝒟Y(ΩY)(4) \Phi_K:\mathcal D_Y(\Omega_Y)\longrightarrow\mathcal D_X(\Omega_X), \qquad \Psi_K:\mathcal D_X(\Omega_X)\longrightarrow\mathcal D_Y(\Omega_Y) \qquad\text{(4)}

are inverse equivalences. For all bounded G1,G2G_1,G_2, there is a natural isomorphism

χ*(μhom⁡Y(G2,G1)|ΩY)≃μhom⁡X(ΦKG2,ΦKG1)|ΩX.(5) \chi_*\bigl(\mu\operatorname{hom}_Y(G_2,G_1)|_{\Omega_Y}\bigr) \simeq \mu\operatorname{hom}_X(\Phi_KG_2,\Phi_KG_1)|_{\Omega_X}. \qquad\text{(5)}

The direct image in (5) is by a homeomorphism, so it is exact. Both sides depend only on the indicated localized input objects.

Detecting a cone supported in a conormal

Here is the exact use of the local coefficient-model prerequisite. If S⊂MS\subset M is a closed smooth submanifold and a bounded complex CC has microsupport contained in TS*MT_S^*M near p∈TS*Mp\in T_S^*M, then

C≃ASin 𝒟M({p})(6) C\simeq A_S\quad\text{in }\mathcal D_M(\{p\}) \qquad\text{(6)}

for some bounded complex AA of kk-modules. This is local at the conormal point. It permits arbitrary modules and makes no assertion about an ordinary neighborhood of the original sheaf.

The submanifold formula and specialization of a supported coefficient complex give

μhom⁡M(kS,C)=μSC,μS(AS)≃π−1Aon TS*M.(7) \mu\operatorname{hom}_M(k_S,C)=\mu_SC, \qquad \mu_S(A_S)\simeq\pi^{-1}A \quad\text{on }T_S^*M. \qquad\text{(7)}

The second formula has no codimension shift: specialization of ASA_S is supported on the zero section of the normal bundle, and its negative Fourier transform is the coefficient complex on the whole dual normal bundle. The first formula uses the specified conormal identification and the closed-embedding comparison.

Consequently (μSC)p=0(\mu_SC)_p=0 implies C=0C=0 in the localization at pp, whenever the conormal-containment hypothesis holds. Indeed microlocal Hom inverts denominators in its second argument by its support bound, so (6) and (7) identify this stalk with AA. If it is zero, the coefficient model is zero and so is CC at pp.

We will apply this to the cone of a map of diagonal kernels. It would be false to treat ordinary cotangent projection recovery as conservative on every conic sheaf; the conormal hypothesis is what makes this particular detection work.

The adjunction in the presence of a parameter

Condition (2) and the two homeomorphisms in (1) make KK both forward and reverse admissible. Each relevant restricted relation is a relatively closed subset of a graph whose two projections are proper homeomorphisms. Thus both operators descend, and ΦK⊣ΨK\Phi_K\dashv\Psi_K.

Let ZZ be another manifold. The parameter kernel ΘZK\Theta_ZK acts between Y×ZY\times Z and X×ZX\times Z, on regions ΩY×T*Z\Omega_Y\times T^*Z and ΩX×T*Z\Omega_X\times T^*Z. Its relation is contained in the graph of χZ=χ×id⁡T*Z\chi_Z=\chi\times\operatorname{id}_{T^*Z}. It is admissible in both directions. Apply the two microlocal Hom comparisons to this kernel. Their common intermediate sheaf is supported on that graph, because the first input of its microlocal Hom is ΘZK\Theta_ZK. Projection along the two sides of a graph is then just transport by χZ\chi_Z. We obtain

χZ*μhom⁡(G,ΨΘZKF)≃μhom⁡(ΦΘZKG,F)(8) \chi_{Z*}\mu\operatorname{hom}(G,\Psi_{\Theta_ZK}F) \simeq \mu\operatorname{hom}(\Phi_{\Theta_ZK}G,F) \qquad\text{(8)}

on ΩX×T*Z\Omega_X\times T^*Z.

To justify the support argument without losing ordinary direct-image information, let ii be the closed graph embedding inside the selected cotangent correspondence. A complex with closed support in this graph equals i*i−1i_*i^{-1} of that complex by the open–closed triangle. The two restricted projections composed with ii are homeomorphisms. Applying their ordinary direct images therefore produces (8), even if the original cotangent projections are not globally proper.

We also need to identify the operator on the left. With

KR=tRℋom(K,q2−1ωY),(9) K_R=\mathrm tR\mathcal Hom(K,q_2^{-1}\omega_Y), \qquad\text{(9)}

the dual-kernel theorem and parameter identity give

ΨΘZK(F)≃KR∘XF.(10) \Psi_{\Theta_ZK}(F)\simeq K_R\circ_XF. \qquad\text{(10)}

Here the constructibility of KK is essential. For completeness, relative duality commutes with this parameter identity with no added ZZ-shift. The ordinary coefficient dual of the diagonal factor is its constant sheaf tensored by the relative orientation complex of the diagonal, of degree −dim⁡Z-\dim Z. The right relative dual also tensors by the input factor ωZ\omega_Z, of degree dim⁡Z\dim Z. These cancel, together with their inverse orientation lines. External constructible duality and the tensor symmetry then identify the right dual of ΘZK\Theta_ZK with ΘZKR\Theta_ZK_R. This computation also proves its required constructibility from that of KK and of a smooth closed diagonal. Apply the dual-kernel theorem and then the parameter convolution identity to obtain (10).

The maps in (8) preserve the adjunction’s units. More precisely, the image of ηG:G→ΨΘZKΦΘZKG\eta_G:G\to\Psi_{\Theta_ZK}\Phi_{\Theta_ZK}G under the directional adjunction is the identity of ΦΘZKG\Phi_{\Theta_ZK}G. Here is the map check being used. Before specialization, represent a morphism by its diagonal Hom kernel. Its adjoint is obtained by inserting the kernel unit, then applying the given morphism inside the right operator. Applying the inverse construction evaluates against the kernel and its proper-support counit. In the fourfold comparison these are the same evaluation and counit, after the exceptional repeated-coordinate restriction. The inserted unit followed by that counit is the identity by the triangle identity. Specialization and Fourier transformation send this equality of actual kernel maps to equality of the directional maps. The orientation cancellations are the ones displayed in the preceding lesson; the remaining maps are the specified adjunction mates. Thus the identity here is checked before normal limits, not inferred from equality after ordinary cotangent recovery.

The kernel unit is invertible

Take Z=YZ=Y, G=kΔYG=k_{\Delta_Y}, and F=KF=K in (8). The parameter identity gives ΦΘYK(kΔY)=K\Phi_{\Theta_YK}(k_{\Delta_Y})=K, and (10) gives ΨΘYK(K)≃KR∘XK\Psi_{\Theta_YK}(K)\simeq K_R\circ_XK. The unit at the diagonal is therefore the actual kernel morphism

α:kΔY⟶KR∘XK(11) \alpha:k_{\Delta_Y}\longrightarrow K_R\circ_XK \qquad\text{(11)}

in the localization on ΩY×T*Y\Omega_Y\times T^*Y. By the map check above, (8) sends its microlocalized section to the identity section of KK.

One can also construct (11) directly. On X×Y1×Y2X\times Y_1\times Y_2, the operator’s Hom kernel is Rℋom(KXY1,KXY2⊗ωY1)R\mathcal Hom(K_{XY_1},K_{XY_2}\otimes\omega_{Y_1}). Let j(x,y)=(x,y,y)j(x,y)=(x,y,y). Exceptional restriction changes this kernel to Rℋom(K,K)R\mathcal Hom(K,K): the repeated-coordinate relative orientation cancels ωY1\omega_{Y_1}. The identity of KK thus gives a morphism from the diagonal-supported constant sheaf into the Hom kernel by closed-embedding adjunction. Push it ordinarily along XX and use the identification (10). This is (11). It is the unit because its ordinary adjoint is id⁡K\operatorname{id}_K. This direct construction retains the exceptional cancellation and specifies the same map used in (8).

Restrict (8) to

{(y,y;α,−α):(y;α)∈ΩY}⊂TΔY*(Y2).(12) \{(y,y;\alpha,-\alpha):(y;\alpha)\in\Omega_Y\} \subset T^*_{\Delta_Y}(Y^2). \qquad\text{(12)}

The right side is the self microlocal Hom of KK on Λ\Lambda. The left side is the microlocal Hom from kΔYk_{\Delta_Y} to the target of (11) on (12). Formula (7) identifies it with diagonal microlocalization. Under this identification, μΔYα\mu_{\Delta_Y}\alpha is exactly (3), transported by the graph homeomorphism. It is therefore an isomorphism.

The convolution microsupport estimate gives

SS⁡(KR∘XK)∩(ΩY×T*Y)⊂TΔY*(Y2).(13) \operatorname{SS}(K_R\circ_XK) \cap(\Omega_Y\times T^*Y) \subset T^*_{\Delta_Y}(Y^2). \qquad\text{(13)}

Indeed the twisted relation of KRK_R is the reciprocal graph, so its composition with the graph of KK is the identity relation. The same containment holds for kΔYk_{\Delta_Y}. The microsupport triangle bound puts the cone CC of (11) in this conormal on the selected region. Its diagonal microlocalization is zero on (12). The detection argument (6)–(7) makes CC invisible at every point of (12). At every other point in the selected region it is already invisible by (13). Hence (11) is an isomorphism in the full selected kernel localization.

Convolving this isomorphism with any localized input and using associativity shows that the unit G→ΨKΦKGG\to\Psi_K\Phi_KG is an isomorphism. Thus ΦK\Phi_K is fully faithful. Notice that a single objectwise test on ordinary stalks would not have established the kernel isomorphism (11); the conormal cone and the parameter variable are both used.

Full faithfulness of the other adjoint

The constructible microlocal duality input is

μhom⁡(A,B)≃a−1μhom⁡(DB,DA),(14) \mu\operatorname{hom}(A,B) \simeq a^{-1}\mu\operatorname{hom}(D B,D A), \qquad\text{(14)}

for cohomologically constructible A,BA,B. It is induced by external Hom exchange, bidual evaluation and exchanging the two diagonal factors. The exchange supplies the antipode. Applied to the same object in both arguments, it sends the identity to the dual identity; the bidual evaluation is the specified one.

Relative coefficient duality differs from Verdier duality by an invertible locally constant complex. Simultaneously tensoring both Hom inputs by such a complex cancels it. Factor transposition in (9) then changes the graph into its reciprocal. It follows from (14) that KRK_R, on Y×XY\times X, satisfies all three conditions (1)–(3) for χ−1\chi^{-1}. Its identity-induced morphism is invertible, with the dual identity normalization. It is cohomologically constructible, and its two selected microsupport containments are exactly the reciprocals of (2).

Apply the unit argument just proved to KRK_R. It shows that ΦKR=ΨK\Phi_{K_R}=\Psi_K is fully faithful. We have now shown that both functors in the adjoint pair (4) are fully faithful. To see explicitly that the counit is invertible, write L=ΦKL=\Phi_K, R=ΨKR=\Psi_K. The unit at RFRF is invertible, and the triangle identity gives R(ϵF)ηRF=1RFR(\epsilon_F)\eta_{RF}=1_{RF}. Therefore R(ϵF)R(\epsilon_F) is invertible. A fully faithful functor reflects isomorphisms: lift the inverse through its Hom bijection and use faithfulness to check the two composites. Hence ϵF:LRF→F\epsilon_F:LRF\to F is invertible too. This proves that (4) consists of inverse equivalences.

Finally take ZZ to be a point, G=G2G=G_2, and F=ΦKG1F=\Phi_KG_1 in (8). The now-invertible unit identifies ΨKΦKG1\Psi_K\Phi_KG_1 with G1G_1. The resulting natural isomorphism is (5). All comparison maps invert input denominators by the previously proved support/cone bounds. The isomorphism therefore holds for localized inputs as stated. ▫\square

The geometric word “contact”

If the graph in (1) is smooth Lagrangian, the two physical cotangent factors carry opposite signs because the input covector has been negated. Pulling the product symplectic form back to the graph says χ*dθX=dθY\chi^*d\theta_X=d\theta_Y, where θ\theta is the tautological one-form. Conicity makes χ\chi commute with positive fiber scaling. Its derivative carries the fiber Euler vector field EYE_Y to EXE_X. Since ιEdθ=θ\iota_Ed\theta=\theta, contraction of the symplectic identity gives χ*θX=θY\chi^*\theta_X=\theta_Y. Thus the sheaf equivalence lies above the homogeneous symplectic transformation with the exact input sign used throughout. The theorem itself used the graph homeomorphisms and the kernel identity condition; smooth geometric realization is an additional question.

Exercises with complete solutions

A graph with a doubled coefficient

Difficulty: Introductory.

Let kk be a field, f:Y→Xf:Y\to X a diffeomorphism, and K=(k⊕k)ΓfK=(k\oplus k)_{\Gamma_f}. Its relation is the graph of the cotangent lift and has proper projections. Which condition fails, and why is the transform not an equivalence?

Solution. The kernel is constructible. Along its conormal, self microlocal Hom has fiber End⁡k(k2)\operatorname{End}_k(k^2), in degree zero. The identity-induced map sends a scalar to the corresponding scalar matrix. This is not an isomorphism: its target has dimension four. Thus (3) fails. The transform sends GG to f*(G⊕G)f_*(G\oplus G). At a point, the map on endomorphisms of kk is k→M2(k)k\to M_2(k), so this functor is not fully faithful. Even the exact graph relation and perfect coefficients do not replace the identity condition.

An orientation line on the graph

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Let f:Y→Xf:Y\to X be a diffeomorphism and let LL be a locally free rank-one kk-local system on YY. For K=LΓf[s]K=L_{\Gamma_f}[s], determine the two inverse operators and verify (3) without choosing a global trivialization of LL.

Solution. The left operator is G↦f*(L⊗G)[s]G\mapsto f_*(L\otimes G)[s]. Its inverse and right adjoint is F↦L−1⊗f−1F[−s]F\mapsto L^{-1}\otimes f^{-1}F[-s]. All coefficients are locally perfect. For a nonzero coefficient ring, the relation is the same proper graph as for kΓfk_{\Gamma_f}. Over the zero ring the relation is empty, both categories are zero, and the inverse formulas and identity condition still hold. Locally trivialize LL. The submanifold formula reduces self microlocal Hom on the graph conormal to RHom⁡k(k[s],k[s])=kR\operatorname{Hom}_k(k[s],k[s])=k. Its identity is one. Changing trivialization multiplies source and target by inverse units, which cancel in self Hom; these local identity maps glue to exactly (3). Monodromy affects the operator but does not obstruct this identity condition. The kernel and its inverse shifts have opposite signs.

Why the parameter variable is needed

Difficulty: Advanced.

In the proof of (11), replace the choice Z=YZ=Y, G=kΔYG=k_{\Delta_Y} by a point parameter and one input sheaf GG. Explain what information the latter test yields and why it does not by itself prove a kernel inverse.

Solution. The point-parameter comparison describes the directional map G→ΨKΦKGG\to\Psi_K\Phi_KG through morphisms to ΦKG\Phi_KG. It concerns that particular input. It does not produce or detect a map on Y×YY\times Y, nor does it put its cone in the diagonal conormal. With Z=YZ=Y, the diagonal is the identity kernel and its transform is the original kernel KK. Its unit is the universal map (11); (13) confines the whole cone to a conormal, where (7) detects it. Associativity then gives invertibility of every input unit from one kernel isomorphism. A single chosen input may have zero directional data, so its unit can be invertible even for a kernel whose operator is not fully faithful.

Two fully faithful adjoints

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Let L⊣RL\dashv R be an adjoint pair of additive categories. Assume its unit is an isomorphism and RR is fully faithful. Prove that the counit is an isomorphism. Apply this to the two relative-dual steps in Theorem 1.

Solution. For any FF, the triangle identity is RϵF∘ηRF=1RFR\epsilon_F\circ\eta_{RF}=1_{RF}. Since ηRF\eta_{RF} is invertible, RϵF=(ηRF)−1R\epsilon_F=(\eta_{RF})^{-1} is invertible. Fullness lifts its inverse to a map F→LRFF\to LRF; faithfulness shows that the two composites with ϵF\epsilon_F are identities. Thus ϵF\epsilon_F is invertible. In Theorem 1 the first kernel-unit argument makes ΦK\Phi_K fully faithful, so its unit is invertible. Constructible microlocal duality transfers the identity condition to KRK_R, whose kernel-unit argument makes ΦKR=ΨK\Phi_{K_R}=\Psi_K fully faithful. Both hypotheses of the categorical calculation are now satisfied. No claim that the left and right relative dual kernels were identical was needed.
