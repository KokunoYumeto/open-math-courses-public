Involutive subsets of subanalytic isotropic sets

Involutivity says that Hamiltonian directions forced by vanishing secants remain tangent to a set. Isotropy puts an upper bound on dimension. When an involutive set lies inside a subanalytic isotropic set, these two requirements leave no room for a hidden lower-dimensional residue: the subset becomes subanalytic and Lagrangian, even though its subanalyticity was not assumed.

We work on P=T*XP=T^*X, where XX is a real analytic nn-manifold, Hausdorff and countable at infinity. Use α=∑iξidxi\alpha=\sum_i\xi_i dx_i and ω=dα=∑idξi∧dxi\omega=d\alpha=\sum_i d\xi_i\wedge dx_i. Positive conicity means invariance under positive fibre dilation. Isotropy for a subanalytic cotangent set means that its canonical one-form vanishes in the singular one-form sense developed in Isotropic cotangent transport and discrete critical values.

Kashiwara and Schapira, Microlocal Study of Sheaves, Astérisque 128 (1985), Definition 8.2.1 and Propositions 8.2.2–8.2.4, printed pp. 144–145, develop conic isotropy and recovery from selected smooth Lagrangian pieces. Proposition 8.2.4 treats a closed involutive set through a locally finite family of such pieces. Here we prove the needed Hamiltonian-flow and dimension arguments and recover a subset closed in its locally closed isotropic containing set. For bounded sheaf complexes over arbitrary commutative rings, microsupport involutivity supplies this geometric argument with an involutive closed set. That sheaf-theoretic proof uses the directional identity and an empty-cone contradiction; it does not depend on the subanalytic recovery theorem below.

Exact inputs and the scope of the argument

The flow argument uses finite-dimensional coordinate calculus, completeness of the continuous-path space, compactness, integration of continuous functions, and the inverse function theorem. Its existence, uniqueness, differentiable dependence and closed-set invariance steps are proved below. No theorem about microsupport is an input: involutivity is an explicit hypothesis on the subset.

For the final two subanalytic arguments, the intrinsic regular-locus theorem gives a relatively open subanalytic regular locus, and the dimension-drop argument proves that its complement has strictly smaller dimension. The local set and component calculus proves that closures and finite Boolean operations preserve subanalyticity and that connected components are subanalytic and form an ambient locally finite family. Any subfamily is still locally finite, so its union is subanalytic by the same local calculus. The local curve-selection proof supplies the analytic approaches used in the singular one-form calculus.

Given those inputs, the companion cotangent reading proves that canonical-form vanishing restricts to subanalytic subsets and passes to closures, and that positive-conic canonical-form isotropy gives symplectic isotropy. The dimension bound follows directly: an isotropic tangent space WW satisfies W⊂WωW\subset W^\omega, while nondegeneracy gives dim⁡Wω=2n−dim⁡W\dim W^\omega=2n-\dim W. Hence dim⁡W≤n\dim W\le n; taking the supremum over the regular locus gives the subanalytic dimension bound. These are the exact geometric consequences used below.

Secants and the Hamiltonian sign

For a locally closed subset S⊂PS\subset P, the point and two-set normal cones are

Cp(S)={limksk−phk:sk∈S,sk→p,hk↓0},Cp(S,S)={limksk−tkhk:sk,tk∈S,sk,tk→p,hk↓0}.(1) C_p(S)=\left\{\lim_k\frac{s_k-p}{h_k}:s_k\in S,\ s_k\to p,\ h_k\downarrow0\right\}, \qquad C_p(S,S)=\left\{\lim_k\frac{s_k-t_k}{h_k}:s_k,t_k\in S,\ s_k,t_k\to p,\ h_k\downarrow0\right\}. \qquad\text{(1)}

Only convergent quotients enter. Coordinates identify these limits with tangent vectors; the two-endpoint coordinate comparison below makes the definitions invariant. The first set in the ordered pair contributes with a plus sign.

Define the Hamiltonian isomorphism H:T*P→TPH:T^*P\to TP by

H(∑iaidxi+bidξi)=∑ibi∂xi−ai∂ξi,ιHθω=−θ.(2) H\left(\sum_i a_i dx_i+b_i d\xi_i\right) =\sum_i b_i\partial_{x_i}-a_i\partial_{\xi_i}, \qquad \iota_{H\theta}\omega=-\theta. \qquad\text{(2)}

The subset SS is involutive at p∈Sp\in S if

Cp(S,S)⊂ker⁡θ⟹−Hθ∈Cp(S)for every θ∈Tp*P.(3) C_p(S,S)\subset\ker\theta \quad\Longrightarrow\quad -H\theta\in C_p(S) \quad\text{for every }\theta\in T_p^*P. \qquad\text{(3)}

It is involutive if this holds at all its points. Applying (3) to both θ\theta and −θ-\theta also puts HθH\theta in Cp(S)C_p(S). This does not change the sign convention in (2).

For a smooth submanifold SS, both cones in (1) equal TpST_pS. Since HH takes the annihilator of TpST_pS to its symplectic orthogonal, (3) is equivalent to

(TpS)ω⊂TpS.(4) (T_pS)^\omega\subset T_pS. \qquad\text{(4)}

Thus a smooth involutive manifold is coisotropic. A smooth isotropic manifold satisfies the reverse inclusion. Both together imply equality and dimension nn.

The precise flow prerequisite

If SS is involutive and closed in an open subset U⊂PU\subset P, and φ∈C2(U;ℝ)\varphi\in C^2(U;\mathbb R) vanishes on SS, then every maximal Hamiltonian trajectory through a point of SS remains in SS as long as it exists in UU. Its equations are

ẋi=∂ξiφ,ξ̇i=−∂xiφ.(5) \dot x_i=\partial_{\xi_i}\varphi,\qquad \dot\xi_i=-\partial_{x_i}\varphi. \qquad\text{(5)}

We now prove this statement, including both time directions. There is no completeness or global compactness hypothesis on the field or the set. A critical point of φ\varphi gives a constant trajectory.

Differentiating two moving endpoints

Work in a convex coordinate ball. For a C1C^1 map ff, two endpoints sj,tj→ps_j,t_j\to p satisfy

f(sj)−f(tj)=dfp(sj−tj)+rj,|rj|≤supz∈[sj,tj]∥dfz−dfp∥|sj−tj|.(F1) \begin{aligned} f(s_j)-f(t_j)&=df_p(s_j-t_j)+r_j,\\ |r_j|&\le \sup_{z\in[s_j,t_j]}\|df_z-df_p\|\,|s_j-t_j|. \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(F1)}

If (sj−tj)/hj(s_j-t_j)/h_j converges, dividing the remainder by hjh_j makes it tend to zero. Applying this to a coordinate change and its inverse proves that both cones in (1) transform by its derivative. The cones are also unchanged by restricting to an ambient open neighborhood of pp. Thus the coordinate computations below prove intrinsic assertions.

If f=φf=\varphi vanishes on SS, the left side of (F1) is zero for endpoints in SS, so dφpd\varphi_p annihilates Cp(S,S)C_p(S,S). The estimate uses the distance between the endpoints; separate Taylor remainders at pp would not control division by hjh_j. Testing (3) with both signs of dφpd\varphi_p puts both signs of the Hamiltonian vector in Cp(S)C_p(S).

A closed set containing its tangent field

Suppose SS is closed in an open manifold UU, and VV is a C1C^1 field on UU with V(q)∈Cq(S)V(q)\in C_q(S) at every q∈Sq\in S. We prove that a trajectory starting in SS stays there for all its nonnegative existence times.

A closed involutive subset of a smooth isotropic manifold

Let N⊂PN\subset P be a smooth locally closed isotropic submanifold. Let S⊂NS\subset N be a subset closed in NN, and suppose SS is involutive as a locally closed subset of PP. Then SS is open in NN. At every point of nonempty SS, the local dimension of NN and SS is nn.

Here isotropic for NN means ω|TN=0\omega|_{TN}=0. The assertion holds in this smooth symplectic form, and hence also for the regular locus of a conic isotropic cotangent set.

Proof. Fix p∈Sp\in S. Since S⊂NS\subset N, straightening NN gives

Cp(S,S)⊂TpN,Cp(S)⊂TpN.(6) C_p(S,S)\subset T_pN,\qquad C_p(S)\subset T_pN. \qquad\text{(6)}

For every covector θ\theta annihilating TpNT_pN, equations (3) and (6) imply Hθ∈TpNH\theta\in T_pN. Therefore

(TpN)ω⊂TpN.(7) (T_pN)^\omega\subset T_pN. \qquad\text{(7)}

Isotropy gives TpN⊂(TpN)ωT_pN\subset(T_pN)^\omega. Thus equality holds, and dim⁡TpN=n\dim T_pN=n. Shrink to a smooth local piece of NN of this dimension. It is itself Lagrangian; no enlargement of NN is needed.

Choose an open neighborhood UU of pp where NN is closed and is cut out by smooth independent functions φ1,…,φn\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_n. Since SS is closed in NN, it is closed in this UU. Each φi\varphi_i vanishes on SS, so its local Hamiltonian flow preserves SS.

Along NN, the differentials dφid\varphi_i form a basis of its conormal space. Equation (2) and the Lagrangian equality take them to a basis of TNTN. Thus the Hamiltonian fields also preserve NN. For their local flows Φiti\Phi_i^{t_i}, consider

F(t1,…,tn)=Φntn∘⋯∘Φ1t1(p).(8) F(t_1,\ldots,t_n) =\Phi_n^{t_n}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_1^{t_1}(p). \qquad\text{(8)}

For sufficiently small times all stages remain in UU, and flow invariance puts their endpoints in SS. The differential at zero sends the ii-th coordinate vector to Hφi(p)H_{\varphi_i}(p), so it is an isomorphism to TpNT_pN. The inverse function theorem makes the image of a small time neighborhood a neighborhood of pp in NN. It lies in SS, proving openness. The dimension assertion follows there. If n=0n=0, the same conclusion follows from the zero-dimensional local neighborhood. ▫\square

The dimension assertion is pointwise where SS is nonempty. An empty subset is always open and has no point at which to assert dimension nn. Also, the dimension in this cotangent setting is the dimension of the base XX, half the symplectic ambient dimension 2n2n.

No involutive subset can hide below half dimension

Let A⊂T*XA\subset T^*X be a locally closed positive-conic subanalytic isotropic set with

dim⁡A<n.(9) \dim A<n. \qquad\text{(9)}

If V⊂AV\subset A is locally closed in T*XT^*X and involutive, then V=∅V=\varnothing. No subanalyticity, conicity, or global closedness in AA is assumed for VV.

Proof. Induct on dim⁡A\dim A. The empty containing set is immediate. If p∈V∩Aregp\in V\cap A_{\mathrm{reg}}, choose a sufficiently small ambient neighborhood where AA is a closed analytic submanifold and where VV is closed. This is possible by regularity and local closedness. The regular piece of AA is symplectically isotropic: positive conicity and canonical-form vanishing imply ω|TAreg=0\omega|_{TA_{\mathrm{reg}}}=0, as proved by the Euler-field argument in the cotangent reading. The smooth result would force its dimension to be nn, contradicting (9). Thus

V∩Areg=∅.(10) V\cap A_{\mathrm{reg}}=\varnothing. \qquad\text{(10)}

Set A1=A\AregA_1=A\setminus A_{\mathrm{reg}}. This is subanalytic, closed in AA, and hence locally closed. It is positive-conic because regularity is preserved by each dilation diffeomorphism. Canonical-form vanishing passes to this subanalytic subset, so it is isotropic. Its dimension is strictly smaller by the dimension-drop argument (2). We have V⊂A1V\subset A_1, with the same local closedness and involutivity as before. Induction proves that it is empty. ▫\square

The argument does not assume that VV has regular points. It uses regularity only on the subanalytic containing set and descends through its singular residues.

Subanalyticity forced by an isotropic containing set

Let Λ⊂Λ0⊂T*X\Lambda\subset\Lambda_0\subset T^*X be locally closed positive-conic subsets. Assume:

  1. Λ0\Lambda_0 is subanalytic and isotropic;
  2. Λ\Lambda is closed in Λ0\Lambda_0;
  3. Λ\Lambda is involutive.

Then Λ\Lambda is subanalytic and Lagrangian, meaning isotropic and involutive. The theorem does not assume Λ\Lambda subanalytic.

Proof. Put

R=(Λ0)reg,B=Λ∩R.(11) R=(\Lambda_0)_{\mathrm{reg}},\qquad B=\Lambda\cap R. \qquad\text{(11)}

The smooth result shows that BB is open in RR. In applying it locally, RR agrees with Λ0\Lambda_0, so BB is a local open restriction of the involutive set Λ\Lambda; involutivity is unchanged. Relative closedness of Λ\Lambda also makes BB closed in RR. Consequently BB is a union of connected components of RR.

The components of a subanalytic set are subanalytic and ambient locally finite. Any subfamily is still locally finite; its union is subanalytic by the local finite-union calculus. Thus BB is subanalytic. Relative closedness gives

B¯∩Λ0⊂Λ.(12) \overline B\cap\Lambda_0\subset\Lambda. \qquad\text{(12)}

We prove the reverse inclusion. Consider the possible leftover

V=Λ\B¯.(13) V=\Lambda\setminus\overline B. \qquad\text{(13)}

It is an open restriction of the locally closed involutive set Λ\Lambda, so it is locally closed and involutive. Equation (11) gives

V⊂Λ0\R.(14) V\subset\Lambda_0\setminus R. \qquad\text{(14)}

The dimension argument above gives dim⁡Λ0≤n\dim\Lambda_0\le n. If Λ0≠∅\Lambda_0\ne\varnothing, its singular residue is subanalytic, positive-conic and isotropic, and has dimension strictly less than dim⁡Λ0\dim\Lambda_0, hence strictly less than nn. The previous result makes VV empty. The empty Λ0\Lambda_0 case is immediate. Therefore

Λ=B¯∩Λ0.(15) \Lambda=\overline B\cap\Lambda_0. \qquad\text{(15)}

Closure and intersection preserve subanalyticity, so Λ\Lambda is subanalytic. Canonical-form vanishing on Λ0\Lambda_0 passes to its subanalytic subset Λ\Lambda, proving isotropy. Involutivity was assumed. These are exactly the stated Lagrangian conditions. ▫\square

Formula (15) identifies what has been recovered: the selected regular components, together with precisely their limits that lie in the given locally closed containing set. Limits outside Λ0\Lambda_0 are not inserted. A globally closed containing set is not required.

Exercises with complete solutions

A truncated zero section loses a Hamiltonian direction

Difficulty: Introductory.

In T*ℝT^*\mathbb R, let S={(x;0):x≥0}S=\{(x;0):x\ge0\}. Compute both cones at p=(0;0)p=(0;0) and test (3).

Solution. The point cone is Cp(S)={a∂x:a≥0}C_p(S)=\{a\partial_x:a\ge0\}. The two-set cone is the full line ℝ∂x\mathbb R\partial_x: differences of two nonnegative base points can have either sign. Take θ=dξ\theta=d\xi, which annihilates this line. Equation (2) gives Hθ=∂xH\theta=\partial_x, so −Hθ=−∂x∉Cp(S)-H\theta=-\partial_x\notin C_p(S). Hence SS is not involutive at its endpoint. It is closed in the isotropic zero section, but the failed direction prevents application of the openness theorem.

The smooth dimension bound needs no normal-form enlargement

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Let SS be involutive and contained in a smooth isotropic mm-manifold N⊂T*XN\subset T^*X. Show at any p∈Sp\in S that m=nm=n, using only cones and symplectic linear algebra. Does this argument need relative closedness?

Solution. Inclusion gives both cones in (6). Every θ∈(TpN)⟂\theta\in(T_pN)^\perp annihilates the two-set cone, so involutivity puts HθH\theta in TpNT_pN. This pointwise argument needs no relative closedness. Closedness in a local ambient neighborhood is needed for the Hamiltonian-flow invariance step that subsequently proves openness of SS.

Which components of a discrete conormal may be retained?

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Let Λ0=Tℤ*ℝ\Lambda_0=T_{\mathbb Z}^*\mathbb R. For an arbitrary subset J⊂ℤJ\subset\mathbb Z, set Λ=⋃m∈JTm*ℝ\Lambda=\bigcup_{m\in J}T_m^*\mathbb R. Verify the theorem’s hypotheses and its conclusion. What changes if one retains only a half-fibre at some integer?

Solution. The family of integer fibres is ambient locally finite. Each fibre is a smooth closed conormal line, with zero canonical form and Lagrangian tangent space; hence it is involutive. Near each point of Λ\Lambda, no other integer fibre occurs, so Λ\Lambda is locally closed and involutive. Any subfamily is closed in the full locally finite union Λ0\Lambda_0, and both sets are positive-conic. The theorem applies; directly, the same local finiteness makes Λ\Lambda subanalytic and Lagrangian. No finiteness condition on JJ is required.

For the half-fibre {(m;ξ):ξ≥0}\{(m;\xi):\xi\ge0\}, at ξ=0\xi=0 the point cone is the positive ∂ξ\partial_\xi ray and the two-set cone is its full line. A base covector dxdx annihilates that line, while H(dx)=−∂ξH(dx)=-\partial_\xi is absent from the point cone. Applying (3) also to −dx-dx forces that missing sign. Thus the retained half-fibre is not involutive at zero.

A singular containing set cannot support an isolated involutive point

Solution. The containing crossing is closed, subanalytic and positive-conic. At the singleton both cones are {0}\{0\}. Every cotangent covector annihilates the two-set cone, but any nonzero θ\theta has Hθ≠0H\theta\ne0, which cannot belong to the point cone. The resulting Lagrangian notion

For a conic subanalytic cotangent set, Lagrangian means canonical-form isotropy together with the singular involutivity test (3). At its regular points this is the usual dimension-nn Lagrangian condition. The theorem shows how a subset initially lacking subanalytic regularity acquires it from a subanalytic isotropic containing set, while retaining the exact local closedness and Hamiltonian information.
