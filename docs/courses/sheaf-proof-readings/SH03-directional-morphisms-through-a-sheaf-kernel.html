Sheaf proof readings

Directional morphisms through a sheaf kernel

A kernel transform has an ordinary adjunction. It also has a comparison at each cotangent direction. The latter is an isomorphism of sheaves on a cotangent region, so it contains more information than an adjunction between global morphism groups. We will construct this comparison in both directions and prove its invertibility by cutting off the input over a compact part of the chosen cotangent region.

Use Kernels that preserve chosen cotangent directions and Adjoints of localized sheaf kernels. The definition, support bound and natural transformations for microlocal Hom come from Local morphisms in cotangent directions. We use its underlying microlocal direct-image comparison and the inverse-image comparison for a map whose restriction to the centers is a submersion. Their precise hypotheses are recalled below. The proofs remain relative to those operation and microlocalization prerequisites.

Kashiwara and Schapira’s Microlocal Study of Sheaves, Theorem 6.3.9 and its proof, pp. 115–117, is the human antecedent for the common diagonal-Hom construction: both sides are obtained from one fourfold kernel using direct and inverse microlocalization comparisons. That theorem is stated for a contact correspondence satisfying the constructibility and microlocal endomorphism assumptions of Theorem 6.3.4. The one-sided admissibility theorem proved here has different scope. Its extra work is the two separate no-escape checks, the orientation calculation for each repeated coordinate, and compact replacement over each output neighborhood. The source theorem alone would not justify discarding its additional assumptions.

A sheaf of directional morphisms

Let kk be a commutative unital ring of finite global dimension. Manifolds are smooth, Hausdorff, finite dimensional and countable at infinity. All input complexes are globally bounded complexes of arbitrary sheaves of kk-modules. We impose no constructibility, perfectness or Noetherian condition.

For the projections r1,r2:M×M→Mr_1,r_2:M\times M\to M, set

μhom⁡M(A,B)=μΔMRℋom(r2−1A,r1!B).(1) \mu\operatorname{hom}_M(A,B) =\mu_{\Delta_M}R\mathcal Hom(r_2^{-1}A,r_1^!B). \qquad\text{(1)}

Here μΔM\mu_{\Delta_M} means normal specialization followed by Fourier–Sato transformation with kernel ⟨v,ξ⟩≤0\langle v,\xi\rangle\leq0. Identify the diagonal conormal with T*MT^*M by (m,m;ξ,−ξ)↦(m;ξ)(m,m;\xi,-\xi)\mapsto(m;\xi). Thus the retained covector belongs to the target coordinate in (1).

The prerequisite gives boundedness, functoriality in both arguments, the support inclusion and ordinary recovery

supp⁡μhom⁡M(A,B)⊂SS⁡(A)∩SS⁡(B),Rπ*μhom⁡M(A,B)≃Rℋom(A,B),(2) \begin{split} \operatorname{supp}\mu\operatorname{hom}_M(A,B) &\subset\operatorname{SS}(A)\cap\operatorname{SS}(B),\\ R\pi_*\mu\operatorname{hom}_M(A,B) &\simeq R\mathcal Hom(A,B), \end{split} \qquad\text{(2)}

where support is closed support and π:T*M→M\pi:T^*M\to M. Restricting to an arbitrary open cotangent region is permitted; the region need not be conic. Formula (2) over the whole bundle does not identify morphisms in every localized category with sections over every open region.

Let P=X×YP=X\times Y, with projections q1,q2q_1,q_2. For a bounded kernel KK on PP, write

ΦKG=Rq1!(K⊗Lq2−1G),ΨKF=Rq2*Rℋom(K,q1!F),Q(F,G)=Rℋom(q2−1G,q1!F).(3) \begin{split} \Phi_KG&=Rq_{1!}(K\otimes^Lq_2^{-1}G),\\ \Psi_KF&=Rq_{2*}R\mathcal Hom(K,q_1^!F),\\ Q(F,G)&=R\mathcal Hom(q_2^{-1}G,q_1^!F). \end{split} \qquad\text{(3)}

Boundedness of these complexes is supplied by the manifold bounds used in the adjoint lesson. On T*PT^*P, use

p1(x,y;ξ,η)=(x;ξ),p2a(x,y;ξ,η)=(y;−η).(4) p_1(x,y;\xi,\eta)=(x;\xi),\qquad p_2^a(x,y;\xi,\eta)=(y;-\eta). \qquad\text{(4)}

The twisted kernel relation is

CK={((x;ξ),(y;α)):(x,y;ξ,−α)∈SS⁡(K)}.(5) C_K=\{((x;\xi),(y;\alpha)): (x,y;\xi,-\alpha)\in\operatorname{SS}(K)\}. \qquad\text{(5)}

Recall the two separate conditions. Forward admissibility over ΩX\Omega_X says that the part of CKC_K with first coordinate in ΩX\Omega_X is contained in ΩX×ΩY\Omega_X\times\Omega_Y, and its projection to ΩX\Omega_X is proper. Reverse admissibility over ΩY\Omega_Y says that the part with second coordinate in ΩY\Omega_Y is contained in ΩX×ΩY\Omega_X\times\Omega_Y, and its projection to ΩY\Omega_Y is proper. Both properness statements control the entire forgotten cotangent coordinate, including its base point.

Theorem 1. There are natural comparisons, invertible under the indicated one-sided conditions:

μhom⁡X(ΦKG,F)|ΩX→∼(Rp1*μhom⁡P(K,Q(F,G)))|ΩXif K is forward admissible,μhom⁡Y(G,ΨKF)|ΩY→∼(R(p2a)*μhom⁡P(K,Q(F,G)))|ΩYif K is reverse admissible.(6) \begin{split} \mu\operatorname{hom}_X(\Phi_KG,F)|_{\Omega_X} &\xrightarrow{\sim} (Rp_{1*}\mu\operatorname{hom}_P(K,Q(F,G)))|_{\Omega_X} &&\text{if }K\text{ is forward admissible},\\ \mu\operatorname{hom}_Y(G,\Psi_KF)|_{\Omega_Y} &\xrightarrow{\sim} (R(p_2^a)_*\mu\operatorname{hom}_P(K,Q(F,G)))|_{\Omega_Y} &&\text{if }K\text{ is reverse admissible}. \end{split} \qquad\text{(6)}

In the first line, GG may be taken in the localized category over ΩY\Omega_Y; FF may be taken over ΩX\Omega_X. The same is true in the second line. The theorem asserts a comparison of cotangent sheaves, with the actual ordinary direct images and antipode displayed. Its proof uses neither duality of arbitrary sheaves nor a compact-support tensor description of their internal Hom.

The two microlocal comparison inputs

We use the following exact parts of the microlocalization calculus.

For inverse image, the comparison is traced to Microlocal Study of Sheaves, Theorem 5.4.1, pp. 85–89. Remark 5.4.3, p. 90, removes its first and third conditions when the map of centers is a submersion, leaving the local noncharacteristic condition. The sequence criterion for that condition is Proposition 1.2.6(ii), p. 19, together with Definition 5.3.1, p. 83. These locators explain why boundedness of the entire ambient covector is the decisive check below. The direct-image antecedent is Proposition 2.3.4, p. 48; its stated isomorphism case is transverse and proper on support. The ordinary direct comparison and the clean-map normal-support formulation used here are supplied by the linked microlocalization prerequisite. In the actual applications below the maps are product projections with transverse inverse centers, so the proof verifies this more concrete geometry as well.

First, suppose h:(A,N)→(B,M)h:(A,N)\to(B,M) is a map of manifolds with centers, N=h−1MN=h^{-1}M, and h|Nh|_N is a submersion. On an open part of the conormal of NN, assume the local noncharacteristic condition at infinity for a complex EE on BB: there is no sequence

an→a0,(bn;βn)∈SS⁡(E),bn→h(a0),dhan,tβn→γ0,|bn−h(an)||βn|→0,|βn|→∞,(7) a_n\to a_0,\quad (b_n;\beta_n)\in\operatorname{SS}(E), \quad b_n\to h(a_0),\quad dh_{a_n}^{,t}\beta_n\to\gamma_0, \quad |b_n-h(a_n)|\,|\beta_n|\to0, \quad |\beta_n|\to\infty, \qquad\text{(7)}

with (a0;γ0)(a_0;\gamma_0) in that open conormal part. The exceptional inverse microlocal comparison is then invertible there. In our applications the map of centers is the identity. Its cotangent base change is therefore the identity as well, so no exceptional orientation factor remains on that side of the comparison. This is the submersive-centers case of the inverse theorem; a check of the ordinary kernel of dhtdh^t alone would not suffice.

Second, for a clean map of pairs h:(A,N)→(B,M)h:(A,N)\to(B,M) with N=h−1MN=h^{-1}M, the ordinary direct microlocal comparison is invertible if hh is proper on the closed support of EE. Clean means that the normal derivative embeds the normal bundle of NN into the pulled-back normal bundle of MM. The prerequisite’s normal-cone properness lemma then supplies the additional properness on the normal support. The support and inverse-center conditions are also needed; this statement does not discard them for an arbitrary proper map.

All the comparisons are the ones formed from specialization, Fourier transformation, adjunction units and counits, and relative trace. We use their specified maps, not merely isomorphisms between their endpoint objects.

One fourfold kernel, with every orientation retained

Put

B=X1×Y2×X3×Y4,N=ΔP⊂B. B=X_1\times Y_2\times X_3\times Y_4, \qquad N=\Delta_P\subset B.

Subscripts indicate coordinates rather than copies of the coefficient ring. Let

H=Rℋom(K34⊗LG2,F1⊗ωY2⊗ωX3⊗ωY4).(8) H=R\mathcal Hom\left(K_{34}\otimes^LG_2, F_1\otimes\omega_{Y_2}\otimes\omega_{X_3}\otimes\omega_{Y_4}\right). \qquad\text{(8)}

Inverse images along the displayed projections are implicit. Each ωM=or⁡M[dim⁡M]\omega_M=\operatorname{or}_M[\dim M] is retained in its displayed order; every permutation uses the Koszul tensor symmetry.

By tensor–Hom adjunction, (8) is the defining kernel of μhom⁡P(K,Q(F,G))\mu\operatorname{hom}_P(K,Q(F,G)): applying the target projection’s exceptional inverse image on P×PP\times P adds ωX3⊗ωY4\omega_{X_3}\otimes\omega_{Y_4}, while q1!Fq_1^!F inside QQ already adds ωY2\omega_{Y_2}. Hence

μNH=μhom⁡P(K,Q(F,G)).(9) \mu_NH=\mu\operatorname{hom}_P(K,Q(F,G)). \qquad\text{(9)}

This is the external estimate of Kashiwara–Schapira, Proposition 4.2.2, pp. 64–65, also stated by Schapira as Theorem 2.8. Its proof treats each independent coordinate by a local support test and an ordinary/compact-support Hom adjunction. It does not replace an arbitrary sheaf by the dual of a dual. Applied in the independent coordinates of the present fourfold product, it yields the following bound.

The external Hom estimate, applied twice, gives

SS⁡(H)⊂SS⁡(F)1×SS⁡(G)2a×SS⁡(K)34a.(10) \operatorname{SS}(H)\subset \operatorname{SS}(F)_1\times\operatorname{SS}(G)^a_2 \times\operatorname{SS}(K)^a_{34}. \qquad\text{(10)}

To apply it to an ordinary inverse-image factor, insert and cancel its invertible projection orientation. Such factors do not change microsupport. The coordinates in (10) are independent, so this is the external Hom bound, without a constructibility-dependent dual–tensor substitution.

The forward comparison

Define

AX=X1×X3×Y,jX(x1,x3,y)=(x1,y,x3,y),uX(x1,x3,y)=(x1,x3).(11) A_X=X_1\times X_3\times Y, \quad j_X(x_1,x_3,y)=(x_1,y,x_3,y), \quad u_X(x_1,x_3,y)=(x_1,x_3). \qquad\text{(11)}

The inverse image of NN under jXj_X is MX=ΔX×YM_X=\Delta_X\times Y. The map of centers is the identity of X×YX\times Y. Under the first-covector identification, its conormal correspondence projects (x,y;ξ,η)↦(x,y;ξ)(x,y;\xi,\eta)\mapsto(x,y;\xi), forgetting η\eta.

No escape for jXj_X. Consider a sequence as in (7), whose pulled-back limit is (x0,x0,y0;ξ0,−ξ0,0)(x_0,x_0,y_0;\xi_0,-\xi_0,0), with (x0;ξ0)∈ΩX(x_0;\xi_0)\in\Omega_X. Write a covector of (10) as (ξ1,η2,ξ3,η4)(\xi_1,\eta_2,\xi_3,\eta_4). Its pullback is (ξ1,ξ3,η2+η4)(\xi_1,\xi_3,\eta_2+\eta_4). Therefore (x3;−ξ3)→(x0;ξ0)(x_3;-\xi_3)\to(x_0;\xi_0), and (x3,y4;−ξ3,−η4)∈SS⁡(K)(x_3,y_4;-\xi_3,-\eta_4)\in\operatorname{SS}(K).

Choose a compact neighborhood of (x0;ξ0)(x_0;\xi_0) inside ΩX\Omega_X. Forward properness bounds η4\eta_4 for all sufficiently late terms. The convergence of η2+η4\eta_2+\eta_4 then bounds η2\eta_2; the other two components already converge. Thus the whole ambient covector is bounded, contradicting (7). This works for every y0y_0 and for every relevant conormal covector. It uses actual nearby points, so the weighted base error in (7) causes no omitted escape.

The submersive-centers inverse comparison consequently gives

μMXjX!H≃RrX*μNHover the part projecting to ΩX.(12) \mu_{M_X}j_X^!H\simeq Rr_{X*}\mu_NH \quad\text{over the part projecting to }\Omega_X. \qquad\text{(12)}

Here rXr_X is the conormal projection just described. To compute the left side, use the exact internal Hom identity j!Rℋom(A,E)=Rℋom(j−1A,j!E)j^!R\mathcal Hom(A,E)=R\mathcal Hom(j^{-1}A,j^!E). The target of (8) is locally constant in the repeated YY-normal coordinate except for its invertible orientation factors. Exceptional restriction cancels ωY4\omega_{Y_4}, leaving

jX!H≃Rℋom(K3Y⊗LGY,F1⊗ωX3⊗ωY).(13) j_X^!H\simeq R\mathcal Hom(K_{3Y}\otimes^LG_Y, F_1\otimes\omega_{X_3}\otimes\omega_Y). \qquad\text{(13)}

There is no leftover [dim⁡Y][\dim Y] shift: it has been cancelled by the embedding’s relative dualizing complex. Internal Hom adjunction for proper-support image now gives

RuX*jX!H≃Rℋom(r2−1ΦKG,r1!F)on X×X.(14) Ru_{X*}j_X^!H\simeq R\mathcal Hom(r_2^{-1}\Phi_KG,r_1^!F) \quad\text{on }X\times X. \qquad\text{(14)}

Indeed the complex before integration is K3Y⊗GYK_{3Y}\otimes G_Y; its proper-support image along uXu_X is r2−1ΦKGr_2^{-1}\Phi_KG by proper-support product base change. The target in (13) equals uX!r1!Fu_X^!r_1^!F. Applying Ru*Rℋom(A,u!E)=Rℋom(Ru!A,E)Ru_*R\mathcal Hom(A,u^!E)=R\mathcal Hom(Ru_!A,E) proves (14).

Apply the ordinary direct microlocal comparison to uXu_X, then (12). This constructs the first map of (6). If GG has compact closed support, it is an isomorphism: the support of (13) is contained in X2×supp⁡GX^2\times\operatorname{supp}G, so uXu_X is proper there. It is a product projection, transverse to ΔX\Delta_X, with inverse center MXM_X. Its normal derivative is the identity in the XX-difference coordinate. Thus the exact clean normal-support criterion applies. Finally uXu_X after rXr_X is p1p_1; composition of ordinary direct images gives the displayed target of (6).

The reverse comparison

For the second map, retain the same B,N,HB,N,H, and instead put

AY=Y2×Y4×X,jY(y2,y4,x)=(x,y2,x,y4),uY(y2,y4,x)=(y2,y4).(15) A_Y=Y_2\times Y_4\times X, \quad j_Y(y_2,y_4,x)=(x,y_2,x,y_4), \quad u_Y(y_2,y_4,x)=(y_2,y_4). \qquad\text{(15)}

Now the inverse center is MY=ΔY×XM_Y=\Delta_Y\times X, again mapping identically onto NN. Its conormal projection rYr_Y retains the Y2Y_2-covector η\eta and forgets the XX-covector.

No escape for jYj_Y. A pulled-back covector of (10) is (η2,η4,ξ1+ξ3)(\eta_2,\eta_4,\xi_1+\xi_3). Suppose it converges to (η0,−η0,0)(\eta_0,-\eta_0,0), with (y0;−η0)∈ΩY(y_0;-\eta_0)\in\Omega_Y. The kernel’s twisted input coordinate is (y4;η4)(y_4;\eta_4), which tends to (y0;−η0)(y_0;-\eta_0). Reverse properness therefore bounds its other coordinate (x3;−ξ3)(x_3;-\xi_3). The convergence of ξ1+ξ3\xi_1+\xi_3 bounds ξ1\xi_1. Both YY-components already converge. Once again every ambient component is bounded, excluding (7). Consequently

μMYjY!H≃RrY*μNHover the part with (y;−η)∈ΩY.(16) \mu_{M_Y}j_Y^!H\simeq Rr_{Y*}\mu_NH \quad\text{over the part with }(y;-\eta)\in\Omega_Y. \qquad\text{(16)}

Exceptional restriction now cancels ωX3\omega_{X_3}. Its precise result is

jY!H≃Rℋom(KXY4⊗LGY2,FX⊗ωY2⊗ωY4).(17) j_Y^!H\simeq R\mathcal Hom(K_{XY_4}\otimes^LG_{Y_2}, F_X\otimes\omega_{Y_2}\otimes\omega_{Y_4}). \qquad\text{(17)}

Use tensor–Hom adjunction to put GY2G_{Y_2} in the outer Hom. Ordinary direct image commutes with this outer Hom by internal Hom adjunction, since that input is pulled back from Y2Y^2. Smooth product base change for the independent Y2Y_2-coordinate, followed by the projection formula for its invertible orientation complex, gives

RuY*jY!H≃Rℋom(s1−1G,s2!ΨKF)on Y2×Y4.(18) Ru_{Y*}j_Y^!H\simeq R\mathcal Hom(s_1^{-1}G,s_2^!\Psi_KF) \quad\text{on }Y_2\times Y_4. \qquad\text{(18)}

In detail, integrating the inner Hom in (17) over XX is the pullback from Y4Y_4 of Rq2*Rℋom(K,FX⊗ωY4)=ΨKFRq_{2*}R\mathcal Hom(K,F_X\otimes\omega_{Y_4})=\Psi_KF. The remaining ωY2\omega_{Y_2} is exactly the orientation of the fiber of s2s_2. This argument uses ordinary product base change for a submersion with an independent coordinate, not unrestricted nonproper base change.

The kernel in (18) has its source in the first YY-coordinate. Exchanging the two factors turns it into the kernel in (1); the exchange sends the diagonal conormal (η,−η)(\eta,-\eta) to (−η,η)(-\eta,\eta). Its microlocalization is therefore aY−1μhom⁡Y(G,ΨKF)a_Y^{-1}\mu\operatorname{hom}_Y(G,\Psi_KF).

The ordinary direct comparison for uYu_Y, followed by (16) and this factor exchange, constructs the second map of (6). If FF has compact closed support, (17) is supported in Y2×supp⁡FY^2\times\operatorname{supp}F. The same clean product-projection argument as before makes the direct comparison invertible. The final projection is aY∘p2=p2aa_Y\circ p_2=p_2^a, proving the second formula with its sign. No constructibility-dependent bidual identification was used in this argument.

Compact replacement proves the general case

This is the point where the present argument goes beyond the contact-graph proof in Theorem 6.3.9. We first isolate a compact set of forgotten base points from cotangent properness. A compactly supported replacement agrees with the input near that set, and both sides of the actual natural comparison kill its cone over the chosen output neighborhood. The compact-support isomorphism therefore transfers through a naturality square. This step needs the forward or reverse transform estimate from the corresponding prerequisite; ordinary recovery of microlocal Hom by itself would not supply it.

We remove the temporary support assumptions and make the locality argument explicit. The external Hom estimate gives

SS⁡(Q(F,G))⊂SS⁡(F)×SS⁡(G)a.(19) \operatorname{SS}(Q(F,G)) \subset\operatorname{SS}(F)\times\operatorname{SS}(G)^a. \qquad\text{(19)}

By (2), the support of the cotangent sheaf on the right side of (6) is contained in SS⁡(K)∩SS⁡(Q(F,G))\operatorname{SS}(K)\cap\operatorname{SS}(Q(F,G)).

For the forward formula, choose a compact neighborhood AA inside ΩX\Omega_X. The part of CKC_K over AA is compact. Its projection to the base YY is a compact set DD. If the closed support of an input G0G_0 misses DD, (19) and (2) make Rp1*μhom⁡P(K,Q(F,G0))Rp_{1*}\mu\operatorname{hom}_P(K,Q(F,G_0)) zero on the interior of AA. Ordinary direct image is local on its target, so this follows by restricting to p1−1(Int⁡A)p_1^{-1}(\operatorname{Int}A) before pushing.

The other side also vanishes there. The kernel microsupport bound from the forward lesson gives SS⁡(ΦKG0)∩A=⌀\operatorname{SS}(\Phi_KG_0)\cap A=\varnothing, since a possible input covector would have its base in DD. Then (2) gives the required vanishing of microlocal Hom.

Choose a relatively compact open V⊂YV\subset Y containing DD, and write G′=j!j−1GG'=j_!j^{-1}G, where j:V↪Yj:V\hookrightarrow Y. Its closed support lies in the compact set V¯\overline V. The canonical map G′→GG'\to G is an isomorphism near DD; its cone G0G_0 has closed support disjoint from DD. Apply both functors in the first line of (6) to this triangle. They are contravariant in GG, so both arrows from the value at GG to the value at G′G' are isomorphisms on Int⁡A\operatorname{Int}A. Naturality gives a square whose bottom comparison is already an isomorphism by the compact-support proof. Its top comparison is therefore an isomorphism too.

For the reverse formula, choose a compact neighborhood A⊂ΩYA\subset\Omega_Y, project the compact part of CKC_K over it to a compact D⊂XD\subset X, and use F′=j!j−1FF'=j_!j^{-1}F for a relatively compact open neighborhood of DD. Formula (19) makes the right side zero for the cone of F′→FF'\to F. The reverse microsupport bound makes ΨK\Psi_K of that cone invisible over AA, so (2) makes the left side zero as well. Both functors are covariant in FF; the same naturality square now has vertical arrows in the opposite direction and proves the claim. Compact neighborhoods of this kind cover both open cotangent regions. This completes the proof of Theorem 1. ▫\square

The same support argument proves the stated use of localized inputs. A denominator has a cone whose microsupport misses the appropriate input region. Admissibility and (19) make its contribution to the right side invisible, while the localized transform and (2) do the same on the left. The comparison consequently descends in both input variables. This is a cone argument for the actual morphism of cotangent sheaves; no identification of localized Hom groups with global cotangent sections has been inserted.

Exercises with complete solutions

A shifted dilation

Difficulty: Introductory.

Let X=Y=ℝX=Y=\mathbb R, f(y)=3yf(y)=3y, and K=kΓf[s]K=k_{\Gamma_f}[s]. Write both transform formulas and the covector relation. Check that shifting the target by [t][t] produces the same net shift in both comparisons in (6).

Solution. The graph projection to XX is the diffeomorphism ff, so ΦKG=Rf*G[s]\Phi_KG=Rf_*G[s] and ΨKF=f−1F[−s]\Psi_KF=f^{-1}F[-s]. The twisted relation is (y;α)↦(3y;α/3)(y;\alpha)\mapsto(3y;\alpha/3); both projections are proper isomorphisms. Shifts leave this relation unchanged. Microlocal Hom satisfies μhom⁡(A[u],B[v])=μhom⁡(A,B)[v−u]\mu\operatorname{hom}(A[u],B[v])=\mu\operatorname{hom}(A,B)[v-u], directly from (1). The first formula for F[t]F[t] thus has shift [t−s][t-s], and the second has the same shift because ΨK(F[t])=f−1F[t−s]\Psi_K(F[t])=f^{-1}F[t-s]. On the kernel side the first Hom input is shifted by [s][s], while Q(F[t],G)=Q(F,G)[t]Q(F[t],G)=Q(F,G)[t], again giving [t−s][t-s]. The input covector in the second comparison is α\alpha, obtained by negating the physical kernel covector −α-\alpha.

Integration around a circle

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Take X={*}X=\{*\}, Y=S1Y=S^1, and field coefficients. Let K=kS1K=k_{S^1}, G=kS1G=k_{S^1}, and F=kF=k. Use the full cotangent regions. Compute the complexes on both sides of each comparison, including their degrees.

Solution. Choose an orientation of the circle for this calculation. Then ωS1=kS1[1]\omega_{S^1}=k_{S^1}[1], ΦKG=RΓ(S1;k)=k⊕k[−1]\Phi_KG=R\Gamma(S^1;k)=k\oplus k[-1], and ΨKF=kS1[1]\Psi_KF=k_{S^1}[1]. The kernel microsupport is the zero section. Its projection to the point is proper because the circle is compact, and its projection to T*S1T^*S^1 is a proper closed embedding. Thus both conditions hold.

For locally constant perfect complexes, (1) gives microlocal Hom supported on the zero section with value ordinary internal Hom. Therefore μhom⁡S1(kS1,ωS1)\mu\operatorname{hom}_{S^1}(k_{S^1},\omega_{S^1}) is kS1[1]k_{S^1}[1] on that zero section. Its direct image to the point is RΓ(S1;k)[1]=k[1]⊕kR\Gamma(S^1;k)[1]=k[1]\oplus k. On the other side, RHom⁡k(k⊕k[−1],k)=k⊕k[1]R\operatorname{Hom}_k(k\oplus k[-1],k)=k\oplus k[1]. These agree, with cohomology in degrees −1-1 and 00. For the reverse formula, both sides are kS1[1]k_{S^1}[1] on the zero section and zero away from it. The antipode fixes the zero section but is still part of the formula.

Cutting off a noncompact input

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Let X=Y=ℝX=Y=\mathbb R, K=kΔ⊕kℝ2K=k_\Delta\oplus k_{\mathbb R^2}, and ΩX=ΩY=T*ℝ\Tℝ*ℝ\Omega_X=\Omega_Y=T^*\mathbb R\setminus T^*_{\mathbb R}\mathbb R. For the compact set A={(x;ξ):|x|≤1,1≤|ξ|≤2}A=\{(x;\xi):|x|\leq1,\ 1\leq|\xi|\leq2\}, find a compact base set that supports the replacement argument. Explain why the constant-plane summand creates no additional directional morphisms over AA, even for noncompact GG.

Solution. The diagonal relation is the identity. The constant-plane summand has only zero covectors and contributes no relation above AA. The relevant forgotten base set is therefore D=[−1,1]D=[-1,1]. Take V=(−2,2)V=(-2,2) and G′=j!j−1GG'=j_!j^{-1}G. Its closed support is compact, and the cone of G′→GG'\to G is zero on a neighborhood of DD.

Microlocal Hom is additive in a finite direct sum in its first argument. The summand with first input kℝ2k_{\mathbb R^2} has support contained in its zero-section microsupport by (2); hence it is zero above AA. On the transform side, the constant-plane contribution is locally constant in xx, with value RΓc(ℝ;G)R\Gamma_c(\mathbb R;G); it likewise has no nonzero output covectors. Thus the compact replacement works over the interior of AA, despite the nonproper support of the original kernel and the possible noncompactness of GG. This conclusion uses cotangent properness on the selected region.

The orientation hidden in an omitted factor

Difficulty: Advanced.

In (8), suppose one drops ωX3⊗ωY4\omega_{X_3}\otimes\omega_{Y_4}, retaining only ωY2\omega_{Y_2}. Determine the error in the forward and reverse restrictions. Explain why an arbitrary-sheaf biduality claim would not repair it.

Solution. Forward restriction along jXj_X contributes the inverse normal orientation ωY−1\omega_Y^{-1}. Without ωY4\omega_{Y_4}, it cancels the remaining ωY2\omega_{Y_2}, leaving a target F1F_1 rather than F1⊗ωX3⊗ωYF_1\otimes\omega_{X_3}\otimes\omega_Y in (13). Both the exceptional factor for integration over YY and the exceptional factor for the second coordinate of X2X^2 have been lost. The resulting direct-image kernel need not be (14).

Reverse restriction along jYj_Y contributes ωX−1\omega_X^{-1}. Without ωX3\omega_{X_3}, this factor remains, and without ωY4\omega_{Y_4} the inner Hom no longer computes ΨKF\Psi_KF. The target is FX⊗ωY2⊗ωX−1F_X\otimes\omega_{Y_2}\otimes\omega_X^{-1}, instead of the target in (17). These are degree and orientation errors before microlocalization; on nonorientable manifolds the line error is also nontrivial. Formula (8) is forced by the exceptional target projection in the definition (1). Biduality is neither used nor available for general bounded sheaves, and it cannot change the definition’s missing projection orientation.

Conventions and mathematical credit. Definition 5.5.1 in the monograph and Definition 4.5 in the review use ordinary pullback in the source Hom argument and exceptional pullback in the target argument, with the first diagonal conormal covector retained. Those conventions produce the three orientation factors in (8). The factor exchange in the reverse calculation then accounts for the input antipode; it is not an orientation correction. The common-kernel proof organization has a concrete precedent in the proof of Theorem 6.3.9, and that dependence is acknowledged even though the present coordinates, exposition and exercises are independently written.

Extent of this proof. The two one-sided conclusions here retain arbitrary bounded sheaves, general open cotangent regions and separate properness assumptions. The no-escape, orientation and compact-replacement arguments establish the stated comparisons relative to the specified microlocal operation maps, the external Hom estimate, the forward and reverse transform estimates, and the manifold boundedness results. The selected source passages do not by themselves supply this whole formulation, and their comparison is not a claim that every prerequisite proof is closed. In particular, neither a dual–tensor substitution for arbitrary sheaves nor an identification of localized morphism groups with sections over every open region has been added. All four complete solutions remain part of the argument’s checks.
