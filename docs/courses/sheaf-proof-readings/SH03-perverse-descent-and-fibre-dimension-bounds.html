Perverse descent and fibre dimension bounds

Perverse objects glue because their degree-zero morphisms form a sheaf. A countable construction gives the glued complex, and the ordinary bounds on the perverse heart keep it globally bounded. Functorial degree bounds come from a different mechanism: an analytic inverse image can increase a support dimension by at most the largest fibre dimension. Point exceptional composition gives the same geometric estimate for cosupport. Adjunction then gives conditional bounds for the two direct images.

Restrictions and morphisms descend

Both perverse cuts are local on an ambient open cover. For the upper cut, a subanalytic support has dimension less than an integer rr exactly when it does locally at every point. Its global dimension is the supremum of its local dimensions, all integers in one finite range. The same argument applies to the point cosupport test for the lower cut. Ordinary restriction to an open set retains point costalks. Thus open restriction preserves the two cuts and their intersection, and local membership implies global membership once the object is globally bounded and weakly constructible.

Write 𝒫p(U)=pD≤0(U)∩pD≥0(U)\mathcal P_p(U)={}^pD^{\le0}(U)\cap{}^pD^{\ge0}(U). For F,G∈𝒫p(U)F,G\in\mathcal P_p(U), the preceding Hom theorem identifies, including restriction maps,

V⟼Hom⁡D(V)(F|V,G|V)=Γ(V,H0Rℋom(F,G))(V⊂U).(2) V\longmapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{D(V)}(F|_V,G|_V) =\Gamma\bigl(V,H^0R\mathcal Hom(F,G)\bigr) \quad(V\subset U). \qquad\text{(2)}

Therefore compatible local morphisms glue uniquely. If they are locally invertible, their local inverses also agree and glue, so the resulting global morphism is invertible. In particular, two objects realizing the same marked descent data have a unique isomorphism compatible with those markings. This does not eliminate the automorphisms of an unmarked object.

Glue two objects by an actual overlap map

Let X=U∪VX=U\cup V, W=U∩VW=U\cap V, and let FU,FVF_U,F_V be perverse heart objects with an isomorphism α:FU|W→FV|W\alpha:F_U|_W\to F_V|_W. Denote the three open inclusions into XX by jU,jV,jWj_U,j_V,j_W. Form the map whose components are the open counits and the specified overlap isomorphism:

jW!(FU|W)→(u,−vα)jU!FU⊕jV!FV⟶F⟶.(3) j_{W!}(F_U|_W) \xrightarrow{\ (u,-v\alpha)\ } j_{U!}F_U\oplus j_{V!}F_V \longrightarrow F\longrightarrow. \qquad\text{(3)}

This triangle is formed in the ambient derived category. The open sets need not be subanalytic, so its intermediate extensions need not be constructible.

The two summand maps induce βU:FU→F|U\beta_U:F_U\to F|_U and βV:FV→F|V\beta_V:F_V\to F|_V. They are isomorphisms. For example, on UU the second summand is the extension from WW of FV|WF_V|_W. The second component of the first arrow identifies its source with this summand, with a minus sign. A triangular automorphism of the middle object removes its first component. Its cone is consequently FUF_U, with βU\beta_U as that identification. The same calculation works on VV.

On WW, composition of the first and second arrows of (3) is zero, so

βU|W=βV|W∘α.(4) \beta_U|_W=\beta_V|_W\circ\alpha. \qquad\text{(4)}

These are the desired actual overlap maps. Local identification with FU,FVF_U,F_V gives local weak constructibility and perverse membership. The finite cone is globally bounded; the local microsupport criterion gives global weak constructibility. Hence F∈𝒫p(X)F\in\mathcal P_p(X). Perfectness of stalks is also local, so the construction realizes strong descent data when the strong heart is defined.

Inductively this proves effective descent for a finite open cover. Uniqueness and all compatible identifications at each step come from (2), rather than a purported functorial choice of cones.

Countable gluing retains one global bound

There are two distinct steps in descent. Orthogonality in the glued t-structure makes degree-zero morphisms local, but it does not itself produce a single bounded complex on an infinite union. The construction below chooses injective representatives with the same lower bound before taking the stalkwise exact filtered colimit. Local identifications then preserve constructibility, while the common upper bound returns the colimit to the bounded category. This is the part of the argument for which an unrestricted statement that objects glue would leave a real gap.

Theorem. The categories U↦𝒫p(U)U\mapsto\mathcal P_p(U), with all their morphisms, form a stack on the open subsets of XX. The same holds for the strong perverse hearts over a Noetherian coefficient ring.

Proof. The morphism part and uniqueness were proved in (2). Let a possibly arbitrary open cover carry objects and isomorphisms satisfying the cocycle condition. Our manifold convention gives a countable subcover: cover each compact set of a countable compact exhaustion by finitely many members of the given cover. Enumerate the resulting subcover V1,V2,…V_1,V_2,\ldots, and put Um=V1∪⋯∪VmU_m=V_1\cup\cdots\cup V_m. Finite descent gives marked heart objects FmF_m on UmU_m, with compatible isomorphisms αm:Fm→Fm+1|Um\alpha_m:F_m\to F_{m+1}|_{U_m}.

If nn is the uniform dimension bound, the ordinary bounds on every heart object are

Fm∈D[a,b](Um),a=min0≤s≤np(s),b=max0≤s≤np(s).(5) F_m\in D^{[a,b]}(U_m),\qquad a=\min_{0\le s\le n}p(s),\quad b=\max_{0\le s\le n}p(s). \qquad\text{(5)}

The exact foundational inputs are the same-lower-bound injective construction used in the preceding t-exact lesson and the current programme proof of bounded-below K-injectivity. For an open inclusion restriction of an injective sheaf is injective, since restriction is right adjoint to exact extension by zero. Thus Im+1|UmI_{m+1}|_{U_m} is K-injective. Each αm\alpha_m is represented by an actual chain map Im→Im+1|UmI_m\to I_{m+1}|_{U_m}.

jm!Im⟶jm+1,!Im+1,L•=colim⁡mjm!Im.(6) j_{m!}I_m\longrightarrow j_{m+1,!}I_{m+1},\qquad L^\bullet=\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_m j_{m!}I_m. \qquad\text{(6)}

At x∈Umx\in U_m the later chain maps are all quasi-isomorphisms on stalks. Exactness of the colimit gives the canonical identifications

Hr(L)x=colim⁡q≥mHr((Iq)x)≃Hr((Im)x).(7) H^r(L)_x =\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_{q\ge m}H^r((I_q)_x) \simeq H^r((I_m)_x). \qquad\text{(7)}

Consequently the actual map Im→L|UmI_m\to L|_{U_m} is a quasi-isomorphism. All its identifications with the next such map satisfy (4) in the derived category, because the transition chain maps represent the chosen αm\alpha_m.

Two ambient support operations

Let i:T↪Xi:T\hookrightarrow X be locally closed subanalytic, possibly singular. On the ambient manifold define FT=i!i−1FF_T=i_!i^{-1}F and RΓTF=Ri*i!F=Rℋom(kT,F)R\Gamma_TF=Ri_*i^!F=R\mathcal Hom(k_T,F). Then

F∈pD≤0⟹FT∈pD≤0,F∈pD≥0⟹RΓTF∈pD≥0.(8) F\in{}^pD^{\le0}\Longrightarrow F_T\in{}^pD^{\le0}, \qquad F\in{}^pD^{\ge0}\Longrightarrow R\Gamma_TF\in{}^pD^{\ge0}. \qquad\text{(8)}

Extension by zero and ordinary inverse restriction are exact. Their cohomology has the original stalk on TT and zero stalk elsewhere, so its support dimension can only decrease. This proves the upper assertion.

For the lower assertion, point exceptional adjunction gives, for any HH on TT, ix!Ri*H=RHom⁡(i−1k{x},H)i_x^!Ri_*H=R\operatorname{Hom}(i^{-1}k_{\{x\}},H). If x∉Tx\notin T the first argument is zero. If x∈Tx\in T, it is the point sheaf on TT, so exceptional composition, with H=i!FH=i^!F, gives ix!Ri*i!F=ix!Fi_x^!Ri_*i^!F=i_x^!F. Hence

cosupp⁡r(RΓTF)=T∩cosupp⁡rF.(9) \operatorname{cosupp}^r(R\Gamma_TF) =T\cap\operatorname{cosupp}^rF. \qquad\text{(9)}

Its dimension does not increase, and the cosupport criterion proves (8). Both operations stay globally bounded weakly constructible: kTk_T is a bounded weak coefficient, and the established internal-Hom theorem applies to RΓTFR\Gamma_TF. The extension has weak constructibility by the subanalytic support operation theorem. These are ambient assertions and do not presuppose a perverse t-structure on a singular space TT.

Thus the first operation is right t-exact and the second left t-exact. These adjectives alone do not say that either functor preserves the heart.

The dimension of an analytic inverse image

Let f:Y→Xf:Y\to X be analytic and assume dim⁡f−1(x)≤d\dim f^{-1}(x)\le d for every x∈Xx\in X. The dimensions here are real dimensions. For every subanalytic A⊂XA\subset X,

dim⁡f−1(A)≤dim⁡A+d.(10) \dim f^{-1}(A)\le\dim A+d. \qquad\text{(10)}

Here is a local proof relative to the stated subanalytic stratification prerequisites. Stratify AA into analytic smooth pieces and stratify their inverse images compatibly. On a connected source piece, analytic differential minors give an open locus of its maximal rank; its complement has smaller dimension. Stratify that complement and repeat. There are finitely many dimension stages, so one obtains source pieces on which ff has constant rank and takes values in one target piece. All constructions are local; they assert no global subanalyticity for a nonproper image.

On a source piece of dimension ss and rank rr, the constant-rank theorem makes its fibre dimension s−rs-r. This fibre lies in an actual fibre of ff, hence s−r≤ds-r\le d. Since its image lies in a target piece of dimension at most dim⁡A\dim A, r≤dim⁡Ar\le\dim A. Therefore s≤dim⁡A+ds\le\dim A+d. Taking the supremum of the local piece dimensions proves (10). If AA is empty, both sides use the empty-dimension convention.

This proof retains the analytic stratification, rank-minor and set-operation prerequisites from Subanalytic sets and limiting tangent directions. It does not certify their unfinished lower foundations. When Y≠∅Y\ne\varnothing, the fibre hypothesis forces d≥0d\ge0; for empty YY all assertions below are vacuous.

Inverse images shift the dimension argument

The exact bounds are

f−1:pD≤0(X)⟶p[−d]D≤0(Y),f!:pD≥0(X)⟶p[−d]D≥−d(Y).(11) \begin{aligned} f^{-1}:{}^pD^{\le0}(X)&\longrightarrow{}^{p[-d]}D^{\le0}(Y),\\ f^!:{}^pD^{\ge0}(X)&\longrightarrow{}^{p[-d]}D^{\ge-d}(Y). \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(11)}

The existing analytic weak-operation theorem supplies globally bounded weak membership for both outputs, with their full arbitrary-coefficient contracts.

For the upper statement, ordinary inverse image is exact and its stalk at yy is the stalk at f(y)f(y). Thus the degree-jj nonzero locus is exactly f−1supp⁡HjFf^{-1}\operatorname{supp}H^jF. If j>p(r−d)j>p(r-d), its source locus has dimension less than r−dr-d. Formula (10) makes its inverse image have dimension less than rr, as required.

For the lower statement, let y∈Yy\in Y, x=f(y)x=f(y). Exceptional composition gives iy!f!F=ix!Fi_y^!f^!F=i_x^!F, with no extra point-degree shift. Therefore

cosupp⁡j(f!F)=f−1cosupp⁡jF.(12) \operatorname{cosupp}^j(f^!F) =f^{-1}\operatorname{cosupp}^jF. \qquad\text{(12)}

Put q(s)=p(s−d)−dq(s)=p(s-d)-d, so qD≥0=p[−d]D≥−d{}^qD^{\ge0}={}^{p[-d]}D^{\ge-d}. If j<q(r)+r=p(r−d)+(r−d)j<q(r)+r=p(r-d)+(r-d), the original cosupport has dimension less than r−dr-d. Its inverse image again has dimension less than rr. This proves the second line of (11). Ordinary cohomology of f!Ff^!F may have dimension shifts even though its point costalk identity (12) has none.

Direct images need actual output membership

For the complex middle perversity, Massey’s Notes on Perverse Sheaves and Vanishing Cycles, §5, states the corresponding four fibre-dimension bounds with the direct-image constructibility conditions explicit. Here the real perversity translation is obtained from the dimension estimate and the actual adjunctions already established. Thus this step proves a degree inequality for outputs in the stated category; it does not turn a nonproper analytic map into a constructibility theorem. The zero-dimensional-fibre and countable-space examples below test that distinction separately.

Suppose GG is globally bounded weakly constructible on YY. The two statements are

G∈pD≤0(Y),Rf!G∈Dw-ℝcb(X)⟹Rf!G∈p[d]D≤d(X),G∈pD≥0(Y),Rf*G∈Dw-ℝcb(X)⟹Rf*G∈p[d]D≥0(X).(13) \begin{aligned} G\in{}^pD^{\le0}(Y),\ Rf_!G\in D^b_{\mathrm w\text{-}\mathbb R\mathrm c}(X) &\Longrightarrow Rf_!G\in{}^{p[d]}D^{\le d}(X),\\ G\in{}^pD^{\ge0}(Y),\ Rf_*G\in D^b_{\mathrm w\text{-}\mathbb R\mathrm c}(X) &\Longrightarrow Rf_*G\in{}^{p[d]}D^{\ge0}(X). \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(13)}

The image-membership clauses retain both weak constructibility and global boundedness. A fibre-dimension inequality supplies neither of them automatically.

For the first assertion put q(s)=p(s+d)+dq(s)=p(s+d)+d. Apply (11) to qq: f!f^! sends the qq-lower cut on XX into the pp-lower cut on YY, because q[−d]−d=pq[-d]-d=p. For H∈qD≥1(X)H\in{}^qD^{\ge1}(X), adjunction gives Hom⁡(Rf!G,H)=Hom⁡(G,f!H)=0. \operatorname{Hom}(Rf_!G,H) =\operatorname{Hom}(G,f^!H)=0. The actual membership of Rf!GRf_!G permits the orthogonal cut criterion inside that category. It gives Rf!G∈qD≤0=p[d]D≤dRf_!G\in{}^qD^{\le0}={}^{p[d]}D^{\le d}.

For the second put q=p[d]q=p[d]. Then q[−d]=pq[-d]=p, and f−1f^{-1} sends the qq-upper cut on XX into the pp-upper cut on YY. For H∈qD≤−1(X)H\in{}^qD^{\le-1}(X), Hom⁡(H,Rf*G)=Hom⁡(f−1H,G)=0\operatorname{Hom}(H,Rf_*G)=\operatorname{Hom}(f^{-1}H,G)=0. Again the separate output membership permits the orthogonal criterion, giving the second line of (13). These are precisely the restricted adjoint arguments, with their actual source and target t-structures.

Field duality exchanges a perversity and its dual

Now let kk be a field and FF strongly constructible. The proved local pairings identify

(DXF)x≃RHom⁡k(ix!F,k),ix!DXF≃RHom⁡k(Fx,k).(14) (D_XF)_x\simeq R\operatorname{Hom}_k(i_x^!F,k), \qquad i_x^!D_XF\simeq R\operatorname{Hom}_k(F_x,k). \qquad\text{(14)}

Perfect stalks and costalks are retained. Field dualization is exact, so Hj(P∨)=(H−jP)∨H^j(P^\vee)=(H^{-j}P)^\vee, and the latter is nonzero exactly when H−jPH^{-j}P is nonzero. Consequently supp⁡HjDXF=cosupp⁡−jF\operatorname{supp}H^jD_XF=\operatorname{cosupp}^{-j}F and cosupp⁡jDXF=supp⁡H−jF\operatorname{cosupp}^jD_XF=\operatorname{supp}H^{-j}F. Since p*(r)+r=−p(r)p^*(r)+r=-p(r), the support and cosupport inequalities give

DX:pDℝc≤0(X)⟶p*Dℝc≥0(X),DX:pDℝc≥0(X)⟶p*Dℝc≤0(X).(15) D_X:{}^pD^{\le0}_{\mathbb R\mathrm c}(X) \longrightarrow{}^{p^*}D^{\ge0}_{\mathbb R\mathrm c}(X), \qquad D_X:{}^pD^{\ge0}_{\mathbb R\mathrm c}(X) \longrightarrow{}^{p^*}D^{\le0}_{\mathbb R\mathrm c}(X). \qquad\text{(15)}

For example the first target condition is j<p*(r)+r=−p(r)j<p^*(r)+r=-p(r), equivalently −j>p(r)-j>p(r), exactly the original upper support test. The other is j>p*(r)j>p^*(r), equivalently −j<p(r)+r-j<p(r)+r, exactly the original lower cosupport test.

The actual bidual evaluation is an isomorphism on the strong category. Applying it and p**=pp^{**}=p gives the reverse implications and a contravariant equivalence between the two hearts. On a triangle its arrows reverse with the derived shift convention; the heart short exact sequence therefore reverses to a short exact sequence in the dual heart. The coefficient-ring and perfectness assumptions have not been replaced by a claim about arbitrary infinite coefficients.

A closed submanifold separates the two tests

Let i:Z↪Xi:Z\hookrightarrow X be a closed analytic submanifold and j:X\Z↪Xj:X\setminus Z\hookrightarrow X its open complement. For any globally bounded weakly constructible FF,

F∈pD≤0(X)⟺j−1F∈pD≤0(X\Z),i−1F∈pD≤0(Z),F∈pD≥0(X)⟺j−1F∈pD≥0(X\Z),i!F∈pD≥0(Z).(16) \begin{aligned} F\in{}^pD^{\le0}(X) &\Longleftrightarrow j^{-1}F\in{}^pD^{\le0}(X\setminus Z),\ i^{-1}F\in{}^pD^{\le0}(Z),\\ F\in{}^pD^{\ge0}(X) &\Longleftrightarrow j^{-1}F\in{}^pD^{\ge0}(X\setminus Z),\ i^!F\in{}^pD^{\ge0}(Z). \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(16)}

Use the same function pp on each manifold’s intrinsic dimensions. No codimension shift has been inserted.

For the upper assertion the degree-rr nonzero stalk locus is partitioned by its intersections with ZZ and its open complement. Each is the support locus of the stated ordinary restriction. Dimension of the finite union is the maximum of their dimensions, so all upper tests are equivalent.

For the lower assertion point exceptional composition identifies the costalk on ZZ with the ambient point costalk of FF. On the complement ordinary open restriction retains that same costalk. Thus the degree-wise cosupport has exactly the corresponding partition; the cosupport test proves the second assertion. The operations have the required weak bounded membership by the existing analytic operation theorem. In particular, ordinary restriction cannot replace exceptional restriction in the lower test.

Exercises with complete solutions

Two overlap components retain monodromy

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Cover a circle by two open arcs U,VU,V, with overlap components A,BA,B. Glue constant rank-one field coefficients by α=1\alpha=1 on AA and α=t≠0\alpha=t\ne0 on BB. Orient a traversal so it first passes from UU to VV through BB, then returns through AA. Compute its monodromy and global cohomology, and explain the sign in (3).

Solution. Transport across BB multiplies by tt, and the return across AA multiplies by 1−1=11^{-1}=1, so the monodromy is tt. Formula (3) imposes the relation βU=βVα\beta_U=\beta_V\alpha; the minus sign makes it an equality rather than its negative. A one-vertex, one-edge cellular model for the circle with this local coefficient is k→t−1kk\xrightarrow{t-1}k in degrees zero and one, since the two edge ends are identified by transport tt. If t≠1t\ne1, the differential is invertible and both groups vanish. If t=1t=1, both are kk. The local object is always rank one; global acyclicity does not mean that the glued sheaf is zero.

Derived morphisms outside the heart can fail descent

Difficulty: Intermediate.

For p=0p=0 on a circle, consider F=kXF=k_X and G=kX[1]G=k_X[1] in the ambient derived category. Show that a nonzero global morphism F→GF\to G can vanish on every sufficiently small arc. Explain why this does not contradict (2).

Solution. Hom⁡(kX,kX[1])=H1(X;k)=k\operatorname{Hom}(k_X,k_X[1])=H^1(X;k)=k. One explicit computation uses the two-arc cover: its acyclic-cover cochain complex is k2→k2k^2\to k^2, with (a,b)↦(b−a,b−a)(a,b)\mapsto(b-a,b-a), whose cokernel is kk. On a contractible arc H1=0H^1=0, so every such global morphism restricts to zero. The morphism presheaf is therefore not separated. Here GG has ordinary degree −1-1 and is outside the p=0p=0 heart. Indeed Rℋom(F,G)=kX[1]R\mathcal Hom(F,G)=k_X[1] has negative cohomology. The Hom sheaf theorem for heart objects excludes this example.

The support operations have only one guaranteed side

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Take X=ℝX=\mathbb R and Z={0}Z=\{0\}. First use p(s)=−sp(s)=-s and F=kX[1]F=k_X[1]. Then use p=0p=0 and G=kXG=k_X. Show respectively that FZF_Z can leave the lower cut and RΓZGR\Gamma_ZG can leave the upper cut.

The fibre shifts are attained

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Let f:ℝd→{pt}f:\mathbb R^d\to\{\mathrm{pt}\}, d>0d>0, with the usual orientation and p=0p=0. Compute f!kf^!k, its point costalks, and Rf!kℝdRf_!k_{\mathbb R^d}. Check the sharp degree boundaries in (11) and (13).

Solution. The manifold orientation formula gives f!k=kℝd[d]f^!k=k_{\mathbb R^d}[d], with ordinary degree −d-d. Thus the lower target in (11) must allow degree −d-d. At a point yy, its costalk is k[d][−d]=kk[d][-d]=k, agreeing with (12) and the costalk at the target point. Zero-dimensional fibres do not ensure constructible images

Difficulty: Advanced.

Let Y=ℕY=\mathbb N be a discrete zero-manifold, X=ℝX=\mathbb R, and f(m)=1/mf(m)=1/m, with m≥1m\ge1. Take G=kYG=k_Y over a field, and p=0p=0. Verify the fibre bound and show that the output membership in (13) cannot be dropped.

Solution. The map is analytic on every zero-dimensional component. Each nonempty fibre is one point, so d=0d=0. The input is a globally bounded strong heart object with stalk kk. Since YY is discrete, GG is the sheaf coproduct of its point coefficients. Proper-support image commutes with that coproduct, giving Rf!G=⨁m≥1k{1/m}Rf_!G=\bigoplus_{m\ge1}k_{\{1/m\}} in degree zero. Its stalks are kk at 1/m1/m, and zero elsewhere, including zero; this also follows from the point-fibre proper-support comparison. Its nonzero-stalk locus {1/m}\{1/m\} is not subanalytic near zero: a one-dimensional subanalytic set has a locally finite analytic partition, so a zero-dimensional locus there cannot contain infinitely many distinct points accumulating at an ambient point.

Thus Rf!GRf_!G is globally bounded but not weakly ℝ\mathbb R-constructible. The actual support is not proper over the compact interval [0,1][0,1], whose inverse image is the noncompact discrete set YY. The fibre dimension bound has not supplied the omitted category-membership clause.

Duality retains a nontrivial orientation line

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Let M=ℝℙ2M=\mathbb{RP}^2, take a field of characteristic different from two, and p(s)=−⌊s/2⌋p(s)=-\lfloor s/2\rfloor. Determine the Verdier dual of kM[1]k_M[1], its perverse degree, and the role of orientation.

Solution. The manifold has real dimension two, with p(2)=p*(2)=−1p(2)=p^*(2)=-1. Its constant complex kM[1]k_M[1] is in the pp-heart by the smooth-stratum tests. With ωM=orM[2]\omega_M=\mathrm{or}_M[2], its dual is Rℋom(kM[1],orM[2])=orM[1]R\mathcal Hom(k_M[1],\mathrm{or}_M[2])=\mathrm{or}_M[1]. This has ordinary degree −1-1, so belongs to the p*p^*-heart. The orientation line on the projective plane has monodromy −1-1 on its fundamental loop. In the stated characteristic it is not the constant line, and the answer cannot be replaced by kM[1]k_M[1]. Duality exchanges the dual coefficient and retains the manifold orientation.

For the second example choose the standard basis of VV. Then V∨=kℕV^\vee=k^{\mathbb N}. A functional on this product that vanishes on its finite-support subspace but is nonzero on the constant sequence (1,1,…)(1,1,\ldots) exists: define it on the span of that sequence modulo the finite-support subspace, and extend a basis. Countable gluing and nonproper strong images

Difficulty: Intermediate.

On X=ℕX=\mathbb N let Um={1,…,m}U_m=\{1,\ldots,m\}, with p=0p=0 and compatible coefficients Fm=kUmF_m=k_{U_m} in degree zero over a field. Carry out (6)–(7). For a:X→{pt}a:X\to\{\mathrm{pt}\}, compare Ra!FRa_!F and Ra*FRa_*F and their strong membership.

Solution. On a discrete space all coefficient vector spaces are injective, so Im=FmI_m=F_m in degree zero is an allowed model. Extension by zero gives the first mm point coefficients and the transition includes them into the first m+1m+1. Their sheaf colimit has stalk kk at every point and no other cohomology, so F=kXF=k_X, with the uniform interval [0,0][0,0]. Its global ordinary sections are all sequences, ∏ℕk\prod_{\mathbb N}k; compact subsets of this discrete space are finite, so compact sections are the finite-support sequences, ⨁ℕk\bigoplus_{\mathbb N}k. Both section functors are exact here, so these are also Ra*FRa_*F and Ra!FRa_!F in degree zero.

Both outputs satisfy the weak perverse bounds for d=0d=0, but neither is a finite-dimensional coefficient or a perfect complex at the target point. Thus the strong input and the zero-dimensional fibre bound do not imply strong direct-image membership. Countable descent itself kept perfect stalks because it was locally identified with one FmF_m, rather than taking this nonproper direct image.
