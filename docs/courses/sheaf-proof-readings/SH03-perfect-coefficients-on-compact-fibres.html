Perfect coefficients on compact fibres

Proper pushforward of a constructible complex preserves its geometric constructibility. To prove that it also preserves perfect stalks, we must calculate cohomology on compact fibres. Finitely many stalk values are not enough by themselves: the maps between them contribute degrees, kernels and cokernels. We will assemble those values through finite localization and descent, preserving the whole complexes and their gluing.

Use the weak inverse-image theorem and proper-on-support weak direct image for geometric constructibility. Constructible gluing on an interval gives the local support fibres, while the open-star cohomology theorem gives the section calculation used in finite descent. Compatible subanalytic triangulation is the geometric input in the common triangulation lemma.

The sheaf-operation inputs are the actual derived proper-support fibre comparison, proper-support base change and derived composition maps. Their section and c-soft-resolution proofs precede the constructible applications. The oriented interval calculation fixes the compact-support shift in (4).

Perfection and ordinary inverse image

Let kk be commutative of finite global dimension. Manifolds and maps are real analytic, finite dimensional with uniform dimension bounds, Hausdorff and countable at infinity. All complexes are globally bounded. A coefficient complex is perfect when it is quasi-isomorphic to a bounded complex of finitely generated projective kk-modules.

Perfect complexes are closed under finite sums, shifts and cones. For the cone assertion, choose bounded finite-projective representatives for the two inputs. Such a representative computes its derived maps by actual chain maps: it is a bounded projective complex. The mapping cone again has bounded finite-projective terms. This proves the closure without Noetherianity.

If f:Y→Xf:Y\to X is analytic and FF is ℝ\mathbb R-constructible, the weak inverse-image theorem gives weak constructibility of f−1Ff^{-1}F, and

(f−1F)y≃Ff(y).(1) (f^{-1}F)_y\simeq F_{f(y)}. \qquad\text{(1)}

Every stalk on the right is perfect. Thus ordinary inverse image preserves ℝ\mathbb R-constructibility. The same stalk identity applies to restriction to any topological subset; when we use a subanalytic subset below, compatible triangulation supplies its local constancy statements. No exceptional-inverse-image identification is used in (1).

Proper support and the comparison of the two images

Let f:Y→Xf:Y\to X be a continuous map of locally compact Hausdorff spaces, let S⊂YS\subset Y be closed, and let G∈D+(kY)G\in D^+(k_Y) have all its cohomology supported on SS. Write i:S↪Yi:S\hookrightarrow Y and h=f∘i:S→Xh=f\circ i:S\to X, and assume that hh is proper. The adjunction map G→Ri*i−1GG\to Ri_*i^{-1}G is an isomorphism: closed direct image and inverse image are exact, its stalks on SS are the identity, and its stalks off SS are zero on both sides. Moreover, i*i_* preserves injectives because it is right adjoint to exact inverse image. These facts give the ordinary derived composition comparison, while proper-image composition (D3) gives the proper-support one:

Rf!G≃Rh!i−1G⟶Rh*i−1G≃Rf*G.(1a) \begin{aligned} Rf_!G&\simeq Rh_!i^{-1}G\\ &\longrightarrow Rh_*i^{-1}G\\ &\simeq Rf_*G. \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(1a)}

For the proper map hh, every section has support proper over its target open set, so the inclusion h!Q→h*Qh_!Q\to h_*Q is the identity on the same sections for every sheaf QQ on SS. Thus the middle arrow in (1a) is an isomorphism after taking an injective resolution on SS. The composition maps in (D3) retain those sections, so (1a) is the canonical comparison Rf!G→Rf*GRf_!G\to Rf_*G, not an unrelated isomorphism between the two objects. This argument needs neither smooth fibres nor a finite-generation condition on their coefficients. Together with the fibre map (F6) and its base-change comparison (D4), it justifies every passage between the two images below, including the map to a point for compactly supported coefficients.

Compact support on the line

Line lemma. If G∈Dℝ-cb(kℝ)G\in D^b_{\mathbb R\text{-}c}(k_{\mathbb R}) has compact support, then RΓ(ℝ;G)R\Gamma(\mathbb R;G) is perfect.

Proof. Choose a locally finite subanalytic stratification for all its cohomology sheaves, compatible with its compact support. Near that support only finitely many strata occur. On a real analytic line, every stratum is a union of open intervals or isolated points; the zero-dimensional strata are locally finite. Choose finitely many cuts t0<⋯<tNt_0<\cdots<t_N containing all changes near the support and enclosing it. Include any additional endpoints needed so that the restrictions outside [t0,tN][t_0,t_N] are zero. On each Ij=(tj−1,tj)I_j=(t_{j-1},t_j), every cohomology sheaf is locally constant, hence constant. Interval acyclicity and the bounded hypercohomology counit give

G|Ij≃(Bj)Ij,Bj≃Gx(x∈Ij).(2) G|_{I_j}\simeq (B_j)_{I_j},\qquad B_j\simeq G_x\quad(x\in I_j). \qquad\text{(2)}

Each BjB_j is perfect. Put Aj=GtjA_j=G_{t_j}, also perfect.

Let i:P↪ℝi:P\hookrightarrow\mathbb R be the finite closed set of cuts, and j:ℝ\P↪ℝj:\mathbb R\setminus P\hookrightarrow\mathbb R its open complement. The actual localization triangle is

j!j−1G⟶G⟶i*i−1G→+1.(3) j_!j^{-1}G\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow i_*i^{-1}G\xrightarrow{+1}. \qquad\text{(3)}

Its first term is the finite sum of extensions from the bounded intervals with nonzero coefficient. Its last is the finite sum of point coefficients. Since the interval closures are compact, ordinary ambient sections of each open extension equal compactly supported interval sections. In the increasing orientation of ℝ\mathbb R,

RΓ(ℝ;j!(Bj)Ij)≃Bj[−1].(4) R\Gamma(\mathbb R;j_!(B_j)_{I_j})\simeq B_j[-1]. \qquad\text{(4)}

The shift is the one-dimensional compact integration degree. Apply derived global sections to (3). It gives a triangle

⨁j=1NBj[−1]⟶RΓ(ℝ;G)⟶⨁j=0NAj→+1,(5) \bigoplus_{j=1}^N B_j[-1]\longrightarrow R\Gamma(\mathbb R;G) \longrightarrow\bigoplus_{j=0}^N A_j\xrightarrow{+1}, \qquad\text{(5)}

with zero coefficients allowed. Both finite outer sums are perfect. Closure under cones proves the lemma. The connecting map in (5) retains the attachment of interval coefficients to their endpoint values. ▫\square

The local support version of this argument is useful as well. For a cut tjt_j, a small interval meeting its two neighbors gives

RΓ{tj}(ℝ;G)≃Fib⁡(Aj⟶Bj⊕Bj+1),(6) R\Gamma_{\{t_j\}}(\mathbb R;G) \simeq\operatorname{Fib}\bigl(A_j\longrightarrow B_j\oplus B_{j+1}\bigr), \qquad\text{(6)}

where an absent neighboring coefficient is zero. This is the interval localization triangle with its actual two restriction maps. All three inputs are perfect, so its fibre is perfect. At a point inside a constant interval the two maps are the diagonal and the fibre is Bj[−1]B_j[-1]. These are the local costalk triangles underlying the compact-line reduction.

Coordinate induction has no perfect-push assumption

We next prove

G∈Dℝ-cb(kℝN),supp⁡(G) compact⟹RΓ(ℝN;G) perfect.(7) G\in D^b_{\mathbb R\text{-}c}(k_{\mathbb R^N}),\quad \operatorname{supp}(G)\text{ compact} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad R\Gamma(\mathbb R^N;G)\text{ perfect}. \qquad\text{(7)}

The case N=0N=0 is a point and the case N=1N=1 is the line lemma. For N≥2N\geq2, use the coordinate projection p:ℝN→ℝN−1p:\mathbb R^N\to\mathbb R^{N-1}. It is proper on the compact support. The weak operation theorem makes Rp*GRp_*G bounded and weakly constructible. For any x∈ℝN−1x\in\mathbb R^{N-1}, proper-support base change gives

(Rp*G)x≃RΓ({x}×ℝ;G|{x}×ℝ).(8) (Rp_*G)_x\simeq R\Gamma(\{x\}\times\mathbb R;G|_{\{x\}\times\mathbb R}). \qquad\text{(8)}

The restriction is ℝ\mathbb R-constructible by (1), and its support is compact. The line lemma therefore makes this stalk perfect. We have proved that Rp*GRp_*G is ℝ\mathbb R-constructible at this step. Its support lies in the compact projection of supp⁡(G)\operatorname{supp}(G). Apply the induction hypothesis in dimension N−1N-1 and derived composition:

RΓ(ℝN;G)≃RΓ(ℝN−1;Rp*G).(9) R\Gamma(\mathbb R^N;G) \simeq R\Gamma(\mathbb R^{N-1};Rp_*G). \qquad\text{(9)}

This proves (7). Perfection of each intermediate direct image was obtained from its one-dimensional fibres; the general perfect-push theorem has not been assumed.

Compact support in finitely many coordinate charts

The Euclidean result also calculates compactly supported coefficients on an arbitrary manifold in our analytic range. A finite closed cover makes the reduction without a global analytic embedding.

Compact-chart lemma. If G∈Dℝ-cb(kM)G\in D^b_{\mathbb R\text{-}c}(k_M) has compact closed support SS, then RΓ(M;G)R\Gamma(M;G) is perfect.

Proof. If S=∅S=\varnothing, the stalk criterion gives G=0G=0. Otherwise, choose finitely many closed coordinate balls C1,…,CmC_1,\ldots,C_m whose interiors cover SS, each compact inside an analytic chart ci:Ui→Vi⊂ℝnic_i:U_i\to V_i\subset\mathbb R^{n_i}. Such balls exist around every point by shrinking a Euclidean chart ball; compactness selects finitely many. Each CiC_i is a closed subanalytic subset of MM: its chart description is a closed ball, and every point outside that compact ball has a neighborhood missing it. Put CI=⋂i∈ICiC_I=\bigcap_{i\in I}C_i for nonempty index sets II, and write aI:CI↪Ma_I:C_I\hookrightarrow M.

Closed-support localization from (1a), with the identity map on MM, identifies GG with the exact closed direct image of its restriction to SS. Choose a bounded complex representing that restriction and push it forward; we may therefore represent GG by a bounded complex which vanishes termwise off SS. Closed inverse restriction and closed direct image are exact. There is an augmented finite complex of complexes

G⟶C0(G)⟶⋯⟶Cm−1(G),Cp(G)=⨁i0<⋯<ipaI*aI−1G,I={i0,…,ip}.(CC1) \begin{aligned} G&\longrightarrow C^0(G)\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow C^{m-1}(G),\\ C^p(G)&=\bigoplus_{i_0<\cdots<i_p}a_{I*}a_I^{-1}G,\\ I&=\{i_0,\ldots,i_p\}.\\ &\text{(CC1)} \end{aligned}

Its arrows are the alternating ordinary restriction maps, including restriction to closed intersections. At a point xx, let Jx={i:x∈Ci}J_x=\{i:x\in C_i\}. If JxJ_x is empty, the entire augmented stalk complex is zero, because SS is covered. Otherwise it is the augmented cochain complex of the full simplex on JxJ_x, with coefficient complex GxG_x and identity restriction maps. Fix j∈Jxj\in J_x, regard cochains as alternating functions of their indices, and insert jj as the first index. This gives a contraction (sc)i0…ip−1=cji0…ip−1(sc)_{i_0\ldots i_{p-1}}=c_{j i_0\ldots i_{p-1}}, with repeated indices interpreted as zero and with s(c)=cjs(c)=c_j in degree zero. In the alternating coboundary, the terms containing jj cancel in pairs and leave ds+sd=1ds+sd=1, including the augmentation. It commutes with the internal differential. Thus (CC1) has a quasi-isomorphic finite totalization, with the usual total-complex signs.

For each nonempty CIC_I, choose i∈Ii\in I. The chart sends it to a compact, hence closed, subanalytic subset DI=ci(CI)D_I=c_i(C_I) of ℝni\mathbb R^{n_i}. Local subanalyticity on the chart is preserved by its analytic coordinate changes; at points outside ViV_i, compact containment gives a neighborhood disjoint from DID_I. Let HIH_I be the exact closed extension to ℝni\mathbb R^{n_i} of the transported restriction G|CIG|_{C_I}.

This extension is constructible, including at its boundary. For each of the finitely many cohomology sheaves of GG, take its defining locally finite cover by locally closed subanalytic sets. Only finitely many members meet the compact CIC_I. Intersect those members with CIC_I, transport them through cic_i, and add ℝni\DI\mathbb R^{n_i}\setminus D_I. These form a finite cover by locally closed subanalytic sets. Compact containment makes their closures miss the outside of the chart, so local closedness and subanalyticity hold in the whole Euclidean space. On the inside pieces the cohomology restrictions remain locally constant by ordinary restriction; on the outside piece they are zero. This is exactly the weak cover criterion, without a new triangulation. The stalks of HIH_I are original stalk complexes of GG, or zero, hence perfect, and the same global cohomological bounds remain valid. Its support is compact. Equation (7) applies and gives

RΓ(M;aI*aI−1G)≃RΓ(CI;G|CI)≃RΓ(ℝni;HI).(CC2) \begin{aligned} R\Gamma(M;a_{I*}a_I^{-1}G) &\simeq R\Gamma(C_I;G|_{C_I})\\ &\simeq R\Gamma(\mathbb R^{n_i};H_I).\\ &\text{(CC2)} \end{aligned}

The section objects in (CC2) are therefore perfect. The comparisons come from the actual closed embeddings and the chart homeomorphism. Closed direct image preserves injectives, being right adjoint to exact inverse restriction, so these are also the derived section comparisons. Empty intersections contribute zero. Applying derived sections to the finite totalization (CC1) gives a finite filtration whose quotients are finite sums of (CC2), shifted by [−p][-p]. Derived sections preserve these finite cones. Hence RΓ(M;G)R\Gamma(M;G) is perfect. No infinite-cover totalization, finite-generation criterion or perfect-push theorem has been used. ▫\square

The balls only cover the coefficient support, not necessarily the manifold. Their intersections may be singular and need not be balls. What matters is compactness, subanalyticity and containment in one analytic chart. The argument also covers dimension zero and disconnected manifolds; the finite cover can use charts of different dimensions within the given uniform bound.

Finite descent on a compact triangulation

Here is a second compact-cohomology proof which also treats compact singular subanalytic subsets directly.

Compact-set lemma. Let F∈Dℝ-cb(kX)F\in D^b_{\mathbb R\text{-}c}(k_X) and let K⊂XK\subset X be compact and subanalytic. Then RΓ(K;F|K)R\Gamma(K;F|_K) is perfect.

Proof. Refine a common locally finite subanalytic cover for the finitely many cohomology sheaves, with membership in KK and its complement. Choose a compatible locally finite triangulation i:|S|≃Xi:|S|\simeq X. The set KK is a union of open simplex images. It is closed, so it contains the faces of every simplex it contains; its inverse image is the realization of a subcomplex SKS_K.

The exact input here is the common triangulation lemma, (3). Use its partition-refinement proof with the finitely many cohomology sheaves and the two additional membership conditions for the compact set and its complement. That proof accepts a locally finite subanalytic partition and arbitrary stalk modules. It therefore supplies the simultaneous triangulation used here without a Noetherian coefficient assumption. This step uses neither the finite-stalk abelian-category equivalence nor the toric-orbit theorem. The geometric construction can be taken directly from TC3–TC5, with globalization in TC4. TC4 applies to an arbitrary locally finite family of locally subanalytic subsets, including the membership refinement just used. Its proper subanalytic embedding and analytic recovery on each open simplex do not require a global analytic embedding. Its finite preparation, cell and coordinate proof inputs remain as stated there.

This subcomplex is finite. The open simplices form a locally finite family: near a point in a simplex, only its finitely many cofaces can occur. A finite neighborhood cover of the compact inverse image of KK then meets only finitely many simplices. The restricted complex F|KF|_K has simplex-constructible cohomology and perfect stalks on this finite complex.

Cover |SK||S_K| by the finitely many open vertex stars UvU_v. An intersection of stars for distinct vertices is empty or is the open star of their face. By star acyclicity and its bounded hypercohomology comparison with the actual stalk map,

RΓ(Uv0∩⋯∩Uvp;F|K)≃Fx(10) R\Gamma(U_{v_0}\cap\cdots\cap U_{v_p};F|_K) \simeq F_x \qquad\text{(10)}

for a point xx on that face, when the intersection is nonempty. Every such section complex is perfect.

For completeness, finite derived Čech descent does retain the whole object here. Take a bounded-below injective resolution I•I^\bullet of the restricted complex. Its terms are flabby, including on open restrictions. The augmented finite Čech complex of their sections is exact: for two opens the difference map onto sections on the intersection is surjective by flabbiness, giving the Mayer–Vietoris exact sequence; induction on a finite cover gives the augmented Čech exactness. Thus the total complex of

Čp(I•)=⨁v0<⋯<vpΓ(Uv0∩⋯∩Uvp;I•)(11) \check C^p(I^\bullet)= \bigoplus_{v_0<\cdots<v_p} \Gamma(U_{v_0}\cap\cdots\cap U_{v_p};I^\bullet) \qquad\text{(11)}

computes RΓ(K;F|K)R\Gamma(K;F|_K). Empty intersections contribute zero. The Čech degree is finite, and the resolution is bounded below, so this totalization and its augmentation converge without an infinite-degree interchange.

Filter the total complex by its finitely many Čech columns. Its successive quotients are the column complexes shifted by [−p][-p]. Each column is, in the derived category, a finite sum of the perfect complexes (10). Finite cone closure makes the total perfect. Its differentials are the actual alternating restriction maps; they have not been discarded. ▫\square

This proof uses perfect complexes at the faces. It needs no description by finitely generated cohomology modules, and therefore no Noetherian hypothesis.

Proper direct image with perfect stalks

Theorem. Let f:Y→Xf:Y\to X be analytic and let G∈Dℝ-cb(kY)G\in D^b_{\mathbb R\text{-}c}(k_Y). If ff is proper on supp⁡(G)\operatorname{supp}(G), then

Rf*G∈Dℝ-cb(kX).(12) Rf_*G\in D^b_{\mathbb R\text{-}c}(k_X). \qquad\text{(12)}

The same conclusion holds for Rf!GRf_!G, which agrees with Rf*GRf_*G under this support properness.

Proof. Weak constructibility and boundedness are already proved by the weak operation theorem. At x∈Xx\in X, put Z=f−1(x)Z=f^{-1}(x). Proper-support base change gives

(Rf*G)x≃RΓ(Z;G|Z).(13) (Rf_*G)_x\simeq R\Gamma(Z;G|_Z). \qquad\text{(13)}

The closed support supp⁡(G)\operatorname{supp}(G) is subanalytic: it is the zero-section base of its closed conic subanalytic microsupport. Hence

K=Z∩supp⁡(G)(14) K=Z\cap\operatorname{supp}(G) \qquad\text{(14)}

is subanalytic and compact by the stated properness. The restricted complex on ZZ vanishes outside KK. Closed-support localization consequently identifies it with the direct image from its ordinary restriction to KK, so

RΓ(Z;G|Z)≃RΓ(K;G|K).(15) R\Gamma(Z;G|_Z)\simeq R\Gamma(K;G|_K). \qquad\text{(15)}

The compact-set lemma makes this perfect. Thus every stalk in (13) is perfect, proving (12). The fibre ZZ can be singular, disconnected or noncompact away from the coefficient support; none of those possibilities changes (14)–(15). ▫\square

If GG has compact support and ff is the map to a point, the theorem says that its ordinary and compactly supported global cohomology are the same perfect complex. It asserts perfection as a complex. Over Noetherian kk, one can further express this as bounded finitely generated cohomology; that equivalence uses the extra ring hypothesis.

The graph and manifold-fibre reduction

The graph route gives the coordinate proof of the same general theorem, using the explicit graph factorization below. Factor

Y→γfX×Y→pX,γf(y)=(f(y),y).(16) Y\xrightarrow{\gamma_f}X\times Y\xrightarrow{p}X, \qquad \gamma_f(y)=(f(y),y). \qquad\text{(16)}

The graph is closed analytic. Its direct image is exact, is weakly constructible by the closed-embedding operation, and has stalks either GyG_y on the graph or zero off it. Thus γf*G\gamma_{f*}G is ℝ\mathbb R-constructible. The projection is proper on that object’s support precisely by the original support properness. Its fibre over xx is the smooth manifold {x}×Y\{x\}\times Y, and ordinary restriction to that fibre preserves constructibility and perfection by (1). Its coefficient support is compact.

To finish the coordinate route, apply the compact-chart lemma to this manifold fibre and its compactly supported coefficient. Each closed chart intersection is calculated by (7), which in turn uses coordinate induction and the compact-line calculation (2)–(5). The finite closed-cover restriction maps in (CC1) assemble those Euclidean complexes. Proper base change and composition in (16) therefore give the perfect stalk in (13). This completes the graph, manifold-fibre, coordinate-chart, Euclidean-coordinate and compact-line chain without a global analytic embedding. The separate proof (14)–(15) calculates a compact singular fibre by compatible triangulation.

Conditional global-embedding variant. If a closed real analytic embedding of the manifold fibre into some ℝN\mathbb R^N is separately supplied, its exact closed direct image has the original perfect stalks on the image and zero stalks off it. Closed-embedding weak constructibility and (7) then give the same compact cohomology, followed by base change and (16). This retains the earlier embedding reduction as a conditional variant. No analytic embedding theorem is asserted here. The proper smooth embedding theorem does not establish that analytic premise. The supplied triangulation is analytic on open simplex images; it is not a global analytic embedding either. Neither completed proof above needs this additional hypothesis.

Examples and exercises with solutions

An interval needs its attachment map

Difficulty: Introductory.

For G=k[0,1]G=k_{[0,1]} on ℝ\mathbb R, compute the triangle (5), including its connecting map, and find RΓ(ℝ;G)R\Gamma(\mathbb R;G).

Solution. The single interval coefficient is B=kB=k, and both endpoint coefficients are kk. In the increasing interval orientation the connecting map k2→kk^2\to k is (a,b)↦b−a(a,b)\mapsto b-a. Equivalently the section calculation is [k2→k][k^2\to k] in degrees zero and one. The map is onto and its kernel is the diagonal kk, so global cohomology is kk in degree zero. The individual strata contribute two point terms and one interval term shifted by [−1][-1]; adding their dimensions without the attachment map would not give the section complex.

Finite free stalks can yield torsion cohomology

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Over ℤ\mathbb Z, construct a sheaf supported on [0,1][0,1] with stalk ℤ\mathbb Z at both endpoints and on the open interval, and endpoint-to-interior maps multiplication by 22 and 66, respectively. Compute its global section complex and its endpoint costalks.

Solution. This is the face-diagram sheaf for the closed interval, extended by its closed embedding into the line. Its derived section complex is [ℤ⊕ℤ→(a,b)↦6b−2aℤ] [\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{(a,b)\mapsto6b-2a}\mathbb Z] in degrees zero and one. The kernel has generator (3,1)(3,1) and is ℤ\mathbb Z; the image is 2ℤ2\mathbb Z, so the cokernel is ℤ/2\mathbb Z/2. Thus H0=ℤH^0=\mathbb Z, H1=ℤ/2H^1=\mathbb Z/2, and the complex is perfect because its displayed terms are finite free. At the left endpoint, (6) is the fibre of ℤ→2ℤ\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{2}\mathbb Z, namely (ℤ/2)[−1](\mathbb Z/2)[-1]. At the right it is the fibre of multiplication by 66, namely (ℤ/6)[−1](\mathbb Z/6)[-1]. Outside coefficients are zero. Perfection permits this torsion and preserves the local degree.

A circle projection has merging fibre values

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Let Y=S1⊂ℝ2Y=S^1\subset\mathbb R^2, f(x,y)=xf(x,y)=x, and G=kYG=k_Y. Compute the stalks of Rf*GRf_*G, and describe its restriction maps from an endpoint of [−1,1][-1,1] into its interior.

Solution. The source is compact, so support properness holds. For |t|<1|t|<1 the fibre has two points, giving k2k^2 in degree zero. At t=±1t=\pm1 there is one point, giving kk, and for |t|>1|t|>1 the fibre is empty. All fibre higher cohomology vanishes. Near either endpoint the two interior points belong to the two sides of one connected small arc of the circle. A section on that arc has the same value on both sides; the endpoint-to-interior restriction is the diagonal k→k2k\to k^2. These stalk complexes are perfect, and the direct image is constructible for the two endpoints and complementary intervals. Derived composition gives its global cohomology as that of the circle, with one kk in degrees zero and one; the attachment maps retain the circle class.

A compact singular fibre is allowed

Difficulty: Advanced.

Take f:ℝ2→ℝf:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R, f(x,y)=xyf(x,y)=xy, and G=kKG=k_K, where KK is the closed unit disk. Describe the coefficient fibre over zero and compute (Rf*G)0(Rf_*G)_0. What are the fibres for 0<|t|<1/20<|t|<1/2?

Solution. The coefficient support is compact, so ff is proper on it. At zero the compact coefficient fibre is the union of the horizontal and vertical diameter segments, a tree with four arms. A finite graph model has five vertices and four edges. Its constant-coefficient section complex has difference map k5→k4k^5\to k^4, sending each arm endpoint value minus the central value to its edge. This map is onto with diagonal kernel kk. Hence (Rf*G)0=k(Rf_*G)_0=k in degree zero. For 0<|t|<1/20<|t|<1/2, the hyperbola inside the disk has two compact interval components, so its coefficient cohomology is k2k^2 in degree zero. At |t|=1/2|t|=1/2 there are two tangency points, and beyond that the fibre is empty. The central fibre is singular, but its finite compact calculation and every displayed stalk are perfect. Smoothness of the original map or of every fibre was unnecessary.

Finite star descent on a triangle-shaped circle

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Take the simplicial circle with vertices 1,2,31,2,3, edges 12,13,2312,13,23, and no two-dimensional face. Cover it by the three vertex stars and calculate the constant-coefficient Čech complex.

Solution. Each star and each nonempty pair intersection has coefficient kk in degree zero by star acyclicity. The triple intersection is empty, since there is no face 123123. The complex is [k3⟶k3],(a1,a2,a3)⟼(a2−a1,a3−a1,a3−a2), [k^3\longrightarrow k^3],\qquad (a_1,a_2,a_3)\longmapsto(a_2-a_1,a_3-a_1,a_3-a_2), with pairs ordered 12,13,2312,13,23. Its kernel is the diagonal kk, and its cokernel is kk, detected by (c12,c13,c23)↦c12+c23−c13(c_{12},c_{13},c_{23})\mapsto c_{12}+c_{23}-c_{13}. The latter map is onto and its kernel is the image: for a triple in its kernel choose a1=0,a2=c12,a3=c13a_1=0,a_2=c_{12},a_3=c_{13}. Thus the derived cohomology is perfect with H0=H1=kH^0=H^1=k. Acyclic stars compute the global complex; they do not force its positive cohomology to vanish.

Perfect complexes on a compact circle need no Noetherian reduction

Difficulty: Advanced.

Let PP be any perfect coefficient complex and let PS1P_{S^1} be its constant sheaf complex. Compute its derived global cohomology, and explain which property makes it perfect.

Solution. The finite star Čech model in the preceding exercise tensors with a bounded finite-projective representative of PP. Its matrix consists only of signed identity maps. The constant-coefficient complex can be split, by elementary changes of basis, into the kernel kk in degree zero, the cokernel kk in degree one, and two contractible identity complexes. Tensoring this decomposition gives RΓ(S1;PS1)≃P⊕P[−1]R\Gamma(S^1;P_{S^1})\simeq P\oplus P[-1]. It is perfect by finite direct-sum and shift closure. This uses finite-projective representatives and actual finite descent. No equivalence between perfection and finitely generated cohomology over an arbitrary ring was invoked; such an equivalence would require the additional Noetherian hypothesis used elsewhere in the course.

Proposition 8.3.1, printed pp. 148–149, proves proper constructible direct image using that remark. Ordinary inverse image is included in Proposition 8.3.3, printed p. 149. The compact calculation here is separately proved by localization on a line, coordinate induction and finite star descent; the compatible-triangulation route retains its exact geometric proof inputs, while the compact-chart route completes the manifold reduction without a global analytic embedding.

Readable source and dependency account

A finite list of perfect stalks is not by itself a perfect global section object. The proof retains the restriction maps in finite localization triangles and in star descent; finite sums and cones of perfect complexes then give the required fibre object. Proper-support base change identifies that complex with the image stalk. The graph route uses the finite closed-chart resolution (CC1), with its actual restriction maps and stalk contraction. The earlier analytic-embedding reduction is retained only as an explicitly conditional variant. The no-Noetherian claim is established by perfect-complex operations, not by replacing perfectness with finite cohomology modules.

For the classical operation statements, see Pierre Schapira, An Introduction to Sheaves on Grothendieck Topologies, 1 August 2026, Theorem 6.2.7, p. 128. That section states the results without proof and uses the Noetherian coefficient convention on p. 127. The argument above establishes its perfect-complex conclusion without that convention. Schapira’s A short review on microlocal sheaf theory, 19 January 2016, p. 7, identifies the closed support through the zero covectors of microsupport; Theorem 2.9, pp. 10–11, gives the proper-image microsupport estimate and its closed-embedding equality.
