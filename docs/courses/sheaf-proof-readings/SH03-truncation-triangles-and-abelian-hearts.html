Truncation triangles and abelian hearts

A triangulated category supplies cones and connecting maps. A t-structure specifies which part of an object lies below a degree and which part lies above it. Its degree-zero objects form an abelian category: the kernel and cokernel of a map are the two cohomology objects of its cone. The octahedral axiom identifies their image and coimage. We prove these statements, including the exactness of the resulting cohomology sequence, before using them to construct perverse sheaves.

The cone that the heart must recover

The degree convention and the three axioms

Let 𝒟\mathcal D be a triangulated category. Full subcategories below are closed under isomorphisms. Given 𝒟≤0,𝒟≥0\mathcal D^{\le0},\mathcal D^{\ge0}, put

𝒟≤n=𝒟≤0[−n],𝒟≥n=𝒟≥0[−n],𝒜=𝒟≤0∩𝒟≥0.(1) \mathcal D^{\le n}=\mathcal D^{\le0}[-n],\qquad \mathcal D^{\ge n}=\mathcal D^{\ge0}[-n],\qquad \mathcal A=\mathcal D^{\le0}\cap\mathcal D^{\ge0}. \qquad\text{(1)}

Thus [1][1] lowers a cohomological degree. A t-structure satisfies

𝒟≤−1⊂𝒟≤0,𝒟≥1⊂𝒟≥0,Hom⁡(U,V)=0(U∈𝒟≤0,V∈𝒟≥1),U⟶X⟶V⟶U[1](U∈𝒟≤0,V∈𝒟≥1)for every X.(2) \begin{gathered} \mathcal D^{\le-1}\subset\mathcal D^{\le0},\qquad \mathcal D^{\ge1}\subset\mathcal D^{\ge0},\\ \operatorname{Hom}(U,V)=0 \quad(U\in\mathcal D^{\le0},\ V\in\mathcal D^{\ge1}),\\ U\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow U[1] \quad(U\in\mathcal D^{\le0},\ V\in\mathcal D^{\ge1}) \quad\text{for every }X. \end{gathered} \qquad\text{(2)}

The last line asserts existence of a distinguished triangle. The subcategory 𝒜\mathcal A is its heart. Shifting (2) gives the same assertions at every integer cut.

In the opposite triangulated category the shift is the original [−1][-1]. Reversing a triangle and its Hom sequence makes ((𝒟≥0)op,(𝒟≤0)op)( (\mathcal D^{\ge0})^{\mathrm{op}},(\mathcal D^{\le0})^{\mathrm{op}} ) a t-structure. Replacing both halves by 𝒟≤s,𝒟≥s\mathcal D^{\le s},\mathcal D^{\ge s} also satisfies the axioms, by shifting (2). Its heart is 𝒜[−s]\mathcal A[-s].

The standard and dual constructible examples

For any abelian category ℬ\mathcal B, the standard halves of D(ℬ)D(\mathcal B) are the complexes whose cohomology vanishes in positive degrees or in negative degrees. Smart truncation puts ker⁡d0\ker d^0 at the top of the lower complex and coker⁡d0\operatorname{coker}d^0 at the bottom of the upper complex. The quotient of the original complex by its lower truncation maps quasi-isomorphically to the upper one: its extra two-term part is im⁡d0→1im⁡d0\operatorname{im}d^0\xrightarrow{1}\operatorname{im}d^0, which is acyclic. This gives (2)’s triangle.

Orthogonality can be checked on roofs. Replace the source of a roof for a lower object by its lower smart truncation, still a quasi-isomorphism; replace the upper target by its upper smart truncation. A chain map from a complex in degrees at most zero to one in degrees at least one is zero. Thus the represented derived map is zero. Nesting is immediate from cohomological degrees. The heart is ℬ\mathcal B: a complex with only degree-zero cohomology truncates to that object in degree zero. For two such objects, the same roof calculation identifies derived degree-zero Hom with their ordinary Hom, by factoring the degree-zero component through its cohomology.

A truncation is characterized by its adjunction

Choose a triangle at cut nn, with first term UX∈𝒟≤nU_X\in\mathcal D^{\le n} and third term VX∈𝒟≥n+1V_X\in\mathcal D^{\ge n+1}. If W∈𝒟≤nW\in\mathcal D^{\le n}, both Hom⁡(W,VX)\operatorname{Hom}(W,V_X) and Hom⁡(W,VX[−1])\operatorname{Hom}(W,V_X[-1]) vanish: the second target lies in 𝒟≥n+2\mathcal D^{\ge n+2}. The Hom sequence therefore gives

Hom⁡(W,UX)→∼Hom⁡(W,X).(3) \operatorname{Hom}(W,U_X)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Hom}(W,X). \qquad\text{(3)}

Dually, for W∈𝒟≥n+1W\in\mathcal D^{\ge n+1}, the groups Hom⁡(UX,W)\operatorname{Hom}(U_X,W) and Hom⁡(UX[1],W)\operatorname{Hom}(U_X[1],W) vanish. Hence Hom⁡(VX,W)≃Hom⁡(X,W)\operatorname{Hom}(V_X,W)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(X,W).

Representing these two Hom functors defines the truncations τ≤nX=UX\tau^{\le n}X=U_X and τ≥n+1X=VX\tau^{\ge n+1}X=V_X, uniquely up to the unique isomorphism preserving their maps to and from XX. For f:X→Yf:X\to Y, (3) gives the unique first arrow commuting with ff, and the dual statement gives the unique third arrow. Uniqueness proves identity and composition compatibility. Thus these are functors: the right adjoint and left adjoint, respectively, to the indicated inclusions.

Their connecting arrow is also canonical. More generally, suppose two distinguished triangles have the same first two arrows U→X→VU\to X\to V. Complete their identity square to a triangle map with third component a:V→Va:V\to V. Since aa fixes the arrow X→VX\to V, exactness of Hom makes 1−a1-a factor through U[1]→VU[1]\to V. If Hom⁡(U[1],V)=0\operatorname{Hom}(U[1],V)=0, then a=1a=1 and the two connecting arrows agree. In a truncation triangle this vanishing follows from U[1]∈𝒟≤n−1U[1]\in\mathcal D^{\le n-1}, V∈𝒟≥n+1V\in\mathcal D^{\ge n+1}.

Completing the square for an arbitrary ff now gives a triangle map whose first and third arrows are exactly the adjoint-defined ones. Its last square proves naturality of the connecting arrow. We have a natural distinguished triangle

τ≤nX⟶X⟶τ≥n+1X⟶(τ≤nX)[1].(4) \tau^{\le n}X\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow\tau^{\ge n+1}X \longrightarrow(\tau^{\le n}X)[1]. \qquad\text{(4)}

This conclusion does not assert that cones of all maps in 𝒟\mathcal D are functorial.

For (T1), the lower comparison at −1-1 is z↦(−2z,z)z\mapsto(-2z,z) into the negative term, and the upper comparison is reduction modulo two on the degree-zero term. A chain map from a complex in degrees at most −1-1 has top image in ker⁡(2,4)\ker(2,4), so its top component factors through that inclusion; the adjunction proves the corresponding statement for all derived maps. Choosing another generator, such as (2,−1)(2,-1), changes its coordinates but not the comparison into CC: the unique isomorphism preserving that map changes sign. This is a concrete use of the uniqueness just proved. The nonzero boundary in (T2) likewise cannot be changed while fixing its triangle maps.

Orthogonality recognizes the two halves

An object already in a half is unchanged by that half’s truncation, because it represents its own restricted Hom functor. Triangle (4) then gives

X∈𝒟≤n⟺τ≥n+1X=0,X∈𝒟≥n⟺τ≤n−1X=0,𝒟≤n=⟂𝒟≥n+1,𝒟≥n=(𝒟≤n−1)⟂.(5) \begin{aligned} X\in\mathcal D^{\le n}&\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \tau^{\ge n+1}X=0,\\ X\in\mathcal D^{\ge n}&\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \tau^{\le n-1}X=0,\\ \mathcal D^{\le n}&={}^{\perp}\mathcal D^{\ge n+1},\qquad \mathcal D^{\ge n}=(\mathcal D^{\le n-1})^{\perp}. \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(5)}

Here a left or right orthogonal means vanishing of every Hom to or from objects in the other subcategory. To check the converse in the right-orthogonal identity, let U=τ≤n−1XU=\tau^{\le n-1}X. The hypothesis kills Hom⁡(U,X)\operatorname{Hom}(U,X); adjunction identifies this with Hom⁡(U,U)\operatorname{Hom}(U,U), so its identity is zero and U=0U=0. The left-orthogonal converse uses V=τ≥n+1XV=\tau^{\ge n+1}X and Hom⁡(V,V)≃Hom⁡(X,V)\operatorname{Hom}(V,V)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(X,V). The first two equivalences then finish both arguments.

Each half is extension closed. For example, if X′→X→X′′→X′[1]X'\to X\to X^{\prime\prime}\to X'[1] is distinguished and both outside objects lie in 𝒟≥n\mathcal D^{\ge n}, the Hom sequence kills Hom⁡(W,X)\operatorname{Hom}(W,X) for every W∈𝒟≤n−1W\in\mathcal D^{\le n-1}. Formula (5) puts XX in the same half. The other half follows by the dual Hom sequence. In particular the heart is extension closed. It contains zero and finite biproducts, using the split triangle A→A⊕B→BA\to A\oplus B\to B; it is an additive category.

Cuts commute and finite intervals have a canonical object

The adjunctions give the shift and nested-cut formulas

τ≤n(X[m])≃(τ≤n+mX)[m],τ≥n(X[m])≃(τ≥n+mX)[m],τ≤aτ≤b≃τ≤min⁡(a,b),τ≥aτ≥b≃τ≥max⁡(a,b).(6) \begin{aligned} \tau^{\le n}(X[m])&\simeq(\tau^{\le n+m}X)[m],& \tau^{\ge n}(X[m])&\simeq(\tau^{\ge n+m}X)[m],\\ \tau^{\le a}\tau^{\le b}&\simeq\tau^{\le\min(a,b)},& \tau^{\ge a}\tau^{\ge b}&\simeq\tau^{\ge\max(a,b)}. \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(6)}

For the first row, shift the representing Hom problem. For nested lower cuts with a≤ba\le b, the composite τ≤aτ≤bX\tau^{\le a}\tau^{\le b}X has the same Hom functor on 𝒟≤a\mathcal D^{\le a} as XX, so it is τ≤aX\tau^{\le a}X. In the other order τ≤b\tau^{\le b} fixes the object already in 𝒟≤a\mathcal D^{\le a}. The upper-cut identities use the dual adjunction. Each comparison is the unique one preserving the units or counits, hence is natural.

The mixed cuts satisfy

τ≥bτ≤aX≃τ≤aτ≥bX=:τ[b,a]X,τ[b,a]X=0(b>a).(7) \tau^{\ge b}\tau^{\le a}X \simeq\tau^{\le a}\tau^{\ge b}X =:\tau^{[b,a]}X,\qquad \tau^{[b,a]}X=0\quad(b>a). \qquad\text{(7)}

If b>ab>a, the first composite is zero by (5). The second is zero by the dual assertion.

If b≤ab\le a, set P=τ≤b−1XP=\tau^{\le b-1}X, Q=τ≤aXQ=\tau^{\le a}X, R=τ≥bXR=\tau^{\ge b}X, N=τ≥a+1XN=\tau^{\ge a+1}X. Adjunction gives the map P→QP\to Q compatible with their maps to XX. The octahedron for P→Q→XP\to Q\to X supplies one object MM and triangles

P⟶Q⟶M⟶P[1],M⟶R⟶N⟶M[1].(8) P\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow P[1], \qquad M\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow M[1]. \qquad\text{(8)}

The first puts MM in 𝒟≤a\mathcal D^{\le a}, by extension closure applied to Q→M→P[1]Q\to M\to P[1]. The second puts MM in 𝒟≥b\mathcal D^{\ge b}, applied to N[−1]→M→RN[-1]\to M\to R. The triangles are consequently the truncation triangles of QQ at b−1b-1 and of RR at aa. Their adjunctions identify MM with both sides of (7), compatibly with the maps Q→M→RQ\to M\to R. This specifies the mixed comparison independently of choices and makes it natural.

On the same cone, both orders of the interval cut [0,0][0,0] give ℤ/2\mathbb Z/2, with the target quotient; both orders of [−1,−1][-1,-1] give ℤ[1]\mathbb Z[1], with the kernel inclusion. The interval comparison in (7) identifies these maps, rather than merely identifying abstract isomorphism classes.

Cohomology and the boundedness needed for detection

Define heart-valued functors

Htj(X)=Ht0(X[j]),Ht0(X)=τ[0,0]X,Htj(X)≃(τ[j,j]X)[j]∈𝒜.(9) H_t^j(X)=H_t^0(X[j]),\qquad H_t^0(X)=\tau^{[0,0]}X,\qquad H_t^j(X)\simeq(\tau^{[j,j]}X)[j]\in\mathcal A. \qquad\text{(9)}

τ≤j−1X⟶τ≤jX⟶Htj(X)[−j]⟶(τ≤j−1X)[1].(10) \tau^{\le j-1}X\longrightarrow\tau^{\le j}X \longrightarrow H_t^j(X)[-j]\longrightarrow(\tau^{\le j-1}X)[1]. \qquad\text{(10)}

Indeed its third object is τ[j,j]X\tau^{[j,j]}X, by (8).

If X∈𝒟≥a∩𝒟≤bX\in\mathcal D^{\ge a}\cap\mathcal D^{\le b}, (10) is a finite filtration from zero at j=a−1j=a-1 to XX at j=bj=b. Each successive quotient is Htj(X)[−j]H_t^j(X)[-j]. It retains its connecting maps; the existence of these triangles supplies no splitting.

If XX is bounded below for this t-structure, then

X∈𝒟≥0⟺Htj(X)=0(j<0).(11) X\in\mathcal D^{\ge0} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H_t^j(X)=0\quad(j<0). \qquad\text{(11)}

For the reverse implication take X∈𝒟≥aX\in\mathcal D^{\ge a}. Dually, for XX bounded above, X∈𝒟≤0X\in\mathcal D^{\le0} exactly when all Htj(X)H_t^j(X) with j>0j>0 vanish. These bounds cannot be omitted from an arbitrary t-structure. A different sufficient hypothesis, nondegeneracy of the whole t-structure, will give detection even for unbounded objects below.

Check the bound at this point. For the degenerate t-structure (𝒟,0)(\mathcal D,0), every lower cut is identity, every upper cut is zero, and all heart cohomology vanishes. A nonzero object belongs to no 𝒟≥a\mathcal D^{\ge a}. Thus the finite induction proving (11) cannot even start. The complete degenerate example below tests exactly this absent hypothesis.

Two cohomology objects of a cone give the kernel and cokernel

Let f:A→Bf:A\to B be a map in 𝒜\mathcal A, and choose a distinguished triangle A→B→C→A[1]A\to B\to C\to A[1]. Extension closure puts CC in 𝒟≥−1∩𝒟≤0\mathcal D^{\ge-1}\cap\mathcal D^{\le0}. Thus

K=Ht−1(C)=τ≤0(C[−1]),Q=Ht0(C)=τ≥0Cbelong to 𝒜.(12) K=H_t^{-1}(C)=\tau^{\le0}(C[-1]),\qquad Q=H_t^0(C)=\tau^{\ge0}C \quad\text{belong to }\mathcal A. \qquad\text{(12)}

The canonical arrows are K→C[−1]→AK\to C[-1]\to A and B→C→QB\to C\to Q. For every W∈𝒜W\in\mathcal A, the Hom sequence gives Hom⁡(W,C[−1])≃ker⁡(Hom⁡(W,A)→Hom⁡(W,B))\operatorname{Hom}(W,C[-1])\simeq\ker(\operatorname{Hom}(W,A)\to\operatorname{Hom}(W,B)), because Hom⁡(W,B[−1])=0\operatorname{Hom}(W,B[-1])=0. Dually, Hom⁡(C,W)\operatorname{Hom}(C,W) is the kernel of Hom⁡(B,W)→Hom⁡(A,W)\operatorname{Hom}(B,W)\to\operatorname{Hom}(A,W), since Hom⁡(A[1],W)=0\operatorname{Hom}(A[1],W)=0. The octahedron makes image and coimage identical

An additive category with kernels and cokernels in which every coimage-to-image map is an isomorphism is abelian. We have proved all three properties. Thus the heart is abelian.

Nondegenerate cohomology detects every object

The t-structure is nondegenerate when its two infinitely distant tails contain only zero:

⋂n∈ℤ𝒟≤n={0},⋂n∈ℤ𝒟≥n={0}.(17) \bigcap_{n\in\mathbb Z}\mathcal D^{\le n}=\{0\},\qquad \bigcap_{n\in\mathbb Z}\mathcal D^{\ge n}=\{0\}. \qquad\text{(17)}

Theorem. Under (17), an object is zero if and only if every HtjH_t^j vanishes. For every integer mm,

X∈𝒟≤m⟺Htj(X)=0(j>m),X∈𝒟≥m⟺Htj(X)=0(j<m).(18) \begin{aligned} X\in\mathcal D^{\le m}&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H_t^j(X)=0\quad(j>m),\\ X\in\mathcal D^{\ge m}&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H_t^j(X)=0\quad(j<m). \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(18)}

A morphism is an isomorphism if and only if all its cohomology morphisms are isomorphisms. No boundedness, completeness, or convergence of an infinite tower is assumed.

Proof. First suppose all cohomology of XX vanishes. Fix an integer nn. The successive lower-cut triangles (10) identify τ≤nX\tau^{\le n}X with τ≤rX\tau^{\le r}X for every r<nr<n, by a finite sequence of isomorphisms for each such rr. Thus τ≤nX\tau^{\le n}X belongs to every lower half: for r≥nr\ge n use nesting, and for r<nr<n use the isomorphism just obtained. The first intersection in (17) makes it zero. Triangle (4) now identifies XX with τ≥n+1X\tau^{\ge n+1}X. This holds for every nn, so the second intersection in (17) makes X=0X=0. The converse is immediate.

If Htj(X)=0H_t^j(X)=0 for j>mj>m, let V=τ≥m+1XV=\tau^{\ge m+1}X. The commuting-cut identities (6)–(7) show that Htj(V)=0H_t^j(V)=0 for j≤mj\le m and that Htj(V)≃Htj(X)H_t^j(V)\simeq H_t^j(X) for j>mj>m. Thus all its cohomology vanishes, so V=0V=0. Formula (5) gives X≤mX\le m. Conversely an object in that half has the stated vanishing by (7). The other equivalence follows in the same way with U=τ≤m−1XU=\tau^{\le m-1}X.

Finally, for a morphism u:X→Yu:X\to Y, choose a cone triangle. If all Htj(u)H_t^j(u) are isomorphisms, exactness of (15) makes every cohomology object of the cone zero. The first assertion makes the cone zero, so uu is an isomorphism. A functor takes isomorphisms to isomorphisms, proving the converse. ▫\square

The standard t-structure on D(ℬ)D(\mathcal B) is nondegenerate: belonging to every lower half, or every upper half, forces ordinary complex cohomology to vanish in every degree. Such a complex is isomorphic to zero in the derived category by its definition through quasi-isomorphisms. Thus (18) applies to unbounded complexes as well. A bounded t-structure also satisfies (17): an object in every lower half has a lower bound and is therefore zero by orthogonality; the other tail is dual. The degenerate example below fails (17), so it remains a genuine limitation of cohomological detection.

Exercises with complete solutions

A shift changes the degree cut

Difficulty: Introductory.

Let A≠0A\ne0 lie in the heart, and put X=A[−2]X=A[-2]. Compute its heart cohomology, τ≤1X\tau^{\le1}X, τ≥2X\tau^{\ge2}X, and τ≤0(X[2])\tau^{\le0}(X[2]).

Solution. The sole cohomology is Ht2X=AH_t^2X=A. Formula (7) gives τ≤1X=0\tau^{\le1}X=0 and τ≥2X=X\tau^{\ge2}X=X. Since X[2]=AX[2]=A, its lower zero cut is AA. Formula (6) gives the same result as (τ≤2X)[2](\tau^{\le2}X)[2]. The sign is n+mn+m in the shifted cut.

A smart cutoff keeps a kernel

Difficulty: Intermediate.

In Db(ℤ)D^b(\mathbb Z), let CC be ℤ2→(2,4)ℤ\mathbb Z^2\xrightarrow{(2,4)}\mathbb Z in degrees minus one and zero. Find its cohomology and its two cuts at −1-1 and 00. Compare with deleting the degree-zero term.

Solution. The kernel is the free subgroup generated by (−2,1)(-2,1), and the cokernel is ℤ/2\mathbb Z/2. Thus H−1C=ℤH^{-1}C=\mathbb Z, H0C=ℤ/2H^0C=\mathbb Z/2, τ≤−1C=ℤ[1]\tau^{\le-1}C=\mathbb Z[1] and τ≥0C=(ℤ/2)[0]\tau^{\ge0}C=(\mathbb Z/2)[0]. The lower comparison includes the kernel subgroup into ℤ2\mathbb Z^2; the upper comparison is the quotient of the target. Deleting the degree-zero term instead produces ℤ2[1]\mathbb Z^2[1], with the wrong negative cohomology. A smart cutoff must change its boundary term.

An epimorphism has a shifted kernel as its cone

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Take the quotient f:ℤ→ℤ/6f:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/6 in the standard heart. Compute the heart kernel and cokernel from its cone, and determine whether that cone lies in the heart.

Solution. The cone is represented by ℤ→ℤ/6\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/6 in degrees minus one and zero. Its negative cohomology is 6ℤ≃ℤ6\mathbb Z\simeq\mathbb Z; its degree-zero cohomology is zero. Formula (12) gives kernel 6ℤ6\mathbb Z with its actual inclusion and cokernel zero. Therefore ff is epic and not monic. The cone is 6ℤ[1]6\mathbb Z[1], so it is outside the nonzero standard heart. An abelian heart does not require cones of its maps to stay in that heart.

A nonzero connecting map remembers a nonsplit extension

Difficulty: Advanced.

Find the connecting class of 0→ℤ→6ℤ→ℤ/6→00\to\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{6}\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/6\to0 in Db(ℤ)D^b(\mathbb Z). Explain why the triangle does not split.

Solution. Represent ℤ/6\mathbb Z/6 by the free complex ℤ→6ℤ\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{6}\mathbb Z in degrees minus one and zero. A chain map from it to ℤ[1]\mathbb Z[1] is multiplication by an integer on the negative-degree term. Chain homotopies identify two such integers exactly when their difference is divisible by six. The boundary map of the cone triangle is the identity on that term, so its class is 1∈ℤ/61\in\mathbb Z/6, which is nonzero. The free complex is a bounded projective resolution, so this calculation computes the derived maps. A split triangle has zero boundary. Equivalently a splitting would lift 1∈ℤ/61\in\mathbb Z/6 to a six-torsion element of ℤ\mathbb Z, which cannot occur. The connecting arrow retains information lost by listing the three terms.

Mono and epi tests use different cone degrees

Difficulty: Intermediate.

For an arbitrary heart map f:A→Bf:A\to B, show that it is monic exactly when Ht−1(Cone⁡f)=0H_t^{-1}(\operatorname{Cone}f)=0, and epic exactly when Ht0(Cone⁡f)=0H_t^0(\operatorname{Cone}f)=0. What if both vanish?

Solution. Formula (12) identifies those objects with the kernel and cokernel. In an abelian category a map is monic exactly when its kernel is zero, and epic exactly when its cokernel is zero: this follows from their universal properties and the image/coimage factorization. If both vanish, the cone lies in [−1,0][-1,0] with no cohomology; its one truncation triangle makes it zero. The original triangle therefore makes ff an isomorphism. This also follows from (13), whose two outer defects are zero.

A finite filtration and its interval cut

Difficulty: Intermediate.

In Db(k)D^b(k) for a field, take X=k[1]⊕k[−2]X=k[1]\oplus k[-2]. Compute its nonzero cohomology, τ≤0X\tau^{\le0}X, τ≥1X\tau^{\ge1}X, and both mixed cuts for the interval [−1,0][-1,0]. State what the general filtration would retain for a nonsplit object.

Solution. We have H−1X=kH^{-1}X=k, H2X=kH^2X=k, and no other cohomology. The lower zero cut is k[1]k[1], and the upper one cut is k[−2]k[-2]. Both τ≥−1τ≤0X\tau^{\ge-1}\tau^{\le0}X and τ≤0τ≥−1X\tau^{\le0}\tau^{\ge-1}X are k[1]k[1], as (7) predicts. The finite filtration has its two nonzero quotients in degrees −1-1 and 22; the intervening zero quotients give isomorphisms. This particular direct sum splits. For a general object, (10) retains the actual boundary maps between successive stages; its cohomology objects alone do not specify or split those extensions.

An invisible object in a degenerate t-structure

Difficulty: Advanced.

On a nonzero triangulated category, check the t-structure (𝒟≤0,𝒟≥0)=(𝒟,0)(\mathcal D^{\le0},\mathcal D^{\ge0})=(\mathcal D,0). What are its heart, truncations and cohomology? Identify the absent bound that prevents (11) from forcing every object to be zero.

Solution. Nesting and orthogonality hold, and the required triangle is X→1X→0→X[1]X\xrightarrow{1}X\to0\to X[1]. Every lower cut is the identity and every upper cut is zero. The heart and all heart cohomology are zero. Nevertheless nonzero objects remain in 𝒟\mathcal D. Such an object belongs to no 𝒟≥a\mathcal D^{\ge a}, since every upper half is zero. It is not bounded below. Formula (11) explicitly requires that bound, while finite-interval detection requires both bounds. The opposite degenerate t-structure exchanges this missing condition with boundedness above.

Kernels in a shifted heart

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Shift the standard t-structure on Db(ℤ)D^b(\mathbb Z) to the cut s=2s=2. For multiplication by six on ℤ[−2]\mathbb Z[-2], compute the kernel and cokernel in the new heart and compare with standard cohomological degrees.

Solution. The shifted heart consists of abelian groups placed in degree two, namely 𝒜[−2]\mathcal A[-2]. Shifting the cone calculation for multiplication by six gives a cone with standard H1=0H^1=0, H2=ℤ/6H^2=\mathbb Z/6, and no other cohomology. In the shifted structure, Htsj(X)=Htj+2(X)[−2]H_{t_s}^j(X)=H_t^{j+2}(X)[-2], by (6) and (9). Thus its negative heart cohomology is zero and its degree-zero heart cohomology is (ℤ/6)[−2](\mathbb Z/6)[-2]. Formula (12) gives kernel zero and that cokernel. The object remains in degree two as an object of the original derived category; the heart’s zero label is relative to its shifted cut.

Optional constructible duality application

Let AA now be Noetherian, commutative, of finite global dimension, and let XX be a finite-dimensional real analytic manifold with the standing bounded constructible conventions. Standard truncation preserves Dℝ-cb(AX)D^b_{\mathbb R\text{-c}}(A_X). In a common local stratification, kernels and cokernels of maps of finite locally constant modules are locally constant and finitely generated; Noetherianity retains finiteness. Finite global dimension makes the bounded finite coefficient cohomology perfect. Truncation preserves the global degree bounds. Thus restriction of the standard t-structure is valid.

The written constructible duality equivalence DXD_X, with DX(F[1])=(DXF)[−1]D_X(F[1])=(D_XF)[-1] and its actual biduality map, transports the opposite t-structure back to this same category. It gives

d𝒟≤0=DX(𝒟≥0),d𝒟≥0=DX(𝒟≤0).(16) {}^d\mathcal D^{\le0}=D_X(\mathcal D^{\ge0}),\qquad {}^d\mathcal D^{\ge0}=D_X(\mathcal D^{\le0}). \qquad\text{(16)}

Reversal of triangles, Hom directions and shifts verifies every axiom by (2). Its heart is DXD_X of the ordinary constructible heart, with arrows reversed. This proves the claimed t-structure using the exact existing duality theorem; it does not identify it with a middle perverse t-structure.
