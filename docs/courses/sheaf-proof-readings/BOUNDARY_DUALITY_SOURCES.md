# Sources and scope of the boundary and duality prerequisite proofs

This account describes the six programme proofs included in the boundary and duality downloads and five geometric companion proofs linked online. All eleven have independently written English exposition. Their scholarly sources remain credited. Each source link identifies a freely accessible edition; the coefficient and category assumptions of a human source are not silently enlarged when it is cited.

Original programme text and programme figure sources are dedicated to CC0 1.0. The cited human works retain their own terms. The readings contain their own arguments and examples; no human PDF pages or source diagrams are included in this download.

## Finite resolutions and proper supports

[Read the programme proof](https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/open-math-courses-public-public/blob/main/docs/courses/SH-02/src/finite-resolutions-and-proper-supports.md).

The topological inputs are locally compact Hausdorff spaces with a uniform finite c-soft dimension. The resolution criterion is tested on all abelian sheaves and all open subsets before it is transferred to module coefficients. The finite integral model constructs the exceptional adjoint and fixes its trace. Kernel tensor and mixed projection arguments retain the separately stated commutativity and finite-global-dimension assumptions.

The classical source is Pierre Schapira, [*An Introduction to Sheaves on Grothendieck Topologies*, 1 August 2026](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~pierre.schapira/LectNotes/SHV.pdf), §§4.3–4.6 and 4.9. Its compact-support and soft-sheaf facts motivate the construction. The programme supplies its explicit finite resolution and both mixed comparison maps; a reference to Brown representability or an omitted associativity calculation is not used as their proof.

## Derived module coefficients

[Read the programme proof](https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/open-math-courses-public-public/blob/main/docs/courses/SH-02/src/derived-module-coefficients.md).

For every commutative unital ring, the comparison concerns all module complexes, including unbounded complexes. Unit-cell generation, the bar adjunction and the balanced tensor map identify the derived coefficient category with module spectra. No finite global dimension is needed for this coefficient equivalence.

The source is Jacob Lurie, [*Higher Algebra*, 18 September 2017](https://www.math.ias.edu/~lurie/papers/HA.pdf#page=1212), Theorem 7.1.2.13, pp. 1212–1213. The compact-unit mechanism is credited; the programme develops its cell and bar calculations explicitly.

## Bounded section recognition

[Read the programme proof](https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/open-math-courses-public-public/blob/main/docs/courses/SH-02/src/bounded-section-recognition.md).

This is the all-module comparison on the open-set site of an arbitrary topological space. Its classical image consists of objects with one lower section bound on every open. The proof uses injective mapping complexes, bounded stalk detection and the actual truncation and telescope maps. It does not identify every unbounded ordinary infinity-sheaf with a classical derived sheaf.

The source is Lurie, [*Derived Algebraic Geometry VIII*, 5 November 2011](https://www.math.ias.edu/~lurie/papers/DAG-VIII.pdf#page=31), Proposition 2.1.3, Remark 2.1.5 and Proposition 2.1.8 with Lemmas 2.1.9–2.1.10, pp. 31–35. The module-sheaf t-structure is the stated categorical model; the topological bounded recognition argument is supplied in the programme reading.

## Compact supports and covariant Verdier duality

[Read the programme proof](https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/open-math-courses-public-public/blob/main/docs/courses/SH-02/src/modern-proper-support-construction.md).

The coefficient category is stable and has all small limits and colimits. The construction also covers nonpresentable categories. Compact restrictions and the complement cofiber calculation give a support cosheaf and its inverse. Conjugation by these actual equivalences supplies composition, proper direct image and open extension. This statement is distinct from internal-Hom biduality for arbitrary modules.

The sources are Lurie's [*Higher Topos Theory*, inspected author PDF revised 9 April 2017](https://www.math.ias.edu/~lurie/papers/HTT.pdf#page=788), Lemma 7.3.4.8 and Theorem 7.3.4.9, printed pp. 770–773 (PDF pp. 788–791); [*Higher Algebra*, §5.5.5](https://www.math.ias.edu/~lurie/papers/HA.pdf#page=992), pp. 992–994; and Marco Volpe, [*The six operations in topology*, published 2025 edition](https://epub.uni-regensburg.de/78207/), Theorem 5.10, PDF pp. 42–45. The 2017 author PDF of *Higher Topos Theory* retains the book's 2009 publication identity; it is the exact inspected copy.

## Proper base change and projection

[Read the programme proof](https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/open-math-courses-public-public/blob/main/docs/courses/SH-02/src/general-basechange-and-tensor.md).

The proof first treats space-valued proper base change, then its spectral and arbitrary stable coefficient comparisons. It keeps the actual adjunction mate and the direction of supported multiplication. The external-product target remains the tensor sheaf model when the coefficient tensor is not itself complete. The bounded classical specialization separately retains the ring dimension needed for tensor bounds and the map dimension needed for exceptional inverse image.

The sources are [*Higher Topos Theory*](https://www.math.ias.edu/~lurie/papers/HTT.pdf#page=771), §§7.3.1–7.3.4, including Proposition 7.3.1.11, Theorem 7.3.1.16 and Corollary 7.3.1.18 (PDF pp. 771–774 for these steps); and [Volpe's published paper](https://epub.uni-regensburg.de/78207/), Propositions 6.9 and 6.13–6.14, PDF pp. 54–56. The programme expands the compact and closed-graph arguments and the specific transported maps.

## Finite holomorphic maps with weak coefficients

[Read the programme proof](https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/open-math-courses-public-public/blob/main/docs/courses/SH-02/src/weak-coefficients.md).

The programme result covers every commutative coefficient ring and bounded complexes with weak complex-constructibility. Infinitely generated and nonperfect stalks are allowed. Its finite Morse filtration uses nonzero quotients and subsequent injections, not a splitting into field-valued summands. The actual nearby-cycle unit, its cone and the finite direct-sum stalk maps are retained through ramification and positive-codimension images.

The credited sources are Kashiwara–Schapira, [*Microlocal Study of Sheaves*, Astérisque 128 (1985)](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~pierre.schapira/BooksMono/Ast128.pdf), for the classical microsupport and complex-constructibility framework; Maxim–Schürmann, [*Constructible sheaf complexes in complex geometry and applications*, the 94-page author manuscript dated 2 June 2021](https://people.math.wisc.edu/~lmaxim/handbook.pdf), especially the coefficient convention on p. 6 and the normal-Morse discussion on pp. 31–33; and David B. Massey, [*A Little Microlocal Morse Theory*, arXiv v2, 18 January 2001](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0006185v2), for the visible-conormal and isolated-support Morse arguments, including Theorem 5.3 on pp. 16–17.

The Maxim–Schürmann manuscript works with a Noetherian commutative ring of finite global dimension. Massey's displayed decomposition uses a principal ideal domain. The arbitrary-ring extension here is proved by the programme's WC1–WC6 and is not attributed to either source with its wider coefficient scope. The admitted author manuscript is a specific free edition; this account does not assert byte identity with the separately published Springer chapter.

## Normal Morse data and change of coefficients

[Read the programme proof](https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/open-math-courses-public-public/blob/main/docs/courses/SH-02/src/normal-morse-coefficients.md).

The normal objects, supported tests and full conormal detection use bounded weakly complex-constructible complexes over every commutative unital ring. Their comparisons retain the original restriction and specialization maps. The later perfection and residue-field conclusions keep their separate perfect-stalk and finite-global-dimension hypotheses. The normal-degree extension over the original ring is proved in WC1–WC2; it does not follow by changing the coefficient convention in a cited source.

The normal-Morse and visible-conormal antecedents are Maxim–Schürmann's [94-page author manuscript dated 2 June 2021](https://people.math.wisc.edu/~lmaxim/handbook.pdf#page=27), Definition 3.9, Theorem 3.12 and Propositions 3.13–3.14, pp. 27–30, together with Corollary 3.25 and Example 3.26, pp. 35–36. The separately cited [arXiv:2105.13069v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.13069v2#page=28) was posted on 2 June 2021 and prints a manuscript date of 3 June 2021. These are distinct free copies. Their inspected theorem and coefficient passages agree; neither copy is identified here with the paid published chapter. Both retain the Noetherian, finite-global-dimension convention stated on p. 6.

The geometric antecedent is Mark Goresky and Robert MacPherson, [*Stratified Morse Theory*, the author-hosted 279-page copy](https://www.math.ias.edu/~goresky/pdf/SMT.pdf). Its bibliographic page identifies the 1988 first hardcover edition and its softcover reprint. The moving-wall statements are on printed pp. 71–73 (PDF pp. 80–82), the normal-slice rank arguments on printed pp. 84–85 (PDF pp. 93–94), and the normal/tangential product on printed pp. 100–102 (PDF pp. 109–111). The programme supplies its explicit projection estimates, parameter families, proper pair comparisons and coefficient maps in E07, NMG and the continuation sections.

Controlled lifts and flows are credited to John Mather, [*Notes on Topological Stability*, the 34-page typeset copy](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/surgery/mather.pdf), Proposition 7.1, Lemma 7.3, Proposition 9.1, Proposition 10.1 and Corollary 10.2 (PDF pp. 15–18 and 21–25). The [156-page original 1970 manuscript](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/matherj.pdf) is a separate source copy; the corresponding proposition starts are PDF pp. 113, 127 and 133. The programme's controlled product checks properness and submersivity on every retained stratum, including boundary faces. Compatible triangulation is supplied by the [complete programme construction TC1–TC5 and its source account](https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/open-math-courses-public-public/blob/main/docs/courses/SH-02/src/compatible-whitney-triangulation.md#proof-bindings), which credits the Valette and Kankaanrinta antecedents.

## Perverse degrees and bounded Whitney recollement

[Read the programme proof](https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/open-math-courses-public-public/blob/main/docs/courses/SH-02/src/perverse-normal-morse-inputs.md).

The degree tests distinguish stalks, point costalks and stratum costalks. On a complex stratum of dimension d, the stalk upper threshold is minus d, the point-costalk lower threshold is d, and the stratum-costalk lower threshold is minus d. The orientation shift has real dimension twice d. The programme constructs the original-stratum products, intrinsic dimension layers, finite link cores and natural truncation comparisons needed to realize these tests.

The source is the [2 June 2021 Maxim–Schürmann author manuscript](https://people.math.wisc.edu/~lmaxim/handbook.pdf#page=12), Definition 2.16 and Theorem 2.18, pp. 12–13; Theorem 3.19 and its complex-link triangles, pp. 32–33; and Corollary 3.25 with Example 3.26, pp. 35–36. The source works over a Noetherian commutative ring of finite global dimension. The programme's bounded weak recollement over every commutative ring is proved in WC1, using its specified localization and truncation maps. Assertions of finite-dimensional stalks retain the separate field and finite-coefficient hypotheses. The abstract gluing proof and its classical antecedent remain identified in the programme reading.

## Finite nearby-cycle models

[Read the programme proof](https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/open-math-courses-public-public/blob/main/docs/courses/SH-02/src/general-nearby-cycle-models.md).

The logarithmic covering model keeps the actual adjunction unit, its unshifted cone, the deck transformation and the normal-slice comparison. If the function is identically zero on a component, the nearby object is zero and the cone is the original object shifted by one. The perfect-stalk formulation and its residue-field comparison retain their coefficient assumptions. WC3 supplies the bounded arbitrary-module extension without changing those separate perfection conclusions.

The finite Milnor-fibre and monodromy antecedents are Maxim–Schürmann, [the author manuscript, Proposition 4.11 and Corollary 4.14, p. 65, with Remark 4.18, p. 66](https://people.math.wisc.edu/~lmaxim/handbook.pdf#page=65); these passages are also present in the [inspected arXiv v2 copy](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.13069v2#page=65). The programme proves its cofinal finite model, collar localization and specialization square using its relative-conormal and controlled-geometry providers. Mather's controlled lifts and proper products and Goresky–MacPherson's moving-wall geometry have the exact source locators listed above; their hypotheses are checked for the chosen parameter families.

## Holomorphic critical support

[Read the programme proof](https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/open-math-courses-public-public/blob/main/docs/courses/SH-02/src/general-critical-support.md).

The criterion concerns the closed support of vanishing cycles at the value of the function at the point. A statement using the union over all levels is specialized only after retaining that value condition. The isolated perturbation argument keeps every critical point in the compact trap, the radial boundary margin and the actual endpoint square. Its finite filtration over the original coefficient ring uses nonzero quotients and injections; it requires no canonical direct-sum splitting.

The antecedent is David B. Massey, [*A Little Microlocal Morse Theory*, arXiv:math/0006185v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0006185v2): Theorem 4.13 and Corollary 4.15, PDF pp. 13–14; Lemma 5.2 and the perturbation discussion, PDF pp. 15–16; and Theorem 5.3, PDF pp. 16–17. Massey works over a principal ideal domain and describes a noncanonical decomposition. The programme instead proves the simultaneous conormal preparation, proper radial exhaustion, compact-band continuation and module filtration at its explicitly retained analytic and controlled-geometry foundations. The source's coefficient convention and the full closed-support quantifier remain visible.

## Finite holomorphic microsupport

[Read the programme proof](https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/open-math-courses-public-public/blob/main/docs/courses/SH-02/src/finite-holomorphic-microsupport.md).

The finite-fibre restriction and support maps are constructed branch by branch. The reverse inclusion uses the finite cotangent-image geometry, a symmetric-Hessian test and the integer local Morse filtration before residue-field recovery. The primary finite/perfect route and the bounded weak arbitrary-ring extension in WC1–WC6 retain their respective hypotheses. The nearby-cycle and critical-support readings above also supply the alternative route, with its actual specialization map.

The classical microsupport and complex-constructible direct-image framework is credited to Kashiwara–Schapira, [*Microlocal Study of Sheaves*, Astérisque 128 (1985), §8.6](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~pierre.schapira/BooksMono/Ast128.pdf#page=157). Proposition 8.6.1, printed pp. 154–155 (PDF pp. 157–158), gives the two direct-image upper inclusions under its own constructibility assumptions. This source passage is not cited as a proof of the reverse inclusion or of an arbitrary-ring extension. Those deductions use the programme's finite-map geometry, normal-Morse and perverse providers, integer filtration and coefficient arguments. Massey's Corollary 4.15 supplies the credited critical-support antecedent at its PID scope; the wider programme statement has its own proof.
