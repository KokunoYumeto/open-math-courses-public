SH02-MST — Detecting and removing directional obstructions

Local unit: SH02-MST. The directional test equivalence, propagation theorem, and cone cutoff theorem are proved below relative to the explicitly stated sheaf-theoretic imports.

Throughout, kk is a commutative ring of finite global dimension, XX is a finite-dimensional real manifold countable at infinity, and F∈Db(kX)F\in D^b(k_X). No constructibility or finiteness of stalks is assumed. The notation supp⁡(F)\operatorname{supp}(F) means the closure of the union of the supports of its cohomology sheaves. A closed convex cone contains its vertex 00; it is pointed when it contains no nonzero vector together with its negative. For a cone C⊂EC\subset E put

C∘={ξ∈E*:⟨v,ξ⟩≥0 for every v∈C},C∘a=−C∘. C^\circ=\{\xi\in E^*: \langle v,\xi\rangle\geq0\text{ for every }v\in C\}, \qquad C^{\circ a}=-C^\circ.

The antipodal operation and the polar operation have different meanings. In particular, C∘aC^{\circ a} consists of the functionals nonpositive on CC.

The source for the three-test mechanism is Kashiwara and Schapira, Microlocal Study of Sheaves, Astérisque 128 (1985), Theorem 3.1.1, printed pp. 49–53; the cone-projector criterion is Proposition 3.2.2, printed pp. 58–60. The bounded-complex and coefficient conventions agree with Schapira, A short review on microlocal sheaf theory, 19 January 2016, §2.2 and Theorem 2.6. We retain the explicit cap, proper-support, and neighborhood arguments below, so these references identify the mathematics being developed rather than replace a proof.

SH02-MST-TEST — The local experiment

Local identifier: SH02-MST-TEST.

Let x∈Xx\in X, and let ff be real valued and C1C^1 on a neighborhood of xx. Subtracting its value at xx does not change its differential. Define its support test by

𝒯x,f(F)=(RΓ{f≥f(x)}F)x.(T1) \mathcal T_{x,f}(F)= \bigl(R\Gamma_{\{f\geq f(x)\}}F\bigr)_x. \qquad\text{(T1)}

This is a complex of kk-modules, not just a group of sections. If j:{f<f(x)}↪Xj:\{f<f(x)\}\hookrightarrow X denotes the local open inclusion, the localization triangle identifies it as

𝒯x,f(F)⟶Fx⟶(Rj*j−1F)x→+1.(T2) \mathcal T_{x,f}(F)\longrightarrow F_x \longrightarrow (Rj_*j^{-1}F)_x\xrightarrow{+1}. \qquad\text{(T2)}

Thus its vanishing says that restriction towards the smaller values of ff loses neither sections nor their higher obstructions. It does not assert that either term in the middle is zero.

Definition. A covector pp is absent from SS⁡(F)\operatorname{SS}(F) if there is an open neighborhood WW of pp in T*XT^*X such that

𝒯x,f(F)=0whenever (x,dfx)∈W.(T3) \mathcal T_{x,f}(F)=0 \quad\text{whenever }(x,df_x)\in W. \qquad\text{(T3)}

Every C1C^1 function defined near the testing point is allowed. The neighborhood is chosen before the function and the point. Testing only the single differential at pp, or using only linear functions without a neighborhood argument, is not this definition.

For a morphism u:F→Gu:F\to G and a subset A⊂T*XA\subset T^*X, say that uu is a microlocal isomorphism on AA when SS⁡(Cone⁡(u))∩A=⌀\operatorname{SS}(\operatorname{Cone}(u))\cap A=\varnothing. Different cones are isomorphic, so the definition is independent of that choice. Applying (T1) to a triangle proves the equivalent formulation: near each point of AA, all the support-test morphisms 𝒯x,f(u)\mathcal T_{x,f}(u) are isomorphisms. The neighborhood can depend on the point of AA.

SH02-MST-IMPORTS — Exact dependencies of the geometric proofs

Local identifier: SH02-MST-IMPORTS.

The proofs use the following typed results. Their proofs belong to the foundational sheaf course or the directional-topology unit; stating them here does not close their independent verification.

  1. Localization and continuity. Derived sections with support satisfy localization triangles, excision, and the composition rule for intersections of closed supports. For a compact subset of a locally compact Hausdorff space, cohomology of the restriction is the filtered colimit of cohomology over open neighborhoods. For an increasing sequence of open sets, an eventually constant system of cohomology groups computes the cohomology of the union. These assertions apply to bounded-below complexes, with no finite-rank assumption.
  2. Noncharacteristic deformation. Suppose ZZ is Hausdorff, A∈D+(ℤZ)A\in D^+(\mathbb Z_Z), and UtU_t is an increasing family of open sets indexed by ℝ\mathbb R. Assume Ut=⋃s<tUsU_t=\bigcup_{s<t}U_s, assume Ut\Us¯∩supp⁡(A)\overline{U_t\setminus U_s}\cap\operatorname{supp}(A) is compact for s<ts<t, and put Bs=⋂t>sUt\Us¯. B_s=\bigcap_{t>s}\overline{U_t\setminus U_s}. Assume (RΓZ\UtA)z=0\bigl(R\Gamma_{Z\setminus U_t}A\bigr)_z=0 for s≤ts\leq t and z∈Bs\Utz\in B_s\setminus U_t. Then restriction RΓ(⋃tUt;A)→RΓ(Us;A)R\Gamma(\bigcup_tU_t;A)\to R\Gamma(U_s;A) is an isomorphism for every ss. An open parameter interval can be reparametrized monotonically by ℝ\mathbb R. Notice the closures, the support intersection in compactness, and the condition at points outside UtU_t.
  3. Directional projector. For qC:E→ECq_C:E\to E_C, where ECE_C has the ordinary open sets stable under addition by CC, put PC=qC−1RqC*P_C=q_C^{-1}Rq_{C*}. The inverse image is fully faithful on the appropriate derived image, the unit RqC*qC−1≃idRq_{C*}q_C^{-1}\simeq\mathrm{id} is an isomorphism, and the counit PCA→AP_C A\to A satisfies the usual adjunction identities. Localization by a locally closed subset of ECE_C commutes with RqC*Rq_{C*} in the manner proved in the directional-topology unit. The projector is bounded on bounded complexes in the present finite-dimensional setting.
  4. Proper cone fibers. If a kernel correspondence is proper on the closed support of its coefficient complex over the base, its stalk is computed by derived sections on its fiber. We use this only after proving properness. An arbitrary ordinary direct image cannot be replaced by cohomology of a closed fiber.

The first two results now have an explicit limited prerequisite bridge: compact-neighborhood continuity, the open-union comparison, and the full deformation theorem. Their stated hypotheses above match those proofs, including arbitrary Hausdorff spaces and bounded-below complexes. The directional-topology construction is treated in Directional neighborhoods and a sheaf projector. In the deformation import, the closure is taken before the intersection. This is the front used in Astérisque Theorem 1.4.3, printed pp. 30–31, including the endpoint condition. The cited internal proof supplies the stated version; the external theorem alone is not a prerequisite proof. All local identifications below use the natural restriction or adjunction maps supplied by these imports.

SH02-MST-EQUIVALENCE — Three ways of removing one covector

Local identifier: SH02-MST-EQUIVALENCE.

Work in an open subset XX of a finite-dimensional vector space EE, and fix (x0,ξ0)∈T*X(x_0,\xi_0)\in T^*X. The following conditions are equivalent.

  • Test vanishing: (T3) holds at (x0,ξ0)(x_0,\xi_0).
  • A local representative killed by a cone: there are a pointed closed convex cone CC and A∈Db(kE)A\in D^b(k_E) such that ⟨v,ξ0⟩<0(v∈C\{0}),RqC*A=0,(T4) \langle v,\xi_0\rangle<0\quad(v\in C\setminus\{0\}), \qquad Rq_{C*}A=0, \qquad\text{(T4)} and AA agrees with FF on a neighborhood of x0x_0.
  • A compact cap has no extra cohomology: there are such a cone CC, a neighborhood VV of x0x_0, and h>0h>0 such that, for H={x:⟨x−x0,ξ0⟩≥−h},L={x:⟨x−x0,ξ0⟩=−h},(T5) H=\{x:\langle x-x_0,\xi_0\rangle\geq-h\}, \qquad L=\{x:\langle x-x_0,\xi_0\rangle=-h\}, \qquad\text{(T5)} one has H∩(V+C)⊂XH\cap(V+C)\subset X and, for every x∈Vx\in V, restriction is an isomorphism RΓ((x+C)∩H;F)→∼RΓ((x+C)∩L;F).(T6) R\Gamma((x+C)\cap H;F) \xrightarrow{\sim}R\Gamma((x+C)\cap L;F). \qquad\text{(T6)}

Moreover one obtains the same test condition by using CrC^r functions for any integer r≥1r\geq1, smooth functions, or real analytic functions in analytic coordinates. In the analytic case this is a statement about an analytic manifold and its analytic charts, not an assertion that arbitrary smooth manifolds carry a chosen analytic atlas.

We prove the three implications in an order that separates the sheaf operations from the moving-boundary calculation.

SH02-MST-CONE-TO-TEST — A cone-acyclic representative passes every C1C^1 test

Local identifier: SH02-MST-CONE-TO-TEST.

Suppose (T4) holds. For ξ0≠0\xi_0\ne0, compactness of the unit section of CC implies that strict negativity persists for covectors in an open neighborhood of ξ0\xi_0. Hence it suffices to consider ff with dfx∈Int⁡(C∘a)df_x\in\operatorname{Int}(C^{\circ a}).

On a small convex coordinate ball, the derivative of ff in every unit direction of CC is bounded above by a negative constant. Therefore ff decreases along every cone segment contained in the ball. The local open set {f<f(x)}\{f<f(x)\} is the germ of a CC-open set OO: take a smaller negative sublevel patch and add CC. Near xx, a point reached from that patch by a cone segment is still in the negative sublevel set; the segment stays in the original ball until it reaches the smaller ball. Conversely each nearby negative point already belongs to the patch.

We also need a cofinality statement. If Nϵ=Bϵ(x)+CN_\epsilon=B_\epsilon(x)+C, then Nϵ\ON_\epsilon\setminus O is contained in a ball of radius KϵK\epsilon about xx, for a fixed KK and small ϵ\epsilon. Indeed |f(y)−f(x)|≤Mϵ|f(y)-f(x)|\leq M\epsilon at a starting point y∈Bϵ(x)y\in B_\epsilon(x), while advancing a distance (M/c+1)ϵ(M/c+1)\epsilon along CC decreases ff by more than this error. That point enters the negative patch and every later point of the cone ray is in OO. This argument uses a uniform negative directional bound −c-c and compactness of the unit directions. Thus the differences Nϵ\ON_\epsilon\setminus O form a neighborhood basis at xx in the ordinary closed complement of OO.

Localization on ECE_C, followed by this cofinality, identifies the support-test stalk with the corresponding stalk of

qC−1RΓEC\O(RqC*A).(T7) q_C^{-1}R\Gamma_{E_C\setminus O}(Rq_{C*}A). \qquad\text{(T7)}

One can check (T7) by writing the localization triangle on each NϵN_\epsilon and passing to the filtered colimit. No closed-fiber base-change assertion is involved. Its value is zero by (T4). Since AA and FF agree near x0x_0, they pass the same local tests there.

SH02-MST-CAP-TO-CONE — Cap vanishing produces a cone-acyclic representative

Local identifier: SH02-MST-CAP-TO-CONE.

Assume (T6), and first extend FF by zero from XX to EE. Let B=FH\LB=F_{H\setminus L}, with this extension understood. The triangle

B⟶FH⟶FL→+1(T8) B\longrightarrow F_H\longrightarrow F_L\xrightarrow{+1} \qquad\text{(T8)}

shows that RΓ(x+C;B)=0R\Gamma(x+C;B)=0 for x∈Vx\in V. This inference uses the natural restriction map in (T6).

Here is why these closed-cone sections compute the relevant projector stalks. On the kernel support, y−x∈Cy-x\in C and y∈Hy\in H. Strict negativity in (T4) gives a constant c>0c>0 with

⟨y−x,ξ0⟩≤−c‖y−x‖. \langle y-x,\xi_0\rangle\leq-c\lVert y-x\rVert.

When xx ranges in a compact set, the inequality y∈Hy\in H therefore bounds y−xy-x. The kernel support over that compact set is closed and bounded. Its projection is proper. Proper base change and the directional kernel formula give

(PCB)x≃RΓ(x+C;B)=0(x∈V).(T9) (P_C B)_x\simeq R\Gamma(x+C;B)=0\qquad(x\in V). \qquad\text{(T9)}

Shrink VV into the interior of HH. Choose CC-open sets O0⊂O1O_0\subset O_1 so that x0∈Int⁡(O1\O0)x_0\in\operatorname{Int}(O_1\setminus O_0) and O1\O0¯⊂V\overline{O_1\setminus O_0}\subset V. Such a pair exists for every pointed CC: enlarge it slightly to a full-dimensional pointed cone, take a small translate of that cone, and truncate its head by a strictly decreasing linear functional. Its head is bounded by the same inequality used above; shrinking both the aperture patch and the truncation keeps its closure in VV.

Set

A=RΓO1\O0B.(T10) A=R\Gamma_{O_1\setminus O_0}B. \qquad\text{(T10)}

Near x0x_0, the locally closed support set has interior and B=FB=F, so A=FA=F. Directional localization, idempotence, and (T9) give

RqC*A≃RΓO1\O0RqC*B≃RqC*RΓO1\O0PCB=0.(T11) Rq_{C*}A \simeq R\Gamma_{O_1\setminus O_0}Rq_{C*}B \simeq Rq_{C*}R\Gamma_{O_1\setminus O_0}P_CB=0. \qquad\text{(T11)}

This proves (T4). Boundedness follows from the finite-dimensional cohomological bounds in the declared imports.

The support estimate before (T9) is indispensable in comparing this step with the proof of Astérisque Theorem 3.1.1. The closed support of the restricted coefficient complex is contained in the closed halfspace, even though the complex itself is extended by zero from its open part. Intersecting that closed support with the closed cone relation gives a closed set. Over any compact set of vertices the strict polar estimate bounds the cone displacement, so this whole support is compact over that set. Proper base change therefore computes the natural projector stalk and identifies its counit with restriction; an abstract isomorphism of the two cap cohomology groups would not establish (T11).

SH02-MST-ANALYTIC-CAP — An analytic family that pushes a cap onto its base

Local identifier: SH02-MST-ANALYTIC-CAP.

It remains to obtain (T6) from analytic tests. This is the implication that prevents a hidden regularity restriction. Translate and linearly change coordinates so that x0=0x_0=0, ξ0=dx1\xi_0=dx_1, and write x=(x1,x′)x=(x_1,x'). After shrinking the covector neighborhood, choose h>0h>0 and δ>0\delta>0 so small that every cap used below lies in the coordinate domain and every positive multiple of a covector

dx1+η⋅dx′,|η|≤δ,(T12) dx_1+\eta\cdot dx',\qquad |\eta|\leq\delta, \qquad\text{(T12)}

based in that cap lies in the conic enlargement of the vanishing neighborhood. Multiplying a test function by a positive constant leaves its support set unchanged, so testing in that enlargement is legitimate. Put

C={(v1,v′):v1≤−δ|v′|}.(T13) C=\{(v_1,v'):v_1\leq-\delta|v'|\}. \qquad\text{(T13)}

For a point a=(a1,a′)a=(a_1,a') just above the base plane L={x1=−h}L=\{x_1=-h\}, set R=h+a1>0R=h+a_1>0, q=δ2|x′−a′|2q=\delta^2|x'-a'|^2, and for t>0t>0 define

gt(q)=q+(R2−q)tanh⁡(R2−qt),Dt(a)={x:x1<a1−gt(q)}.(T14) g_t(q)=q+(R^2-q)\tanh\!\left(\frac{R^2-q}{t}\right), \qquad D_t(a)=\{x:x_1<a_1-\sqrt{g_t(q)}\}. \qquad\text{(T14)}

These are auxiliary domains for the proof; they do not redefine the cone projector. The function under the square root is positive: it is at least qq, and at q=0q=0 it is strictly positive. Thus each boundary is a real analytic graph. The domains increase continuously with tt, are contained in D(a)=a+Int⁡CD(a)=a+\operatorname{Int}C, and their union is D(a)D(a).

D̃t(a)=Dt(a)∪(D(a)\H),A=Rj*j−1F,(T17) \widetilde D_t(a)=D_t(a)\cup(D(a)\setminus H), \qquad A=Rj_*j^{-1}F, \qquad\text{(T17)}

where j:D(a)↪Ej:D(a)\hookrightarrow E. The closures of all differences in this family lie in the compact closed cap. (RΓE\Dt(a)F)y=0,(RΓE\D(a)F)y=0.(T18) \bigl(R\Gamma_{E\setminus D_t(a)}F\bigr)_y=0, \qquad \bigl(R\Gamma_{E\setminus D(a)}F\bigr)_y=0. \qquad\text{(T18)}

The family (T14) is a different analytic barrier: (T15) fixes its intersection with the base, and (T16) controls its conormals. Noncharacteristic deformation now gives

RΓ(D(a);F)→∼RΓ(D̃t(a);F).(T19) R\Gamma(D(a);F)\xrightarrow{\sim} R\Gamma(\widetilde D_t(a);F). \qquad\text{(T19)}

RΓ(D(a)∩H;F|H)→∼RΓ(Dt(a)∩H;F|H).(T20) R\Gamma(D(a)\cap H;F|_H) \xrightarrow{\sim} R\Gamma(D_t(a)\cap H;F|_H). \qquad\text{(T20)}

SH02-MST-FORMAL — Consequences that do not require a propagation estimate

Local identifier: SH02-MST-FORMAL.

The complement of SS⁡(F)\operatorname{SS}(F) is open by its definition. Multiplication of test functions by positive constants shows that it is invariant under positive scaling of covectors; hence SS⁡(F)\operatorname{SS}(F) is a closed conic subset of T*XT^*X. The zero-covector argument above gives

SS⁡(F)∩TX*X=supp⁡(F),SS⁡(F[m])=SS⁡(F).(T21) \operatorname{SS}(F)\cap T_X^*X=\operatorname{supp}(F), \qquad \operatorname{SS}(F[m])=\operatorname{SS}(F). \qquad\text{(T21)}

The first equality identifies the zero section with XX. The closure in the definition of support is essential: a sheaf may have zero stalk at a boundary point while remaining nonzero in every neighborhood of it.

For a distinguished triangle F1→F2→F3→+1F_1\to F_2\to F_3\xrightarrow{+1}, apply each exact test functor and use that two zero terms force the third to be zero. For distinct i,j,ki,j,k this proves

SS⁡(Fi)⊂SS⁡(Fj)∪SS⁡(Fk).(T22) \operatorname{SS}(F_i)\subset \operatorname{SS}(F_j)\cup\operatorname{SS}(F_k). \qquad\text{(T22)}

Equivalently, the portions of either of two microsupports outside the other must lie in the third. Finite induction on the truncation triangles of a bounded complex gives

SS⁡(F)⊂⋃jSS⁡(ℋjF).(T23) \operatorname{SS}(F)\subset \bigcup_j\operatorname{SS}(\mathcal H^jF). \qquad\text{(T23)}

The reverse inclusion can fail; an explicit example appears below. Truncation is therefore not a microlocally exact operation in the sense suggested by reversing (T23).

The forgetful functor from sheaves of kk-modules to abelian sheaves commutes with the tests and detects their vanishing. One justification uses flabby resolutions: forgetting scalars preserves exactness and flabbiness, and flabby sheaves are acyclic for the support functors appearing in the localization triangles. Therefore microsupport is unchanged on forgetting the coefficient ring. This statement changes the category in which the same complex is viewed; it does not identify the microsupports of unrelated complexes obtained by a nonfaithful change of scalars.

Finally, the test definition uses only C1C^1 coordinate changes. The chain rule carries a test function and its covector to the corresponding test in another chart, while the support set and its local cohomology are topologically unchanged. Microsupport consequently depends only on the C1C^1 structure. The analytic equivalence does not add an analytic-constructibility assumption.

SH02-MST-CUTOFF-FORWARD — What a cone projector already proves

Local identifier: SH02-MST-CUTOFF-FORWARD.

Let X=Y×EX=Y\times E, with YY a manifold, and let C⊂EC\subset E be any closed convex cone. Lines are now permitted. Write qC:X→Y×ECq_C:X\to Y\times E_C and PC=qC−1RqC*P_C=q_C^{-1}Rq_{C*}. The full cutoff assertion is

PCF→∼F⟺SS⁡(F)⊂T*Y×(E×C∘a).(T24) P_CF\xrightarrow{\sim}F \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \operatorname{SS}(F)\subset T^*Y\times(E\times C^{\circ a}). \qquad\text{(T24)}

We first prove the forward implication and the microlocal accuracy of the counit. These parts do not use the propagation theorem whose localization is treated separately below.

Work locally on YY and replace CC by {0}×C\{0\}\times C in the product vector space. If ξ∉C∘a\xi\notin C^{\circ a}, choose v∈Cv\in C with ⟨v,ξ⟩>0\langle v,\xi\rangle>0. There is a full-dimensional pointed cone DD centered tightly around −v-v, with ξ∈Int⁡D∘a\xi\in\operatorname{Int}D^{\circ a}. Its aperture can be chosen so that D+CD+C is the entire vector space: a cone neighborhood of −v-v, translated by sufficiently large positive multiples of vv, contains any prescribed vector. For any nonempty convex DD-open set OO, this gives O+C=EO+C=E.

If F=PCFF=P_CF, the directional section theorem gives

RΓ(O;F)≃RΓ(O+C;F)=RΓ(E;F).(T25) R\Gamma(O;F)\simeq R\Gamma(O+C;F)=R\Gamma(E;F). \qquad\text{(T25)}

The isomorphisms respect restriction. Choose nested convex DD-open sets whose difference has xx in its interior and is locally bounded near xx, as in (T10).
