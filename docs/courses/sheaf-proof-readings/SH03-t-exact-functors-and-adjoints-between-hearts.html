T-exact functors and adjoints between hearts

A triangulated functor can respect the upper degree bound, the lower degree bound, or both. These conditions determine what its degree-zero functor does to the abelian hearts. Respecting one bound gives one-sided exactness and a cohomology comparison on that half of the category. Respecting both bounds gives every truncation and cohomology comparison. An adjunction exchanges the upper-bound condition on its left adjoint with the lower-bound condition on its right adjoint.

The t-exactness and adjunction arguments themselves are proved here.

Tensor and Hom test the two bounds

Use the bounded free resolution P=[ℤ→2ℤ]in degrees −1,0(F1) P=[\,\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{2}\mathbb Z\,] \quad\text{in degrees }-1,0 \qquad\text{(F1)} of ℤ/2\mathbb Z/2. The adjoint triangulated functors L=P⊗−L=P\otimes- and G=Hom⁡(P,−)G=\operatorname{Hom}(P,-) preserve quasi-isomorphisms: their total complexes are cones of multiplication by two, with a shift for Hom, and cones of maps between acyclic complexes are acyclic. The chain tensor–Hom unit and counit descend to the derived categories and retain their triangular identities.

For an input with terms in degrees at most zero, LL still has terms at most zero. For an input with terms at least zero, GG still has terms at least zero. Their failures in the other directions are already explicit: L(ℤ/2)=(ℤ/2)[1]⊕(ℤ/2),G(ℤ)=(ℤ/2)[−1].(F2) L(\mathbb Z/2)=(\mathbb Z/2)[1]\oplus(\mathbb Z/2),\qquad G(\mathbb Z)=(\mathbb Z/2)[-1]. \qquad\text{(F2)} Tensor kills the differential in (F1), while total Hom places multiplication by two in degrees zero and one. Thus one functor preserves the upper bound and the other the lower bound. On degree-zero modules their degree-zero values are respectively A/2AA/2A and A[2]={a:2a=0}A[2]=\{a:2a=0\}. Here A[2]A[2] denotes the two-torsion subgroup, not a derived shift.

Which half of the category is preserved

Let 𝒟i\mathcal D_i have t-structure (𝒟i≤0,𝒟i≥0)(\mathcal D_i^{\le0},\mathcal D_i^{\ge0}), heart 𝒜i\mathcal A_i, and cohomology HinH_i^n, for i=1,2i=1,2. An exact functor of triangulated categories F:𝒟1→𝒟2F:\mathcal D_1\to\mathcal D_2 comes with its shift comparison and sends distinguished triangles to distinguished triangles. This use of “exact” concerns triangles; exactness on the abelian hearts will be a separate conclusion. Keep the convention

𝒟i≤n=𝒟i≤0[−n],𝒟i≥n=𝒟i≥0[−n].(1) \mathcal D_i^{\le n}=\mathcal D_i^{\le0}[-n], \qquad \mathcal D_i^{\ge n}=\mathcal D_i^{\ge0}[-n]. \qquad\text{(1)}

Thus [1][1] lowers cohomological degree. We call FF

left t-exact⟺F(𝒟1≥0)⊂𝒟2≥0,right t-exact⟺F(𝒟1≤0)⊂𝒟2≤0,t-exact⟺F satisfies both conditions.(2) \begin{aligned} \text{left t-exact}&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F(\mathcal D_1^{\ge0})\subset\mathcal D_2^{\ge0},\\ \text{right t-exact}&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F(\mathcal D_1^{\le0})\subset\mathcal D_2^{\le0},\\ \text{t-exact}&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F\text{ satisfies both conditions}. \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(2)}

The shift comparison transports either inclusion to every integer cut in (1). The induced additive functor of hearts is

F♡:𝒜1⟶𝒜2,A⟼H20(F(A)).(3) F_{\heartsuit}:\mathcal A_1\longrightarrow\mathcal A_2, \qquad A\longmapsto H_2^0(F(A)). \qquad\text{(3)}

This definition makes sense even if FF satisfies neither condition. In that case there is no claimed one-sided exactness.

The cohomology comparison needs its degree bound

Theorem. If FF is left t-exact, there is a natural isomorphism

F♡(H10X)→∼H20(FX)(X∈𝒟1≥0).(4) F_{\heartsuit}(H_1^0X)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }H_2^0(FX) \qquad(X\in\mathcal D_1^{\ge0}). \qquad\text{(4)}

If FF is right t-exact, there is a natural isomorphism

H20(FX)→∼F♡(H10X)(X∈𝒟1≤0).(5) H_2^0(FX)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }F_{\heartsuit}(H_1^0X) \qquad(X\in\mathcal D_1^{\le0}). \qquad\text{(5)}

Proof. For X≥0X\ge0, the truncation triangle is H10X→X→τ1≥1X→(H10X)[1]H_1^0X\to X\to\tau_1^{\ge1}X\to(H_1^0X)[1]. Apply FF. Its third object lies in 𝒟2≥1\mathcal D_2^{\ge1}. Both its H2−1H_2^{-1} and H20H_2^0 vanish, so the long exact sequence gives (4). The map is the actual map induced by H10X→XH_1^0X\to X. Functoriality of that triangle gives naturality.

For X≤0X\le0, apply FF to τ1≤−1X→X→H10X→(τ1≤−1X)[1]\tau_1^{\le-1}X\to X\to H_1^0X\to(\tau_1^{\le-1}X)[1]. The first object after applying FF lies in 𝒟2≤−1\mathcal D_2^{\le-1}. Its H20H_2^0 and H21H_2^1 vanish. The middle-to-third map induces (5), again naturally. ▫\square

Shifting the proof gives (4) in degree nn for X≥nX\ge n, and (5) in degree nn for X≤nX\le n. It does not give these comparisons on the opposite half. Exercises below exhibit both failures.

On X=(ℤ/2)[−1]X=(\mathbb Z/2)[-1], outside the upper half, H0L(X)=ℤ/2H^0L(X)=\mathbb Z/2 but L♡(H0X)=0L_\heartsuit(H^0X)=0. One-sided exactness on the heart

Theorem. A left t-exact FF induces a left exact F♡F_{\heartsuit}. A right t-exact FF induces a right exact F♡F_{\heartsuit}.

Proof. A short exact sequence in 𝒜1\mathcal A_1, 0→A→B→C→00\to A\to B\to C\to0, determines a distinguished triangle A→B→C→A[1]A\to B\to C\to A[1]. If FF is left t-exact, FA,FB,FC≥0FA,FB,FC\ge0, and the long exact sequence contains

0=H2−1(FC)⟶F♡A⟶F♡B⟶F♡C.(6) 0=H_2^{-1}(FC)\longrightarrow F_{\heartsuit}A \longrightarrow F_{\heartsuit}B \longrightarrow F_{\heartsuit}C. \qquad\text{(6)}

It is exact at the first two heart objects. This is left exactness. If FF is right t-exact, FA,FB,FC≤0FA,FB,FC\le0, and the sequence instead contains

F♡A⟶F♡B⟶F♡C⟶H21(FA)=0.(7) F_{\heartsuit}A\longrightarrow F_{\heartsuit}B \longrightarrow F_{\heartsuit}C \longrightarrow H_2^1(FA)=0. \qquad\text{(7)}

This is right exactness. Additivity follows from that of FF and H20H_2^0. ▫\square

The two missing end terms measure the possible failure of the other exactness condition. Their vanishing cannot be deduced from a single inclusion in (2).

For the sequence tested in the opening, (6) identifies the missing Hom surjection with the degree-one extension term, and (7) identifies the missing tensor injection with negative-degree Tor. Both failures persist even when the two functors are adjoints. Requiring both halves is the additional condition used in the next theorem.

Full t-exactness transports the actual truncation triangles

Theorem. If FF is t-exact, F(𝒜1)⊂𝒜2F(\mathcal A_1)\subset\mathcal A_2, its restriction to the heart is exact, and there are natural comparisons

F(τ1≤nX)≃τ2≤n(FX),F(τ1≥nX)≃τ2≥n(FX),F(H1nX)≃H2n(FX)(n∈ℤ,X∈𝒟1).(8) \begin{aligned} F(\tau_1^{\le n}X)&\simeq\tau_2^{\le n}(FX),\\ F(\tau_1^{\ge n}X)&\simeq\tau_2^{\ge n}(FX),\\ F(H_1^nX)&\simeq H_2^n(FX) \qquad(n\in\mathbb Z,\ X\in\mathcal D_1). \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(8)}

They respect the truncation maps, connecting arrows, and the long exact cohomology sequences.

Proof. An object in both halves has image in both halves, so F(A)F(A) is a heart object and canonically equals F♡(A)F_{\heartsuit}(A). Combining (6) and (7) proves that this heart restriction is exact.

Apply FF to the actual triangle at cut nn: τ1≤nX→X→τ1≥n+1X→(τ1≤nX)[1]\tau_1^{\le n}X\to X\to\tau_1^{\ge n+1}X\to(\tau_1^{\le n}X)[1]. Its first object lies in 𝒟2≤n\mathcal D_2^{\le n}, and its third lies in 𝒟2≥n+1\mathcal D_2^{\ge n+1}. It is therefore a truncation triangle for FXFX. The proved uniqueness of truncation triangles gives its unique isomorphism with the chosen one for FXFX, with the identity on the middle object. Uniqueness makes these isomorphisms natural in XX and retains all three arrows. This proves the first two lines of (8).

Use the interval description H1nX=(τ1[n,n]X)[n]H_1^nX=(\tau_1^{[n,n]}X)[n]. Applying the two truncation comparisons and the shift comparison gives the last line of (8). The heart cohomology sequence was constructed from successive truncation triangles and their maps. The comparisons just obtained identify these constructions before taking heart kernels or cokernels. The exact heart restriction preserves those kernels and cokernels, so it identifies the connecting maps as well as the objects. ▫\square

For the standard t-structure of an abelian category ℬ\mathcal B, these abstract truncations are the usual smart truncations:

(τ≤nK)j={Kjj<n,ker⁡(dn)j=n,0j>n,(τ≥nK)j={0j<n,coker⁡(dn−1)j=n,Kjj>n.(9) (\tau^{\le n}K)^j= \begin{cases}K^j&j<n,\\ \ker(d^n)&j=n,\\0&j>n,\end{cases} \quad (\tau^{\ge n}K)^j= \begin{cases}0&j<n,\\ \operatorname{coker}(d^{n-1})&j=n,\\K^j&j>n.\end{cases} \qquad\text{(9)}

Indeed these complexes and their maps give the truncation triangle already proved in the preceding lesson. Uniqueness identifies their images in D(ℬ)D(\mathcal B) with the abstract adjoint truncations. The degree convention in (8) consequently agrees with ordinary complex cohomology.

Why a bounded-below right derived functor is left t-exact

Let ℬ1,ℬ2\mathcal B_1,\mathcal B_2 be abelian categories, assume ℬ1\mathcal B_1 has enough injectives, and let Φ:ℬ1→ℬ2\Phi:\mathcal B_1\to\mathcal B_2 be additive and left exact. Use the standard t-structures. The required derived-category prerequisite is:

A complex with zero terms below aa admits a quasi-isomorphism to a complex II of injectives with Ij=0I^j=0 below aa. A bounded-below injective complex is K-injective, and applying an additive functor to it computes the right derived functor on D+D^+.

The exact internal provider is Injective modules, flasque sheaves and bounded-below derived functors, Theorem 4.1(1)–(3), together with Lemma 3.2 and Theorem 3.3. Theorem 4.1 is stated for arbitrary abelian categories with enough injectives and any additive functor: its pushout construction preserves the specified lower term bound, its K-injective comparison computes all derived maps, and its cone argument constructs the triangulated functor on D+D^+. Left exactness identifies R0Φ=ΦR^0\Phi=\Phi. Theorem 2.3 separately proves enough injectives for sheaves of associative unital rings, including modules on a point. Thus the provider supplies every clause of the italicized interface, with no commutativity restriction on its underlying ring and no uniform upper-bound claim. The component retains the Stacks project authors’ GFDL-1.2-or-later text, the earlier GPT-6 Astra proof contributions and the GPT-6.1 Sol edits; its licence and source notices remain separate from this CC0 lesson.

Theorem. The exact triangulated functor

RΦ:D+(ℬ1)⟶D+(ℬ2)(10) R\Phi:D^+(\mathcal B_1)\longrightarrow D^+(\mathcal B_2) \qquad\text{(10)}

is left t-exact. Its heart functor is naturally Φ\Phi.

Proof. If X≥0X\ge0, choose a bounded-below representative KK and replace it by its smart truncation τ≥0K\tau^{\ge0}K. Since the negative cohomology of KK vanishes, this replacement is quasi-isomorphic to KK, and its terms are zero below zero. The prerequisite supplies K′→IK'\to I with Ij=0I^j=0 for j<0j<0. Thus RΦ(X)R\Phi(X) is represented by Φ(I)\Phi(I), whose terms below zero are also zero: an additive functor sends the zero object to a zero object. This proves left t-exactness.

For A∈ℬ1A\in\mathcal B_1, use 0→A→I0→I1→⋯0\to A\to I^0\to I^1\to\cdots. Left exactness of Φ\Phi identifies Φ(A)\Phi(A) with the kernel of Φ(I0)→Φ(I1)\Phi(I^0)\to\Phi(I^1), which is H0(Φ(I))H^0(\Phi(I)). This identification respects maps of resolutions and hence gives the natural heart isomorphism. ▫\square

The lower-bound argument works for every additive Φ\Phi for which (10) is constructed by that prerequisite. Left exactness is what identifies its degree-zero functor with the original Φ\Phi. In the stated left exact case, (4) becomes

H0(RΦX)≃Φ(H0X)(X≥0).(11) H^0(R\Phi X)\simeq\Phi(H^0X)\qquad(X\ge0). \qquad\text{(11)}

The domain and codomain in (10) are D+D^+. Enough injectives does not give a uniform upper bound on RΦ(A)R\Phi(A), even when AA is concentrated in degree zero. Exercise 6 computes an explicit failure to remain in DbD^b.

Adjoint functors exchange the two one-sided conditions

Let f:𝒟1→𝒟2f:\mathcal D_1\to\mathcal D_2 and g:𝒟2→𝒟1g:\mathcal D_2\to\mathcal D_1 be exact triangulated functors with f⊣gf\dashv g.

Theorem.

f is right t-exact⟺g is left t-exact.(12) f\text{ is right t-exact} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g\text{ is left t-exact}. \qquad\text{(12)}

Proof. Suppose ff is right t-exact. For Y∈𝒟2≥0Y\in\mathcal D_2^{\ge0} and U∈𝒟1≤−1U\in\mathcal D_1^{\le-1}, the shift comparison gives fU≤−1fU\le-1, so Hom⁡𝒟1(U,gY)≃Hom⁡𝒟2(fU,Y)=0\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D_1}(U,gY) \simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D_2}(fU,Y)=0. The right orthogonal description of 𝒟1≥0\mathcal D_1^{\ge0} proves gY≥0gY\ge0.

Conversely, if gg is left t-exact, then for X∈𝒟1≤0X\in\mathcal D_1^{\le0} and V∈𝒟2≥1V\in\mathcal D_2^{\ge1}, one has gV≥1gV\ge1. Adjunction gives Hom⁡𝒟2(fX,V)≃Hom⁡𝒟1(X,gV)=0\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D_2}(fX,V) \simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D_1}(X,gV)=0. The left orthogonal description of 𝒟2≤0\mathcal D_2^{\le0} proves fX≤0fX\le0. ▫\square

Neither direction asserts the other half of t-exactness for either functor. For example, the adjoint shifts [1]⊣[−1][1]\dashv[-1] satisfy exactly the two conditions in (12) for a nonzero standard heart.

Restricting an ambient adjunction requires membership

Suppose instead that the adjunction lives in larger triangulated categories: f:𝒟̃1⇄𝒟̃2:gf:\widetilde{\mathcal D}_1\rightleftarrows\widetilde{\mathcal D}_2:g. Let 𝒟i\mathcal D_i be full triangulated subcategories equipped with their own t-structures.

Theorem. There are two precise restricted assertions:

f(𝒟1)⊂𝒟2,f|𝒟1 right t-exact,Y∈𝒟2≥0,gY∈𝒟1⟹gY∈𝒟1≥0;(13)g(𝒟2)⊂𝒟1,g|𝒟2 left t-exact,X∈𝒟1≤0,fX∈𝒟2⟹fX∈𝒟2≤0. \begin{aligned} &f(\mathcal D_1)\subset\mathcal D_2,\quad f|_{\mathcal D_1}\text{ right t-exact},\\ &Y\in\mathcal D_2^{\ge0},\quad gY\in\mathcal D_1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad gY\in\mathcal D_1^{\ge0}; \qquad\text{(13)}\\[2pt] &g(\mathcal D_2)\subset\mathcal D_1,\quad g|_{\mathcal D_2}\text{ left t-exact},\\ &X\in\mathcal D_1^{\le0},\quad fX\in\mathcal D_2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad fX\in\mathcal D_2^{\le0}. \end{aligned}

Proof. In the first case, test gYgY against every U∈𝒟1≤−1U\in\mathcal D_1^{\le-1}. Fullness identifies these Hom groups with their ambient Hom groups. Adjunction identifies them with Hom⁡(fU,Y)\operatorname{Hom}(fU,Y), which vanishes by the t-structure on 𝒟2\mathcal D_2. The hypothesis gY∈𝒟1gY\in\mathcal D_1 permits the orthogonal membership test inside 𝒟1\mathcal D_1, proving the first conclusion.

In the second case, test fXfX against every V∈𝒟2≥1V\in\mathcal D_2^{\ge1}. Fullness and adjunction identify the test with Hom⁡(X,gV)=0\operatorname{Hom}(X,gV)=0. Now fX∈𝒟2fX\in\mathcal D_2 permits the left orthogonal membership test there. ▫\square

Orthogonality against objects of a full subcategory does not force an ambient object into that subcategory. The explicit membership clauses in (13) have that separate job.

The induced adjunction on hearts

Theorem. Under the conditions of (12), the two heart functors satisfy f♡⊣g♡f_{\heartsuit}\dashv g_{\heartsuit}. The first is right exact and the second left exact.

Proof. For A∈𝒜1A\in\mathcal A_1, B∈𝒜2B\in\mathcal A_2, right t-exactness gives fA≤0fA\le0 and left t-exactness gives gB≥0gB\ge0. Truncation adjunctions at zero then give

Hom⁡𝒜2(f♡A,B)≃Hom⁡𝒟2(fA,B)≃Hom⁡𝒟1(A,gB)≃Hom⁡𝒜1(A,g♡B).(14) \begin{aligned} \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A_2}(f_{\heartsuit}A,B) &\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D_2}(fA,B)\\ &\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D_1}(A,gB)\\ &\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A_1}(A,g_{\heartsuit}B). \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(14)}

For clarity, the first isomorphism follows by applying Hom⁡(−,B)\operatorname{Hom}(-,B) to τ≤−1fA→fA→H0fA\tau^{\le-1}fA\to fA\to H^0fA; both the Hom terms from τ≤−1fA\tau^{\le-1}fA and its shift [1][1] vanish. The last follows by applying Hom⁡(A,−)\operatorname{Hom}(A,-) to H0gB→gB→τ≥1gBH^0gB\to gB\to\tau^{\ge1}gB; both Hom terms to τ≥1gB\tau^{\ge1}gB and its shift [−1][-1] vanish. The isomorphisms are natural, so they define an adjunction. Equations (6)–(7) give its stated exactness properties. ▫\square

Detecting equivalences without bounding every object

Theorem. Suppose F:𝒟1→𝒟2F:\mathcal D_1\to\mathcal D_2 is t-exact, the t-structure on 𝒟1\mathcal D_1 is nondegenerate, and F♡F_{\heartsuit} reflects isomorphisms. Then FF reflects isomorphisms on all of 𝒟1\mathcal D_1. The target t-structure need not be nondegenerate.

Proof. Let u:X→Yu:X\to Y have invertible FuFu. The natural comparisons (8) identify F♡(H1nu)F_{\heartsuit}(H_1^n u) with H2n(Fu)H_2^n(Fu), which is an isomorphism for every integer nn. Reflection on the heart makes every H1nuH_1^n u an isomorphism. The nondegenerate detection theorem in the preceding lesson then makes uu an isomorphism. The reverse implication holds for every functor. ▫\square

Only the source needs nondegeneracy: it is where the cohomology test is used to recover the original morphism. For instance, an exact conservative functor between abelian categories, applied term by term to unbounded complexes, descends to a t-exact conservative functor of their derived categories. It descends because exactness preserves kernels, images and cokernels and hence cohomology and quasi-isomorphisms; its heart restriction is the original functor. The standard source t-structure is nondegenerate by the preceding lesson. Bounded-interval detection without a global nondegeneracy hypothesis is proved separately in Exercise 8.

Eight exercises with complete solutions

Shifts distinguish left and right

Difficulty: Introductory.

For the standard t-structure on Db(k)D^b(k), with kk a field, determine the one-sided t-exactness of [1][1] and [−1][-1]. Compute their heart functors and verify (12) and (14).

Solution. Hj(X[1])=Hj+1(X)H^j(X[1])=H^{j+1}(X), so [1][1] preserves the upper bound zero. It fails to preserve the lower bound: k[1]k[1] has nonzero cohomology in degree −1-1. Thus [1][1] is right t-exact but not left t-exact. The shift [−1][-1] preserves the lower bound and fails the upper bound, since k[−1]k[-1] has degree 11. On a degree-zero vector space, both shifted objects have zero H0H^0, so both heart functors are zero. The shifts are inverse equivalences and [1]⊣[−1][1]\dashv[-1]. Their heart adjunction is the zero-to-zero adjunction: both Hom spaces in (14) are the zero vector space. A heart adjunction can consequently lose information retained by its ambient adjunction.

A left t-exact functor can see a negative input

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Set G=RHom⁡ℤ(ℤ/2,−)G=R\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Z/2,-) on Db(ℤ)D^b(\mathbb Z). Prove that GG is left t-exact, compute G(ℤ)G(\mathbb Z), and test (11) on X=ℤ[1]X=\mathbb Z[1]. Show that G♡G_{\heartsuit} fails right exactness.

Solution. The free resolution P=(ℤ→2ℤ)P=(\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{2}\mathbb Z), in degrees −1,0-1,0, gives Hom⁡(P,Y)\operatorname{Hom}(P,Y) as the derived Hom complex. It computes the derived functor because PP is bounded free: Hom from each term preserves exact complexes, and its two-term total Hom is a shifted cone, which also preserves acyclicity. For a smart representative YY with zero terms below zero, this total Hom has zero terms below zero. Hence GG is left t-exact.

For Y=ℤY=\mathbb Z, the resulting complex is isomorphic to ℤ→2ℤ\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{2}\mathbb Z in degrees 0,10,1 (change the sign of one term if the total Hom differential is −2-2). Its only cohomology is ℤ/2\mathbb Z/2 in degree 11; thus G(ℤ)≃(ℤ/2)[−1]G(\mathbb Z)\simeq(\mathbb Z/2)[-1]. Shifting gives H0G(ℤ[1])=ℤ/2H^0G(\mathbb Z[1])=\mathbb Z/2, whereas Hom⁡(ℤ/2,H0(ℤ[1]))=0\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z/2,H^0(\mathbb Z[1]))=0. The input is outside D≥0D^{\ge0}, exactly the bound needed in (11). On the heart, G♡=Hom⁡(ℤ/2,−)G_{\heartsuit}=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z/2,-). The epimorphism ℤ→ℤ/2\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/2 maps to 0→ℤ/20\to\mathbb Z/2, which is not an epimorphism. Left t-exactness has not supplied right exactness.

A right t-exact functor can see a positive input

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Set L=(ℤ/2)⊗ℤL−L=(\mathbb Z/2)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}^L- on Db(ℤ)D^b(\mathbb Z). Verify right t-exactness and compute L(ℤ/2)L(\mathbb Z/2). Test (5) on X=(ℤ/2)[−1]X=(\mathbb Z/2)[-1], and show that L♡L_{\heartsuit} fails left exactness.

Solution. The same free resolution PP computes L(Y)=P⊗YL(Y)=P\otimes Y. Tensoring its two terms gives the cone of multiplication by 22 on YY, so it preserves acyclic complexes and computes the derived tensor. If a smart representative YY has zero terms above zero, the total tensor also has zero terms above zero. Thus LL is right t-exact.

Tensor PP with ℤ/2\mathbb Z/2. Its differential becomes zero, so L(ℤ/2)L(\mathbb Z/2) has one ℤ/2\mathbb Z/2 in degree −1-1 and one in degree 00. After shifting by [−1][-1], these degrees are 0,10,1. Hence H0(LX)=ℤ/2H^0(LX)=\mathbb Z/2, whereas L♡(H0X)=(ℤ/2)⊗0=0L_{\heartsuit}(H^0X)=(\mathbb Z/2)\otimes0=0. The positive input is outside the upper-bound hypothesis in (5). Finally, the heart monomorphism 2:ℤ→ℤ2:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z becomes the zero map ℤ/2→ℤ/2\mathbb Z/2\to\mathbb Z/2, which is not monic.

Scalar extension commutes with every truncation

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Let k⊂Kk\subset K be fields. Show that extension of scalars E=K⊗k−E=K\otimes_k- and restriction of scalars UU on their bounded derived categories are t-exact adjoints. Identify the comparisons (8).

Solution. Every short exact sequence of vector spaces splits. Tensoring a splitting with KK preserves the sequence, so EE is exact on vector spaces; UU preserves the underlying kernels and cokernels and is also exact. Both therefore commute with complex cohomology. They descend to the derived categories and preserve both cohomological bounds, proving t-exactness. The usual tensor–restriction adjunction on complexes, with its natural unit and counit, respects homotopies and descends through quasi-isomorphisms, retaining the two triangular identities. Thus E⊣UE\dashv U on the derived categories.

Since the two functors preserve kernels and cokernels, applying either to the explicit complexes in (9) gives the corresponding smart truncations. Their comparisons in (8) are these actual degreewise maps. In particular Hn(EX)≃K⊗kHnXH^n(EX)\simeq K\otimes_kH^nX, naturally, including the connecting arrows of every triangle sequence. No finite extension-degree hypothesis is needed.

Derived tensor–Hom induces an adjunction with unequal exactness

Difficulty: Advanced.

For L,GL,G in Exercises 2–3, establish the derived adjunction L⊣GL\dashv G and compute its heart adjunction. Does either heart functor become exact because the two are adjoints?

Solution. Use the bounded free complex PP. The total-complex tensor–Hom adjunction Hom⁡(P⊗X,Y)≃Hom⁡(X,Hom⁡(P,Y))\operatorname{Hom}(P\otimes X,Y)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(X,\operatorname{Hom}(P,Y)) has the usual degree signs. It gives unit and counit maps on complexes satisfying the triangular identities. Both functors preserve quasi-isomorphisms by the cone computations in Exercises 2–3. Consequently the unit and counit descend to Db(ℤ)D^b(\mathbb Z), still satisfy those identities, and give L⊣GL\dashv G. Boundedness is preserved because PP has only two nonzero terms.

On a heart object AA, the degree-zero tensor cohomology is A/2AA/2A, and the degree-zero Hom cohomology is A[2]={a:2a=0}A[2]=\{a:2a=0\}. Formula (14) is thus

Hom⁡ℤ(A/2A,B)≃Hom⁡ℤ(A,B[2]).(15) \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(A/2A,B) \simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(A,B[2]). \qquad\text{(15)}

Indeed a map from A/2AA/2A into BB has image killed by 22, and a map from AA into B[2]B[2] vanishes on 2A2A. These inverse factorizations are natural. The failures on 2:ℤ→ℤ2:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z and ℤ→ℤ/2\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/2 in the earlier exercises still apply. Adjunction gives right exactness for the left heart functor and left exactness for the right one, without making either exact.

A right derived functor need not preserve boundedness

Difficulty: Advanced.

Let R=ℤ/4R=\mathbb Z/4, M=R/(2)M=R/(2), and Φ=Hom⁡R(M,−)\Phi=\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,-). Compute every Ext⁡Rj(M,M)\operatorname{Ext}_R^j(M,M) and show that RΦ(M)R\Phi(M) lies in D+D^+ but not DbD^b. The category of RR-modules has enough injectives; this is also the point-space case of the programme’s sheaf-module enough-injective theorem.

Solution. There is a projective resolution

⋯→2R→2R→2R⟶M⟶0.(16) \cdots\xrightarrow{2}R\xrightarrow{2}R \xrightarrow{2}R\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow0. \qquad\text{(16)}

Successive differentials compose to multiplication by 44, which is zero in RR. At every RR the kernel and image of multiplication by 22 are both (2)(2); the augmentation has that same kernel. Thus (16) is exact. Applying Hom⁡R(−,M)\operatorname{Hom}_R(-,M) gives MM in each degree j≥0j\ge0, with every differential zero because 2M=02M=0. Therefore Ext⁡Rj(M,M)=M\operatorname{Ext}_R^j(M,M)=M for every j≥0j\ge0.

For completeness this projective-resolution calculation agrees with the injective definition: for an injective resolution M→IM\to I, use the first-quadrant double complex Hom⁡R(P−p,Iq)\operatorname{Hom}_R(P^{-p},I^q), p,q≥0p,q\ge0. Each diagonal has finitely many terms. Taking vertical cohomology leaves Hom⁡R(P−p,M)\operatorname{Hom}_R(P^{-p},M), since P−pP^{-p} is free; taking horizontal cohomology leaves Hom⁡R(M,Iq)\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,I^q), since IqI^q is injective and the augmented resolution is exact. The two filtrations of each finite diagonal consequently compute the same total cohomology, giving the claimed Ext groups. They are nonzero in arbitrarily high degrees. Left t-exactness is respected, but no bounded-above target is possible.

Orthogonal tests cannot create subcategory membership

Difficulty: Advanced.

Set 𝒟̃1=𝒟̃2=Db(k)×Db(k)\widetilde{\mathcal D}_1=\widetilde{\mathcal D}_2=D^b(k)\times D^b(k), and take f=g=idf=g=\mathrm{id}. Solution. On the second factor the new cuts at zero are Dstd≤−1D^{\le-1}_{\mathrm{std}} and Dstd≥−1D^{\ge-1}_{\mathrm{std}}; they are a shifted t-structure and their intersection has degree −1-1. On the first factor the cuts remain standard. The inclusion f(𝒟1)⊂𝒟2f(\mathcal D_1)\subset\mathcal D_2 holds, and (X,0)(X,0) with X≤0X\le0 satisfies the product upper cut. The object Y=(0,k[1])Y=(0,k[1]) is in the heart of 𝒟2\mathcal D_2, and thus in its positive half. Every Hom from (U,0)(U,0) to gYgY vanishes, even without a degree condition on UU. The missing membership hypothesis is exactly what fails; an orthogonal test inside the first factor is unable to detect any second-factor object.

Conservativity on a bounded t-structure and its limitation

Difficulty: Advanced.

Suppose FF is t-exact and its heart restriction reflects isomorphisms. Prove that FF reflects isomorphisms between objects lying in finite t-structure intervals. Explain why the boundedness assumption matters.

Solution. If ZZ lies in an interval [a,b][a,b] and FZ=0FZ=0, then (8) gives F♡(H1nZ)=0F_{\heartsuit}(H_1^nZ)=0 for every nn. Apply reflection of isomorphisms to 0→H1nZ0\to H_1^nZ: its image is the isomorphism 0→00\to0, so H1nZ=0H_1^nZ=0. Finite successive truncation triangles then give Z=0Z=0.

For a map u:X→Yu:X\to Y between finite-interval objects, its cone CC also lies in a finite interval: choose common bounds [a,b][a,b]; the triangle Y→C→X[1]→Y[1]Y\to C\to X[1]\to Y[1] and extension closure place CC in [a−1,b][a-1,b]. If FuFu is invertible, its cone FCFC vanishes. The preceding argument gives C=0C=0, so uu is invertible.

Without boundedness or a suitable nondegeneracy assumption, cohomology can miss an object. In a nonzero triangulated category take the degenerate t-structure (𝒟,{0})(\mathcal D,\{0\}), whose heart and all cohomology functors are zero. The zero endofunctor is t-exact and its restriction to the zero heart reflects isomorphisms. It does not reflect isomorphisms in 𝒟\mathcal D. The finite interval subcategory here contains only zero, so this example does not contradict the proved assertion.
