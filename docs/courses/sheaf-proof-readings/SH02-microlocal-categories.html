SH02-MC — Categories and operations in one cotangent direction

Local identifier: SH02-MC. Independently authored programme expression: CC0 1.0 Universal. Formalization: absent. The human sources identified below retain their own terms.

Directional localization permits many representatives of a single object. Applying a sheaf operation to those representatives first gives a formal system. Replacing that system by one bounded sheaf requires control of its support and of the relevant cotangent incidence. We develop the formal operations first, construct that geometric control, and then prove the representation criteria. The final morphism calculation identifies the information carried by the resulting category.

All manifolds are finite-dimensional real smooth manifolds, Hausdorff and second countable. The coefficient ring kk is commutative and has finite global dimension. Db(kX)D^b(k_X) means the bounded derived category of all sheaves of kk-modules. No constructibility, finite-rank, field, orientability, or compactness condition is understood. A locally closed subspace has its subspace topology. A subscript FZF_Z means extension by zero of the restriction, whereas RΓZF=Rℋom(kZ,F)R\Gamma_ZF=R\mathcal Hom(k_Z,F) means local cohomology with support. These are different functors. Tensor products are derived. The antipodal map is a(x;ξ)=(x;−ξ)a(x;\xi)=(x;-\xi). Positive homogeneity always uses ℝ>0\mathbb R_{>0}.

For any C∞C^\infty map f:Y→Xf:Y\to X, put Ef=Y×XT*X,fπ(y;ξ)=(f(y);ξ),fd(y;ξ)=(y;dfytξ). E_f=Y\times_XT^*X,\qquad f_\pi(y;\xi)=(f(y);\xi),\qquad f_d(y;\xi)=(y;d f_y^t\xi). The relative dualizing object is ωY/X=f!kX\omega_{Y/X}=f^!k_X. Locally it is the relative orientation line shifted by [dim⁡Y−dim⁡X][\dim Y-\dim X]; thus the shift in ωY/X⊗f−1F→f!F\omega_{Y/X}\otimes f^{-1}F\to f^!F is fixed by this definition. We never replace f!f^! by a shifted f−1f^{-1} without a noncharacteristic hypothesis.

SH02-MC-LOCAL — A category that records specified directions

Fix any subset Ω⊂T*X\Omega\subset T^*X. It need not be open or conic. Let 𝒩Ω={F∈Db(kX):SS⁡(F)∩Ω=⌀},𝒟X(Ω)=Db(kX)/𝒩Ω. \mathcal N_\Omega=\{F\in D^b(k_X):\operatorname{SS}(F)\cap\Omega=\varnothing\}, \qquad \mathcal D_X(\Omega)=D^b(k_X)/\mathcal N_\Omega. An arrow is an Ω\Omega-denominator when the microsupport of its cone avoids Ω\Omega.

Proposition. 𝒩Ω\mathcal N_\Omega is a thick triangulated subcategory. Denominators form a saturated multiplicative system admitting both fraction calculi. An arrow is invertible in 𝒟X(Ω)\mathcal D_X(\Omega) precisely when it is a denominator. For G,F∈Db(kX)G,F\in D^b(k_X) there are natural identifications Hom⁡𝒟X(Ω)(G,F)=colimF→sF′,s a denominatorHom⁡Db(kX)(G,F′)=colimG′→tG,t a denominatorHom⁡Db(kX)(G′,F).(MC.1) \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D_X(\Omega)}(G,F) =\underset{F\xrightarrow{s}F',\ s\text{ a denominator}}{\operatorname{colim}} \operatorname{Hom}_{D^b(k_X)}(G,F') =\underset{G'\xrightarrow{t}G,\ t\text{ a denominator}}{\operatorname{colim}} \operatorname{Hom}_{D^b(k_X)}(G',F). \qquad\text{(MC.1)} In the second colimit arrows between denominators are reversed before applying Hom⁡(−,F)\operatorname{Hom}(-,F). These are colimits of abelian groups over denominator categories, not colimits over a chosen sequence of neighborhoods.

Proof. Shifting a complex preserves its microsupport. For a triangle A→B→C→A[1]A\to B\to C\to A[1], microsupport of any one term is contained in the union of the other two. Finally SS⁡(A⊕B)=SS⁡(A)∪SS⁡(B)\operatorname{SS}(A\oplus B)=\operatorname{SS}(A)\cup\operatorname{SS}(B), since a local-cohomology test of a direct sum is the direct sum of the two tests. These facts prove thickness. The Stacks quotient theorem applies exactly to this thick subcategory. Its kernel is this subcategory, rather than a larger saturation, because thickness was checked. Thus an arrow becomes invertible precisely when its cone lies there. The two fraction descriptions give (MC.1): the first represents G→FG\to F by G→F′←FG\to F'\leftarrow F, the second by G←G′→FG\leftarrow G'\to F. A representative becomes zero precisely when a further denominator kills it. This last assertion is the criterion we shall actually use. ▫\square

A triangle with an invisible third term gives equality of the first two microsupports on Ω\Omega. SH02-MC-FOUR — Four microlocal operations and their variance

The quotient tells us which changes of representative are invisible. We now apply each sheaf operation to every such representative, retaining the direction of its comparison arrows.

For the first arrow of (MC.13), take 𝒞=𝒟X(pX)\mathcal C=\mathcal D_X(p_X). The incoming pro-object has its canonical projection to the identity stage Rf!GRf_!G, the outgoing ind-object has its structural map from the identity stage Rf*GRf_*G, and the ordinary comparison lies between these stages. Their composite in (MC.13a) is exactly the first displayed stage composite. Refining an incoming denominator precomposes it, and refining an outgoing one postcomposes it, leaving its class in (MC.13b) unchanged.

For the second arrow take 𝒞=𝒟Y(pY)\mathcal C=\mathcal D_Y(p_Y). Tensoring by ωY/X\omega_{Y/X} is an ordinary functor on this localization: locally this object is an invertible orientation line with the stated shift, so tensoring preserves microsupport and hence denominators. Extend that functor termwise to the pro-category, and use the identity-stage projection, the ordinary noncharacteristic comparison map, and the outgoing structural map. This gives exactly the second stage composite. The middle map exists before imposing a noncharacteristic hypothesis; that hypothesis concerns its invertibility.

Both composites are natural on ordinary arrows, by the naturality of the usual comparisons and the structural maps. The formal incoming and outgoing functors invert denominators, so this naturality identity also holds for their inverses and consequently for every roof. Thus (MC.13) is canonical and natural on localized objects. If both formal values are represented by ordinary objects, full faithfulness of the twice-constant embedding identifies it with a unique ordinary morphism. Representability alone does not make that morphism invertible. The forthcoming geometric hypotheses prove invertibility where it is asserted. ▫\square

An object in an additive category whose identity is zero is a zero object: every arrow to or from it factors through its zero identity and is zero. Equation (MC.2) now excludes pp from microsupport. This proves the reverse inclusion. ▫\square

The proof uses the identity in a localized category and does not assume finite-dimensional endomorphism spaces. It therefore detects infinite-rank sheaves just as well as finite-rank ones.

SH02-MC-EXTERNAL — Multiplying localized morphisms

Let pX∈T*Xp_X\in T^*X, pY∈T*Yp_Y\in T^*Y and p=(pX,pY)∈T*(X×Y)p=(p_X,p_Y)\in T^*(X\times Y). External derived tensor product induces a bifunctor 𝒟X(pX)×𝒟Y(pY)⟶𝒟X×Y(p).(MC.29) \mathcal D_X(p_X)\times\mathcal D_Y(p_Y) \longrightarrow\mathcal D_{X\times Y}(p). \qquad\text{(MC.29)} In particular there is a canonical bilinear map Hom⁡𝒟X(pX)(F1,F2)×Hom⁡𝒟Y(pY)(G1,G2)⟶Hom⁡𝒟X×Y(p)(F1⊠G1,F2⊠G2).(MC.30) \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D_X(p_X)}(F_1,F_2) \times\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D_Y(p_Y)}(G_1,G_2) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D_{X\times Y}(p)} (F_1\boxtimes G_1,F_2\boxtimes G_2). \qquad\text{(MC.30)}

Proof. If s:F′→Fs:F'\to F is a denominator at pXp_X with cone CC, the cone of s⊠id⁡Gs\boxtimes\operatorname{id}_G is C⊠GC\boxtimes G. The product estimate SS⁡(C⊠G)⊂SS⁡(C)×SS⁡(G)\operatorname{SS}(C\boxtimes G)\subset\operatorname{SS}(C)\times\operatorname{SS}(G) excludes pp. Thus tensoring in either argument preserves the appropriate denominators. The two localization universal properties give (MC.29). Concretely, for left fractions represented by F1←F1′→F2F_1\leftarrow F_1'\to F_2 and G1←G1′→G2G_1\leftarrow G_1'\to G_2, tensor the arrows to get a fraction with denominator F1′⊠G1′→F1⊠G1F_1'\boxtimes G_1'\to F_1\boxtimes G_1. Factor this denominator into one denominator in each variable to see that it is invertible. Common refinements and additivity give a well-defined bilinear map. Associativity, composition and identities follow from the corresponding tensor identities before localization. This proves (MC.30), without claiming it is an isomorphism or that all product morphisms are decomposable. ▫\square

SH02-MC-PROBLEMS — Examples and exercises with solutions

1. A large stalk remains visible. Let MM be a nonzero kk-module, with no finite-generation assumption, and let F=M[2,∞)F=M_{[2,\infty)} on the real line. Show that the positive covector p=(2;dx)p=(2;dx) detects FF, whereas q=(2;−dx)q=(2;-dx) does not.

Solution. The test ϕ(x)=x\phi(x)=x has RΓ{x≥2}F=FR\Gamma_{\{x\ge2\}}F=F near 22, so the test stalk is M≠0M\ne0. The closed-half-space calculation gives SS⁡(F)2=ℝ≥0dx\operatorname{SS}(F)_2=\mathbb R_{\ge0}dx; the opposite direction has a vanishing test uniformly in a small negative cone. Hence FF is nonzero in 𝒟ℝ(p)\mathcal D_{\mathbb R}(p) and zero in 𝒟ℝ(q)\mathcal D_{\mathbb R}(q). The identity section of its self microlocal Hom is nonzero at pp by (MC.6). No count of stalk dimensions enters the argument.

2. Representability alone does not identify the two direct images. Let kk be a nonzero field, f:ℝ→{*}f:\mathbb R\to\{*\}, G=kℝG=k_{\mathbb R}, and p=(0;0)∈Efp=(0;0)\in E_f. Compute both microlocal direct images.

Solution. The open intervals V=(−r,r)V=(-r,r) form a neighborhood basis. Their compact cohomology is k[−1]k[-1], with the transition maps identified by the positive orientation of the line. Thus (MC.25) gives f!,pμG≃k[−1]f_{!,p}^{\mu}G\simeq k[-1]. On the other side, RΓ(ℝ;RΓVG)=RΓ(V;k)=kR\Gamma(\mathbb R;R\Gamma_VG)=R\Gamma(V;k)=k, with identity transition maps, so f*,pμG≃kf_{*,p}^{\mu}G\simeq k. Both formal objects are represented, but their canonical comparison is zero: Hom⁡D(k)(k[−1],k)=Ext⁡k1(k,k)=0\operatorname{Hom}_{D(k)}(k[-1],k)=\operatorname{Ext}^1_k(k,k)=0. The incidence is the entire zero section of ℝ\mathbb R, which is not isolated at 00. The sufficient isolated-incidence theorem therefore makes no false identification in this example.

3. Properness does not select a branch. Let YY be two disjoint copies of the real line and let f:Y→ℝf:Y\to\mathbb R be the identity on each component. Take one skyscraper k{0}k_{\{0\}} on each component and let GG be their direct sum. Choose a nonzero covector pX=(0;dx)p_X=(0;dx) and the lift pp on the first component. Compare the microlocal and ordinary direct images.

Solution. The map is proper, but the entire incidence fibre has two points. Near the chosen pp it has just one. A neighborhood of its basepoint contained in the first component removes the second skyscraper, so (MC.25) gives f!,pμG=f*,pμG=k{0}f_{!,p}^{\mu}G=f_{*,p}^{\mu}G=k_{\{0\}} in the localization at pXp_X. The ordinary direct image is k{0}⊕k{0}k_{\{0\}}\oplus k_{\{0\}}. Its second summand remains visible at pXp_X by the closed-point microsupport calculation. In particular the canonical map from the first summand to the ordinary image has a nonzero localized cone. The entire-fibre hypothesis in (MC.28) cannot be replaced by its local version.

4. Where the orientation shift goes. For the projection f:ℝm×X→Xf:\mathbb R^m\times X\to X, let pp be any lift of a covector pXp_X and let F∈Db(kX)F\in D^b(k_X). Identify its two microlocal inverse images.

Solution. The transpose differential is injective and has at most one lift of a prescribed covector at the fixed basepoint. It has zero characteristic kernel, so (MC.18) applies. Hence fμ,p−1F=f−1Ff_{\mu,p}^{-1}F=f^{-1}F and fμ,p!F=ωY/X⊗f−1Ff_{\mu,p}^{!}F=\omega_{Y/X}\otimes f^{-1}F. With the standard orientation, ωY/X=k[m]\omega_{Y/X}=k[m]. The second object is therefore f−1F[m]f^{-1}F[m], in the cohomological convention H−m(k[m])=kH^{-m}(k[m])=k. The first object carries no such shift.

5. Empty directions. Compute 𝒟X(⌀)\mathcal D_X(\varnothing) and the corresponding comparison (MC.3).

Solution. Every bounded sheaf belongs to the null subcategory, so the quotient is the zero category. The sheaf on the empty space has zero derived sections, and (MC.3) is the unique map 0→00\to0. This agrees with the arbitrary-subset definition and requires no nonempty or conic assumption.
