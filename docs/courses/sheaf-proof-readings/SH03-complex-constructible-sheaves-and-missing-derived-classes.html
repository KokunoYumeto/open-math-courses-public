Complex-constructible sheaves and missing derived classes

A bounded complex can have complex-constructible cohomology without admitting a representative whose terms are complex-constructible sheaves. The same distinction appears in morphisms: an ambient higher extension class need not be a Yoneda extension through such sheaves. Failure of a real triangulation proof does not by itself establish this. We will give a counterexample that allows every complex refinement, for both weak and perfect coefficients.

David Massey’s freely available Notes on Perverse Sheaves and Vanishing Cycles, §§1–2, records the complex support and stratum conventions for finite constructible coefficients. Here the weak category also permits arbitrary vector-space stalks; the distinction between that larger heart and its finite subcategory is part of the argument. We specialize to k=ℚk=\mathbb Q, a commutative field of global dimension zero. All complexes are bounded. Choosing this valid coefficient ring suffices to disprove a proposed general equivalence; we are not asserting that failure occurs on every complex manifold or over every ring.

Let 𝒜w\mathcal A_w be the abelian category of weakly complex-constructible sheaves on XX, and let 𝒜f\mathcal A_f be its finite-stalk subcategory. Over a field, finite stalks in degree zero are precisely perfect stalks. Their natural realization functors are

ℛw:Db(𝒜w)⟶Dw-C-cb(kX),ℛf:Db(𝒜f)⟶DC-cb(kX).(1) \mathcal R_w:D^b(\mathcal A_w)\longrightarrow D^b_{w\text{-}C\text{-}c}(k_X), \qquad \mathcal R_f:D^b(\mathcal A_f)\longrightarrow D^b_{C\text{-}c}(k_X). \qquad\text{(1)}

They send the same complex to the ambient sheaf derived category and preserve its cohomology sheaves. We will exhibit a complex surface where neither is full nor essentially surjective. Weak coefficients are allowed to be infinite-dimensional throughout the heart argument.

The geometric input

Choose a complex K3 surface with Pic⁡(X)=0\operatorname{Pic}(X)=0. The precise geometric facts needed are existence of such a compact connected surface, simple connectivity, and H2(X;ℚ)≃ℚ22H^2(X;\mathbb Q)\simeq\mathbb Q^{22}. The Picard-zero deformation of the Fermat quartic constructs such a fibre in a proper holomorphic submersion connected to the quartic. Its finite-regularity coordinate theorem supplies the actual complex family from the convergent integrable deformation. The ordinary topology of the Fermat quartic proves ordinary simple connectivity and the second Betti number twenty-two for that quartic, and proves their transport along such a family. It also supplies the finite-point and arbitrary-rank local-system consequences used below. The projective quartic itself is not the Picard-zero surface. Huybrechts’s author-posted draft gives Picard-rank-zero existence and the second Betti number in Chapter 1, §3.3; simple connectivity in Chapter 7, Corollary 1.4; and Picard-zero existence explicitly in Chapter 16, §4.4. Huybrechts, Lectures on K3 surfaces, author draft.

There are no nonempty curves on this XX. Indeed an effective curve on a smooth complex surface is a Cartier divisor. Its associated holomorphic line bundle would be trivial because Pic⁡(X)=0\operatorname{Pic}(X)=0. Its canonical nonzero section, under a trivialization, would then be a global holomorphic function with a nonempty zero set. Compactness and connectedness make every such function constant, a contradiction. This is an inference from the stated Picard condition; it needs no projective embedding of XX.

Consequently every proper closed complex analytic subset of XX is finite: its positive-dimensional components would be curves, and its zero-dimensional components form a locally finite discrete set in a compact space. The real dimension of XX is four.

Every sheaf has only finitely many exceptional points

For F∈𝒜wF\in\mathcal A_w, the actual SS⁡(F)\operatorname{SS}(F) is closed complex analytic, complex-conic and Lagrangian. The analytic conormal-cover theorem in Analytic conormal covers and singular involutivity bounds it by limiting conormals of a finite number of dimension-grouped closed analytic base sets. On our surface the positive-dimensional proper base sets are absent; the dimension-two base set can only be XX, with zero-section conormal. The zero-dimensional base sets are finite. Thus, for some finite S⊂XS\subset X,

SS⁡(F)⊂TX*X∪⋃p∈STp*X.(2) \operatorname{SS}(F)\subset T_X^*X\ \cup\ \bigcup_{p\in S}T_p^*X. \qquad\text{(2)}

The zero-microsupport local-constancy criterion shows that F|X\SF|_{X\setminus S} is locally constant. This deduction uses the full category, not a preassigned stratification. Conversely a sheaf locally constant outside finitely many points, with arbitrary stalks at those points, is weakly complex constructible: the open complex manifold and the point strata form a finite complex analytic partition. Finite stalks give the perfect case.

Fix S={p1,…,ps}S=\{p_1,\ldots,p_s\} and put U=X\SU=X\setminus S. This UU is connected and simply connected. Paths can be moved around finitely many points in a real four-manifold, so it remains connected. Take pairwise disjoint coordinate balls BiB_i around the points. Each punctured ball Bi\{pi}B_i\setminus\{p_i\} retracts onto S3S^3, which is connected and simply connected. Attaching the balls one at a time to UU, van Kampen leaves its fundamental group unchanged. The resulting space is XX, whose fundamental group is trivial. This proves the assertion. In particular every local system of vector spaces on UU is a constant sheaf, even with infinite-dimensional coefficients.

Ordinary sheaf gluing is a finite star diagram

Write j:U↪Xj:U\hookrightarrow X, i:S↪Xi:S\hookrightarrow X. Let 𝒜w,S\mathcal A_{w,S} consist of the sheaves locally constant on UU, with no further restrictions at the points. A sheaf is specified by vector spaces and maps

M,Ai,ui:Ai⟶M(1≤i≤s).(3) M,\quad A_i,\quad u_i:A_i\longrightarrow M\quad(1\leq i\leq s). \qquad\text{(3)}

Here MM is the common constant module on UU, and AiA_i is the stalk at pip_i. The maps are actual specialization maps, not exceptional restrictions. We prove this classification.

Since small punctured four-balls are connected,

j*MU≃MX.(4) j_*M_U\simeq M_X. \qquad\text{(4)}

The map from the constant sheaf is a stalk isomorphism both on UU and at every pip_i. The restriction unit F→j*j−1FF\to j_*j^{-1}F therefore supplies the maps uiu_i. Conversely, from (3) construct the ordinary sheaf fibre product

F(M,Ai,ui)=MX×i*MSi*A,A|pi=Ai.(5) F(M,A_i,u_i)=M_X\times_{\,i_*M_S}i_*A, \qquad A|_{p_i}=A_i. \qquad\text{(5)}

The first map to i*MSi_*M_S is ordinary restriction; the second is induced by the uiu_i. On UU the fibre product stalk is MM. At pip_i it consists of pairs (m,ai)(m,a_i) with m=ui(ai)m=u_i(a_i), so is canonically AiA_i. Its specialization is uiu_i. The two natural restriction units give a map from any original FF to this fibre product, and it is an isomorphism on every stalk. This proves reconstruction.

A morphism is exactly a map v:M→M′v:M\to M' and maps ai:Ai→Ai′a_i:A_i\to A_i' satisfying vui=ui′aivu_i=u_i'a_i. Exactness is vertexwise, since stalks are exact. Thus (3) is an exact equivalence with representations of the finite quiver having arrows i→0i\to0. The finite-stalk subcategory corresponds to finite-dimensional representations.

This is an ordinary abelian gluing statement. It does not set Rj*MU=j*MURj_*M_U=j_*M_U: the higher punctured-link cohomology can be nonzero. That lost higher information will matter in the ambient category.

A length-one projective resolution

Define P0P_0 to have M=kM=k, all Ai=0A_i=0. Define PiP_i to have M=kM=k, Ai=kA_i=k, its arrow the identity, and all other point modules zero. The exact identities

Hom⁡(P0,F)=M,Hom⁡(Pi,F)=Ai(6) \operatorname{Hom}(P_0,F)=M, \qquad\operatorname{Hom}(P_i,F)=A_i \qquad\text{(6)}

make them projective. Tensoring with any vector space keeps them projective: over a field every vector space is projective, so Hom⁡k(V,−)\operatorname{Hom}_k(V,-) is exact. This includes infinite vector spaces in the weak category.

For (3) there is an exact sequence

0⟶⨁iP0⊗Ai⟶(P0⊗M)⊕⨁i(Pi⊗Ai)⟶F⟶0.(7) 0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_i P_0\otimes A_i \longrightarrow (P_0\otimes M)\oplus\bigoplus_i(P_i\otimes A_i) \longrightarrow F\longrightarrow0. \qquad\text{(7)}

At the generic vertex, the last map is (m,(ai))↦m+∑iui(ai)(m,(a_i))\mapsto m+\sum_i u_i(a_i), and the first is (ai)↦(−∑iui(ai),(ai))(a_i)\mapsto(-\sum_i u_i(a_i),(a_i)). At point ii, the last map is the identity on AiA_i, and the first term has zero stalk. These descriptions prove exactness and compatibility with the arrows. Both nonzero terms are projective. In the finite category all their vector spaces remain finite-dimensional. If S=∅S=\varnothing, the category is just vector spaces and needs no nontrivial resolution.

Hence every object of each 𝒜w,S\mathcal A_{w,S}, and of its finite subcategory, has projective dimension at most one. In particular all its Yoneda extensions of degree at least two vanish.

The whole heart still has no second extensions

Let EE be any degree-nn Yoneda extension in 𝒜w\mathcal A_w, with n≥2n\geq2. It is an exact sequence containing finitely many sheaves. By (2) each has a finite exceptional set. Their union is one finite SS, so the entire sequence lies in 𝒜w,S\mathcal A_{w,S}. It represents zero there by (7). The exact inclusion into 𝒜w\mathcal A_w preserves its equivalence with the zero Yoneda extension. Therefore

Ext⁡𝒜wn(B,A)=0(n≥2),Ext⁡𝒜fn(B,A)=0(n≥2).(8) \operatorname{Ext}^n_{\mathcal A_w}(B,A)=0 \quad(n\geq2), \qquad \operatorname{Ext}^n_{\mathcal A_f}(B,A)=0 \quad(n\geq2). \qquad\text{(8)}

This proves the claim after allowing all finite complex refinements. We did not assume that a fixed-quiver projective stays projective in the union; Exercise 2 shows that it need not.

The following proof identifies Yoneda nn-extensions with Hom⁡Db(𝒜)(B,A[n])\operatorname{Hom}_{D^b(\mathcal A)}(B,A[n]) for any abelian category. No supply of global injectives or projectives in the full heart is required. The bounded category is a full subcategory of the ordinary derived category. Extension classes and derived morphisms

Here is the extension comparison used in (8), including the homotopy step. Let 𝒜\mathcal A be any abelian category and let A,BA,B be its objects. No existence of enough injectives or projectives is assumed. Use a larger universe if needed for the localization. The required roof construction and equality criterion are proved in Complexes, cones and localization, Theorem 4.2; its Proposition 5.2 and Lemma 5.3 give the truncations and their triangles.

Extension comparison. For every integer n≥1n\geq1, equivalence classes of exact sequences

E:0⟶A→iE−n+1⟶⋯⟶E0→qB⟶0 E:\quad 0\longrightarrow A\xrightarrow{i}E^{-n+1} \longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow E^0\xrightarrow{q}B \longrightarrow0

are naturally identified with Hom⁡Db(𝒜)(B,A[n])\operatorname{Hom}_{D^b(\mathcal A)}(B,A[n]). Equivalence means the relation generated by maps of exact sequences that are the identity on both ends; in fact two equivalent sequences have a common sequence mapping to both. The identification respects Baer addition and sends the split zero extension to the zero morphism.

Proof. Put AA in degree −n-n, followed by the displayed middle terms, and call the resulting complex KEK_E. Exactness says that qE:KE→B[0]q_E:K_E\to B[0] is a quasi-isomorphism. Projection pE:KE→A[n]p_E:K_E\to A[n], identity in degree −n-n and zero elsewhere, is a chain map. Define the extension class to be the roof pEqE−1p_Eq_E^{-1}. A map of extensions gives a common roof refinement, so equivalent extensions have the same class. This fixes the sign convention by the projection itself; no convention for a connecting arrow is silently substituted.

The construction is natural in the endpoints. Ambient second classes are nonzero

Apply the existing ordinary derived inverse/direct adjunction to p:X→ptp:X\to\mathrm{pt}. Since p−1k=kXp^{-1}k=k_X and kk is projective over itself,

Hom⁡Db(kX)(kX,kX[2])≃Hom⁡D(k)(k,RΓ(X;k)[2])≃H2(X;k)≃k22.(9) \begin{split} \operatorname{Hom}_{D^b(k_X)}(k_X,k_X[2]) &\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{D(k)}(k,R\Gamma(X;k)[2])\\ &\simeq H^2(X;k)\simeq k^{22}. \end{split} \qquad\text{(9)}

The exact adjunction is proved in Sheaves of modules and their derived categories, Proposition 10.2. It applies to constant coefficients without a commutativity restriction. The sheaf-to-singular identification in (9) is proved in Sheaf cohomology and singular cohomology on manifolds, Theorem 1.3. Apply it to the underlying real four-manifold of the K3 surface and the additive group of the coefficient field. Its naturality in coefficient homomorphisms makes this an identification of vector spaces, since scalar multiplication is such a homomorphism. The dimension twenty-two comes from the quartic topology and proper-family transport; the convergent Picard-zero deformation supplies the fibre and the proper holomorphic family connecting it to the quartic. The target complex is bounded; Corollary 5.1 of that same lesson gives vanishing above degree four for this constant-sheaf global cohomology calculation.

The constant sheaf kXk_X belongs to both hearts. By (8), its second self-morphism group in either source derived category of (1) is zero. In the target it is (9), since the constructible target is a full ambient subcategory. Therefore neither realization functor is full. This alone disproves equivalence.

A constructible object with no constructible-term representative

We can also identify an object missing from the image. By (8), each bounded source complex LL splits as

L≃⨁qHq(L)[−q].(10) L\simeq\bigoplus_q H^q(L)[-q]. \qquad\text{(10)}

Here is the bounded argument. Induct on its cohomology range. The good-truncation triangle, with its naturality proved in Lemma 5.3, has connecting map from Hb(L)[−b]H^b(L)[-b] to (τ≤b−1L)[1](\tau_{\leq b-1}L)[1]. After the lower truncation has split by induction, its components belong to Ext⁡b−q+1(Hb(L),Hq(L))\operatorname{Ext}^{b-q+1}(H^b(L),H^q(L)), with q<bq<b. Each degree is at least two and is zero by (8). The triangle splits, completing the induction. A splitting exists; no canonical choice is asserted.

Choose nonzero u:kX→kX[2]u:k_X\to k_X[2] in (9), and use the standard cone/rotation convention to set

E=Cone⁡(−u)[−1].(11) E=\operatorname{Cone}(-u)[-1]. \qquad\text{(11)}

Rotating its shifted cone triangle gives

kX[1]⟶E⟶kX→ukX[2].(12) k_X[1]\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow k_X \xrightarrow{\ u\ }k_X[2]. \qquad\text{(12)}

The minus sign in (11) is deliberate: rotation negates the shifted first map. Thus the last map in (12) is uu, not −u-u. The cohomology sequence gives

H−1(E)=kX,H0(E)=kX,Hq(E)=0(q≠−1,0).(13) H^{-1}(E)=k_X,\qquad H^0(E)=k_X, \qquad H^q(E)=0\ (q\neq-1,0). \qquad\text{(13)}

The first and second maps of (12) identify it with the canonical truncation triangle. Its connecting class, or two-stage cohomology gluing class, is the nonzero uu. Hence this triangle does not split. The object EE lies in the perfect complex-constructible target: both cohomology sheaves in (13) have perfect constant stalks.

If E≃ℛw(L)E\simeq\mathcal R_w(L), cohomology preservation gives only these same two cohomology sheaves for LL. Formula (10) would then make E≃kX[1]⊕kXE\simeq k_X[1]\oplus k_X. Its canonical truncation class would be zero, contrary to (12). The same argument applies to ℛf\mathcal R_f. Thus neither functor is essentially surjective. No bounded complex with complex-constructible terms represents EE, although its cohomology sheaves are as simple as possible.

Why the real equivalence remains compatible with this example

The complex surface is also a real analytic four-manifold. The two constant cohomology sheaves make EE perfect real constructible. Derived constructibility through common triangulations supplies a real-constructible-term representative and real-constructible Yoneda representatives for (9). These can use real simplex and face strata. On this XX, complex analytic refinements only add finitely many points, and (8) shows that such additions cannot supply the missing two-stage class. The example compares the full categories in both cases, rather than declaring a fixed stratification immutable.

1. Check the resolution at every vertex

Difficulty: Introductory.

For two exceptional points let F=(M,A1,A2,u1,u2)F=(M,A_1,A_2,u_1,u_2). Write the generic and point maps in (7), prove exactness, and explain why the proof works for infinite vector spaces. What changes when both point modules vanish?

Solution. At the generic vertex the sequence is

0→A1⊕A2→(a1,a2)↦(−u1a1−u2a2,a1,a2)M⊕A1⊕A2→(m,a1,a2)↦m+u1a1+u2a2M→0. 0\to A_1\oplus A_2 \xrightarrow{(a_1,a_2)\mapsto(-u_1a_1-u_2a_2,a_1,a_2)} M\oplus A_1\oplus A_2 \xrightarrow{(m,a_1,a_2)\mapsto m+u_1a_1+u_2a_2}M\to0.

The first map is injective; the last is surjective by choosing the MM-component. Its kernel is exactly the displayed graph. At point ii the sequence is 0→0→Ai→1Ai→00\to0\to A_i\xrightarrow{1}A_i\to0. The generic maps on the PiP_i-summands are uiu_i, so they commute with the identity point arrows. This proves a short exact sequence of representations and hence of sheaves. No finite-dimensional step was used. Projectivity follows from (6) and exactness of Hom from any vector space over a field. 2. Refining the point set can destroy projectivity

Difficulty: Intermediate.

With only one exceptional point, prove that kXk_X is projective in its diagram category. Add a second point and show that

0⟶P0⟶P1⊕P2⟶kX⟶0 0\longrightarrow P_0\longrightarrow P_1\oplus P_2 \longrightarrow k_X\longrightarrow0

is nonsplit. Compute Ext⁡1(kX,P0)\operatorname{Ext}^1(k_X,P_0) in the two-point category.

Solution. For one point the constant sheaf is P1P_1: its point and generic modules are kk, with identity arrow. It is projective by (6). With two points its diagram instead has generic kk, both point modules kk, and both arrows identity. The displayed surjection is identity at each point and addition k⊕k→kk\oplus k\to k at the generic vertex. Its kernel has zero point modules and generic {(a,−a)}\{(a,-a)\}, namely P0P_0.

A splitting would have one generic map k→k2k\to k^2. Compatibility at point 1 forces that map to send 11 to (1,0)(1,0); compatibility at point 2 forces (0,1)(0,1). This is impossible. Moreover Hom⁡(Pi,P0)=0\operatorname{Hom}(P_i,P_0)=0, since the point identity arrow of PiP_i forces its generic map to P0P_0 to vanish. The higher-extension proof uses a common finite category for an entire extension, not that false persistence claim.

3. Two nonsplit extensions cannot produce the missing class

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Let 0→A→B→C→00\to A\to B\to C\to0 and 0→C→D→F→00\to C\to D\to F\to0 be short exact sequences of weakly complex-constructible sheaves on this XX, with no chosen common stratification. Solution. Each of the five sheaves has a finite exceptional set by (2). Take the union SS. 6. A real representative must use additional sheaves

A fixed projective-line stratification already loses a class

A useful way to read the second example is to ask where an internally acyclic resolution ceases to compute the ambient functor. This fixed-stratification example has a different scope from the K3 argument: refining the projective line can remove the obstruction, whereas the K3 proof handles every allowable complex refinement.

The preceding K3 argument permits every complex refinement. There is also a simpler failure for one fixed stratification, which must be distinguished from that stronger assertion.
