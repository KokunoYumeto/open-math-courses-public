Small balls, central fibres and supported cohomology

The three small-ball comparisons can be read from one arrow between two coefficient complexes. The ordinary sections give its source, the punctured sections give its target, and supported sections give its fibre. We first calculate that arrow on an interval, including its closed endpoint and compact-support maps, then transfer the calculation along the proper scalar map h=|φ|2h=|\varphi|^2.

Scope. The three comparisons

Let XX be a finite-dimensional real analytic manifold, Hausdorff and countable at infinity. Assume φ\varphi is proper on the closed support of FF. No finite-generation or perfect-stalk hypothesis is imposed.

Local support before and after an ordinary image

We first supply the sheaf-theoretic image estimate used by the geometric argument. Let f:Y→Xf:Y\to X be C1C^1, and let G∈D+(kY)G\in D^+(k_Y). The manifolds have the standing finite dimension bounds; neither constructibility nor finite coefficients are needed in this section. For a closed subset ZZ of a local open domain, write ℒZG\mathcal L_ZG for the derived sheaf of sections supported in ZZ. Its derived global sections are RΓZ(Y;G)R\Gamma_Z(Y;G). This distinguishes a supported sheaf from a single complex of global sections.

For a real C1C^1 function ψ\psi near yy, its local support test is

𝒯y,ψ(G)=(ℒ{ψ≥ψ(y)}G)y.(T1) \mathcal T_{y,\psi}(G) =\bigl(\mathcal L_{\{\psi\geq\psi(y)\}}G\bigr)_y. \qquad\text{(T1)}

A covector pp is outside SS⁡(G)\operatorname{SS}(G) when some open cotangent neighbourhood WW of pp has every test (T1) zero whenever (y,dψy)∈W(y,d\psi_y)\in W. The neighbourhood precedes the choices of point and function. This definition includes zero covectors and implies closedness and positive conicity: multiplying a test function by a positive constant preserves its support set and scales its differential.

Put S=supp⁡(G)S=\operatorname{supp}(G), the closure of the union of the nonzero cohomology stalks. Outside SS the complex vanishes on a neighbourhood, so all its tests vanish there. Conversely, at a nonzero stalk the constant-function test is that stalk itself. Taking closure shows (y,0)∈SS⁡(G)(y,0)\in\operatorname{SS}(G) at every y∈Sy\in S. Hence the base projection of the actual microsupport is exactly SS.

For a closed Z⊂XZ\subset X, there is a natural equality of derived sheaf operations

ℒZRf*G≃Rf*ℒf−1ZG.(T2) \mathcal L_Z Rf_*G \simeq Rf_*\mathcal L_{f^{-1}Z}G. \qquad\text{(T2)}

Here is a resolution proof with its acyclicity checked. For a closed inclusion i:Z↪Xi:Z\hookrightarrow X, the underived support functor is Γ_Z=i*i!\underline\Gamma_Z=i_*i^!. The functor i!i^! sends injectives to injectives because its left adjoint i*i_* is exact. The functor i*i_* also preserves injectives because its left adjoint i−1i^{-1} is exact. Thus Γ_Z\underline\Gamma_Z preserves injectives. Likewise f*f_* preserves injectives by exactness of f−1f^{-1}.

For any sheaf II, a section of f*If_*I on UU vanishes on U\ZU\setminus Z exactly when its corresponding section of II vanishes on f−1(U\Z)f^{-1}(U\setminus Z). Consequently Γ_Zf*I=f*Γ_f−1ZI\underline\Gamma_Z f_*I=f_*\underline\Gamma_{f^{-1}Z}I, naturally on all opens. Apply this equality to a bounded-below injective resolution of GG. The preservation just proved shows that both sides compute the derived composites, proving (T2). This identity itself does not require properness.

Now assume f|Sf|_S is proper. The complex ℒf−1ZG\mathcal L_{f^{-1}Z}G is still supported on SS: localization is local, and it vanishes where GG vanishes. Write it as a closed direct image from SS and resolve there by injectives. Their closed images on YY are injective and supported on SS, so their ordinary and proper images by ff agree. The derived fibre formula (F6) of the duality lesson applies. Its compact and ordinary fibre sections agree on the compact coefficient support. It therefore turns (T2) into

(ℒZRf*G)x≃RΓ(f−1(x);(ℒf−1ZG)|f−1(x)).(T3) \bigl(\mathcal L_ZRf_*G\bigr)_x \simeq R\Gamma\bigl(f^{-1}(x); (\mathcal L_{f^{-1}Z}G)|_{f^{-1}(x)}\bigr). \qquad\text{(T3)}

The same statement holds after restricting to any open domain of a test function. Properness survives that base restriction. This is the precise point where a closed fibre may be used; the formula is not asserted for an arbitrary nonproper ordinary image.

The proper-image microsupport estimate

In the cotangent correspondence set

A={(y;ξ):(y,dfytξ)∈SS⁡(G)}⊂Y×XT*X,fπ(y;ξ)=(f(y);ξ),Γ=fπ(A).(T4) \begin{aligned} A&=\{(y;\xi): (y,df_y^t\xi)\in\operatorname{SS}(G)\} \subset Y\times_XT^*X,\\ f_\pi(y;\xi)&=(f(y);\xi),\qquad \Gamma=f_\pi(A). \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(T4)}

(S∩f−1Q)×XK.(T5) \bigl(S\cap f^{-1}Q\bigr)\times_X K. \qquad\text{(T5)}

The first factor is compact by support properness, and the fibre product is closed in the product of two compact spaces. Thus (T5) is compact. Proper maps between these locally compact Hausdorff spaces are closed, as proved in (F1) of the duality lesson, so Γ\Gamma is closed. It is conic because transpose differentials commute with positive scaling.

Take p∉Γp\notin\Gamma and an open cotangent neighbourhood WW of pp disjoint from Γ\Gamma. If a C1C^1 function ϕ\phi near xx has (x,dϕx)∈W(x,d\phi_x)\in W, then (y,d(ϕ∘f)y)∉SS⁡(G)(y,d(\phi\circ f)_y)\notin\operatorname{SS}(G) for every y∈f−1(x)y\in f^{-1}(x): otherwise (T4) would put (x,dϕx)(x,d\phi_x) in Γ\Gamma.

Apply (T3) to Z={ϕ≥ϕ(x)}Z=\{\phi\geq\phi(x)\}. At each fibre point the restricted complex in its right side has stalk (T1) for ψ=ϕ∘f\psi=\phi\circ f, and that stalk is zero. A sheaf complex whose cohomology stalks all vanish is zero, so the whole fibre complex is zero. Hence the target local support test vanishes. The same argument works for every point and function with differential in the already chosen WW. By the neighbourhood-uniform definition,

SS⁡(Rf*G)⊂fπfd−1SS⁡(G),fd(y;ξ)=(y;dfytξ).(T6) \operatorname{SS}(Rf_*G)\subset f_\pi f_d^{-1}\operatorname{SS}(G), \qquad f_d(y;\xi)=(y;df_y^t\xi). \qquad\text{(T6)}

Properness on SS also gives Rf!G≃Rf*GRf_!G\simeq Rf_*G, so the same estimate applies to that image. This proof includes critical points, zero covectors and arbitrary bounded-below coefficients. It proves an inclusion; no equality for every proper map is inferred. The classical support-test argument is Proposition 4.1.1(i), printed p. 61, of Kashiwara and Schapira’s freely available Microlocal Study of Sheaves. The resolution argument (T2), the support check for (T3) and the compactness in (T5) supply the required operation details here.

Closed embeddings preserve every local support test

Let i:S↪Ui:S\hookrightarrow U be a closed smooth embedding and H∈D+(kS)H\in D^+(k_S), with arbitrary stalk modules. For a local C1C^1 function ψ\psi on UU, the support identity underlying (T2) specializes to

(ℒ{ψ≥ψ(x)}i*H)x≃(ℒ{ψ|S≥ψ(x)}H)x(x∈S).(E1) \bigl(\mathcal L_{\{\psi\ge\psi(x)\}}i_*H\bigr)_x \simeq \bigl(\mathcal L_{\{\psi|_S\ge\psi(x)\}}H\bigr)_x \quad(x\in S). \qquad\text{(E1)}

Here i*i_* is exact. On an injective resolution of HH, sections of its direct image supported in a target closed set are exactly sections supported in the inverse image. Closed direct image preserves injectives, since its left adjoint i−1i^{-1} is exact. Thus the termwise equality derives to the displayed identity; no equivalence between different definitions of microsupport is imported.

Closed-embedding formula. With id:T*U|S→T*Si_d:T^*U|_S\to T^*S restricting covectors and iπi_\pi including them in T*UT^*U,

SS⁡(i*H)=iπid−1SS⁡(H).(E2) \operatorname{SS}(i_*H)=i_\pi i_d^{-1}\operatorname{SS}(H). \qquad\text{(E2)}

The upper inclusion is (T6), since a closed embedding is proper. For the reverse, suppose a target covector (x;η0,ν0)(x;\eta_0,\nu_0) is outside SS⁡(i*H)\operatorname{SS}(i_*H). Choose smooth product coordinates (u,z)(u,z) with S={z=0}S=\{z=0\}, and a single open cotangent neighborhood WW on which every target test vanishes. The preimage of WW under the continuous map (u,η)↦(u,0;η,ν0)(u,\eta)\mapsto(u,0;\eta,\nu_0) is an open neighborhood VV of (x,η0)(x,\eta_0) in T*ST^*S. Every intrinsic C1C^1 test gg at a point with (u,dgu)∈V(u,dg_u)\in V has an ambient extension

g̃(u,z)=g(u)+⟨ν0,z⟩.(E3) \widetilde g(u,z)=g(u)+\langle\nu_0,z\rangle. \qquad\text{(E3)}

Its derivative at (u,0)(u,0) lies in WW, and (E1) identifies its zero local support test with the intrinsic test for gg. All tests in the same VV therefore vanish, so (x,η0)∉SS⁡(H)(x,\eta_0)\notin\operatorname{SS}(H). Outside SS the image sheaf is zero locally. This proves equality, including zero intrinsic covectors and every conormal direction. The formula uses neither a rank assumption on a different map nor a finite-generation hypothesis.

The bounded-below scope in these two sections is justified at the resolution level: (T2) uses a bounded-below injective resolution, and the proper-fibre formula (F6) is already proved for bounded-below inputs. In (T4)–(T6), properness on the closed coefficient support supplies the required compactness, and the local tests annihilate the entire fibre complex. The identical supported-section equality and test-function extensions prove (E1)–(E3) in this scope. None of these arguments uses an upper cohomological truncation. The weakly constructible comparisons and the zero-section criterion below retain their separately stated bounded hypotheses.

Continuing cohomology through a compact moving boundary

The local-constancy criterion needs control of cohomology on nested balls. We prove the deformation result that supplies it. It applies to D+D^+ complexes on locally compact Hausdorff spaces and uses no constructibility assumption. The human source is the freely available Astérisque 128, Theorem 1.4.3, printed pp. 30–31. The proof below includes the inverse-limit and endpoint arguments behind its continuation step.

We use two continuity calculations. First, compact-neighborhood continuity (D5) extends to A∈D+(kX)A\in D^+(k_X):

colim⁡K⊂V openHq(V;A)≃Hq(K;A|K)(K compact).(N1) \mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_{K\subset V\text{ open}}H^q(V;A) \simeq H^q(K;A|_K) \quad(K\text{ compact}). \qquad\text{(N1)}

The same injective-complex proof applies. Restriction to an open preserves injectives, and restriction of each injective term to KK is c-soft, hence acyclic for sections on the compact space. The bounded-below acyclic-resolution comparison used for the fibre formula therefore computes the right side. The compact-germ section identity holds termwise, and exact filtered colimits commute with cohomology. This verifies (N1) for bounded-below complexes, including ones not bounded above.

Second, for increasing opens VnV_n with union VV,

To verify the sequence and the degree q−1q-1, choose a bounded-below injective complex II and put Cn=Γ(Vn;I)C_n=\Gamma(V_n;I). Every restriction is degreewise surjective by flabbiness. For any tower MnM_n with restrictions rnr_n, write

Its kernel is lim⁡Mn\lim M_n; by definition its cokernel is lim⁡1Mn\lim{}^1 M_n. For the complexes CnC_n, the map Δ\Delta is degreewise onto: choose a0a_0, then recursively lift an−bna_n-b_n to an+1a_{n+1}. Its kernel is Γ(V;I)\Gamma(V;I), by sheaf gluing. Products of modules are exact, so the long exact cohomology sequence gives (N2). The same recursion shows lim⁡1Mn=0\lim{}^1 M_n=0 whenever the transitions are surjective. In particular, it vanishes for a constant system of isomorphisms. Constancy only in degree qq would not remove the left term of (N2).

Zero microsupport gives a constant bounded complex

For F∈Db(kX)F\in D^b(k_X) on a smooth finite-dimensional manifold, the following are equivalent:

  1. SS⁡(F)⊂TX*X\operatorname{SS}(F)\subset T_X^*X, the zero section.
  2. Locally, FF is a constant bounded coefficient complex.
  3. Every cohomology sheaf of FF is locally constant.

The coefficient modules may be infinite. Condition 2 refers to the whole derived object and retains its extension data.

From zero microsupport to actual descent. Work in a coordinate neighborhood on which condition 1 holds and choose a ball B(x,R)B(x,R) whose closure lies in that neighborhood. For y∈B(x,R)y\in B(x,R) and 0<r<R−|x−y|0<r<R-|x-y|, interpolate centers and radii:

c(t)=(1−t)y+tx,ρ(t)=(1−t)r+tR,Ut=B(c(t),ρ(t)).(Z1) c(t)=(1-t)y+tx,\quad \rho(t)=(1-t)r+tR, \quad U_t=B(c(t),\rho(t)). \qquad\text{(Z1)}

Use a slightly larger open parameter interval containing [0,1][0,1]. It can be chosen so that every radius is positive and the closures of all balls stay in a fixed compact subset of the coordinate neighborhood. Since R−r>|x−y|R-r>|x-y|, the triangle inequality gives Us¯⊂Ut\overline{U_s}\subset U_t for s<ts<t, and continuity gives the increasing-union property. The front (N4) is the sphere ∂Us\partial U_s: interior and exterior points are excluded by continuity, while each sphere point lies in every later ball and outside UsU_s. It is contained in UtU_t for s<ts<t. At the only remaining endpoint s=ts=t, the complement of the ball is tested by ψ(z)=|z−c(s)|2\psi(z)=|z-c(s)|^2, whose differential is nonzero on that sphere. Condition 1 makes each such local support test zero. All deformation hypotheses hold; hence the actual restriction

RΓ(B(x,R);F)→∼RΓ(B(y,r);F)(Z2) R\Gamma(B(x,R);F)\xrightarrow{\sim}R\Gamma(B(y,r);F) \qquad\text{(Z2)}

is an isomorphism. Let r↓0r\downarrow0. With a fixed injective resolution, exact filtered colimits of these section complexes identify with the stalk complex, so (Z2) gives RΓ(B(x,R);F)≃FyR\Gamma(B(x,R);F)\simeq F_y for every yy in the original ball. Put C=RΓ(B(x,R);F)C=R\Gamma(B(x,R);F). The adjunction counit is

CB(x,R)⟶F|B(x,R).(Z3) C_{B(x,R)}\longrightarrow F|_{B(x,R)}. \qquad\text{(Z3)}

Its stalk maps are exactly the restriction maps just proved invertible. It is therefore an isomorphism of sheaf complexes. Since any stalk of this nonempty ball is bounded in the original global degree interval for FF, so is CC. This proves condition 2 without splitting cohomology degrees.

Constant coefficients and the converse. For a constant bounded coefficient complex CUC_U, every C1C^1 test at a nonzero covector has a submersion coordinate chart in which its support set is {u1≥0}\{u_1\ge0\}. The chart and its open negative half are products of contractible intervals. The ordinary homotopy calculation, including its actual unit, identifies the derived sections of CUC_U on each with CC. Restriction is the identity under those units. The localization triangle consequently gives zero for the supported stalk. This applies to every nonzero test in a neighborhood of a nonzero cotangent point, so SS⁡(CU)\operatorname{SS}(C_U) is contained in the zero section.

Condition 2 plainly gives condition 3 by exactness of the constant-sheaf functor. Natural maps distinguish infinite self-similarity from stabilization

Over a field let V=⨁n≥0kenV=\bigoplus_{n\geq0}k e_n. V→ΔV⊕V,Δ(v)=(v,v).(20) V\xrightarrow{\Delta}V\oplus V,\qquad \Delta(v)=(v,v). \qquad\text{(20)}
