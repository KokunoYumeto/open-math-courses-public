Sheaf proof readings

Fourier duality through a test complex on the base

Course: SH-02. Unit: SH02-FDN. Language: English.

The duality results below have proofs relative to the stated sheaf-operation imports. The final section distinguishes the literal and adjunction-normalized Fourier comparisons and uses their separately proved relation. The course supplies an explicit realization of the paired inverse equations without attributing an unrecorded geometric recipe to the antecedent.

Compare the ordinary Fourier-duality statement in Kashiwara and Schapira, Microlocal Study of Sheaves, Astérisque 128 (1985), Theorem 2.1.3(iii), p. 40, and the adjunction proofs in Schapira’s An Introduction to Sheaves on Grothendieck Topologies, 1 August 2026, §4.6. The proof below uses one variable test complex on the base to account for both dualities. Coefficient duals remain internal derived Hom objects; no biduality assumption occurs. The final source account distinguishes the source statements from the additional maps and boundedness arguments supplied here.

SH02-FDN-SETUP. Coordinates, domains, and three different transforms

Let kk be a commutative ring with identity and finite global dimension. Let BB be a locally compact Hausdorff space and let τ:E→B\tau:E\to B be a real vector bundle of fixed finite rank nn. Denote its dual by π:E*→B\pi:E^*\to B. Write

X=E×BE*,p:X→E,q:X→E*,ρ=τp=πq, X=E\times_B E^*,\qquad p:X\to E,\qquad q:X\to E^*, \qquad \rho=\tau p=\pi q,

and put N={(x,ξ):⟨x,ξ⟩≤0}N=\{(x,\xi):\langle x,\xi\rangle\leq0\}. For a closed subset AA, the operations LA=L⊗kLkAL_A=L\otimes_k^L k_A and RΓAL=Rℋom(kA,L)R\Gamma_A L=R\mathcal Hom(k_A,L) remain distinct.

Let OO be the orientation local system of EE, regarded as a sheaf on BB. The positive dual-orientation identification identifies it with the orientation local system of E*E^*: a positively oriented basis determines the positive dual basis. The canonical pairing on the sign local system gives O⊗kO≃kBO\otimes_k O\simeq k_B. Set W=O[n]W=O[n], so W−1=O[−n]W^{-1}=O[-n], where the inverse refers to tensor product.

We use these functors:

TEF=Rq!((p−1F)N),F∈Dℝ>0+(E;k),SEG=Rp*RΓN(q!G),G∈Dℝ>0+(E*;k),IEH=Rq*RΓN(p!H),H∈Dℝ>0+(E;k).(FDN1) \begin{aligned} T_EF&=Rq_!((p^{-1}F)_N), &&F\in D^+_{\mathbb R_{>0}}(E;k),\\ S_EG&=Rp_*R\Gamma_N(q^!G), &&G\in D^+_{\mathbb R_{>0}}(E^*;k),\\ I_EH&=Rq_*R\Gamma_N(p^!H), &&H\in D^+_{\mathbb R_{>0}}(E;k). \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(FDN1)}

The first two are the transform and its inverse from the Fourier kernel unit. The third is the inverse transform with the roles of EE and E*E^* exchanged; thus IE=SE*I_E=S_{E^*} after permuting the coordinates of the product. Both TET_E and IEI_E go from EE to E*E^*. In particular, IEI_E in the duality theorem below is not SES_E, whose domain is different.

The boundedness symbols are global. We take F∈Dℝ>0b(E;k)F\in D^b_{\mathbb R_{>0}}(E;k) in every duality assertion. Its cohomology may have arbitrary stalk modules, angular variation, and base variation. There is no field, Noetherian, constructibility, finite-generation, or compactness assumption. A dual of such an FF is asserted to belong to D+D^+, not automatically to DbD^b.

The sheaf-operation imports used here are the proper-support projection formula, Rf!⊣f!Rf_!\dashv f^!, f−1⊣Rf*f^{-1}\dashv Rf_*, composition of extraordinary inverse images with its adjunction coherence, tensor–Hom adjunction, and the vector-bundle formula

τ!H≃τ−1H⊗kτ−1O[n],π!H≃π−1H⊗kπ−1O[n].(FDN2) \tau^!H\simeq \tau^{-1}H\otimes_k\tau^{-1}O[n], \qquad \pi^!H\simeq \pi^{-1}H\otimes_k\pi^{-1}O[n]. \qquad\text{(FDN2)}

Finite cohomological dimension of the proper direct images of these finite-rank bundle projections supplies their extraordinary inverse images. This is a condition on abelian sheaves in the underlying foundation, not a replacement by a bound for one chosen coefficient object. The projection formula is the one for proper support and has no perfectness requirement on a bounded tensor factor. The kernel unit states these imports and their adjunction maps. Conicity of internal Hom with bounded first input and bounded-below second input is provided by conic descent.

SH02-FDN-PROJECTION-RANGE — Why bounded-below tests are allowed

The projection formula used in FDN5 and FDN6 has one bounded factor and one bounded-below factor. Its extension from the bounded formula can be checked degree by degree, without interchanging a direct image with an infinite limit. Let gg be the finite global dimension of kk, let the bounded factor have lower bound aa, and let CC be the bounded-below factor. Compare CC with τ≤NC\tau_{\leq N}C by their truncation triangle. The tail is in D≥N+1D^{\geq N+1}. In either side of the projection formula its contribution lies in D≥N+1+a−gD^{\geq N+1+a-g}: ordinary inverse image is exact, derived tensor has this lower bound, and a right-derived proper image preserves lower bounds. The same estimate applies when the bounded factor is on the domain, because its proper image still has lower bound aa.

Fix a degree jj and choose NN so that N+a−g>jN+a-g>j. The truncation comparisons then induce isomorphisms in degree jj on both sides. The natural projection-formula map for the truncated bounded factors is an isomorphism by the bounded formula, so naturality gives the same conclusion for the original map in degree jj. Since jj was arbitrary, its cone has zero cohomology and the original map is an isomorphism. Finite cohomological dimension also keeps the proper image of a bounded factor bounded, as required in FDN4. Thus the tests in FDN5–FDN6 range over all of D+D^+, and their Yoneda arguments do not silently replace that category by bounded objects.

SH02-FDN-HOM. Two adjunction calculations with their maps

We give the calculations because replacing an internal Hom by a tensor with a coefficient dual would impose a false finiteness condition here.

Lemma. Suppose f:Y→Zf:Y\to Z is a continuous map of locally compact Hausdorff spaces and f!f_! has finite cohomological dimension. Let A∈Db(Z;k)A\in D^b(Z;k), L∈Db(Y;k)L\in D^b(Y;k), and Q∈D+(Z;k)Q\in D^+(Z;k). There are natural isomorphisms

f!Rℋom(A,Q)→∼Rℋom(f−1A,f!Q),(FDN3) f^!R\mathcal Hom(A,Q) \xrightarrow{\sim} R\mathcal Hom(f^{-1}A,f^!Q), \qquad\text{(FDN3)}

Rf*Rℋom(L,f!Q)→∼Rℋom(Rf!L,Q).(FDN4) Rf_*R\mathcal Hom(L,f^!Q) \xrightarrow{\sim}R\mathcal Hom(Rf_!L,Q). \qquad\text{(FDN4)}

Proof of FDN3. For an arbitrary C∈D+(Y;k)C\in D^+(Y;k), the following natural bijections use, in order, the exceptional adjunction, tensor–Hom adjunction, the proper-support projection formula, and the two adjunctions in reverse order:

Hom⁡(C,f!Rℋom(A,Q))≃Hom⁡(Rf!C,Rℋom(A,Q))≃Hom⁡(Rf!C⊗kLA,Q)≃Hom⁡(Rf!(C⊗kLf−1A),Q)≃Hom⁡(C⊗kLf−1A,f!Q)≃Hom⁡(C,Rℋom(f−1A,f!Q)).(FDN5) \begin{aligned} \operatorname{Hom}(C,f^!R\mathcal Hom(A,Q)) &\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(Rf_!C,R\mathcal Hom(A,Q))\\ &\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(Rf_!C\otimes_k^L A,Q)\\ &\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(Rf_!(C\otimes_k^L f^{-1}A),Q)\\ &\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(C\otimes_k^L f^{-1}A,f^!Q)\\ &\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(C,R\mathcal Hom(f^{-1}A,f^!Q)). \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(FDN5)}

All these objects lie in D+D^+: the bounded tensor factor and finite global dimension give a lower tensor bound, a bounded first input gives a lower internal-Hom bound, and f!f^! has a uniform lower amplitude bound. Yoneda on D+(Y;k)D^+(Y;k) therefore gives FDN3, including its direction.

Its actual morphism is adjoint to an evaluation map. Write J=Rℋom(A,Q)J=R\mathcal Hom(A,Q). The counit Rf!f!J→JRf_!f^!J\to J, tensor–Hom evaluation J⊗kLA→QJ\otimes_k^L A\to Q, and projection formula give

Rf!(f!J⊗kLf−1A)≃Rf!f!J⊗kLA⟶J⊗kLA⟶Q. Rf_!(f^!J\otimes_k^L f^{-1}A) \simeq Rf_!f^!J\otimes_k^L A \longrightarrow J\otimes_k^L A \longrightarrow Q.

Transpose this across Rf!⊣f!Rf_!\dashv f^! and then tensor–Hom. Its effect on Hom⁡(C,−)\operatorname{Hom}(C,-) is precisely FDN5. Thus the isomorphism is specified by the imported counit and evaluation; it is not an arbitrary choice of an isomorphism between the two objects.

Proof of FDN4. For an arbitrary C∈D+(Z;k)C\in D^+(Z;k), similarly

Hom⁡(C,Rf*Rℋom(L,f!Q))≃Hom⁡(f−1C,Rℋom(L,f!Q))≃Hom⁡(f−1C⊗kLL,f!Q)≃Hom⁡(Rf!(f−1C⊗kLL),Q)≃Hom⁡(C⊗kLRf!L,Q)≃Hom⁡(C,Rℋom(Rf!L,Q)).(FDN6) \begin{aligned} \operatorname{Hom}(C,Rf_*R\mathcal Hom(L,f^!Q)) &\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(f^{-1}C,R\mathcal Hom(L,f^!Q))\\ &\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(f^{-1}C\otimes_k^L L,f^!Q)\\ &\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(Rf_!(f^{-1}C\otimes_k^L L),Q)\\ &\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(C\otimes_k^L Rf_!L,Q)\\ &\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(C,R\mathcal Hom(Rf_!L,Q)). \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(FDN6)}

Here Rf!LRf_!L is bounded because LL is bounded and f!f_! has finite cohomological dimension. Hence both representing objects are in D+D^+, and Yoneda again applies.

For an explicit description put J=Rf*Rℋom(L,f!Q)J=Rf_*R\mathcal Hom(L,f^!Q). The ordinary counit f−1J→Rℋom(L,f!Q)f^{-1}J\to R\mathcal Hom(L,f^!Q), evaluation, and the exceptional counit give

J⊗kLRf!L≃Rf!(f−1J⊗kLL)⟶Rf!f!Q⟶Q. J\otimes_k^L Rf_!L \simeq Rf_!(f^{-1}J\otimes_k^L L) \longrightarrow Rf_!f^!Q \longrightarrow Q.

Tensor–Hom transposition is FDN4. In both calculations tensor symmetry means the usual Koszul symmetry. The proof never pulls internal Hom through an arbitrary ordinary inverse image. ▫\square

SH02-FDN-BASE. Duality relative to a variable test object

For H∈D+(B;k)H\in D^+(B;k), define

𝔻E,HF=Rℋom(F,τ!H),𝔻E*,HG=Rℋom(G,π!H).(FDN7) \mathbb D_{E,H}F=R\mathcal Hom(F,\tau^!H),\qquad \mathbb D_{E^*,H}G=R\mathcal Hom(G,\pi^!H). \qquad\text{(FDN7)}

Theorem. For every F∈Dℝ>0b(E;k)F\in D^b_{\mathbb R_{>0}}(E;k) and H∈D+(B;k)H\in D^+(B;k), there is a natural isomorphism

ΘF,H:IE𝔻E,HF→∼𝔻E*,H(TEF).(FDN8) \Theta_{F,H}: I_E\mathbb D_{E,H}F \xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb D_{E^*,H}(T_EF). \qquad\text{(FDN8)}

It is contravariant in FF, covariant in HH, and is compatible with restriction to an open part of the base. Both sides are conic objects of D+(E*;k)D^+(E^*;k).

Proof. The two expressions τp\tau p and πq\pi q define the same map ρ:X→B\rho:X\to B. Composition of extraordinary inverse images gives the specified comparison

p!τ!H≃ρ!H≃q!π!H.(FDN9) p^!\tau^!H\simeq\rho^!H\simeq q^!\pi^!H. \qquad\text{(FDN9)}

Use FDN3 for pp, this comparison, and tensor–Hom associativity. They give

IE𝔻E,HF=Rq*Rℋom(kN,p!Rℋom(F,τ!H))≃Rq*Rℋom(kN,Rℋom(p−1F,p!τ!H))≃Rq*Rℋom(kN,Rℋom(p−1F,q!π!H))≃Rq*Rℋom(kN⊗kLp−1F,q!π!H)→FDN4∼Rℋom(Rq!(kN⊗kLp−1F),π!H)=𝔻E*,H(TEF).(FDN10) \begin{aligned} I_E\mathbb D_{E,H}F &=Rq_*R\mathcal Hom(k_N,p^!R\mathcal Hom(F,\tau^!H))\\ &\simeq Rq_*R\mathcal Hom(k_N,R\mathcal Hom(p^{-1}F,p^!\tau^!H))\\ &\simeq Rq_*R\mathcal Hom(k_N,R\mathcal Hom(p^{-1}F,q^!\pi^!H))\\ &\simeq Rq_*R\mathcal Hom(k_N\otimes_k^L p^{-1}F,q^!\pi^!H)\\ &\xrightarrow[\mathrm{FDN4}]{\sim} R\mathcal Hom(Rq_!(k_N\otimes_k^L p^{-1}F),\pi^!H)\\ &=\mathbb D_{E^*,H}(T_EF). \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(FDN10)}

The displayed composite defines ΘF,H\Theta_{F,H}. In particular, its final arrow is the evaluation followed by the proper-support trace described in FDN4, and its first arrow is the mate described in FDN3. FDN9 is the unique composition comparison compatible with the composite exceptional adjunction; it does not involve choosing a trivialization of the bundle.

For the bounds, write F∈D[a,b]F\in D^{[a,b]} and H∈D≥cH\in D^{\geq c}. FDN2 gives τ!H∈D≥c−n\tau^!H\in D^{\geq c-n}, so 𝔻E,HF∈D≥c−n−b\mathbb D_{E,H}F\in D^{\geq c-n-b}. The sheaf kNk_N is flat over kk, as is seen at its stalks. Thus kN⊗kLp−1Fk_N\otimes_k^L p^{-1}F is bounded in [a,b][a,b]. Proper-support integration over a fiber of dimension nn puts TEFT_EF in D[a,b+n]D^{[a,b+n]}. The right side of FDN8 therefore has lower bound c−b−2nc-b-2n. This also agrees with the lower bound obtained by applying p!p^!, internal Hom with kNk_N, and the bounded-below ordinary direct image to the left side. No upper bound for the two internal Hom objects has been assumed.

The base-pulled object in FDN2 is conic. Conic internal Hom with bounded first input makes 𝔻E,HF\mathbb D_{E,H}F conic, and the equivariant-operation theorem makes its transform conic. The same argument applies to TEFT_EF on the other side. Naturality of all the arrows proves both stated variance assertions. Open restriction commutes with the projection formulas, evaluation, adjunction maps, and composition comparisons used here; consequently the displayed map restricts to the map for the restricted bundle. ▫\square

This theorem does not need Fourier inversion: it is a comparison of two specified expressions.

SH02-FDN-DUALITIES. The ordinary and absolute duals

Define the ordinary coefficient dual by

DE′F=Rℋom(F,kE),DE*′G=Rℋom(G,kE*).(FDN11) D'_E F=R\mathcal Hom(F,k_E),\qquad D'_{E^*}G=R\mathcal Hom(G,k_{E^*}). \qquad\text{(FDN11)}

These objects exist in D+D^+ for bounded inputs on the locally compact base specified above; defining them does not require an absolute dualizing complex.

Corollary. For every F∈Dℝ>0b(E;k)F\in D^b_{\mathbb R_{>0}}(E;k),

IE(DE′F)→∼DE*′(TEF).(FDN12) I_E(D'_E F)\xrightarrow{\sim}D'_{E^*}(T_EF). \qquad\text{(FDN12)}

Proof. Take H=W−1=O[−n]H=W^{-1}=O[-n]. Formula FDN2 and the evaluation of the invertible orientation complex give

τ!W−1≃kE,π!W−1≃kE*. \tau^!W^{-1}\simeq k_E, \qquad \pi^!W^{-1}\simeq k_{E^*}.

For the second identification we use the positive dual-orientation comparison fixed in the setup. Substitution in FDN8 proves FDN12, with the map specified there. ▫\square

For absolute duality, assume in addition that BB has finite c-soft dimension. In this assumption, the bound is the usual bound for abelian sheaves. Let aB:B→{pt}a_B:B\to\{\mathrm{pt}\}, and put

ωB=aB!k,ωE=aE!k,ωE*=aE*!k. \omega_B=a_B^!k,\qquad \omega_E=a_E^!k,\qquad \omega_{E^*}=a_{E^*}^!k.

The spaces EE and E*E^* have finite c-soft dimension as well. To see the required bound, if dd bounds compact-support cohomology on BB, the proper-support Leray sequence for a bundle projection has q≤nq\leq n by the fiber formula and p≤dp\leq d on the base. Thus compact-support cohomology on the total space vanishes above d+nd+n, uniformly in the abelian sheaf. The c-soft dimension criterion supplies the asserted finite bound. This argument uses the proper-support projection and requires no compact total space.

Conversely, a finite c-soft-dimension bound on EE implies one on BB. For the closed zero section i:B↪Ei:B\hookrightarrow E, its exact direct image satisfies RΓc(B;M)=RΓc(E;i*M)R\Gamma_c(B;M)=R\Gamma_c(E;i_*M) for every abelian sheaf MM. The total-space bound therefore bounds the left side, and the same dimension criterion applies. Thus stating the ambient absolute-duality hypothesis on the base does not restrict the class of vector bundles whose total spaces have the required finite dimension.

Define DEF=Rℋom(F,ωE)D_EF=R\mathcal Hom(F,\omega_E) and similarly on E*E^*. The composition comparisons identify

ωE≃τ!ωB,ωE*≃π!ωB.(FDN13) \omega_E\simeq\tau^!\omega_B, \qquad \omega_{E^*}\simeq\pi^!\omega_B. \qquad\text{(FDN13)}

Corollary. Under this additional absolute-duality hypothesis,

IE(DEF)→∼DE*(TEF)(F∈Dℝ>0b(E;k)).(FDN14) I_E(D_EF)\xrightarrow{\sim}D_{E^*}(T_EF) \qquad(F\in D^b_{\mathbb R_{>0}}(E;k)). \qquad\text{(FDN14)}

Proof. Take H=ωBH=\omega_B in FDN8 and use FDN13. The construction of aB!a_B^! supplies ωB∈D+\omega_B\in D^+, exactly the range required in that theorem. No biduality map is used. ▫\square

The absolute-dual formula requires the stated dimension hypothesis because it uses the right adjoint for the map to a point. SHV, Definition 4.7.1, makes the corresponding finite-cohomological-dimension hypothesis explicit. The ordinary-dual formula uses the inverse orientation complex on the base and therefore does not require that additional hypothesis.

If a:E*→E*a:E^*\to E^* is negation, the halfspace comparison in the Fourier kernel unit identifies

IEH≃a−1(TEH)⊗kπ−1O[n].(FDN15) I_EH\simeq a^{-1}(T_EH)\otimes_k\pi^{-1}O[n]. \qquad\text{(FDN15)}

Indeed, IEHI_EH is Rq!((p!H)C)Rq_!((p^!H)_C) with C={⟨x,ξ⟩≥0}C=\{\langle x,\xi\rangle\geq0\}; negation changes CC to NN, and p!p^! contributes the orientation of its fiber E*E^*, identified positively with OO. Projection formula moves that base-pulled invertible factor outside Rq!Rq_!. Therefore either duality formula can equivalently be written

DE*⋄(TEF)≃a−1TE(DE⋄F)⊗kπ−1O[n],⋄∈{′,}.(FDN16) D^{\diamond}_{E^*}(T_EF) \simeq a^{-1}T_E(D^{\diamond}_EF)\otimes_k\pi^{-1}O[n], \qquad \diamond\in\{\prime,\ \}. \qquad\text{(FDN16)}

The blank superscript means absolute duality with its stated extra hypothesis. The antipode and positive shift in FDN16 are essential. In particular the formula is not obtained by simply commuting D⋄D^{\diamond} and TT.

SH02-FDN-EXAMPLE. An infinite coefficient module

Work over a point, take k=ℚk=\mathbb Q, give E=ℝ2E=\mathbb R^2 its usual orientation, and let M=⨁j≥0kM=\bigoplus_{j\geq0}k. Let i:{0}↪Ei:\{0\}\hookrightarrow E, and put F=i*MF=i_*M. Its transform is ME*M_{E^*}: on the support x=0x=0, the pairing restriction is automatic and the output projection is the identity.

Write M*=Hom⁡k(M,k)=∏j≥0kM^*=\operatorname{Hom}_k(M,k)=\prod_{j\geq0}k. Closed-embedding duality and the codimension-two orientation give

DE′F=i*M*[−2]. D'_E F=i_*M^*[-2].

This calculation uses i!kE=k[−2]i^!k_E=k[-2], followed by module Hom over a field. The inverse transform of a zero-section object is the constant object tensored with the fiber orientation in shift [2][2]. Consequently

IE(DE′F)=ME**=DE*′(ME*). I_E(D'_E F)=M^*_{E^*} =D'_{E^*}(M_{E^*}).

The last equality also follows directly from FDN3 for the projection to a point, canceling its invertible orientation complex; it does not rely on commuting an infinite product with an arbitrary inverse image.

This example is outside a finite-dimensional biduality argument. The evaluation map M→M**M\to M^{**} is not surjective. To check this, view the finite-support sequences as a proper subspace of ∏j≥0k\prod_{j\geq0}k, and choose a linear functional on the quotient that is nonzero on the class of (1,1,…)(1,1,\ldots). Composing with the quotient gives a functional λ\lambda on M*M^* that vanishes on every coordinate vector but is not zero. A functional in the image of MM is a finite linear combination of coordinate evaluations. If it vanishes on every coordinate vector, all its coefficients vanish, so it is zero. Thus λ\lambda is not in that image. Fourier duality above remains valid.

SH02-FDN-PROBLEMS. Checking the comparison

Problem 1. Over an oriented real nn-space, compute both sides of FDN12 for F=kEF=k_E, keeping the cohomological shifts. Explain which transform goes in the same direction as TET_E.

Solution. Proper-support halfspace integration gives TEkE=k{0}[−n]T_Ek_E=k_{\{0\}}[-n]. Its ordinary dual is k{0}k_{\{0\}}, since DE*′k{0}=k{0}[−n]D'_{E^*}k_{\{0\}}=k_{\{0\}}[-n] and dualizing the input shift [−n][-n] adds [n][n]. On the other side, DE′kE=kED'_Ek_E=k_E and FDN15 gives IEkE=a−1k{0}[−n][n]=k{0}I_Ek_E=a^{-1}k_{\{0\}}[-n][n]=k_{\{0\}}. It is IEI_E, the inverse transform for the dual bundle, that goes from EE to E*E^*; SES_E goes the other way.

Problem 2. Why is the global boundedness of FF used even though a dual may be only bounded below? Explain why replacing Rℋom(F,Q)R\mathcal Hom(F,Q) with Rℋom(F,k)⊗kLQR\mathcal Hom(F,k)\otimes_k^L Q would not give the same proof.

Solution. A global upper bound bb for FF and a lower bound cc for QQ give a lower bound c−bc-b for internal Hom. Boundedness also makes the kernel input kN⊗p−1Fk_N\otimes p^{-1}F bounded and hence its proper direct image bounded. These are precisely the bounds used in FDN3–FDN10. The proposed tensor replacement requires dualizability and fails for general infinite modules. Over a field, for example, Hom⁡(⨁jk,−)\operatorname{Hom}(\bigoplus_jk,-) is a product functor and is not generally tensoring with ∏jk\prod_jk. The adjunction proof uses internal Hom throughout and therefore applies to these modules.

Problem 3. Set n=0n=0, let BB be arbitrary under the setup, and verify the map in FDN8 itself.

Solution. Then E=E*=X=BE=E^*=X=B, every projection is the identity, N=BN=B, and O=kBO=k_B. Thus TE=IE=idT_E=I_E=\mathrm{id}, while both test objects are HH. The composition comparison FDN9, both adjunction counits, and the tensor-unit comparison in FDN10 are identities. The tensor–Hom transpositions are the unit identities, so ΘF,H\Theta_{F,H} is the identity of Rℋom(F,H)R\mathcal Hom(F,H), not merely some invertible endomorphism of that object.

SH02-FDN-NORMALIZATION. A distinct equality of adjunction maps

There is another issue in the Fourier theory which the preceding proof does not resolve. It concerns the actual units and counits of two adjunctions between opposite halfspace presentations. To state it precisely, put

UEF=Rq*RΓC(p−1F),VEG=Rp!((q!G)C),C={⟨x,ξ⟩≥0}.(FDN17) U_EF=Rq_*R\Gamma_C(p^{-1}F),\qquad V_EG=Rp_!((q^!G)_C), \qquad C=\{\langle x,\xi\rangle\geq0\}. \qquad\text{(FDN17)}

Let c:TE→∼UEc:T_E\xrightarrow{\sim}U_E be the specific comparison formed from the support-restriction maps and the zero-section proper-support comparison in the Fourier kernel unit. There are two comparisons VE→SEV_E\to S_E to distinguish. The literal comparison dlitd_{\mathrm{lit}} has inverse the reversed-halfspace chain NDF2 in The geometric normalization of Fourier adjunctions. The adjunction-normalized comparison dadjd_{\mathrm{adj}} is the explicit mate NDF8. Write dd for a stated choice between them in the formulas below; the name alone does not identify the two maps.

The adjunction TE⊣SET_E\dashv S_E has unit η\eta and counit ϵ\epsilon. A second adjunction VE⊣UEV_E\dashv U_E uses a comparison between the orientation traces for the two vector-bundle fibers. For that second adjunction this course fixes the comparison induced locally by a negative-definite symmetric identification E→E*E\to E^*. Denote its unit and counit by η̃\widetilde\eta and ϵ̃\widetilde\epsilon.

With d=dadjd=d_{\mathrm{adj}}, the proved paired inverse equations, with every composite typed, are

ϵ̃F∘VE(cF)∘dTEF−1=ηF−1:SETEF⟶F,(FDN18) \widetilde\epsilon_F\circ V_E(c_F)\circ d^{-1}_{T_EF} =\eta_F^{-1}:S_ET_EF\longrightarrow F, \qquad\text{(FDN18)}

cVEG−1∘η̃Gfollowed byTE(dG)=ϵG−1:G⟶TESEG.(FDN19) c^{-1}_{V_EG}\circ\widetilde\eta_G \quad\text{followed by}\quad T_E(d_G) =\epsilon_G^{-1}:G\longrightarrow T_ES_EG. \qquad\text{(FDN19)}

Equivalently, the left side of FDN19 is TE(dG)∘cVEG−1∘η̃GT_E(d_G)\circ c^{-1}_{V_EG}\circ\widetilde\eta_G. The proof of equivalence in the Fourier kernel unit makes all four adjunction maps invertible.
