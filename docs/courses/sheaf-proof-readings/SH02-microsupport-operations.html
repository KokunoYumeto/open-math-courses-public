SH02-MO-UNIT — Transporting directional obstructions

Original AI programme expression: CC0 1.0 Universal. The proofs use the exact local tests, propagation results, six-operation identities and Fourier equivalence identified below. Those dependencies require their own verification; a conditional proof here does not close them. This lesson retains bounded complexes of arbitrary sheaves over a commutative ring kk of finite global dimension. Only the Morse inequalities impose field coefficients.

All manifolds are finite dimensional, Hausdorff and countable at infinity. A map is C∞C^\infty unless stated otherwise. We use submersion for a map with surjective differential; mere differentiability is not sufficient for any submersion assertion. Test functions and exhaustion functions may be C1C^1. Tensor products and internal Hom are derived. Put Aa={(x,−ξ):(x,ξ)∈A}A^a=\{(x,-\xi):(x,\xi)\in A\} for a subset of a cotangent bundle.

The organizing question is whether a sheaf operation can create a failed local support test. Products separate the variables of such tests. Proper images collect tests along compact fibres. Noncharacteristic inverse images preserve the direction of a test. An exhaustion handles some nonproper maps by excluding the directions in which information could escape. Fourier transformation then exchanges a vector and a covector.

SH02-MO-CONE-PROPER — No cancellation and closed images

If A,BA,B are closed conic sets and A∩BaA\cap B^a is contained in the zero section, addition A×XB→T*XA\times_XB\to T^*X is proper. In particular A+BA+B is closed. Here properness is local over the base and hence global over compact subsets of the target.

Proof. Choose a relatively compact coordinate neighborhood and a Euclidean norm. If there were no uniform c>0c>0 after shrinking the neighborhood, there would be xn→xx_n\to x, ξn∈Axn\xi_n\in A_{x_n}, ηn∈Bxn\eta_n\in B_{x_n} with

|ξn|+|ηn|=1,|ξn+ηn|⟶0. |\xi_n|+|\eta_n|=1,\qquad |\xi_n+\eta_n|\longrightarrow0.

Compactness of the unit sphere and closedness give a limit ξ=−η≠0\xi=-\eta\neq0, contrary to the hypothesis. Thus

|ξ+η|≥c(|ξ|+|η|).(MO1) |\xi+\eta|\geq c(|\xi|+|\eta|). \qquad\text{(MO1)}

The inverse image of a compact set under addition is closed, has base in a compact set and has bounded fibre coordinates by MO1; it is compact. Finite coordinate covers handle an arbitrary compact target set. A proper map between locally compact Hausdorff spaces is closed, proving the assertion. The same normalization argument proves a second useful fact: if fdf_d has no nonzero kernel vector in fπ−1Af_\pi^{-1}A, then its restriction to this closed conic set is proper. ▫\square

SH02-MO-EXTERNAL-TENSOR — Product tests

SH02-MO-PROPER-PUSH — Collecting tests along a fibre

Let f:Y→Xf:Y\to X be any manifold map and assume that ff is proper on supp⁡(G)\operatorname{supp}(G). Then

SS⁡(Rf*G)⊂fπfd−1SS⁡(G).(MO8) \operatorname{SS}(Rf_*G)\subset f_\pi f_d^{-1}\operatorname{SS}(G). \qquad\text{(MO8)}

For a closed embedding this is an equality.

Proof. The set on the right is closed: fπf_\pi is proper on the closed subset in question, because its base lies in supp⁡(G)\operatorname{supp}(G) and the covector in its source is the target covector. Take a covector outside it and a sufficiently small cotangent neighborhood disjoint from it. Every test function hh whose differential lies there satisfies d(hf)y∉SS⁡(G)d(hf)_y\notin\operatorname{SS}(G) at every relevant point of the fibre. Localization commutes with ordinary direct image, and proper base change on the support gives

(RΓ{h≥h(x)}Rf*G)x≃RΓ(f−1(x);RΓ{hf≥h(x)}G)=0.(MO9) \bigl(R\Gamma_{\{h\geq h(x)\}}Rf_*G\bigr)_x \simeq R\Gamma\bigl(f^{-1}(x); R\Gamma_{\{hf\geq h(x)\}}G\bigr)=0. \qquad\text{(MO9)}

The last complex restricts to zero on the fibre because each of its stalks is a vanishing local test. This proves MO8.

  1. every Hj(G)H^j(G) restricts to a locally constant sheaf on each fibre;

SH02-MO-LEGENDRE — Exchanging a vector with a covector

SH02-MO-SYMPLECTIC — Symplectic, with two different dilations

Taking exterior derivatives in MO37 gives ΦE*dαE*=dαE\Phi_E^*d\alpha_{E^*}=d\alpha_E, so ΦE\Phi_E is symplectic. On every component where EE has positive rank, ΦE\Phi_E does not preserve the tautological one-form or ordinary cotangent dilation: for instance, scaling a nonzero ξ\xi changes the base point in E*E^* in MO38. In rank zero, E=ZE=Z, θE=0\theta_E=0, and ΦE\Phi_E is the identity on T*ZT^*Z, preserving both.

The two natural positive actions on T*ET^*E are

rλ(z,x;ζ,ξ)=(z,x;λζ,λξ),sμ(z,x;ζ,ξ)=(z,μx;ζ,μ−1ξ).(MO40) r_\lambda(z,x;\zeta,\xi)=(z,x;\lambda\zeta,\lambda\xi), \qquad s_\mu(z,x;\zeta,\xi)=(z,\mu x;\zeta,\mu^{-1}\xi). \qquad\text{(MO40)}

ΦErλ=rλsλΦE,ΦEsμ=sμ−1ΦE, \Phi_E r_\lambda=r_\lambda s_\lambda\Phi_E, \qquad \Phi_E s_\mu=s_{\mu^{-1}}\Phi_E,

where the actions on the right are on T*E*T^*E^*. Thus ΦE\Phi_E carries biconic sets to biconic sets.

Hence its microsupport is the cotangent lift aE#SS⁡(F)a_E^\#\operatorname{SS}(F). MO39 says ΦE*ΦE=aE#\Phi_{E^*}\Phi_E=a_E^\#. Applying the inverse of ΦE*\Phi_{E^*} to the second inclusion proves the reverse inclusion in MO45. The orientation and shift affect the sheaf equivalence but do not affect microsupport. ▫\square

SH02-MO-STRICT-PUSH — Two reasons a proper image estimate can be strict

All the examples asserting nonempty microsupport assume k≠0k\neq0.

First consider the proper homeomorphism

f:ℝ2⟶ℝ2,f(s,t)=(s,t3+s2t). f:\mathbb R^2\longrightarrow\mathbb R^2, \qquad f(s,t)=(s,t^3+s^2t).

For each fixed ss, the second coordinate is strictly increasing from −∞-\infty to +∞+\infty. Also |t3+s2t|≥|t|3|t^3+s^2t|\geq|t|^3, so inverse images of compact sets are bounded and closed. Thus the continuous bijection is proper and is a homeomorphism. Its constant sheaf image is constant, so its microsupport is the zero section. At (0,0)(0,0), however, the differential has rank one; every target covector in the second-coordinate direction pulls back to zero. The geometric right side of MO8 contains that entire line of covectors at the origin. The strictness comes from degeneracy of the differential, even though the underlying map loses no topological information.

There is also cancellation in cohomology. Take k=ℚk=\mathbb Q and let LL be the rank-one local system on the circle with monodromy −1-1. A cell decomposition with one vertex and one edge computes its cohomology by [ℚ→−2ℚ][\mathbb Q\xrightarrow{-2}\mathbb Q], which is acyclic. For the proper map to a point, Rf*L=0Rf_*L=0, so its microsupport is empty. The geometric image of the zero-section microsupport of LL is the nonempty zero cotangent fibre of the point. This is strictness without a critical differential in the manifold map to a point.

SH02-MO-HOLOMORPHIC-IMPORT — A distinct finite complex-analytic phenomenon

The stronger equality for a finite holomorphic map and a complex-constructible sheaf is an external import from the complex-constructible microlocal theory assigned to the later sheaf course. Its exact needed statement is

SH02-MO-STRICT-PULL — A disappearing sector with a nonempty estimate

On ℝ2\mathbb R^2 with coordinates (u,v)(u,v), let

F=k{u>0,v≥0},M={u+v=0}. F=k_{\{u>0,\ v\geq0\}},\qquad M=\{u+v=0\}.

At the origin the exact microsupport cone is

SS⁡(F)0={adu+bdv:a≤0,b≥0}.(MO46a) \operatorname{SS}(F)_0 =\{a\,du+b\,dv:a\leq0,\ b\geq0\}. \qquad\text{(MO46a)}

The product estimate and the open/closed half-line calculations give the upper inclusion. For the reverse inclusion first take a<0a<0, b>0b>0 and the linear test h=au+bvh=au+bv. The stalk F0F_0 is zero. In {h<0}\{h<0\} near the origin, the inequality v≥0v\geq0 already forces u>0u>0, so the restricted sheaf is the constant sheaf on a nonempty convex closed half-region, extended from that closed region. Its derived sections are kk in degree zero, by convex acyclicity. The local support triangle then gives the nonzero test complex k[−1]k[-1]. Thus every interior covector of the asserted cone occurs, and closedness supplies its boundary.

The conormal to MM consists of multiples of du+dvdu+dv. It meets MO46a only at zero. Away from the origin, MM does not meet the closed support, so MM is noncharacteristic everywhere. Nevertheless F|M=0F|_M=0, since u+v=0u+v=0 cannot hold with u>0u>0 and v≥0v\geq0. Its exceptional restriction is also zero by MO20. The estimated image at the origin is nonempty: on the tangent vector (1,−1)(1,-1) it takes every value a−b≤0a-b\leq0. Thus both inverse-image estimates can be strict.

If t=(u+v)/2t=(u+v)/2 and y=(u−v)/2y=(u-v)/2, the same region is t>0t>0, −t<y≤t-t<y\leq t. Since

adu+bdv=(a+b)dt+(a−b)dy, a\,du+b\,dv=(a+b)\,dt+(a-b)\,dy,

its cone in covector coordinates (τ,η)(\tau,\eta) is

η≤−|τ|.(MO46b) \eta\leq-|\tau|. \qquad\text{(MO46b)}

This cone meets the conormal to t=0t=0 only at zero. Swapping η\eta and τ\tau would incorrectly make that submanifold characteristic. The calculation derives the sign from the two boundary conditions rather than from a picture of the sector.

SH02-MO-CUTOFF-STRICT-IMAGE — Why the two geometric cutoff images differ

Let f:ℝ→ptf:\mathbb R\to\mathrm{pt}, φ(s)=s\varphi(s)=s, G=k(0,1]G=k_{(0,1]}, and t0=−1t_0=-1. All support sublevels are compact. Both endpoint microsupport cones are nonpositive multiples of dsds, and interior covectors are zero. Therefore MO25+ holds everywhere. The triangle

k(0,1]⟶k[0,1]⟶k{0}→+1 k_{(0,1]}\longrightarrow k_{[0,1]}\longrightarrow k_{\{0\}}\xrightarrow{+1}

shows RΓ(ℝ;G)=0R\Gamma(\mathbb R;G)=0: the two right section complexes are kk and their restriction is the identity. The localized geometric image in MO27 is empty, because the support has no point with s≤−1s\leq-1. The full geometric image is the zero cotangent fibre of the point, which is nonempty. Thus the inclusion between these two images cannot be replaced by equality.

For the exceptional case use G=k[0,1)G=k_{[0,1)}. Both endpoint cones are nonnegative multiples of dsds, so MO25− holds everywhere. Compactly supported cohomology is zero by the corresponding triangle with the removed endpoint 11. Again the localized image is empty and the full image is nonempty. These computations preserve the valid cutoff isomorphisms and microsupport bounds while separating them from a false stronger geometric equality.

SH02-MO-CONIC-NEIGHBORHOOD-TEST — A family of support tests must detect nearby directions

Let EE be a real vector space and F∈Db(kE)F\in D^b(k_E) be conic. For a nonempty convex conic open U⊂E*U\subset E^* write

U∘={x∈E:⟨x,ξ⟩≥0 for every ξ∈U}. U^\circ=\{x\in E:\langle x,\xi\rangle\geq0\text{ for every }\xi\in U\}.

Prove the following neighborhood criterion and explain why a cofinal family consisting only of neighborhoods of the single direction ξ0\xi_0 is insufficient:

(0,ξ0)∉SS⁡(F)⟺there is a conic open neighborhood W of ξ0 such thatRΓU∘(E;F)=0 for every nonempty convex conic open U⊂W.(MO48) (0,\xi_0)\notin\operatorname{SS}(F) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \begin{gathered} \text{there is a conic open neighborhood }W\text{ of }\xi_0\text{ such that}\\ R\Gamma_{U^\circ}(E;F)=0 \text{ for every nonempty convex conic open }U\subset W. \end{gathered} \qquad\text{(MO48)}

The cones UU in the right side may be centered on any direction in WW; they need not contain ξ0\xi_0. Nonemptiness matters for the displayed polar convention: the vacuous polar of an empty set would be all of EE and would not satisfy the open-cone section formula below.

Solution. Put H=TEFH=T_EF. The Fourier open-cone section formula gives

RΓ(U;H)≃RΓU∘(E;F).(MO49) R\Gamma(U;H)\simeq R\Gamma_{U^\circ}(E;F). \qquad\text{(MO49)}

MO45 identifies the condition on the left of MO48 with (ξ0,0)∉SS⁡(H)(\xi_0,0)\notin\operatorname{SS}(H). Zero covectors of microsupport record the closed support, so this means that HH vanishes on an ordinary neighborhood of ξ0\xi_0. Since HH is conic, saturating that neighborhood by positive scalars yields a conic open WW on which it still vanishes. MO49 proves the forward implication.

Conversely assume the right side. At any nonzero ξ∈W\xi\in W, small angular convex cones form a basis of neighborhoods of its radial orbit. Their sections compute the stalk: intersect a cone with a small annulus about ξ\xi, apply conic restriction along the contractible radial parameter intervals, and then shrink the angular cap and the annulus. The groups in MO49 are zero for every such cone, so every cohomology stalk of HH at ξ\xi is zero. If 0∉W0\notin W, this proves H|W=0H|_W=0. If 0∈W0\in W, conic openness forces W=E*W=E^*. We have already shown that HH is supported at zero. Taking U=E*U=E^* in MO49 gives RΓ(E*;H)=0R\Gamma(E^*;H)=0, which for a complex supported at a point is its stalk complex. Thus the zero stalk also vanishes. In either case ξ0\xi_0 lies outside the closed support, proving MO48, including ξ0=0\xi_0=0.

Here is a counterexample to the weaker, single-direction formulation. Take E=ℝ2E=\mathbb R^2, F=kℝ≥0×{0}F=k_{\mathbb R_{\geq0}\times\{0\}}, k≠0k\neq0, and ξ0=(0,1)\xi_0=(0,1) in dual coordinates (u,v)(u,v). The closed-convex vertex calculation gives

SS⁡(F)0={(u,v):u≥0}, \operatorname{SS}(F)_0=\{(u,v):u\geq0\},

so (0,ξ0)(0,\xi_0) belongs to microsupport. Nevertheless every convex conic open neighborhood UU of ξ0\xi_0 contains a covector with u<0u<0. Hence U∘U^\circ meets the positive horizontal ray only at its endpoint. It follows that

RΓU∘(E;F)=RΓ{0}(E;F)=0. R\Gamma_{U^\circ}(E;F)=R\Gamma_{\{0\}}(E;F)=0.

For the last equality, compute the endpoint costalk of the closed ray: the local support triangle compares the constant section on a short closed ray with the same section on the ray with its endpoint deleted. Both section complexes are kk and the map is the identity, so the supported complex is zero. Equivalently TEF=k{u>0}T_EF=k_{\{u>0\}}, whose sections on every convex cone crossing u=0u=0 vanish, although it is nonzero arbitrarily close to ξ0\xi_0. Thus even vanishing on every convex conic neighborhood of that one direction does not replace the uniformly varying family required in MO48. This preserves the useful support-testing phenomenon while correcting the weaker formulation.

SH02-MO-PROBLEM-ESCAPE — Information arriving from infinity

Let C={(x,y):xy=1,x>0}⊂ℝ2C=\{(x,y):xy=1,\ x>0\}\subset\mathbb R^2, let F=kCF=k_C, and let q(x,y)=xq(x,y)=x. Compute Rq*FRq_*F and explain why MO8 does not apply.

Solution. The set CC is closed in ℝ2\mathbb R^2 and q|Cq|_C identifies it with (0,∞)(0,\infty). Therefore Rq*F=Rj*k(0,∞)≃k[0,∞)Rq_*F=Rj_*k_{(0,\infty)}\simeq k_{[0,\infty)}; the stalk at 00 is computed by ordinary cohomology of a contractible positive interval, with no higher cohomology. Its microsupport at 00 consists of nonnegative multiples of dxdx. A tangent vector to CC at (x,1/x)(x,1/x) is (1,−1/x2)(1,-1/x^2). A covector (ξ,0)(\xi,0) conormal to CC must consequently have ξ=0\xi=0. Thus qπqd−1SS⁡(F)q_\pi q_d^{-1}\operatorname{SS}(F) contains only zero covectors over x>0x>0, and no point over 00. Properness on the support fails: the inverse image in CC of a compact interval containing 00 has y→∞y\to\infty. This explicitly exhibits a boundary covector created by escape from every compact set.

SH02-MO-PROBLEM-NO-CANCEL — What failure of transversality looks like

On the line take A={(x,ξ):x>0,ξ≥0}∪T0*ℝA=\{(x,\xi):x>0,\ \xi\geq0\}\cup T_0^*\mathbb R and B=AaB=A^a. Explain why addition on A×ℝBA\times_\mathbb RB is not proper and why ordinary cone sums need a hypothesis even when both summands are closed.

Solution. The term T0*ℝT_0^*\mathbb R retains all finite limits at the base boundary 00, so the displayed sets are closed in T*ℝT^*\mathbb R. At any fixed x>0x>0, the sequence ((x,n),(x,−n))((x,n),(x,-n)) has constant sum (x,0)(x,0) but no convergent subsequence. Hence addition is not proper. The failure is precisely a nonzero covector in A∩Ba=AA\cap B^a=A. This example disproves automatic properness; it does not itself show that every such sum is nonclosed. For an explicit nonclosed sum, take over base x≥0x\geq0 the rays Ax=ℝ≥0(1,0)A_x=\mathbb R_{\geq0}(1,0) and Bx=ℝ≥0(−1,x)B_x=\mathbb R_{\geq0}(-1,x) in a trivial two-dimensional cotangent fibre, and empty fibres for x<0x<0. Both total sets are closed. For x>0x>0, (0,1)=x−1(1,0)+x−1(−1,x)(0,1)=x^{-1}(1,0)+x^{-1}(-1,x) is in the sum, while at x=0x=0 the sum is the horizontal line and omits (0,1)(0,1). Their sum is therefore not closed. This local trivial-bundle example embeds in T*ℝ2T^*\mathbb R^2 by letting xx be the first base coordinate and making the construction independent of the second.

SH02-MO-PROBLEM-MORSE — The two jumps of a circle

Let kk be a field, F=kS1F=k_{S^1} and let φ\varphi be the vertical coordinate on the unit circle. Find the jump complexes and verify MO34.

Solution. Microsupport of the constant sheaf is the zero section, so the graph of dφd\varphi meets it at the bottom and top points. At the minimum the set {φ≥φ(x)}\{\varphi\geq\varphi(x)\} contains a neighborhood and the jump is kk. At the maximum that set is locally the single point; its costalk on the one-dimensional manifold is the orientation complex k[−1]k[-1]. Thus m0=m1=1m_0=m_1=1 and all other mjm_j vanish. Ordinary cohomology of the circle has the same two Betti numbers, so every strong inequality is an equality and both Euler sums are zero. The compactness and finite-dimensionality hypotheses are all satisfied.

SH02-MO-PROBLEM-LINEAR — Fourier transformation of a linear support

Let LL be a linear subspace of a finite-dimensional real vector space EE, and use an orientation of LL to trivialize its orientation line. Verify MO45 for kLk_L.

Solution. Closed-image equality applied to L↪EL\hookrightarrow E gives SS⁡(kL)=L×L⟂\operatorname{SS}(k_L)=L\times L^\perp. The Fourier kernel integrates a constant sheaf on a closed halfspace of LL when the dual vector does not annihilate LL, giving zero compact cohomology, and on all of LL otherwise. Proper-support base change gives

TEkL≃kL⟂[−dim⁡L]. T_Ek_L\simeq k_{L^\perp}[-\dim L].

Without the orientation choice its coefficient is the orientation line of LL. The microsupport of this sheaf is L⟂×LL^\perp\times L, because the annihilator of L⟂L^\perp is LL and a shift and an invertible line do not change support tests. MO38 carries L×L⟂L\times L^\perp to L⟂×(−L)=L⟂×LL^\perp\times(-L)=L^\perp\times L, as required. Both extreme cases L=0L=0 and L=EL=E are included.
