Holomorphic operations and complex Fourier symmetries

Holomorphic maps preserve the complex symmetries of the cotangent estimates for sheaf operations. This proves weak complex constructibility even when coefficient modules are infinite. Perfect stalks require the separate real finiteness arguments already developed. Fourier–Sato has one additional issue: its cotangent map moves a covector into a new base vector, so complex cotangent dilation must be transported using both source actions. Microlocal Hom instead uses an analytic normal cone with its own complex normal-fibre scaling.

Let kk be a commutative ring of finite global dimension gg. All complex manifolds are Hausdorff, countable at infinity, and of uniformly bounded finite dimension. Maps are holomorphic and vector bundles have fixed finite complex rank. Every sheaf complex is globally bounded. Weak complex constructibility imposes the geometric conditions of Complex microlocal stratifications and constructibility; complex constructibility additionally imposes perfect stalks. We assume no Noetherianity, field, noncharacteristic map, or arbitrary support properness.

Use the real covector identification ρ(ξ)(v)=Re⁡ξ(v)\rho(\xi)(v)=\operatorname{Re}\xi(v), with α=∑ξjdzj\alpha=\sum\xi_jdz_j, Ω=dα\Omega=d\alpha, and real symplectic form Re⁡Ω\operatorname{Re}\Omega. For a holomorphic differential, real transpose pullback under ρ\rho agrees with complex-linear transpose pullback. Indeed both real covectors evaluate to Re⁡ξ(df(v))\operatorname{Re}\xi(df(v)). We can therefore use the existing real estimates in these complex cotangent coordinates without conjugating the transpose differential.

Kashiwara and Schapira’s Microlocal Study of Sheaves gives the real operation results in Propositions 8.3.3–8.3.6, printed pp. 149–150, and the complex microsupport criterion in Theorem 8.5.2, pp. 151–152. Its analytic closure input, Proposition 8.5.3, continues through p. 154. These are the classical comparisons for the real operation framework and complex conicity. The independent argument below supplies the holomorphic invariance and the uniform boundedness needed for their combination. The exact bounded sheaf estimates, full Fourier microsupport equality, conic Euler criterion and ordered microlocal Hom normal-cone estimate are current prerequisites from the microlocal foundations. The geometric and boundedness contracts

For a bounded object HH on a complex manifold, we will use

H weakly complex constructible⟺SS⁡(H)⊂A for a closed complex-conic subanalytic real-isotropic A.(1) H\text{ weakly complex constructible} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \operatorname{SS}(H)\subset A \text{ for a closed complex-conic subanalytic real-isotropic }A. \qquad\text{(1)}

Such an HH has actual microsupport closed complex analytic, complex-conic and Lagrangian. “Complex-conic” here refers to cotangent-fibre dilation, not to a vector-bundle dilation on the base of a sheaf.

The real operation theorem in Weak constructibility under sheaf operations supplies boundedness and weak real constructibility for the operations considered below. It also supplies the finite-dimensional exceptional/direct-image and specialization/Fourier amplitude contracts. For arbitrary bounded Hom inputs on a real manifold MM, the bounded-Hom proof gives the explicit sufficient range

A∈D[a,b],B∈D[c,d]⟹Rℋom(A,B)∈D[c−b,d−a+3dim⁡ℝM+g+1].(2) A\in D^{[a,b]},\ B\in D^{[c,d]} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad R\mathcal Hom(A,B)\in D^{[c-b,\ d-a+3\dim_{\mathbb R}M+g+1]}. \qquad\text{(2)}

For a complex nn-manifold this uses dim⁡ℝM=2n\dim_{\mathbb R}M=2n. We use this global bound, not a separate bound chosen independently at every stalk. The perfect real operation theorems in Perfect operations and finite microlocal coefficients and Perfect coefficients on compact fibres will supply the perfect-stalk condition.

Full characteristic inverse image for a holomorphic map

Let f:Y→Xf:Y\to X be holomorphic and Λ⊂T*X\Lambda\subset T^*X closed complex analytic, complex-conic and isotropic. The full characteristic inverse has the local sequence criterion

yj→y,xj→f(y),(xj,ξj)∈Λ,(dfyj)tξj→η,|xj−f(yj)||ξj|→0.(3) \begin{gathered} y_j\to y,\quad x_j\to f(y),\quad (x_j,\xi_j)\in\Lambda,\\ (df_{y_j})^t\xi_j\to\eta,\qquad |x_j-f(y_j)|\,|\xi_j|\to0. \end{gathered} \qquad\text{(3)}

The input covectors may be unbounded. Multiplying every ξj\xi_j by a fixed λ∈ℂ*\lambda\in\mathbb C^* preserves membership in Λ\Lambda, multiplies the final product by |λ||\lambda|, and changes the output to λη\lambda\eta, because the transpose differential is complex-linear. Thus f♯Λf^\sharp\Lambda is complex-conic.

It is also closed analytic. For completeness, the exact graph-conormal model from Limiting cotangent sums and characteristic inverse images is

P=T*(Y×X),L=TΓf*(Y×X),S=TY*Y×Λ,f♯Λ=e−1(KCL(S)).(4) P=T^*(Y\times X),\quad L=T^*_{\Gamma_f}(Y\times X),\quad S=T_Y^*Y\times\Lambda, \qquad f^\sharp\Lambda=e^{-1}\bigl(KC_L(S)\bigr). \qquad\text{(4)}

The first factor is the zero section. The graph is a smooth closed complex submanifold even at critical points of ff. The normal identification KK, whose inverse is induced by −H-H, and the zero-conormal-section embedding ee are holomorphic. The analytic normal-cone argument makes CL(S)C_L(S) analytic in its normal bundle. Formula (4) is consequently a closed analytic inverse image. The full real graph-slice theorem proves its real isotropy. This proves every premise of (1); no rank or properness condition on ff was imposed.

For weakly complex constructible FF, use the actual Λ=SS⁡(F)\Lambda=\operatorname{SS}(F) and the bounded characteristic estimates

SS⁡(f−1F)⊂f♯Λ,SS⁡(f!F)⊂f♯Λ.(5) \operatorname{SS}(f^{-1}F)\subset f^\sharp\Lambda, \qquad \operatorname{SS}(f^!F)\subset f^\sharp\Lambda. \qquad\text{(5)}

Boundedness and (1) prove that both inverse images are weakly complex constructible. The ordinary same-base transpose image alone would omit critical escaping directions in (3).

Proper direct image uses the actual support

Let GG be weakly complex constructible on YY, and assume ff is proper on its closed support. In the holomorphic cotangent correspondence set

Cf=Y×XT*X,fd(y,ξ)=(y,(dfy)tξ),fπ(y,ξ)=(f(y),ξ).(6) C_f=Y\times_XT^*X, \quad f_d(y,\xi)=(y,(df_y)^t\xi), \quad f_\pi(y,\xi)=(f(y),\xi). \qquad\text{(6)}

For Λ=SS⁡(G)\Lambda=\operatorname{SS}(G), put A=fd−1ΛA=f_d^{-1}\Lambda. It is closed complex analytic. Over a compact cotangent set K⊂T*XK\subset T^*X, every point of AA has yy in the compact set supp⁡(G)∩f−1(πK)\operatorname{supp}(G)\cap f^{-1}(\pi K); even a zero transpose covector has this support condition. The preimage of KK is a closed subset of the corresponding compact product with KK. Thus fπ|Af_\pi|_A is proper. This is the full cotangent compactness check, including points at which dfdf has a kernel.

Remmert’s proper mapping theorem makes

B=fπfd−1Λ(7) B=f_\pi f_d^{-1}\Lambda \qquad\text{(7)}

closed complex analytic. Both maps commute with complex cotangent dilation. The real proper cotangent-transport theorem gives real isotropy. The proper-support sheaf estimate and (1) now give

SS⁡(Rf*G)⊂B,Rf*G weakly complex constructible.(8) \operatorname{SS}(Rf_*G)\subset B, \qquad Rf_*G\text{ weakly complex constructible}. \qquad\text{(8)}

Under the same support properness Rf!G≃Rf*GRf_!G\simeq Rf_*G, so the same conclusion holds for that functor. A larger cotangent bound with extra base points could violate the properness check and must not be substituted for the actual microsupport in this proof. Holomorphicity by itself does not permit unrestricted nonproper direct image.

Tensor and Hom use full complex limiting sums

For weakly complex constructible F,GF,G, the bounded tensor and internal-Hom estimates are

SS⁡(F⊗LG)⊂SS⁡(F)+̂SS⁡(G),SS⁡(Rℋom(G,F))⊂SS⁡(F)+̂SS⁡(G)a,a(x,ξ)=(x,−ξ).(9) \begin{aligned} \operatorname{SS}(F\otimes^LG)&\subset \operatorname{SS}(F)\widehat+\operatorname{SS}(G),\\ \operatorname{SS}(R\mathcal Hom(G,F))&\subset \operatorname{SS}(F)\widehat+\operatorname{SS}(G)^a, \qquad a(x,\xi)=(x,-\xi). \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(9)}

The analytic limiting-sum proof from the complex stratification lesson applies to any closed analytic isotropic inputs: its product is analytic and its diagonal normal-cone slice is holomorphic. It is not restricted to ordinary smooth conormals. Multiplying both input covectors by the same complex scalar proves target complex conicity in the full sequence criterion. The antipode is holomorphic and preserves isotropy, changing α\alpha by a minus sign. Hence both right sides of (9) are closed analytic, complex-conic and real-isotropic. With the actual boundedness contracts, (1) proves weak complex constructibility of tensor and internal Hom.

No transversality is needed. The first Hom input carries the antipode. For infinite weak coefficients we used the actual internal-Hom estimate; we have not substituted tensor with a dual, or replaced a Hom stalk by Hom of two ordinary stalks.

Perfect coefficients after the weak geometric proof

If all relevant inputs are complex constructible, every output just considered also has perfect stalks. Here are the separate finiteness mechanisms.

Ordinary inverse image has stalk (f−1F)y=Ff(y)(f^{-1}F)_y=F_{f(y)}. Proper-on-support direct image has perfect stalks by the finite compact-fibre theorem applied in the underlying real analytic manifolds. Fibres may be singular; that theorem uses the compact supported constructible coefficient complex and finite subanalytic descent, not smoothness of the fibre.

Tensor stalks are tensors of perfect kk-complexes, with a bounded finite-projective total tensor representative. For exceptional inverse and Hom use the actual constructible Verdier evaluation and normalized identities

f!F≃DY(f−1DXF),Rℋom(G,F)≃DX(DXF⊗LG).(10) f^!F\simeq D_Y(f^{-1}D_XF),\qquad R\mathcal Hom(G,F)\simeq D_X(D_XF\otimes^LG). \qquad\text{(10)}

The real constructible duality theorem preserves perfection. These comparisons carry their evaluation maps and the usual Koszul symmetry. They are invoked for perfect constructible inputs; the weak proof above does not need biduality of an infinite coefficient module. A complex nn-manifold has its canonical real orientation, so ωX≃kX[2n]\omega_X\simeq k_X[2n]; a general holomorphic map still does not have f!f^! equal to a universally shifted f−1f^{-1}.

Together with the already proved complex geometry, these finiteness facts give complex constructibility of ordinary and exceptional inverse images, proper-on-support direct image, tensor and internal Hom.

The two complex Fourier actions

Let E→ZE\to Z be a holomorphic vector bundle of complex rank rr, with complex dual E*E^*. The real dual is identified with this complex dual by the real part of the pairing. Let FF be bounded, weakly complex constructible on EE, and conic as a sheaf under positive real bundle dilation. This last hypothesis is a sheaf condition on its cohomology along positive radial orbits, including the fixed zero-section orbits. No complex equivariant structure on the sheaf is assumed.

Its actual microsupport Λ\Lambda is closed analytic and complex-conic. In a holomorphic frame, write (z,v;ζ,η)(z,v;\zeta,\eta) for a cotangent point and put

θ(z,v;ζ,η)=⟨v,η⟩∈ℂ.(11) \theta(z,v;\zeta,\eta)=\langle v,\eta\rangle\in\mathbb C. \qquad\text{(11)}

The real conic Euler theorem says Re⁡θ=0\operatorname{Re}\theta=0 on Λ\Lambda, and that Λ\Lambda is invariant under positive bundle lifts as well as positive cotangent dilation. Complex cotangent invariance additionally puts ii times every covector in Λ\Lambda. Testing its Euler equation gives Re⁡(iθ)=−Im⁡θ=0\operatorname{Re}(i\theta)=-\operatorname{Im}\theta=0. Hence

θ|Λ=0as a complex-valued function.(12) \theta|_\Lambda=0\quad\text{as a complex-valued function}. \qquad\text{(12)}

The two actions, now with complex parameters, are

rλ(z,v;ζ,η)=(z,v;λζ,λη),sμ(z,v;ζ,η)=(z,μv;ζ,μ−1η),λ,μ∈ℂ*.(13) \begin{aligned} r_\lambda(z,v;\zeta,\eta)&=(z,v;\lambda\zeta,\lambda\eta),\\ s_\mu(z,v;\zeta,\eta)&=(z,\mu v;\zeta,\mu^{-1}\eta), \qquad \lambda,\mu\in\mathbb C^*. \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(13)}

The first preserves Λ\Lambda by complex constructibility. The second preserves it for positive real μ\mu by the conic sheaf criterion. It preserves it for every complex μ\mu as well: for a fixed point of Λ\Lambda, the holomorphic orbit map μ↦sμp\mu\mapsto s_\mu p has analytic inverse image of Λ\Lambda in ℂ*\mathbb C^*. That inverse image contains the positive real axis. The identity theorem on the connected complex curve makes it the whole curve. Applying the same reasoning to μ−1\mu^{-1} gives set invariance. This extends the microsupport symmetry; it does not assert trivial monodromy of the sheaf along a complex orbit.

Holomorphic Fourier exchange and its cotangent conicity

The Fourier cotangent map is

ΦE(z,v;ζ,η)=(z,η;ζ,−v),ΦE*αE*=αE−dθ.(14) \Phi_E(z,v;\zeta,\eta)=(z,\eta;\zeta,-v), \qquad \Phi_E^*\alpha_{E^*}=\alpha_E-d\theta. \qquad\text{(14)}

The formula is holomorphic. Its intrinsic base map is restriction to the vertical cotangent followed by projection to E*E^*. The canonical-form identity determines the remaining covector coordinates uniquely in every holomorphic frame, so the formulas agree when the frame changes; no horizontal splitting was chosen. Taking real parts identifies this map with the real Fourier cotangent map. Taking exterior derivatives makes it symplectic.

Direct substitution gives the identities for complex parameters

ΦErλ=rλsλΦE,ΦEsμ=sμ−1ΦE,rλΦE=ΦErλsλ.(15) \Phi_Er_\lambda=r_\lambda s_\lambda\Phi_E, \qquad \Phi_Es_\mu=s_{\mu^{-1}}\Phi_E, \qquad r_\lambda\Phi_E=\Phi_Er_\lambda s_\lambda. \qquad\text{(15)}

Therefore ΦEΛ\Phi_E\Lambda is complex-conic in the target cotangent fibre, using both source actions. The map does not commute with cotangent dilation alone: that action changes the target base coordinate η\eta.

The image is closed complex analytic because ΦE\Phi_E is a biholomorphism. Equation (12) makes dθd\theta vanish on regular tangent vectors of Λ\Lambda; (14) and source isotropy thus give target isotropy. In rank zero, θ=0\theta=0 and ΦE\Phi_E is the identity, so the same assertions have no exception.

We use the real Fourier–Sato convention

F∧=Rq!(p−1F⊗k{Re⁡⟨v,η⟩≤0}),SS⁡(F∧)=ΦESS⁡(F).(16) F^\wedge=Rq_!\bigl(p^{-1}F\otimes k_{\{\operatorname{Re}\langle v,\eta\rangle\le0\}}\bigr), \qquad \operatorname{SS}(F^\wedge)=\Phi_E\operatorname{SS}(F). \qquad\text{(16)}

Here p,qp,q are the two projections of E×ZE*E\times_ZE^*. The full bounded conic Fourier theorem includes both zero loci and gives the equality. The analytic image and (15), or (1), prove weak complex constructibility of F∧F^\wedge. The real Fourier perfection theorem gives perfect stalks when FF is complex constructible, proving the perfect assertion as well. Its proof uses the actual zero-section contraction maps on the negative cutoff or positive supported kernel; it does not assume qq is proper on the generally unbounded support in (16).

The fibre has real rank 2r2r. Thus, for example, the constant sheaf on an entire complex fibre transforms to the origin sheaf shifted by −2r-2r, with the canonical complex orientation. Neither the rank nor the kernel sign is to be read as the complex dimension rr in a real Fourier integral.

Ordered microlocal Hom through an analytic normal-cone bound

Let F,GF,G be weakly complex constructible on XX, and let P=T*XP=T^*X. The exact ordered microlocal-Hom estimate is

SS⁡(μhom⁡(G,F))⊂κC(SS⁡(F),SS⁡(G))⊂T*P,κ(w)=ιwΩP.(17) \operatorname{SS}(\mu\operatorname{hom}(G,F)) \subset\kappa C\bigl(\operatorname{SS}(F),\operatorname{SS}(G)\bigr) \subset T^*P, \qquad \kappa(w)=\iota_w\Omega_P. \qquad\text{(17)}

The pair cone is first minus second with positive real scales, based at a common point of PP. For a tangent vector w=(u,v)w=(u,v) in coordinates (z,ξ)(z,\xi), the convention is κ(w)=vdz−udξ\kappa(w)=v\,dz-u\,d\xi. Its inverse is the earlier −H-H identification. Thus the order in (17) is the target FF first and the source GG second.

Both input microsupports are analytic. The analytic pair-cone theorem makes the cone in (17) closed complex analytic and complex-conic in its tangent-vector fibres. The holomorphic fibre-linear isomorphism κ\kappa makes its image complex-conic in the cotangent fibres of PP.

We also need isotropy of that bound. Its exact model uses P1=T*(X×X)P_1=T^*(X\times X), L=TΔ*(X×X)L=T^*_{\Delta}(X\times X), and the isotropic product SS⁡(F)×SS⁡(G)a\operatorname{SS}(F)\times\operatorname{SS}(G)^a. The Lagrangian normal-cone theorem makes KCLKC_L of this product real-isotropic. Under L≃PL\simeq P, (x,x;ξ,−ξ)↦(x;ξ)(x,x;\xi,-\xi)\mapsto(x;\xi), it is exactly the image in (17). To check the sign, use δ=y−x\delta=y-x, ν=η\nu=\eta, s=ξ+ηs=\xi+\eta. With the second input antipodal, the position difference is −δ-\delta, the covector difference is ss, and KK gives sdz−(−δ)dξs\,dz-(-\delta)\,d\xi after the positive scaling. This is κ\kappa of the ordered pair difference. The antipode in the product is part of this identification, not an additional antipode to insert into (17).

Boundedness and weak real constructibility of microlocal Hom are supplied by the existing full diagonal-kernel theorem, with its global Hom and finite specialization/Fourier amplitude. Its definition is

μhom⁡(G,F)=μΔXRℋom(q2−1G,q1!F).(18) \mu\operatorname{hom}(G,F)= \mu_{\Delta_X}R\mathcal Hom(q_2^{-1}G,q_1^!F). \qquad\text{(18)}

Now (1) on the complex manifold PP, together with the analytic complex-conic isotropic bound (17), proves weak complex constructibility there. For perfect constructible F,GF,G, the real microlocal Hom perfection theorem gives perfect output stalks, so the output is complex constructible.

The proof uses the bounded normal-cone estimate directly. The positive real deformation chamber used inside specialization is subanalytic but is not a complex analytic piece; we have not applied holomorphic-operation stability to that chamber. Likewise the exceptional projection in (18) remains present: q1!F≃q1−1F⊗q2−1ωXq_1^!F\simeq q_1^{-1}F\otimes q_2^{-1}\omega_X, with ωX=kX[2n]\omega_X=k_X[2n]. This factor controls the normal Fourier degree even though all complex orientations are canonical.

We have proved every weak and perfect assertion of the complex operation theorem: holomorphic ordinary and exceptional inverse image, proper-on-support direct image, tensor, internal Hom, microlocal Hom, and positive-conic Fourier–Sato on a holomorphic vector bundle. General nonproper holomorphic direct images require the further cutoff and curve-base arguments.

A holomorphic ramification keeps escaping inverse directions

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Let f:ℂw→ℂzf:\mathbb C_w\to\mathbb C_z, f(w)=wmf(w)=w^m, with m≥2m\ge2, and let F=k{0}F=k_{\{0\}}. Compare the ordinary cotangent inverse with the full characteristic inverse, and check complex transpose and the product condition.

Solution. Microsupport of the point sheaf is T0*ℂzT_0^*\mathbb C_z. The ordinary correspondence requires w=0w=0, and its transpose differential is zero there, so gives only the zero covector. For any complex output coefficient η\eta, take positive real wj→0w_j\to0, zj=0z_j=0, and

ξj=ηmwjm−1,mwjm−1ξj=η,|zj−wjm||ξj|=|η|m|wj|→0.(19) \xi_j=\frac{\eta}{m w_j^{m-1}},\qquad m w_j^{m-1}\xi_j=\eta,\qquad |z_j-w_j^m|\,|\xi_j|=\frac{|\eta|}{m}|w_j|\to0. \qquad\text{(19)}

For η=0\eta=0 the witnesses are zero and still valid. No other base can occur, because input base zero must approach f(w)f(w). Thus f♯T0*ℂz=T0*ℂwf^\sharp T_0^*\mathbb C_z=T_0^*\mathbb C_w. The holomorphic transpose uses mwm−1m w^{m-1}, without complex conjugation. Ordinary inverse image is the point sheaf, whose full origin microsupport is bounded by this characteristic inverse. Its stalks kk and zero are perfect.

Proper branched pushforward and its cotangent bound

Difficulty: Intermediate.

For f(w)=wmf(w)=w^m with m≥2m\ge2, take G=kℂG=k_{\mathbb C}. Determine its direct-image stalks, its local monodromy off zero, and the set (7). Verify perfection and properness.

Solution. The polynomial map is proper: a compact target set bounds |w|m|w|^m, and its preimage is closed and bounded. Fibres are finite, so proper base change gives cohomology only in degree zero, with stalk kmk^m away from zero and kk at zero. A loop around zero cyclically permutes the mm roots, giving that permutation monodromy on kmk^m. Restrictions from the central constant section give the diagonal map k→kmk\to k^m. All these finite free stalks are perfect.

Input microsupport is the zero section. Its correspondence inverse is the condition mwm−1ξ=0m w^{m-1}\xi=0, so over w≠0w\ne0 it has only zero covectors, and over w=0w=0 it has every complex covector. Projection gives the zero section of the target together with its full origin fibre. This is the closed analytic complex-conic bound (7). The common analytic cover ℂ*,{0}\mathbb C^*,\{0\} also exhibits weak complex constructibility; perfection gives the strong assertion. Finite-fibre integration inserts no real two-dimensional shift.

Holomorphic nonproper support can accumulate singular values

Difficulty: Advanced.

Let f:ℂ→ℂf:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C, f(z)=ezf(z)=e^z, and let G=⨁m≥1k{−m}G=\bigoplus_{m\ge1}k_{\{-m\}}, for a nonzero field kk. Show that GG is complex constructible, while Rf*GRf_*G is not weakly complex constructible near zero. Identify the failed hypothesis.

Solution. The support is a closed discrete analytic subset of ℂ\mathbb C, with no finite accumulation. The locally finite point/open analytic decomposition has stalks kk or zero, hence perfect; thus GG is complex constructible. Sections over any open set are the product of the point coefficients occurring there, and restrictions are surjective. This sheaf is flabby, so is acyclic on every inverse-image open set. Consequently Rf*G=f*GRf_*G=f_*G.

At each isolated target value e−me^{-m}, a small target neighborhood isolates that value from all other support images, giving stalk kk; on intervening open regions the sheaf is zero. Infinitely many such point jumps approach zero. An analytic μ-stratification of a complex curve is locally a finite set of point strata and open complex strata. Locally constant restriction on the open strata cannot have these accumulating isolated jumps. Therefore condition 1 fails near zero. No assertion that the stalk at zero vanishes is needed; nonproper stalk base change would not be justified there.

Properness on support fails: the inverse image of the compact closed unit disc contains all the support points −m-m, an unbounded set. Thus (8) is inapplicable. Holomorphicity of ff does not replace its support-properness hypothesis.

Fourier of a complex linear subspace

Difficulty: Intermediate.

In E=ℂrE=\mathbb C^r, let LL be a complex linear subspace of dimension dd. Compute (kL)∧(k_L)^\wedge with convention (16), including the real degree, orientation and cotangent sign. Check the case d=0d=0.

Solution. For η∉L⟂\eta\notin L^\perp, the real linear form Re⁡η|L\operatorname{Re}\eta|_L is nonzero: if it were zero on every real vector of the complex space LL, testing imaginary multiples would make η|L=0\eta|_L=0. The allowed part of LL is then a closed real halfspace, whose compactly supported cohomology is zero. For η∈L⟂\eta\in L^\perp, it is all of LL, of real dimension 2d2d, with compact cohomology k[−2d]k[-2d]. The closed-support Fourier comparison therefore gives

(kL)∧=kL⟂[−2d].(20) (k_L)^\wedge=k_{L^\perp}[-2d]. \qquad\text{(20)}

Complex linear transition determinants have positive real determinant |det⁡ℂ|2|\det_{\mathbb C}|^2, so the orientation factor is canonically trivial. Source conormals have v∈Lv\in L, η∈L⟂\eta\in L^\perp; ΦE\Phi_E sends them to base η\eta with covector −v-v, the target conormal, including both zero loci. For d=0d=0, the point coefficient transforms to the constant sheaf on all E*E^*, with shift zero. For d=rd=r, the whole fibre transforms to its origin sheaf shifted by −2r-2r.

A real ray fails the complex Fourier input condition

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Regard the closed positive real ray R={t∈ℂ:t≥0 real}R=\{t\in\mathbb C:t\ge0\text{ real}\} as a subset of E=ℂE=\mathbb C. Compute (kR)∧(k_R)^\wedge and explain why it does not contradict the complex Fourier theorem.

Solution. Write η=a+ib\eta=a+ib. On the ray the cutoff is at≤0at\le0. If a>0a>0, only t=0t=0 is allowed, with compact cohomology kk. If a≤0a\le0, the whole closed half-line is allowed and its compact cohomology vanishes. The resulting negative-cutoff comparison is the constant sheaf on the open half-plane {a>0}\{a>0\} extended by zero, in degree zero. Its real boundary is not a complex analytic boundary, and its microsupport at that boundary is not complex-conic.

Although kRk_R is positive-conic and real constructible, it is not weakly complex constructible: on the nonzero ray its real conormal is not invariant under complex cotangent dilation. Thus it fails the input complex condition. The kernel in (16) is real; analyticity of the output follows from both symmetries of a valid input microsupport, not from analyticity of this inequality.

The exceptional diagonal degree cancels a real normal Fourier degree

Difficulty: Advanced.

For constant inputs F=G=kXF=G=k_X on a complex nn-manifold, compute μhom⁡(kX,kX)\mu\operatorname{hom}(k_X,k_X) from (18). Retain the exceptional projection and the real normal rank, and identify its support.

Solution. Canonical complex orientation gives q1!kX=kX×X[2n]q_1^!k_X=k_{X\times X}[2n]. The internal Hom from q2−1kXq_2^{-1}k_X is therefore this same constant kernel shifted by 2n2n. Specialization along the diagonal keeps the constant sheaf on the real normal bundle with that shift. Its real normal rank is 2n2n, and Fourier of the entire normal fibre is its zero-section coefficient shifted by −2n-2n, with canonical complex orientation. The shifts cancel. Hence the result is kTX*Xk_{T_X^*X}, the zero-section constant sheaf extended to T*XT^*X, in degree zero. Off that zero section it vanishes. Dropping q1!q_1^!’s degree would instead give an incorrect −2n-2n shift; using complex rank nn for the real Fourier degree would also fail.

Perfect torsion coefficients keep opposite derived degrees

Difficulty: Intermediate.

At a closed point of a complex manifold, take coefficients A=ℤ/mA=\mathbb Z/m, B=ℤ/nB=\mathbb Z/n, with m,n>1m,n>1, and d=gcd⁡(m,n)d=\gcd(m,n). Compute their derived tensor and internal Hom cohomology. Explain why degree-zero coefficients alone do not verify the operation theorem.

Solution. The finite free resolution [ℤ→mℤ][\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{m}\mathbb Z] of AA, in degrees −1,0-1,0, gives tensor complex [B→mB][B\xrightarrow{m}B] in those same degrees. Its kernel and cokernel are ℤ/d\mathbb Z/d, so tensor has this group in degrees −1-1 and zero. Applying Hom gives the two-term complex in degrees zero and one, with differential ±m\pm m; both cohomology groups are again ℤ/d\mathbb Z/d. For point-supported sheaves these are the corresponding supported coefficient complexes: closed-embedding adjunction identifies Hom on that support with coefficient Hom, and tensor is stalkwise.

Both outputs are perfect, using finite-projective representatives of the two inputs. The derived terms in opposite degrees are part of the theorem and cannot be omitted by replacing tensor or Hom with only its ordinary degree-zero functor. No complex manifold dimension shift is added to these point-supported coefficient operations.

Remaining scope

The complex operation applications and their independent weak/perfect hypotheses are proved relative to the stated current microlocal and real finiteness contracts. The general complex derived-realization phenomenon and nonproper cutoff and complex-curve direct-image theorem require further arguments; they are not consequences of unrestricted holomorphic pushforward stability.

A holomorphic differential commutes with complex cotangent dilation, but the Fourier cotangent map exchanges a fibre covector with a base vector. The proof therefore uses both complex scaling actions, not just conicity in the original cotangent fibre. Microlocal Hom is handled through the complex normal cone while retaining the exceptional projection’s orientation shift. The positive real deformation chamber is not incorrectly declared a complex analytic stratum.
