Sheaf proof readings

SH02-CA — Extending sections on convex sets

Original programme text: CC0 1.0 Universal. This reading develops interval, compact-convex and locally closed convex extension arguments, including their canonical restriction maps.

Convexity controls how test sets fit together. It does not make an arbitrary sheaf acyclic. This unit proves an extension criterion that the directional-topology construction needs: if sections on every compact convex test set extend to the whole space, then ordinary higher cohomology vanishes. The distinction between extension on points and extension on convex sets becomes essential in dimension two.

Throughout this unit, kk is a commutative ring with identity, VV is a finite-dimensional real vector space, and sheaves are sheaves of arbitrary kk-modules. The argument does not use finite global dimension; it therefore applies, in particular, to the full coefficient convention of the advanced course. No field, flatness, finite rank, constructibility, bounded support, or compact-ambient-space assumption is introduced. For a subset BB of the space carrying FF, the notation Hq(B;F)H^q(B;F) means Hq(B;F|B)H^q(B;F|_B) with the ordinary subspace topology. It does not mean cohomology with support in BB.

Use the sheaf-operation hypotheses for exact inverse image, enough injectives and derived sections. The proper-fibre proof supplies the compact-fibre comparison below, and the bounded-below hypercohomology proof supplies the constant-complex calculation. An injective sheaf is flabby, and flabby sheaves are acyclic for direct image; these facts apply to modules on every ringed space. See Stacks, Tag 09SX and Tag 09T0. We prove the closed-cover, compact-neighborhood and exhaustion arguments actually needed below. Thus none of those three arguments is left as an unnamed SH-01 import.

The finite gluing obstruction

SH02-CA-CLOSED-MV — Closed-cover cohomology

Lemma. Suppose a topological space YY is the union of two closed subsets AA and BB. For every sheaf FF on YY, there is a natural long exact sequence

⋯⟶Hq−1(A∩B;F)⟶Hq(Y;F)⟶Hq(A;F)⊕Hq(B;F)⟶Hq(A∩B;F)⟶⋯.(C1) \cdots\longrightarrow H^{q-1}(A\cap B;F) \longrightarrow H^q(Y;F) \longrightarrow H^q(A;F)\oplus H^q(B;F) \longrightarrow H^q(A\cap B;F)\longrightarrow\cdots. \qquad\text{(C1)}

The map on the right is the difference of the two restrictions.

Proof. Write iA,iB,iA∩Bi_A,i_B,i_{A\cap B} for the closed inclusions. The sequence of sheaves on YY

0⟶F⟶iA*(F|A)⊕iB*(F|B)⟶iA∩B,*(F|A∩B)⟶0(C2) 0\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow i_{A*}(F|_A)\oplus i_{B*}(F|_B) \longrightarrow i_{A\cap B,*}(F|_{A\cap B})\longrightarrow0 \qquad\text{(C2)}

has diagonal first map and difference second map. At a point in A∩BA\cap B, its stalk sequence is 0→Fy→Fy⊕Fy→Fy→00\to F_y\to F_y\oplus F_y\to F_y\to0. At a point in just one of A,BA,B, it is the identity on FyF_y, followed by zero. These are exact. Closedness is used to make the other stalk zero outside its closed set. Thus (C2) is exact.

The pushforward along a closed inclusion is exact, as its stalks have precisely this description. It also preserves injectives, being right adjoint to exact inverse image. Its derived global sections are therefore the derived sections on the closed subset. Apply derived global sections to (C2) to obtain (C1). No statement about an infinite closed cover is being used. ▫\square

SH02-CA-INTERVAL — Interval criterion

Interval criterion. Let II be a compact interval and FF any sheaf on II. Then Hq(I;F)=0H^q(I;F)=0 for q>1q>1. If, in addition, every evaluation

Γ(I;F)⟶Ft,t∈I,(C3) \Gamma(I;F)\longrightarrow F_t,\qquad t\in I, \qquad\text{(C3)}

is surjective, then Hq(I;F)=0H^q(I;F)=0 for every q>0q>0.

Proof. A one-point interval has an exact sections functor, so assume I=[a,b]I=[a,b] with a<ba<b. Fix u∈Hq(I;F)u\in H^q(I;F), where q>0q>0. A positive-degree cohomology class is locally zero: choose an injective resolution and a global cocycle representing uu. Exactness of the resolution as a sheaf complex gives a primitive on a neighborhood of each point. Restriction to an open set preserves injectives, because extension by zero for an open inclusion is exact. The primitive therefore makes the restricted cohomology class zero on that neighborhood.

Choose a finite such open cover of II. A sufficiently fine partition

a=t0<t1<⋯<tm=b a=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_m=b

has every closed piece Jr=[tr−1,tr]J_r=[t_{r-1},t_r] contained in one member of the cover. For example, use a Lebesgue number for the finite cover and take all piece lengths smaller than it. Consequently u|Jr=0u|_{J_r}=0 for every rr.

We now join the pieces successively. Put Lr=[a,tr]L_r=[a,t_r]. The closed cover Lr+1=Lr∪Jr+1L_{r+1}=L_r\cup J_{r+1} has intersection the single point trt_r. If q>1q>1, the group Hq−1({tr};F)H^{q-1}(\{t_r\};F) vanishes, so (C1) makes

Hq(Lr+1;F)⟶Hq(Lr;F)⊕Hq(Jr+1;F) H^q(L_{r+1};F)\longrightarrow H^q(L_r;F)\oplus H^q(J_{r+1};F)

injective. For q=1q=1 under (C3), the same injectivity follows because the preceding difference map

Γ(Lr;F)⊕Γ(Jr+1;F)⟶Ftr \Gamma(L_r;F)\oplus\Gamma(J_{r+1};F)\longrightarrow F_{t_r}

is surjective: a prescribed germ is the germ of a section on II, which can be restricted to LrL_r and paired with zero. Notice that the original global hypothesis supplies the needed surjectivity at every intermediate join; no new hypothesis on F|LrF|_{L_r} is imposed.

Induction on rr now gives u|Lr=0u|_{L_r}=0 and finally u=0u=0. This proves both assertions. The empty interval has zero sections and cohomology and is harmless. ▫\square

Compact convex test sets

SH02-OR-CONVEX-COMPACT — Compact convex criterion

Compact convex criterion. Let K⊂VK\subset V be compact and convex and let FF be a sheaf on KK. Assume that for every compact convex L⊂KL\subset K, restriction

Γ(K;F)⟶Γ(L;F)(C4) \Gamma(K;F)\longrightarrow\Gamma(L;F) \qquad\text{(C4)}

is surjective. Then Hq(K;F)=0H^q(K;F)=0 for q>0q>0.

Proof. The assertion is immediate for K=⌀K=\varnothing. We induct on the dimension dd of the affine hull of nonempty KK. At d=0d=0 the set is one point and sections are exact. If d>0d>0, choose an affine linear function that is nonconstant on KK. It defines a map

p:K⟶I=p(K), p:K\longrightarrow I=p(K),

where II is a nondegenerate compact interval. The map is proper: KK is compact and the target is Hausdorff, so inverse images of compact sets are closed subsets of KK and hence compact.

For t∈It\in I, the fiber Kt=K∩p−1(t)K_t=K\cap p^{-1}(t) is a nonempty compact convex set of affine dimension at most d−1d-1. Its restricted sheaf inherits (C4). Indeed, for compact convex L⊂KtL\subset K_t, an extension of any section on LL to KK can be restricted to KtK_t. The induction hypothesis gives Hq(Kt;F)=0H^q(K_t;F)=0 for q>0q>0.

The proper-fibre formula and its section-restriction identification apply to the compact Hausdorff spaces KK and II, with arbitrary kk-module coefficients. They identify

(Rqp*F)t≃Hq(Kt;F),(p*F)t≃Γ(Kt;F).(C5) (R^q p_*F)_t\simeq H^q(K_t;F), \qquad (p_*F)_t\simeq\Gamma(K_t;F). \qquad\text{(C5)}

Thus Rp*FRp_*F is the sheaf p*Fp_*F in degree zero. The evaluation of a global section of p*Fp_*F at tt corresponds in (C5) to the restriction Γ(K;F)→Γ(Kt;F)\Gamma(K;F)\to\Gamma(K_t;F), which is surjective by (C4). The interval criterion applies to p*Fp_*F.

Finally, direct-image composition gives

RΓ(K;F)≃RΓ(I;Rp*F)≃RΓ(I;p*F).(C6) R\Gamma(K;F)\simeq R\Gamma(I;Rp_*F) \simeq R\Gamma(I;p_*F). \qquad\text{(C6)}

For clarity, this composition can be computed with one injective resolution: p*p_* preserves injectives because its left adjoint p−1p^{-1} is exact. The last complex in (C6) has no positive cohomology by the interval criterion. This completes the induction, including compact convex sets with empty ambient interior. ▫\square

The only fiberwise cohomology identification in this proof is for a proper map. Merely observing that the fibers of a nonproper map are convex would not justify (C5). The canonical maps in (C5) are the proper-base-change maps; the map tested for surjectivity is the actual restriction, not an abstract isomorphism chosen between modules. Stacks, Tag 09V6

Two compactness tools

SH02-CA-COMPACT-NEIGHBORHOOD — Compact-neighborhood extension

Extending a compact-set section to a neighborhood. If YY is locally compact Hausdorff, L⊂YL\subset Y is compact, and EE is a sheaf on YY, then

colim⁡W⊃L openΓ(W;E)⟶Γ(L;E|L)(C7) \mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_{W\supset L\text{ open}} \Gamma(W;E)\longrightarrow\Gamma(L;E|_L) \qquad\text{(C7)}

is an isomorphism. In particular, if EE is flabby, every section on LL extends to YY.

Proof. Let ss be a section on LL. By the construction of inverse image, it has local representatives si∈Γ(Ui;E)s_i\in\Gamma(U_i;E), whose restrictions represent ss on L∩UiL\cap U_i. Local compactness and the Hausdorff property allow a finite family of open sets ViV_i covering LL such that V¯i\overline V_i is compact and contained in the corresponding UiU_i. To obtain them, first choose at each point a relatively compact neighborhood with closure in a representing open neighborhood, and then take a finite subcover of LL.

For each pair i,ji,j, the set

Bij={y∈V¯i∩V¯j:(si)y≠(sj)y} B_{ij}=\{y\in\overline V_i\cap\overline V_j: (s_i)_y\ne(s_j)_y\}

is closed in the compact set V¯i∩V¯j\overline V_i\cap\overline V_j, since equality of germs is open on Ui∩UjU_i\cap U_j. It is compact, hence closed in YY, and disjoint from LL. Remove the finite union of these bad sets from ⋃iVi\bigcup_iV_i. The result is an open neighborhood of LL, and the sis_i agree on all its overlaps. They glue to a representative of ss on that neighborhood.

If two neighborhood sections induce the same section on LL, their germs agree at every point of LL. Their equality locus in the common domain is an open neighborhood of LL, so they represent the same colimit element. This proves injectivity and surjectivity of (C7). For flabby EE, the neighborhood representative extends to YY by flabbiness. ▫\square

SH02-CA-CONVEX-EXHAUSTION-GEOMETRY — Compact convex exhaustion

A compact convex exhaustion. Every nonempty locally closed convex subset C⊂VC\subset V has compact convex subsets KnK_n such that

Kn⊂int⁡CKn+1,C=⋃n≥1int⁡CKn.(C8) K_n\subset\operatorname{int}_C K_{n+1}, \qquad C=\bigcup_{n\ge1}\operatorname{int}_C K_n. \qquad\text{(C8)}

Here int⁡C\operatorname{int}_C is topological interior in CC. It is not the relative interior taken in an affine hull.

Proof. Fix a Euclidean norm. Because CC is locally closed, it is open in its closure C¯\overline C. Hence D=C¯\CD=\overline C\setminus C is closed in VV. Set

Am={x∈C¯:|x|≤m,dist⁡(x,D)≥1/m},m≥1.(C9) A_m=\{x\in\overline C:|x|\le m, \operatorname{dist}(x,D)\ge 1/m\}, \qquad m\ge1. \qquad\text{(C9)}

If D=⌀D=\varnothing, omit the distance condition. Each AmA_m is compact and contained in CC, the sequence is increasing, and its interiors in CC cover CC. In fact, for x∈Cx\in C, choose mm with |x|<m|x|<m and, when needed, dist⁡(x,D)>1/m\operatorname{dist}(x,D)>1/m. Both strict inequalities hold on a neighborhood of xx in CC, contained in AmA_m.

Put Bm=conv⁡(Am)B_m=\operatorname{conv}(A_m), taking the hull of the empty set to be empty. Convexity of CC gives Bm⊂CB_m\subset C. These hulls are compact in finite dimension. Here is the needed finite-dimensional argument. If a convex combination in VV uses more than N+1N+1 points, where N=dim⁡VN=\dim V, their augmented vectors (xi,1)(x_i,1) are linearly dependent. Varying the coefficients in that dependence keeps their sum and barycenter fixed. One may vary until a coefficient first becomes zero, keeping all coefficients nonnegative. Repeating leaves at most N+1N+1 points. Thus the convex hull of a nonempty compact set is the image of the compact space AmN+1×ΔNA_m^{N+1}\times\Delta^N under its continuous barycenter map, and is compact.

The BmB_m are increasing, and int⁡CAm⊂int⁡CBm\operatorname{int}_C A_m\subset\operatorname{int}_C B_m, so their interiors cover CC. After discarding initial empty terms, construct increasing indices mnm_n recursively. Given mnm_n, compactness of BmnB_{m_n} and the increasing open cover by the int⁡CBm\operatorname{int}_C B_m allow an index mn+1>mnm_{n+1}>m_n with Bmn⊂int⁡CBmn+1B_{m_n}\subset\operatorname{int}_C B_{m_{n+1}}. The indices tend to infinity. Taking Kn=BmnK_n=B_{m_n} gives (C8). This argument includes sets of lower affine dimension and sets with some boundary faces retained and others excluded. ▫\square

Passing to a locally closed convex set

SH02-OR-CONVEX-EXHAUSTION — Locally closed convex criterion

Locally closed convex criterion. Let C⊂VC\subset V be locally closed and convex, and let FF be a sheaf on CC. Suppose

Γ(C;F)⟶Γ(L;F)is surjective for every compact convex L⊂C.(C10) \Gamma(C;F)\longrightarrow\Gamma(L;F) \quad\text{is surjective for every compact convex }L\subset C. \qquad\text{(C10)}

Then Hq(C;F)=0H^q(C;F)=0 for every q>0q>0.

Proof. Assume CC is nonempty; otherwise the statement is immediate. It is locally compact Hausdorff. The restriction F|LF|_L to any compact convex L⊂CL\subset C satisfies the compact criterion: for compact convex T⊂LT\subset L, an extension from TT to CC restricts to an extension to LL. Therefore

Hq(L;F)=0(q>0).(C11) H^q(L;F)=0\qquad(q>0). \qquad\text{(C11)}

Choose a monomorphism into an injective sheaf and let QQ be its cokernel:

0⟶F⟶J⟶Q⟶0.(C12) 0\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow J\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow0. \qquad\text{(C12)}

First we prove that Γ(C;J)→Γ(C;Q)\Gamma(C;J)\to\Gamma(C;Q) is onto. Take s∈Γ(C;Q)s\in\Gamma(C;Q) and an exhaustion (C8). Exact restriction of (C12) to KnK_n, followed by (C11) in degree one, permits a lift un∈Γ(Kn;J)u_n\in\Gamma(K_n;J) of s|Kns|_{K_n}. We choose these lifts compatibly. If unu_n is already chosen and vv is any lift on Kn+1K_{n+1}, the difference v|Kn−unv|_{K_n}-u_n is a section aa of FF on KnK_n. Hypothesis (C10) extends aa to CC and hence to a section ã\widetilde a on Kn+1K_{n+1}. Replace vv by v−ãv-\widetilde a, using the inclusion F↪JF\hookrightarrow J. The new lift restricts to unu_n.

The compatible unu_n glue to a section uu of JJ on CC. To justify gluing despite the sets KnK_n being closed, restrict them to the open cover int⁡CKn\operatorname{int}_C K_n. Compatibility makes the restricted sections agree, so the sheaf axiom glues them. The resulting section has the specified germ on every KnK_n, because any point of KnK_n belongs to the interior of some later KmK_m and um|Kn=unu_m|_{K_n}=u_n. Its image is ss. The long exact sequence of (C12) now gives H1(C;F)=0H^1(C;F)=0.

Next we show that QQ again satisfies (C10). Let L⊂CL\subset C be compact convex and take t∈Γ(L;Q)t\in\Gamma(L;Q). Equation (C11) lifts tt to a section of JJ on LL. The sheaf JJ is flabby, so (C7) extends that section to CC. Its image in QQ extends tt. This proves the claim.

The first-degree argument consequently applies to every successive cokernel in an injective resolution. Since positive cohomology of an injective sheaf is zero, the long exact sequence gives, for q≥2q\ge2,

Hq(C;F)≃Hq−1(C;Q). H^q(C;F)\simeq H^{q-1}(C;Q).

Repeat until reaching the already proved first-degree vanishing. This proves all positive-degree vanishing. No interchange of a derived functor with an unverified inverse limit is used; the compatible-lift construction supplies precisely the extension that such an argument would have to establish. ▫\square

SH02-CA-OPEN-AMBIENT — Open ambient sets

Extension criterion in an open ambient set. Let X⊂VX\subset V be open, with no convexity assumption on XX, and let FF be a sheaf on XX. Assume that Γ(X;F)→Γ(K;F)\Gamma(X;F)\to\Gamma(K;F) is onto for every compact convex subset K⊂XK\subset X. Then

Hq(U;F)=0(q>0)(C13) H^q(U;F)=0\qquad(q>0) \qquad\text{(C13)}

for every convex open subset U⊂XU\subset X. More generally the same conclusion holds for every locally closed convex subset C⊂XC\subset X.

Proof. Restricting a global extension on XX to CC proves (C10) for F|CF|_C, so apply the locally closed criterion. A convex open UU is a permitted CC. In particular, passing from XX to UU does not assume XX was convex and does not replace the original global extension hypothesis by an unverified local one. ▫\square

This last assertion contains the full open-ambient theorem used in the manifold theory. The stronger locally closed conclusion is what allows the same proof to handle K+γK+\gamma when that set is closed and unbounded in the directional-topology unit.

Coefficients, a counterexample, and practice

SH02-CA-CONSTANT — Constant coefficients

Constant coefficients. For nonempty locally closed convex CC and any kk-module MM, the canonical map M→RΓ(C;MC)M\to R\Gamma(C;M_C) is an isomorphism. Convex sets are connected and locally connected, so sections of a constant sheaf on a nonempty convex set are exactly the constant functions with values in MM. Restrictions between such sets are the identity on MM; thus (C10) holds and gives higher vanishing. The identification in degree zero is the stated constant-section map.

More generally this canonical map is an isomorphism for a coefficient complex M∈D+(k)M\in D^+(k). The bounded-below hypercohomology construction, including its finite filtration and natural edge, proves the convergent spectral sequence

E2p,q=Hp(C;Hq(N))⟹Hp+qRΓ(C;N),N∈D+(kC). E_2^{p,q}=H^p(C;H^q(N))\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}R\Gamma(C;N), \qquad N\in D^+(k_C).

Apply it to N=MCN=M_C. The constant-sheaf functor is exact, so its cohomology sheaves are Hq(M)CH^q(M)_C, all acyclic by the preceding result. Boundedness below gives convergence in each degree, and the spectral sequence makes the edge map

HqRΓ(C;MC)⟶Γ(C;Hq(M)C) H^qR\Gamma(C;M_C)\longrightarrow\Gamma(C;H^q(M)_C)

an isomorphism. Naturality makes its composite with Hq(M)→HqRΓ(C;MC)H^q(M)\to H^qR\Gamma(C;M_C) the constant-section isomorphism. Here is the actual map identification: the edge is induced by the canonical truncation map, as proved in the natural-edge calculation. Truncation commutes with the exact constant-sheaf functor, and in the single degree qq the composite is the ordinary constant-section map Hq(M)→Γ(C;Hq(M)C)H^q(M)\to\Gamma(C;H^q(M)_C). That map is an isomorphism because CC is nonempty and connected. Hence the canonical coefficient-to-derived-sections map is an isomorphism. No assumption on the size, projectivity or flatness of the modules is used.

SH02-CA-COUNTEREXAMPLE-POINTS — Pointwise extension misses a loop

Pointwise extension misses a loop. Let MM be any nonzero kk-module, take K=[−2,2]×[−1,1]K=[-2,2]\times[-1,1], and let

S={(x,y):(x/2)2+y2=1}⊂K. S=\{(x,y):(x/2)^2+y^2=1\}\subset K.

For the closed inclusion i:S↪Ki:S\hookrightarrow K, put F=i*MSF=i_*M_S. The map Γ(K;F)→Fz\Gamma(K;F)\to F_z is onto at every point zz: on SS it is the identity M→MM\to M, and outside SS its target is zero. Nevertheless,

H1(K;F)≃M≠0.(C14) H^1(K;F)\simeq M\ne0. \qquad\text{(C14)}

Here is a calculation using only this unit. Cover SS by its closed upper and lower half-ellipses. Each is homeomorphic to a compact interval, with constant coefficient cohomology MM in degree zero. Their intersection consists of two points. The difference map in (C1) is

M⊕M⟶M⊕M,(a,b)⟼(a−b,a−b). M\oplus M\longrightarrow M\oplus M, \qquad(a,b)\longmapsto(a-b,a-b).

Its cokernel is MM, identified by subtracting the two coordinates. This proves H1(S;MS)=MH^1(S;M_S)=M, with all higher cohomology zero. Exact closed pushforward identifies this with (C14).

The missing compact-convex test is visible: for the vertical segment L={0}×[−1,1]L=\{0\}\times[-1,1], the restriction F|LF|_L is supported at its two endpoints, so Γ(L;F)=M⊕M\Gamma(L;F)=M\oplus M. Restriction from Γ(K;F)=M\Gamma(K;F)=M is diagonal and is not surjective. Thus the compact convex criterion cannot in dimension two be weakened to its singleton tests. This counterexample involves torsion or infinite modules just as well as vector spaces.

SH02-CA-EXERCISES — Exercises with solutions

Exercises with solutions.

  1. Let C={(x,y):0<x≤1,0≤y≤x}C=\{(x,y):0<x\le1,\ 0\le y\le x\}. Give a compact convex exhaustion satisfying (C8). Explain why affine relative interiors would give the wrong covering condition.

    Solution. Take Kn=C∩{x≥1/(n+1)}K_n=C\cap\{x\ge1/(n+1)\}. These are compact convex quadrilaterals or triangles, increase to CC, and Kn⊂int⁡CKn+1K_n\subset\operatorname{int}_C K_{n+1} because 1/(n+1)>1/(n+2)1/(n+1)>1/(n+2). The interiors in CC cover points on the edges y=0y=0, y=xy=x and x=1x=1 as well. Affine relative interiors of these full-dimensional polygons exclude all those edge points, so their union would omit valid points of CC.

  2. For nonzero MM, show that Mℝ2M_{\mathbb R^2} satisfies (C10) but is not flabby.

    Solution. Every nonempty compact convex subset is connected, so every section there is a single value in MM and extends constantly to ℝ2\mathbb R^2. On the union of two disjoint nonempty open disks, sections are M⊕MM\oplus M. Restriction from the whole plane is diagonal and fails to be surjective. Thus convex-set extension is strictly weaker than extension over every open subset.

  3. Let I=[0,3]I=[0,3], let j:(1,2)↪Ij:(1,2)\hookrightarrow I, and take E=j!M(1,2)E=j_!M_{(1,2)}. Compute RΓ(I;E)R\Gamma(I;E) and identify the failed hypothesis of the interval criterion.

    Solution. Let J=[1,2]J=[1,2]. On II there is an exact sequence

    0⟶E⟶MJ⟶M{1}⊕M{2}⟶0, 0\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow M_J \longrightarrow M_{\{1\}}\oplus M_{\{2\}}\longrightarrow0,

    where closed subsets mean exact closed pushforward. Exactness follows on stalks. The interval constant-sheaf calculation makes the map on degree-zero cohomology the diagonal M→M⊕MM\to M\oplus M, whose kernel is zero and cokernel is MM. Hence H0(I;E)=0H^0(I;E)=0, H1(I;E)=MH^1(I;E)=M, and all higher groups vanish: in cohomological notation, RΓ(I;E)≃M[−1]R\Gamma(I;E)\simeq M[-1]. If M≠0M\ne0, the global-to-stalk map at any point of (1,2)(1,2) is 0→M0\to M and is not surjective. Contractibility of II has not supplied the missing extension property.

  4. Where does finite dimension enter the locally closed criterion, and where does local closedness enter?

    Solution. Finite dimension makes the compact convex induction terminate, supplies a scalar affine projection, and makes the convex hull of a compact set compact through the N+1N+1-point barycenter argument. Local closedness makes CC locally compact Hausdorff and makes C¯\C\overline C\setminus C closed, so the distance cores in (C9) exhaust CC by compact subsets. Those properties are used both to construct (C8) and to extend sections of the injective sheaf from a compact subset to a neighborhood. The proof establishes no corresponding theorem after deleting either hypothesis.
