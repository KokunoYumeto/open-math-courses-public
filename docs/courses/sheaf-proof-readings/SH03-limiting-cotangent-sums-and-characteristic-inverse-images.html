Limiting cotangent sums and characteristic inverse images

Two nearby conormal directions can cancel while each grows without bound. Their finite remainder may contain a direction that no ordinary same-base sum sees. Likewise, the transpose differential of a map can have a finite limit on increasingly large input covectors near a critical point. The limiting cotangent operations retain these phenomena. We will prove that they preserve subanalytic isotropy, using exact slices of Lagrangian normal cones.

All manifolds are real analytic, finite dimensional, Hausdorff and countable at infinity; all maps below are analytic. Conic means invariant under every positive cotangent-fibre dilation. Isotropy of a subanalytic cotangent set means that its canonical one-form restricts to zero on its regular locus. We use the singular analytic-form calculus and the full normal-cone theorem in Boundary forms and Lagrangian normal cones. No sheaf coefficient ring or boundedness hypothesis enters these geometric operations.

Kashiwara and Schapira, Microlocal Study of Sheaves, Astérisque 128 (1985), §1.2, printed pp. 15–17, give the normal-cone sequence definition and Proposition 1.2.1 with its simultaneous-scaling proof for a submanifold restriction. Here the diagonal and arbitrary-map graph slices are proved explicitly, followed by isotropy from the boundary-form theorem. Both directions, zero covectors and unbounded inputs are retained.

The exact dependency of the isotropy assertion

The sequence geometry, joint scale and slice identities below apply to arbitrary positive-conic subsets. Subanalytic isotropy enters through the linked normal-cone theorem. Its boundary calculation uses analytic Taylor expansion on a proper uniformizing smooth source. The proper uniformization construction covers closed subanalytic sets on the stated countable-at-infinity manifolds. The local subanalytic set calculus and singular one-form pullback, closure and surjective detection supply the set and form operations. The latter proof uses Sard for a surjection between manifolds. Proper uniformization itself uses function resolution; the optional second boundary proof resolves the function on the uniformizing source separately. These geometric arguments involve no sheaf coefficients, constructibility assumption or derived-category bound.

The normal-deformation charts are the divided-coordinate charts constructed in the normal-scaling reading. For analytic adapted changes, their extension at parameter zero is analytic: a convergent power series vanishing at t=0t=0 is divisible by tt, with convergent analytic quotient. Thus no global tubular-neighborhood or contact normal-form theorem is needed for the local slice calculations.

The product bounds in the definitions

For conic subsets A,B⊂T*XA,B\subset T^*X, their limiting sum A+̂BA\widehat+ B has the local criterion

(x;σ)∈A+̂B⇔{(xj;ξj)∈A,(zj;ζj)∈B,xj,zj→x,ξj+ζj→σ,|xj−zj||ξj|→0.(1) (x;\sigma)\in A\widehat+ B \iff \begin{cases} (x_j;\xi_j)\in A,\quad (z_j;\zeta_j)\in B,\\ x_j,z_j\to x,\quad \xi_j+\zeta_j\to\sigma,\\ |x_j-z_j|\,|\xi_j|\to0. \end{cases} \qquad\text{(1)}

Covectors at different base points are written in one coordinate trivialization for this criterion. The operation is intrinsically defined by normal cones; the prerequisite coordinate comparison makes (1) independent of the chosen smooth analytic coordinates and local norms. In a fixed relatively compact chart, a change of trivialization alters the transformed sum by a term bounded by C|xj−zj||ξj|C|x_j-z_j|\,|\xi_j|, which tends to zero by the last line.

The finite limiting sum bounds |ξj+ζj||\xi_j+\zeta_j|. Therefore the product condition is equivalent to the one with |ζj||\zeta_j|, or to

|xj−zj|(1+|ξj|+|ζj|)→0.(2) |x_j-z_j|\,(1+|\xi_j|+|\zeta_j|)\to0. \qquad\text{(2)}

For example, |ζj|≤|ξj|+|ξj+ζj||\zeta_j|\le|\xi_j|+|\xi_j+\zeta_j|; multiplying by the base separation proves one implication. The reverse inequality gives the other. In particular the limiting sum is symmetric.

For f:Y→Xf:Y\to X and conic A⊂T*XA\subset T^*X, the characteristic inverse image has criterion

(y;η)∈f♯A⇔{yj→y,xj→f(y),(xj;ξj)∈A,(dfyj)tξj→η,|xj−f(yj)||ξj|→0.(3) (y;\eta)\in f^\sharp A \iff \begin{cases} y_j\to y,\quad x_j\to f(y),\quad (x_j;\xi_j)\in A,\\ (df_{y_j})^t\xi_j\to\eta,\\ |x_j-f(y_j)|\,|\xi_j|\to0. \end{cases} \qquad\text{(3)}

The differential is evaluated at the moving yjy_j. In (1) and (3), individual covectors need not converge. The product controls how fast they may grow relative to the base mismatch. Zero covectors are permitted whenever they belong to the chosen input sets.

The ordinary operations are respectively A+BA+B, with both inputs at one base point, and fdfπ−1Af_df_\pi^{-1}A, with x=f(y)x=f(y) and η=(dfy)tξ\eta=(df_y)^t\xi. Constant witnesses show that these ordinary sets are contained in the limiting operations. This does not give equality in general.

Coordinate covariance with the large covectors retained

The intrinsic slices (11) and (20) already show coordinate independence. The following direct estimates explain the role of their product bounds. Work in smaller relatively compact coordinate neighborhoods. All coordinate derivatives and inverse matrices used below are bounded there. An analytic coordinate change is C2C^2, so its first derivative and its inverse-transpose matrix are locally Lipschitz.

For a base change hh on XX, put P(x)=Dh(x)−TP(x)=Dh(x)^{-T}. The transformed covector sum is

P(xj)ξj+P(zj)ζj=P(zj)(ξj+ζj)+(P(xj)−P(zj))ξj,|(P(xj)−P(zj))ξj|≤C|xj−zj||ξj|.(LG1) P(x_j)\xi_j+P(z_j)\zeta_j =P(z_j)(\xi_j+\zeta_j) +(P(x_j)-P(z_j))\xi_j, \qquad \bigl|(P(x_j)-P(z_j))\xi_j\bigr| \le C|x_j-z_j|\,|\xi_j|. \qquad\text{(LG1)}

The last term tends to zero, even when the individual covectors diverge. The first tends to P(x)σP(x)\sigma. Also

|h(xj)−h(zj)||P(xj)ξj|≤C|xj−zj||ξj|.(LG2) |h(x_j)-h(z_j)|\,|P(x_j)\xi_j| \le C|x_j-z_j|\,|\xi_j|. \qquad\text{(LG2)}

Applying the same argument to h−1h^{-1} gives the reverse implication. Uniform equivalence of norms on these smaller charts proves norm independence as well.

For characteristic inverse image, change the source chart by gg, the target chart by hh, and write f′=hfg−1f'=hfg^{-1}. The covector at the nearby target point xjx_j transforms by Dh(xj)−TDh(x_j)^{-T}, whereas the derivative of the transformed map uses Dh(f(yj))Dh(f(y_j)). Retaining this distinction gives

(Dfg(yj)′)TDh(xj)−Tξj=Dg(yj)−T(Dfyj)Tξj+Rj,|Rj|≤C|xj−f(yj)||ξj|.(LG3) \begin{aligned} (Df'_{g(y_j)})^T Dh(x_j)^{-T}\xi_j &=Dg(y_j)^{-T}(Df_{y_j})^T\xi_j+R_j,\\ |R_j|&\le C|x_j-f(y_j)|\,|\xi_j|. \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(LG3)}

Indeed the extra matrix between (Dfyj)T(Df_{y_j})^T and ξj\xi_j is Dh(f(yj))TDh(xj)−T−1Dh(f(y_j))^T Dh(x_j)^{-T}-1, whose norm is bounded by C|xj−f(yj)|C|x_j-f(y_j)|. The new mismatch product is bounded by the old one as in (LG2). Consequently the limiting output is Dg(y)−TηDg(y)^{-T}\eta; applying the inverse chart changes proves equivalence in both directions. There is no estimate of either |ξj||\xi_j| or |ζj||\zeta_j| by a fixed constant. These are C2C^2 coordinate estimates, as sufficient for the analytic setting; arbitrary merely C1C^1 chart changes are not being asserted to have a Lipschitz derivative.

A positive scale that sends both errors to zero

We will need a scale that works even when the covectors grow. Suppose

aj≥0,aj→0,mj≥1,ajmj→0.(4) a_j\ge0,\quad a_j\to0,\qquad m_j\ge1,\quad a_jm_j\to0. \qquad\text{(4)}

Define

tj=aj/mj+1jmj.(5) t_j=\sqrt{a_j/m_j}+\frac1{jm_j}. \qquad\text{(5)}

Then tj>0t_j>0, tj→0t_j\to0, and

ajtj≤ajmj→0,tjmj=ajmj+1j→0.(6) \frac{a_j}{t_j}\le\sqrt{a_jm_j}\to0, \qquad t_jm_j=\sqrt{a_jm_j}+\frac1j\to0. \qquad\text{(6)}

For aj>0a_j>0, the first bound follows by dropping the positive second term in (5); for aj=0a_j=0, its left side is zero. Also tj≤aj+1/jt_j\le\sqrt{a_j}+1/j, proving its limit. Thus division by tjt_j kills the position error while multiplication by tjt_j kills every covector bounded by mjm_j. This supplies an actual normal-deformation sequence over the zero conormal base.

The joint scale is equivalent to the weighted condition

For mj≥1m_j\ge1 and aj≥0a_j\ge0, the weighted condition has exactly the following content:

ajmj⟶0⟺there are tj>0 with tj→0,aj/tj→0,tjmj→0.(LG4) a_jm_j\longrightarrow0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{there are }t_j>0\text{ with } t_j\to0,\quad a_j/t_j\to0,\quad t_jm_j\to0. \qquad\text{(LG4)}

The forward implication is precisely (5)–(6); the extra hypothesis aj→0a_j\to0 in (4) follows already from mj≥1m_j\ge1. Conversely, ajmj=(aj/tj)(tjmj)→0a_jm_j=(a_j/t_j)(t_jm_j)\to0. This explains why one scale can both move the cotangent base of the normal cone to its zero section and kill the divided position mismatch. If a sequential convention requires parameters to decrease strictly, pass to a subsequence of the positive tj→0t_j\to0. All limits and memberships remain valid.

Positive conicity is used twice in each slice proof: first to multiply a witness covector by tjt_j, and then, in the reverse direction, to divide by tjt_j. Neither use requires zero covectors to belong to the original sets. Zero output covectors arise in the closed limiting set even when an input omits them. No replacement of these positive dilations by an antipodal symmetry is justified or needed.

The diagonal conormal and its exact slice

Let

P=T*(X×X),L=TΔ*(X×X),S=A×B,(7) P=T^*(X\times X),\qquad L=T_\Delta^*(X\times X),\qquad S=A\times B, \qquad\text{(7)}

where Δ\Delta is the diagonal of the base X×XX\times X. The manifold LL is a closed analytic conic Lagrangian. Let r:L→Xr:L\to X project to the diagonal base and j:X→Lj:X\to L be its zero conormal section. Define the analytic bundle embedding

e:T*X⟶T*L,e(x;σ)=(j(x);(drj(x))tσ).(8) e:T^*X\longrightarrow T^*L,\qquad e(x;\sigma)=\bigl(j(x);(dr_{j(x)})^t\sigma\bigr). \qquad\text{(8)}

If λL\lambda_L and λX\lambda_X are the canonical forms on these cotangent bundles, then

e*λL=λX.(9) e^*\lambda_L=\lambda_X. \qquad\text{(9)}

Indeed the value of the left side on a tangent vector is (dr)tσ(dr)^t\sigma applied to its projected tangent at j(x)j(x); since r∘j=idXr\circ j=\mathrm{id}_X, the result is σ\sigma applied to the original projected base tangent.

The normal identification from the preceding lesson is

K:NLP⟶T*L,K([w])(v)=ωP(w,v),(10) K:N_LP\longrightarrow T^*L,\qquad K([w])(v)=\omega_P(w,v), \qquad\text{(10)}

whose inverse is induced by −H-H. The exact geometric identity is

A+̂B=e−1(KCL(A×B)).(11) A\widehat+ B=e^{-1}\bigl(K C_L(A\times B)\bigr). \qquad\text{(11)}

We prove it with both directions and all scaling conditions.

Isotropy of the full limiting sum

Theorem. If A,B⊂T*XA,B\subset T^*X are positive-conic subanalytic isotropic sets, then A+̂BA\widehat+ B is closed, positive-conic, subanalytic and isotropic. The inputs need not be closed.

Proof. The product S=A×BS=A\times B is subanalytic and positive-conic. Its canonical form is the sum of the forms from the two cotangent factors. Analytic pullback of their vanishing gives αP|S=0\alpha_P|_S=0, so SS is isotropic. The full Lagrangian normal-cone theorem makes

D=KCL(S)⊂T*L(17) D=K C_L(S)\subset T^*L \qquad\text{(17)}

subanalytic, positive-conic and isotropic. It is also closed as a normal cone in this bundle. Formula (11) makes the limiting sum an analytic inverse image of DD, so it is closed and subanalytic. The embedding is linear in the cotangent fibre, giving positive conicity. Singular analytic one-form pullback and (9) give λX|e−1D=0\lambda_X|_{e^{-1}D}=0. This proves isotropy. ▫\square

The proof covers cancellation at infinite covector norm through a closed normal-cone slice. It does not require properness of an unrestricted cotangent projection.

The graph conormal and characteristic inverse image

For f:Y→Xf:Y\to X, use the graph G={(y,f(y))}⊂Y×XG=\{(y,f(y))\}\subset Y\times X, and set

P=T*(Y×X),L=TG*(Y×X),S=TY*Y×A.(18) P=T^*(Y\times X),\qquad L=T_G^*(Y\times X),\qquad S=T_Y^*Y\times A. \qquad\text{(18)}

The first factor in SS is the zero section. The graph is a closed analytic submanifold for every analytic ff; no rank condition on dfdf is needed. Its conormal is a closed conic Lagrangian. Let r:L→Yr:L\to Y project to the graph base, let jj be its zero conormal section, and define e:T*Y→T*Le:T^*Y\to T^*L by the analogue of (8). Again,

e*λL=λY.(19) e^*\lambda_L=\lambda_Y. \qquad\text{(19)}

We claim the full identity

f♯A=e−1(KCL(TY*Y×A)).(20) f^\sharp A=e^{-1}\bigl(K C_L(T_Y^*Y\times A)\bigr). \qquad\text{(20)}

In local coordinates, with original covectors (θ,ξ)(\theta,\xi) on Y×XY\times X, use

δ=x−f(y),ν=ξ,s=θ+(dfy)tξ.(21) \delta=x-f(y),\qquad \nu=\xi,\qquad s=\theta+(df_y)^t\xi. \qquad\text{(21)}

The canonical form is exactly sdy+νdδs\,dy+\nu\,d\delta. The conormal is δ=s=0\delta=s=0, and the normal identification sends (v,η)(v,\eta) to ηdy−vdν\eta\,dy-v\,d\nu. Hence ee selects the same zero conormal base and zero position-normal component as before.

For a witness in (3), set aj=|xj−f(yj)|a_j=|x_j-f(y_j)|, mj=1+|ξj|m_j=1+|\xi_j|, and choose (5). Scale the XX covector by tjt_j and keep the YY covector zero. The normal-deformation coordinates are

Conversely, a witness for that central cone point has zero YY covector and XX covectors aj′→0a_j'\to0, with (xj;aj′)∈A(x_j;a_j')\in A, positive tj→0t_j\to0,

Theorem. If A⊂T*XA\subset T^*X is positive-conic, subanalytic and isotropic, then f♯A⊂T*Yf^\sharp A\subset T^*Y is closed, positive-conic, subanalytic and isotropic.

Proof. The product with the zero section in (18) is subanalytic, conic and isotropic. Apply the normal-cone theorem along the graph conormal. The analytic inverse-image identity (20), fibre linearity of ee, and canonical-form identity (19) prove each conclusion exactly as for (17). No input closedness, properness, noncharacteristic hypothesis or constant rank was imposed. ▫\square

Nonclosed inputs and actual closure invariance

The normal cone is closed in its normal bundle and depends only on the closure of its input. This can also be checked with its exact scales: if an input point is only in the closure, approximate it by an actual point within tj/jt_j/j in the adapted coordinates for a witness of deformation parameter tjt_j. The error in its divided normal coordinate is at most 1/j1/j, and its tangent-base error also tends to zero.

For the sequence operations (1) and (3), the following stronger explicit check handles the possibly unbounded covectors:

A¯+̂B¯=A+̂B,f♯A¯=f♯A.(LG5) \overline A\widehat+\overline B=A\widehat+B, \qquad f^\sharp\overline A=f^\sharp A. \qquad\text{(LG5)}

Monotonicity proves one inclusion. For the reverse sum inclusion, take a witness with inputs in the two closures and set mj=1+|ξj|+|ζj|m_j=1+|\xi_j|+|\zeta_j|. Approximate both input points by points in the original sets with each base and covector error less than ϵj=1/(jmj)\epsilon_j=1/(jm_j). The new covector sum changes by at most 2ϵj2\epsilon_j, the base separation increases by at most 2ϵj2\epsilon_j, and the first covector norm increases by at most ϵj\epsilon_j. Its weighted product is therefore at most

|xj−zj||ξj|+|xj−zj|ϵj+2ϵj|ξj|+2ϵj2⟶0.(LG6) |x_j-z_j|\,|\xi_j| +|x_j-z_j|\epsilon_j +2\epsilon_j|\xi_j|+2\epsilon_j^2 \longrightarrow0. \qquad\text{(LG6)}

All required limits survive. For characteristic inverse image keep yjy_j fixed and approximate (xj,ξj)(x_j,\xi_j) within ϵj=1/(j(1+|ξj|))\epsilon_j=1/(j(1+|\xi_j|)). The transpose derivatives are locally bounded, so their output error tends to zero. The mismatch-product estimate is the same calculation with one rather than two base perturbations. This proves the other equality in (LG5). The approximation argument itself requires no conicity; conicity is required by the normal-cone slice interpretations (11) and (20).

In particular, for an analytic diffeomorphism, the closure formula is

f♯A=fdfπ−1(A¯).(LG7) f^\sharp A=f_df_\pi^{-1}(\overline A). \qquad\text{(LG7)}

The inverse matrices bound and recover the convergent input covector, now in A¯\overline A; (LG5) supplies the reverse inclusion from closure witnesses. For the identity map this says id⁡♯A=A¯\operatorname{id}^{\sharp}A=\overline A. Thus closedness of the resulting set does not tacitly impose closedness on the original set.

Exercises with complete solutions

A limiting sum between disjoint zero-section supports

Difficulty: Introductory.

In T*ℝT^*\mathbb R, let A={(x;0):x>0}A=\{(x;0):x>0\} and B={(x;0):x<0}B=\{(x;0):x<0\}. Compute their ordinary sum and their limiting sum.

Solution. The ordinary sum requires a common base point, and the two base supports are disjoint, so A+B=∅A+B=\varnothing. Any limiting sum has covector zero, since both input covectors are zero. Its base must be in [0,∞)∩(−∞,0]={0}[0,\infty)\cap(-\infty,0]=\{0\}. The pair of base points 1/j,−1/j1/j,-1/j, with zero covectors, satisfies (1): the sum and gap product are identically zero. Thus A+̂B={(0;0)}A\widehat+ B=\{(0;0)\}. It is subanalytic and isotropic, although neither input support is closed.
