Sheaf proof readings

SH02-NG-UNIT — Normal geometry as a family with a central fibre

The geometric arguments use the differential-topology prerequisites stated below. This lesson develops the geometric input to specialization; it does not establish the sheaf-theoretic specialization theorems.

The purpose of the deformation parameter is to put two kinds of observation in one space. Away from parameter zero we see the original manifold. At parameter zero we see displacement vectors normal to a submanifold. A subset of the original manifold then determines a closed set of possible limiting displacements. The topology of this family, particularly at zero normal vectors, controls the neighborhoods used by specialization.

The geometric starting points are Kashiwara and Schapira, Microlocal Study of Sheaves, Astérisque 128 (1985), §1.2.1 (printed p. 15) and §2.2.1 (printed pp. 41–43), and Schapira, A short review on microlocal sheaf theory, 19 January 2016, §2.1, equations (2.5)–(2.6), and §4.2. The first gives the scaled-difference definition of a pair cone and the gluing construction of the deformation. The review compares those coordinates with the closure in the positive chamber. We prove their equivalence here, then supply the clean-map estimate, signed relative orientation, and neighborhood refinements needed by the later sheaf arguments.

SH02-NG-PREREQUISITES — Prerequisites and notation

Local identifier: SH02-NG-PREREQUISITES

All manifolds are finite dimensional and countable at infinity. Maps and submanifolds are either smooth or real analytic. We initially take an embedded closed submanifold i:M↪Xi:M\hookrightarrow X. For a locally closed submanifold, choose an open ambient neighborhood in which it is closed; the constructions involving the normal bundle agree after restriction to smaller such neighborhoods. A global deformation of all of XX below uses closedness.

The arguments use the following differential-topology inputs: adapted coordinate charts for embedded submanifolds; the inverse and constant-rank theorems; smooth partitions of unity and bundle metrics; and a smooth tubular neighborhood of a closed embedded submanifold. These are explicit prerequisite contracts, not claims that the sheaf-theoretic open-foundation imports already prove them. In the real analytic case the deformation and its functorial maps are real analytic; the auxiliary neighborhood proof is a topological proof and may use a smooth tubular neighborhood of the underlying smooth manifolds.

The normal bundle is

E=NMX=(TX|M)/TM,τ:E⟶M. E=N_MX=(TX|_M)/TM, \qquad \tau:E\longrightarrow M.

No coefficient ring is needed for the geometric results. When orientation sheaves are mentioned, use the course’s commutative coefficient ring kk of finite global dimension; neither orientability nor constructibility is assumed. For a vector bundle, “conic” means invariant under multiplication by every positive real number, including invariance in both directions.

SH02-NG-CONSTRUCTION — The deformation manifold and its maps

Local identifier: SH02-NG-CONSTRUCTION

As a set put

𝒟MX=(X×ℝ×)⊔E. \mathcal D_MX=(X\times\mathbb R^\times)\sqcup E.

The two pieces are not given their disjoint-union topology. Write n=dim⁡Xn=\dim X and r=codim⁡Mr=\operatorname{codim}M. Let (u,z)(u,z) be an adapted coordinate chart on XX, with u∈ℝru\in\mathbb R^r and M={u=0}M=\{u=0\}. The deformation chart is the open set

Ũ={(v,z,t):(tv,z)∈U}⊂ℝr×ℝdim⁡M×ℝ. \widetilde U=\{(v,z,t):(tv,z)\in U\} \subset\mathbb R^{r}\times\mathbb R^{\dim M}\times\mathbb R.

For t≠0t\ne0, its point (v,z,t)(v,z,t) represents ((tv,z),t)((tv,z),t). For t=0t=0, it represents the normal vector vv based at (0,z)(0,z). Charts disjoint from MM have no central points and describe the usual product with ℝ×\mathbb R^\times.

To verify that this prescription defines a manifold, write a change of adapted coordinates as

(u,z)⟼(a(u,z),b(u,z)),a(0,z)=0. (u,z)\longmapsto (a(u,z),b(u,z)),\qquad a(0,z)=0.

Its deformation is

(v,z,t)⟼{(t−1a(tv,z),b(tv,z),t),t≠0,(Dua(0,z)v,b(0,z),0),t=0. (v,z,t)\longmapsto \begin{cases} \bigl(t^{-1}a(tv,z),b(tv,z),t\bigr),&t\ne0,\\ \bigl(D_u a(0,z)v,b(0,z),0\bigr),&t=0. \end{cases}

The first component extends smoothly because locally

a(tv,z)t=∫01Dua(stv,z)vds. \frac{a(tv,z)}{t} =\int_0^1D_u a(stv,z)v\,ds.

The formula is needed only near t=0t=0, where the intervening segment lies in the coordinate domain. For analytic coordinates, divisibility of the analytic function a(tv,z)a(tv,z) by tt gives an analytic extension. Applying the same construction to the inverse coordinate change proves that the extended maps are diffeomorphisms. Composition agrees for t≠0t\ne0 and hence, by continuity, at t=0t=0. Thus the cocycle identities hold.

This also proves independence of the chosen adapted atlas. Take the union of two adapted atlases: every mixed transition has the same extension just constructed, and the cocycle identity holds on each mixed overlap. The identity map on the set underlying the deformation is consequently a diffeomorphism between the two resulting structures. Its central restriction is the identity on the normal quotient, rather than an unspecified bundle isomorphism.

Here are the separation and countability details. The functions

p:𝒟MX⟶X,t:𝒟MX⟶ℝ p:\mathcal D_MX\longrightarrow X, \qquad t:\mathcal D_MX\longrightarrow\mathbb R

are given by p(v,z,t)=(tv,z)p(v,z,t)=(tv,z) and the last coordinate. Points with different pp-values or different tt-values can be separated using these continuous maps. Distinct remaining central points lie over one point of MM and are separated in one deformation chart. A central point and a noncentral point have different tt-values. This proves Hausdorffness. A countable adapted atlas and the ordinary product charts yield a countable atlas for the deformation. The central coordinate transitions are precisely those of (TX|M)/TM(TX|_M)/TM, so the identification of the central fibre with EE is intrinsic.

The map tt is a submersion. For every c≠0c\ne0, p:t−1(c)→Xp:t^{-1}(c)\to X is a diffeomorphism. Also,

p−1(X\M)=(X\M)×ℝ×,t−1(0)=E. p^{-1}(X\setminus M)=(X\setminus M)\times\mathbb R^\times, \qquad t^{-1}(0)=E.

There is a canonical embedded copy of M×ℝM\times\mathbb R: its point (m,t)(m,t) has zero normal coordinate in every adapted chart. In particular,

p−1(M)=E∪(M×ℝ),E∩(M×ℝ)=M×{0}. p^{-1}(M)=E\cup(M\times\mathbb R), \qquad E\cap(M\times\mathbb R)=M\times\{0\}.

The map pp on the entire deformation need not be a submersion. On the positive chamber

Ω=t−1(0,∞),j:Ω↪𝒟MX,p+=p∘j, \Omega=t^{-1}(0,\infty),\qquad j:\Omega\hookrightarrow\mathcal D_MX, \qquad p_+=p\circ j,

it is the product projection X×(0,∞)→XX\times(0,\infty)\to X under the diffeomorphism (p,t)(p,t). Let s:E↪𝒟MXs:E\hookrightarrow\mathcal D_MX be the central embedding. Then p∘s=i∘τp\circ s=i\circ\tau.

SH02-NG-ACTION — Positive scaling and geometric fibres

There is a signed scaling action

c⋅(v,z,t)=(cv,z,t/c),c∈ℝ×. c\cdot(v,z,t)=(cv,z,t/c),\qquad c\in\mathbb R^\times.

On X×ℝ×X\times\mathbb R^\times this fixes the XX coordinate and rescales the parameter. Consequently it is coordinate independent there and, by continuity, everywhere. It restricts on EE to ordinary scalar multiplication. Positive scalars preserve Ω\Omega. This action keeps the entire zero-vector axis M×ℝM\times\mathbb R; passing only to nonzero normal rays discards that axis and cannot describe neighborhoods of zero normal vectors.

SH02-NG-PUNCTURED-QUOTIENT — A direction space with a signed radial coordinate

Let M⊂XM\subset X be a closed embedded submanifold of fixed codimension rr. Remove the zero-normal-vector axis from the deformation and form the positive scaling quotient:

P=𝒟MX\(M×ℝ),Q=P/ℝ>0,α:P⟶Q. P=\mathcal D_MX\setminus(M\times\mathbb R),\qquad Q=P/\mathbb R_{>0},\qquad \alpha:P\longrightarrow Q.

The quotient has a canonical smooth manifold structure, and the corresponding structure is real analytic when the deformation is real analytic. The map α\alpha is a principal ℝ>0\mathbb R_{>0}-bundle. The deformation projection descends to a proper map b:Q→Xb:Q\to X. Its fibre over x∉Mx\notin M has two points, distinguished by the sign of the parameter. Its fibre over m∈Mm\in M is the positive-ray sphere S(NmX)S(N_mX).

The central quotient A=(NMX\M)/ℝ>0A=(N_MX\setminus M)/\mathbb R_{>0} is a closed embedded hypersurface of QQ when r>0r>0. Every prescribed smooth section of α\alpha over AA extends to a smooth section over QQ. In particular, a bundle metric on NMXN_MX gives such a section over AA by taking the vector of norm one on each positive ray. These section assertions concern smooth maps on the underlying smooth manifolds; they require no real analytic partition of unity.

Proof. On an adapted coordinate neighborhood U⊂XU\subset X, write the original coordinates as (u,z)(u,z) and the deformation coordinates as (v,z,t)(v,z,t), where u=tvu=tv and u,v∈ℝru,v\in\mathbb R^r. On the part of PP above UU we have v≠0v\ne0. Set

θ=v∥v∥,ρ=t∥v∥,a=∥v∥. \theta=\frac{v}{\|v\|},\qquad \rho=t\|v\|,\qquad a=\|v\|.

This gives a diffeomorphism

P∩p−1U≃QU×ℝ>0,QU={(θ,z,ρ):θ∈Sr−1,(ρθ,z)∈U}. P\cap p^{-1}U\simeq Q_U\times\mathbb R_{>0},\qquad Q_U=\{(\theta,z,\rho):\theta\in S^{r-1},\ (\rho\theta,z)\in U\}.

The inverse is v=aθv=a\theta and t=ρ/at=\rho/a. In these coordinates positive scaling fixes (θ,z,ρ)(\theta,z,\rho) and sends aa to caca. Thus the quotient on this saturated open subset is precisely the open manifold QUQ_U. The same formulas are analytic: the norm of a nonzero vector is an analytic positive function. Over an open subset of X\MX\setminus M, ordinary product coordinates identify the quotient with two copies of that subset.

These quotient charts cover QQ. Their transition maps are obtained by applying a deformation coordinate change to the representative with a=1a=1 and then taking (θ,z,ρ)(\theta,z,\rho) in the other chart. They are smooth, or analytic, with inverses obtained in the same manner. The transition identities follow from those for deformation coordinates. Their topology is the quotient topology: the quotient map is open, since the saturation of an open set is a union of its open translates, and each displayed chart is the ordinary projection of a product.

To check Hausdorffness, points of QQ with different images in XX can be separated using the descended map bb. Two points above the same xx lie in one of the quotient neighborhoods just described, where they can be separated. A countable adapted atlas, together with countable sphere atlases and the two charts away from MM, gives second countability. The product descriptions prove the principal-bundle assertion. They also show that AA is the locus ρ=0\rho=0. Its complement is open, because its inverse image is the invariant open subset of PP with t≠0t\ne0. Hence AA is closed, as well as an embedded hypersurface.

In the adapted quotient chart the descended map is

b(θ,z,ρ)=(ρθ,z). b(\theta,z,\rho)=(\rho\theta,z).

For compact K⊂UK\subset U, its inverse image is closed in the product of the compact sphere, the compact set of zz-coordinates of KK, and a bounded closed interval for ρ\rho: one may use |ρ|=∥u∥≤max⁡K∥u∥|\rho|=\|u\|\le\max_K\|u\|. It is therefore compact. Over a coordinate neighborhood disjoint from MM the map is the proper projection from two copies. These local compactness statements imply global properness. Explicitly, cover an arbitrary compact K⊂XK\subset X by finitely many open sets whose closures are compact and contained in such coordinate neighborhoods. Intersect those closures with KK. Their inverse images are compact by the local calculation and cover b−1Kb^{-1}K; since b−1Kb^{-1}K is closed in that finite union, it is compact. The same formula for bb gives the asserted fibres, including the two choices (θ,ρ)=(±u/∥u∥,±∥u∥)(\theta,\rho)=(\pm u/\|u\|,\pm\|u\|) when u≠0u\ne0.

Here are also explicit reasons that sections exist and extend. Choose a locally finite smooth partition of unity χi\chi_i on QQ subordinate to principal-bundle charts. In chart ii, let ai:P|Qi→ℝ>0a_i:P|_{Q_i}\to\mathbb R_{>0} be the fibre coordinate, so ai(cx)=cai(x)a_i(cx)=c a_i(x). The sum

h(x)=∑iχi(α(x))ai(x) h(x)=\sum_i\chi_i(\alpha(x))a_i(x)

is a smooth positive function with h(cx)=ch(x)h(cx)=c h(x). Each summand extends by zero where its partition function vanishes. Normalizing any representative xx to h(x)−1⋅xh(x)^{-1}\cdot x consequently defines a smooth section σ\sigma of α\alpha over all of QQ.

If σ0\sigma_0 is a prescribed smooth section over AA, there is a unique smooth function λ:A→ℝ>0\lambda:A\to\mathbb R_{>0} with σ0=λ⋅σ|A\sigma_0=\lambda\cdot\sigma|_A. To extend it, take a smooth tubular neighborhood of the closed embedded submanifold AA, compose log⁡λ\log\lambda with its retraction onto AA, and multiply by a smooth cutoff equal to one on a neighborhood of AA and supported in the tubular neighborhood. Extend the resulting function by zero outside that neighborhood. This gives a smooth function gg on QQ with g|A=log⁡λg|_A=\log\lambda, so eg⋅σe^g\cdot\sigma is the required extension. Tubular neighborhoods and cutoffs are the differential-topology prerequisites already listed in SH02-NG-PREREQUISITES.

If r=0r=0, then MM is open and closed in XX and P=(X\M)×ℝ×P=(X\setminus M)\times\mathbb R^\times. The quotient is two copies of the closed submanifold X\MX\setminus M, its projection is proper, and the central subset AA is empty. The fibre-coordinate construction still gives a global section; there is no central extension condition. ▫\square

The quotient describes the punctured deformation. The removed axis is still present in the original deformation and must be treated separately when a neighborhood contains a zero normal vector. Thus this auxiliary construction does not replace the four openness checks in SH02-NG-INTERVAL-NEIGHBORHOODS.

SH02-NG-FUNCTORIALITY — Maps of pairs

Local identifier: SH02-NG-FUNCTORIALITY

Let f:Y→Xf:Y\to X be a map and let N⊂YN\subset Y and M⊂XM\subset X be closed embedded submanifolds such that f(N)⊂Mf(N)\subset M. Write fN:N→Mf_N:N\to M for its restriction. The tangent map induces a fibrewise linear map

df¯:NNY⟶fN*NMX, \overline{d f}:N_NY\longrightarrow f_N^*N_MX,

and, after the projection to NMXN_MX, a normal map q:NNY→NMXq:N_NY\to N_MX.

There is a unique smooth, or real analytic, deformation map

f̃:𝒟NY⟶𝒟MX \widetilde f:\mathcal D_NY\longrightarrow\mathcal D_MX

that is (y,t)↦(f(y),t)(y,t)\mapsto(f(y),t) for t≠0t\ne0 and qq on the central fibre. To prove existence, choose adapted source coordinates (w,z)(w,z) and write f=(a,b)f=(a,b) in adapted target coordinates. Since a(0,z)=0a(0,z)=0, the formula

f̃(v,z,t)={(t−1a(tv,z),b(tv,z),t),t≠0,(Dwa(0,z)v,b(0,z),0),t=0 \widetilde f(v,z,t)= \begin{cases} \bigl(t^{-1}a(tv,z),b(tv,z),t\bigr),&t\ne0,\\ \bigl(D_w a(0,z)v,b(0,z),0\bigr),&t=0 \end{cases}

extends by the same integral or analytic-divisibility argument used for coordinate changes. It agrees on overlaps by density of t≠0t\ne0, which also proves uniqueness. Applying uniqueness to compositions and identities proves functoriality.

The equations tY=tX∘f̃t_Y=t_X\circ\widetilde f and pX∘f̃=f∘pYp_X\circ\widetilde f=f\circ p_Y show that the following three squares are Cartesian:

  1. The central-fibre square with horizontal maps sY,sXs_Y,s_X and vertical maps q,f̃q,\widetilde f.
  2. The positive-chamber square with horizontal maps jY,jXj_Y,j_X and vertical maps f×id(0,∞),f̃f\times\mathrm{id}_{(0,\infty)},\widetilde f.
  3. The square of product projections Y×(0,∞)→YY\times(0,\infty)\to Y and X×(0,∞)→XX\times(0,\infty)\to X, with vertical maps f×idf\times\mathrm{id} and ff.

For the first two, taking a fibre or an open inverse image under the common parameter function gives the claimed fibre product, including its topology and smooth structure. The third is the defining Cartesian square for a product projection. The square formed instead by pY,pX,f,f̃p_Y,p_X,f,\widetilde f need not be Cartesian.

SH02-NG-CLEAN-DEFINITION — Clean maps, transversality, and closedness

Local identifier: SH02-NG-CLEAN-DEFINITION

The conormal bundle is the annihilator

NM*X={(m;ξ)∈T*X|M:ξ|TmM=0}. N_M^*X=\{(m;\xi)\in T^*X|_M:\xi|_{T_mM}=0\}.

A map f:Y→Xf:Y\to X is clean with respect to MM when N=f−1(M)N=f^{-1}(M) is an embedded submanifold and the normal differential

df¯:NNY⟶fN*NMX \overline{d f}:N_NY\longrightarrow f_N^*N_MX

is injective on every fibre. Equivalently, its dual map fN*NM*X→NN*Yf_N^*N_M^*X\to N_N^*Y is surjective. Equivalently,

TyN={v∈TyY:dfy(v)∈Tf(y)M}(y∈N). T_yN=\{v\in T_yY:d f_y(v)\in T_{f(y)}M\} \qquad(y\in N).

These equivalences are finite-dimensional linear algebra: the kernel of the induced map on normal quotients is the right-hand side of the last formula modulo TyNT_yN, and a linear map is injective exactly when its dual is surjective.

A map is transverse to MM when, for every y∈f−1(M)y\in f^{-1}(M),

dfy(TyY)+Tf(y)M=Tf(y)X. d f_y(T_yY)+T_{f(y)}M=T_{f(y)}X.

Equivalently, the map from the pulled-back target conormal space to Ty*YT_y^*Y is injective. In adapted target coordinates (u,z)(u,z), transversality says that u∘fu\circ f is a submersion at its zeros. The submersion theorem then makes N=f−1(M)N=f^{-1}(M) a submanifold and identifies its tangent space with ker⁡d(u∘f)\ker d(u\circ f). Thus a transverse map is clean and its normal differential is an isomorphism. None of these assertions supposes that ff is itself a submersion.

SH02-NG-CLEAN-EMBEDDING — Clean embedding estimates

Deformation embedding theorem. If ff is clean with respect to MM, the natural map

Φ=(pY,f̃):𝒟NY⟶Y×X𝒟MX \Phi=(p_Y,\widetilde f):\mathcal D_NY \longrightarrow Y\times_X\mathcal D_MX

is a closed embedding. If ff is transverse to MM, it is an isomorphism, so the square formed by the two pp maps is Cartesian.

Proof. Away from t=0t=0, Φ\Phi is the obvious identification of both spaces with Y×ℝ×Y\times\mathbb R^\times. At the central fibre it is the fibrewise injective normal differential, so it is injective everywhere. A proof of closedness must also prevent normal vectors from escaping to infinity while their images converge.

Fix y0∈Ny_0\in N. In adapted source and target coordinates write f=(a,b)f=(a,b) with N={w=0}N=\{w=0\} and M={u=0}M=\{u=0\}. Locally there is a smooth matrix A(w,z)A(w,z) such that

a(w,z)=A(w,z)w,A(0,z)=Dwa(0,z). a(w,z)=A(w,z)w, \qquad A(0,z)=D_w a(0,z).

The integral formula constructs AA. Cleanliness makes A(0,z0)A(0,z_0) injective. After shrinking the chart it has a smooth left inverse B(w,z)B(w,z); a bundle metric gives the explicit choice B=(A*A)−1A*B=(A^*A)^{-1}A^*. In analytic charts, choosing an invertible minor instead gives an analytic left inverse.

Write a point of the target fibre product locally as (y;vX,zX,t)(y;v_X,z_X,t), so

a(y)=tvX,b(y)=zX. a(y)=t v_X,\qquad b(y)=z_X.

If it comes from a noncentral point of the source deformation, its source normal coordinate is forced to be

vY=w(y)t=B(y)vX. v_Y=\frac{w(y)}{t}=B(y)v_X.

The right-hand side is defined even at t=0t=0. On the image of the central fibre it recovers the source vector because B(0,z)A(0,z)=1B(0,z)A(0,z)=1. Together with the tangential coordinate z(y)z(y) and tt, it supplies a smooth inverse on the image, and a smooth left inverse from an ambient coordinate neighborhood. Thus Φ\Phi is an embedding locally near every central source point.

Now suppose a sequence in the image converges in the fibre product to (y;vX,zX,0)(y;v_X,z_X,0). Necessarily f(y)∈Mf(y)\in M, hence y∈Ny\in N. Choose the preceding charts at yy. The corresponding source vectors are B(yn)vX,nB(y_n)v_{X,n}, including for central members of the sequence, so they converge. Their source tangential coordinates and parameters also converge. Continuity of Φ\Phi identifies the limit’s image with the prescribed target limit. Therefore the image is sequentially closed. The fibre product is a subspace of a manifold and is first countable, so the image is closed. This proves the first assertion without a hidden properness assumption on ff.

The left inverse provides the exact control needed in this limit argument: shrink to a relatively compact source coordinate neighborhood of the limiting base point. The operator norm of the matrix defining the left inverse is bounded there, so bounded target normal coordinates force bounded source normal coordinates. The displayed inverse formula then gives convergence, not merely a convergent subsequence. Thus cleanliness controls the normal directions even though the map on base manifolds need not be proper.

If ff is transverse, AA is square and invertible near NN. Every central point of the fibre product is then in the image, and all noncentral points already were. The same local formula with B=A−1B=A^{-1} is a smooth or analytic inverse. This proves the Cartesian assertion. ▫\square

As a consequence, if ff is clean and proper, f̃\widetilde f is proper: factor it as the closed embedding Φ\Phi followed by the base change of the proper map ff. More generally, this implication can be applied on suitable closed support sets once the relevant normal-cone properness hypotheses are verified; those support hypotheses are part of the specialization unit and are not omitted here.

SH02-NG-CLEAN-PAIRS — Clean pairs and zero completion

For maps f1:Y1→Xf_1:Y_1\to X and f2:Y2→Xf_2:Y_2\to X, define cleanliness or transversality by applying the preceding definitions to (f1,f2):Y1×Y2→X×X(f_1,f_2):Y_1\times Y_2\to X\times X and its diagonal. With Q=Y1×XY2Q=Y_1\times_XY_2, the clean condition says that QQ is a submanifold and

T(y1,y2)Q=ker⁡(df1−df2:Ty1Y1⊕Ty2Y2→TxX). T_{(y_1,y_2)}Q =\ker\bigl(d f_1-d f_2:T_{y_1}Y_1\oplus T_{y_2}Y_2\to T_xX\bigr).

The transverse condition says that this difference map is surjective. These descriptions follow from the normal identification [(a,b)]↦a−b[(a,b)]\mapsto a-b for the diagonal, so they also record the sign convention.

If f2f_2 is the inclusion of an embedded submanifold Y2⊂XY_2\subset X, projection to Y1Y_1 identifies QQ with f1−1(Y2)f_1^{-1}(Y_2). Quotienting the difference map by TY2TY_2 gives df1d f_1 modulo TY2TY_2. Its kernel equality, or its surjectivity, is precisely the corresponding condition for f1f_1 with respect to Y2Y_2. Thus the pair definitions recover the earlier ones.

SH02-NG-PARAMETER-ORIENTATION — The parameter determines a relative orientation

Local identifier: SH02-NG-PARAMETER-ORIENTATION

The map pp can be singular, but its relative determinant line still makes sense:

ℒp=det⁡T*𝒟MX⊗p*(det⁡T*X)−1. \mathcal L_p=\det T^*\mathcal D_MX\otimes p^*(\det T^*X)^{-1}.

It has rank one. With the convention that the base volume precedes the relative factor, the expression t−rdtt^{-r}dt on t≠0t\ne0 defines a nowhere-zero smooth section of ℒp\mathcal L_p that extends uniquely across t=0t=0.

To see this, in the deformation coordinates use the generator

ηU=(dv1∧⋯∧dvr∧dz1∧⋯∧dzn−r∧dt)⊗(du1∧⋯∧dur∧dz1∧⋯∧dzn−r)−1. \eta_U= (dv_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dv_r\wedge dz_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n-r}\wedge dt) \otimes (du_1\wedge\cdots\wedge du_r\wedge dz_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n-r})^{-1}.

On t≠0t\ne0, wedging the pulled-back base volume with dt/trdt/t^r gives the numerator, because all terms containing an extra dtdt vanish and the remaining Jacobian factor is trt^r. Hence ηU\eta_U is the desired extension. On overlap, both such generators equal the same intrinsic expression dt/trdt/t^r on the dense noncentral set, so they agree everywhere. Each is nonzero at the central fibre as well.

Passing from a nonzero real determinant section to its orientation gives a canonical trivialization

or⁡𝒟MX⊗p−1or⁡X−1≃k𝒟MX. \operatorname{or}_{\mathcal D_MX}\otimes p^{-1}\operatorname{or}_X^{-1}\simeq k_{\mathcal D_MX}.

Thus the relative orientation of the whole deformation map is canonically trivial, even when XX and MM are not oriented. On t>0t>0, this agrees with the orientation of increasing tt, since tr>0t^r>0. On t<0t<0, the factor t−rt^{-r} records the parity that an unqualified use of dtdt would lose.

The sheaf-theoretic consequence requires the manifold duality formula for arbitrary smooth maps, including singular maps:

p!kX≃or⁡𝒟MX⊗p−1or⁡X−1[dim⁡𝒟MX−dim⁡X]. p^!k_X\simeq \operatorname{or}_{\mathcal D_MX}\otimes p^{-1}\operatorname{or}_X^{-1} [\dim\mathcal D_MX-\dim X].

With that explicit import the relative dualizing complex is k𝒟MX[1]k_{\mathcal D_MX}[1]. The import follows from p!ωX≃ω𝒟MXp^!\omega_X\simeq\omega_{\mathcal D_MX} and compatibility with tensoring by invertible local systems; a submersion-only formula would not suffice for pp. The determinant proof above is independent of this sheaf-theoretic import.

For the sheaf comparison, the general manifold formula is recorded in Schapira, A short review on microlocal sheaf theory, 19 January 2016, §2.2, pp. 6–7. Here is the reduction that transfers it to a possibly singular map. On a target chart trivializing its orientation local system, the target dualizing complex is the constant coefficient sheaf shifted by the target dimension. Composition of extraordinary inverse images carries this dualizing complex to the dualizing complex of the source. Commutation with shifts gives the required dimension difference. On the overlap of two such charts, changing the orientation generator multiplies it by the orientation transition sign. The extraordinary inverse image is linear over the coefficient ring, so it carries that transition to its pullback on the source. The local identifications therefore glue with the inverse target orientation twist in the displayed formula. The global determinant section fixes the remaining sign by the prescribed base-before-parameter convention. SH02-NG-CONE-CONSEQUENCES — Consequences of normal-cone geometry

The sequence criterion gives the following useful identities:

τ(CM(S))=M∩S¯,CM(S)=CM(S¯), \tau(C_M(S))=M\cap\overline S, \qquad C_M(S)=C_M(\overline S),

CM(S∪T)=CM(S)∪CM(T),C(S2,S1)=−C(S1,S2). C_M(S\cup T)=C_M(S)\cup C_M(T), \qquad C(S_2,S_1)=-C(S_1,S_2).

For the first equality, one inclusion follows from continuity of pp. In the other direction, take a sequence in SS converging to m∈Mm\in M and use the zero-vector construction in the proof above. Thus every point of M∩S¯M\cap\overline S actually contributes its zero vector. For closure invariance, p+p_+ is open, so

p+−1(S)¯∩Ω=p+−1(S¯). \overline{p_+^{-1}(S)}\cap\Omega =p_+^{-1}(\overline S).

Taking the closure in the whole deformation proves the identity. The finite-union formula follows because closure commutes with finite unions, and exchange of the pair factors negates the difference in the sequence criterion. The finite-union qualification matters: a countable union may have new limiting directions.

If O⊂XO\subset X is open, deformation charts identify p−1(O)p^{-1}(O) with 𝒟M∩OO\mathcal D_{M\cap O}O. It follows that

CM(S)|M∩O=CM∩O(S∩O). C_M(S)|_{M\cap O}=C_{M\cap O}(S\cap O).

This is the precise locality that permits the locally closed version of the construction. For the quotient map q:TX|M→NMXq:TX|_M\to N_MX one also has

C(S,M)=q−1CM(S). C(S,M)=q^{-1}C_M(S).

Indeed, the normal component of a scaled difference from a point of MM gives one inclusion. For the reverse inclusion, represent (m;a)(m;a) in the normal cone by xn=(un,zn)x_n=(u_n,z_n) and cn→∞c_n\to\infty. Given any tangential component bb, put yn=(0,zn−b/cn)∈My_n=(0,z_n-b/c_n)\in M for sufficiently large nn. Then cn(xn−yn)→(a,b)c_n(x_n-y_n)\to(a,b). The comparison with the sources preserves a useful difference in scope. The normal-cone definition in the 2016 review initially assumes a locally closed subset, whereas the pair-cone definition in Astérisque §1.2.1 is given for arbitrary subsets. The arguments here need only closure of the positive lift and sequential convergence in a manifold. Closure invariance and the zero-vector rescaling above therefore prove the asserted arbitrary-subset version directly. No stratification or regularity of the subset has been inserted.

An asymmetric limiting set. In X=ℝ2X=\mathbb R^2, deform along the horizontal axis M={y=0}M=\{y=0\}. Let

S={(x,y):y=x4,x≠0}. S=\{(x,y):y=x^4,\ x\ne0\}.

At the origin the normal cone is the nonnegative vertical half-line. Positivity follows because every scaled normal coordinate is cnxn4≥0c_nx_n^4\ge0. Given a>0a>0, take any sequence xn→0x_n\to0, xn≠0x_n\ne0, and set cn=a/xn4c_n=a/x_n^4; the resulting normal limit is aa. The zero vector occurs by slower rescaling. This illustrates why the cone’s base is M∩S¯M\cap\overline S, rather than an assumed constant base.

A proper map whose deformation is not proper. Let f:ℝ→ℝf:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R be f(y)=y3f(y)=y^3, and take N=M={0}N=M=\{0\}. The map ff is proper. In deformation coordinates,

f̃(v,t)=(t2v3,t). \widetilde f(v,t)=(t^2v^3,t).

Moreover the target fibre product contains central pairs (0;u,0)(0;u,0) for every uu, whereas Φ\Phi hits only u=0u=0. A clean map that is not transverse. Let X=ℝ3X=\mathbb R^3, MM the xx-axis, and let YY be the xyxy-plane with its inclusion into XX. The inverse image of MM is that axis. Its normal line in YY maps injectively to the two-dimensional normal space of MM in XX, so the inclusion is clean. It is not transverse because the image normal space misses the zz direction. The deformation embedding identifies a proper normal subbundle on the central fibre.

SH02-NG-SOLVED-EXERCISES — Solved geometric checks

Exercise A: extreme cones. Determine CM(M)C_M(M) and CM(X)C_M(X), and determine C(S2,S1)C(S_2,S_1) from C(S1,S2)C(S_1,S_2).

Solution. The positive lift of MM has v=0v=0 in every deformation chart, so its central closure is the zero section. Every central vector is the limit of (v,z,tn)(v,z,t_n) with tn>0t_n>0, so CM(X)=EC_M(X)=E. Exchanging the sequences in the pair criterion negates their scaled differences. Therefore C(S2,S1)C(S_2,S_1) is the antipodal image of C(S1,S2)C(S_1,S_2).

Exercise B: clean intersections. Suppose S⊂XS\subset X is a closed embedded submanifold clean with respect to MM, and put N=S∩MN=S\cap M. Show that CM(S)C_M(S) is the image of NNS→NMXN_NS\to N_MX.

Solution. Apply the clean deformation embedding theorem to S↪XS\hookrightarrow X. Since the inclusion is closed, the induced map of deformations is also closed: its factorization is a closed embedding followed by the base change of that closed inclusion. Its image contains the positive lift of SS. Conversely every central source point is approached by positive source points, so the image of its central fibre lies in the closure of the positive lift. Any point in that closure lies in the closed image; if its parameter is zero, its preimage has parameter zero. The two inclusions give the result. The image is a vector subbundle over NN, which explains the linearity in the clean case; arbitrary normal cones need not be linear or convex.

Exercise C: the order of a pair matters. At zero in ℝ\mathbb R, compute C([0,∞),{0})C([0,\infty),\{0\}) and C({0},[0,∞))C(\{0\},[0,\infty)).

Solution. In the first pair every difference is nonnegative, and every nonnegative vector is attained by choosing a positive point of size v/cnv/c_n with cn→∞c_n\to\infty. Thus the first cone is [0,∞)[0,\infty) at the base zero; exchange gives (−∞,0](-\infty,0]. The zero vector belongs to both. This sign check is useful before translating any pair-cone statement into a conormal statement.

Exercise D: orientation across the central fibre. For M={0}⊂ℝM=\{0\}\subset\mathbb R, explain how dt/tdt/t can extend as a relative determinant section although it does not extend as an ordinary one-form.

Solution. The deformation is ℝv×ℝt\mathbb R_v\times\mathbb R_t with p(v,t)=tvp(v,t)=tv. The section is (dv∧dt)⊗(dx)−1(dv\wedge dt)\otimes(dx)^{-1} in the determinant ratio. On t≠0t\ne0, the equation dp∧(dt/t)=dv∧dtdp\wedge(dt/t)=dv\wedge dt identifies it with dt/tdt/t along the fibres of pp. The ratio section remains nonzero at t=0t=0; there is no assertion that the ordinary differential form dt/tdt/t extends on the total space. Replacing it by dtdt in the ratio would introduce the vanishing factor tt.

SH02-NG-RESEARCH-ROUTES — Where this geometry is used

Local identifier: SH02-NG-RESEARCH-ROUTES

Specialization uses the positive chamber, central restriction, and the connected-fibre neighborhood refinement to turn sections near normal directions into sections on suitable open subsets of XX. Its proper direct-image theorem also needs control of normal cones of supports; properness of ff alone fails, as the cubic example shows. Microlocalization then applies the Fourier–Sato transform to the normal bundle, so the signed difference convention and positive rather than signed conicity must remain consistent.

For further geometric work, compare a deformation retaining all of EE with a sphere or projective modification retaining only directions. The zero-vector neighborhood argument identifies exactly the information such a directional quotient omits. Another useful route is to study families of clean intersections and track the normal differential as its rank drops; the loss of injectivity gives an explicit place where deformation properness can fail.

The source comparison is mathematical as well as notational. Astérisque §2.2.1 supplies the coordinate gluing, scaling action, and normal-cone avoidance neighborhoods; its sheaf section formula is not used as a proof of the geometric neighborhood refinement. The four openness checks in SH02-NG-INTERVAL-NEIGHBORHOODS supply that refinement, including zero normal vectors. The punctured quotient and the clean-map left-inverse argument remain separate complete geometric arguments under the differential-topology prerequisites. The arbitrary-map duality formula remains a sheaf-theoretic import, explicitly separated from the determinant computation.
