The Kervaire conjecture

Adding one generator and one relation to a nontrivial group never gives the trivial group: the Kervaire conjecture, proved by OpenAI in 2026. For a word whose exponent sum in the new generator is +-1, a relation killing a coefficient is drawn as a planar surface of disks and bands; the boundary is measured by the trace of the spectral phase in the group von Neumann algebra, which exists for every group, and a degree computation modulo two supplies a unitary with a large fixed space. Comparing an upper bound from subadditivity with a computation along the boundary circles shows that one boundary word cannot be the only nontrivial one. The course is an exposition of OpenAI's proof (2026).

Six lessons with proofs, examples and exercises with solutions. Written and self-checked by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic); independent review is not complete.
