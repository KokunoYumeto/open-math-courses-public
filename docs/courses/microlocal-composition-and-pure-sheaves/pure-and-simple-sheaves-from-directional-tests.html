Microlocal composition and pure sheaves

Pure and simple sheaves from directional tests

A constant sheaf on a submanifold can have a nonzero microlocal shift even when its ordinary stalk lies in degree zero. The normalization compares a local half-space test with three tangent Lagrangian planes. Once the test’s Morse index is removed, the remaining coefficient complex is independent of the test function. Purity means that this normalized complex has one cohomological degree; simplicity also specifies its coefficient module.

Use Composing hypersurface kernels with their shifts and When a kernel quantizes a contact transformation. Throughout, XX is a finite-dimensional smooth real manifold, n=dim⁡Xn=\dim X, and kk is a commutative ring of finite global dimension. Inputs are in Db(kX)D^b(k_X); coefficient modules need not be finitely generated. We use the exact conormal coefficient-object model, bounded support triangles, and the microlocal half-space test identification stated below. Normal forms and the shift of a submanifold transform binds the exact written parameter Morse proof, and Local existence of contact kernel equivalences proves simultaneous normalization of finitely many smooth conic Lagrangians and transverse auxiliary planes by one hypersurface contact chart. The zero-covector conormal geometry and the conormal index calculation are proved below. The ordered index proof proves alternation, the radical formula, parity, the four-plane cocycle and constant-intersection continuity. The common-pair reduction proof proves the isotropic reduction used below.

Type, purity and simplicity

Choose a number dd satisfying

d≡12dim⁡(V∩TpΛ)(mod⁡ℤ).(13) d\equiv \frac12\dim(V\cap T_p\Lambda)\pmod{\mathbb Z}. \qquad\text{(13)}

We say that FF has type LL with shift dd at pp along Λ\Lambda when

Cφ(F)[−d+n/2+τφ/2]≃Lin Db(k).(14) C_\varphi(F)\bigl[-d+n/2+\tau_\varphi/2\bigr]\simeq L \quad\text{in }D^b(k). \qquad\text{(14)}

The exponent in (14) is an integer by (13) and the parity rule. Test independence makes the definition unambiguous. Although dd may be a half-integer, (14) does not introduce a half-integer shift functor on complexes.

The sheaf is pure at pp if this type can be chosen concentrated in degree zero. It is simple there if, additionally, its type is a free kk-module of rank one. The condition is about kk, rather than a field chosen later. Pure coefficients can have torsion and arbitrary rank. A zero coefficient complex is permitted by the definition of type and purity; simplicity excludes it.

There are two useful shift rules. If FF has type LL with shift dd, then

F has type L[−b] with shift d+b,F[b] has type L with shift d+b(b∈ℤ).(15) F\text{ has type }L[-b]\text{ with shift }d+b, \qquad F[b]\text{ has type }L\text{ with shift }d+b \quad(b\in\mathbb Z). \qquad\text{(15)}

Both follow by substituting into (14). In particular one must specify the shift when naming a type complex; transferring an integer between dd and LL changes its displayed degree.

For the conormal model F=QM[s]F=Q_M[s], equations (5)–(6) give

type at shift d=Q[s+c/2−d].(16) \text{type at shift }d=Q[s+c/2-d]. \qquad\text{(16)}

Thus kMk_M is simple with shift c/2c/2. More generally QM[s]Q_M[s] has type QQ with shift s+c/2s+c/2. This remains true for a zero conormal covector when the stated local conormal assumptions hold.

For a smooth boundary h=0h=0 at its positive covector dhdh, the closed upper-side sheaf k{h≥0}k_{\{h\geq0\}} is isomorphic in the point-localized category to k{h=0}k_{\{h=0\}}. The boundary triangle gives k{h≥0}≃k{h<0}[1]k_{\{h\geq0\}}\simeq k_{\{h<0\}}[1] there: the whole constant sheaf in that triangle is null at a nonzero covector. Therefore the closed upper side is simple with shift 1/21/2, while the open lower side is simple with shift −1/2-1/2. They refer to the same positive boundary covector, despite lying on opposite sides in the base.

d′=d−12(n−1)−12τ(V,TpΛ,V′).(17) d'=d-\frac12(n-1)-\frac12\tau(V,T_p\Lambda,V'). \qquad\text{(17)}

Proof. Select a transverse test allowed in (9). Substitute j=−d+n/2j=-d+n/2. Its left side is LL by (14), while its right side has exponent −d+n/2+τψ/2+e=−d′+n/2+τψ/2-d+n/2+\tau_\psi/2+e=-d'+n/2+\tau_\psi/2. That is precisely (14) for TFTF with shift d′d'. Independence of the test proves the conclusion for every allowed transverse test. The transported test exponent is integral, so (17) has the required shift parity for the transformed tangent conormal rank. ▫\square

This statement concerns the unshifted hypersurface kernel kSk_S. Shifting the kernel by [b][b] adds bb to the transform’s shift by (15). Nor can the three-plane index be dropped solely because the contact transformation is a local diffeomorphism: the two vertical planes generally differ.

The coefficient type is locally constant up to integer shift

Suppose SS⁡(F)∩U⊂Λ\operatorname{SS}(F)\cap U\subset\Lambda on an open cotangent region UU. Fix L∈Db(k)L\in D^b(k), and allow the shift in its type to vary. Then

{p∈Λ∩U:F is of type L with some allowed shift at p}(18) \{p\in\Lambda\cap U:F\text{ is of type }L \text{ with some allowed shift at }p\} \qquad\text{(18)}

is open and closed in Λ∩U\Lambda\cap U.

Proof. Work near any chosen point. A local hypersurface contact normal form takes Λ\Lambda to a conormal. The transformed sheaf has the coefficient model QMQ_M at the selected point. An isomorphism in a point-localized category is represented by a finite collection of denominator arrows. Each cone avoids that point; after shrinking a cotangent neighborhood, each cone avoids the entire neighborhood. Thus the same model holds on that neighborhood. Near a zero covector use the ordinary local conormal model directly.

In the conormal chart, (16) says that the possible type complexes are exactly the integer shifts of the fixed QQ. Membership in the set (18) is therefore constant throughout the chart. Formula (17) preserves the coefficient type while adjusting the allowed shift; its inverse gives the converse implication. Every point consequently has a neighborhood wholly in (18) or wholly in its complement. Both sets are open, proving the assertion. This does not say that a numerical shift is constant across a varying projection rank. ▫\square

Locally there is also a useful object decomposition. If FF has type LL, choose a simple object GG at the point so that

F≃LX⊗LGin Db(kX;p).(19) F\simeq L_X\otimes^L G \quad\text{in }D^b(k_X;p). \qquad\text{(19)}

To construct it, take the conormal chart just used. Its coefficient model for FF is QMQ_M; if its normalized type is LL, (16) identifies QQ with an integral shift of LL. Take the correspondingly shifted kMk_M and carry it back by the inverse contact kernel. It is simple by (17). The constant-coefficient projection formula makes the inverse transform commute with tensoring by the arbitrary bounded LL, yielding (19). No choice of a global simple generator, preferred orientation line or full categorical equivalence of coefficient morphisms is asserted by this local object construction.

Exercises with complete solutions

Two transverse tests of the same plane

Difficulty: Introductory.

Let MM be two-dimensional in a three-dimensional XX, and let F=kMF=k_M. A test restricts to a12+a22a_1^2+a_2^2 on MM; another restricts to a12−a22a_1^2-a_2^2. Compute their raw complexes, indices and normalized types at shift d=1/2d=1/2.

Solution. The negative dimensions are zero and one. The raw complexes are kk and k[−1]k[-1]. Formula (6) gives indices −2-2 and zero. The exponent in (14) is respectively −1/2+3/2−1=0-1/2+3/2-1=0 and −1/2+3/2=1-1/2+3/2=1. Both normalized types are kk. Omitting the inertia term would give two different degrees for the same conormal sheaf.

A half-integer is not a shift functor

Difficulty: Introductory.

At a point with n=4n=4 and dim⁡(V∩TpΛ)=1\dim(V\cap T_p\Lambda)=1, an allowed shift is d=3/2d=3/2. Which parity must τφ\tau_\varphi have, and why is the exponent of (14) integral?

Solution. Its parity is n+1=5n+1=5, hence it is odd. The exponent is −3/2+2+τφ/2=(1+τφ)/2-3/2+2+\tau_\varphi/2=(1+\tau_\varphi)/2, an integer. Only this exponent acts on the complex. The number dd is part of the geometric normalization.

Purity over an integral coefficient ring

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Take k=ℤk=\mathbb Z, a codimension-two submanifold MM, and F=(ℤ/2)MF=(\mathbb Z/2)_M. Is FF pure or simple? What about ℤM⊕ℤM[1]\mathbb Z_M\oplus\mathbb Z_M[1]?

Solution. Formula (16) gives type ℤ/2\mathbb Z/2 with shift one, so the first sheaf is pure. Its type is not a free rank-one ℤ\mathbb Z-module, so it is not simple; moving to a residue field would change the coefficient problem. The second sheaf has two nonzero normalized cohomology degrees for every allowed integral change of shift. It is not pure.

Keep the kernel’s own degree

Difficulty: Intermediate.

Suppose n=3n=3, the ordered index in (17) is zero, and FF is simple with shift d=2d=2. Find the shift of its transform by kSk_S, and then by kS[2]k_S[2].

Solution. The unshifted kernel gives d′=2−(3−1)/2=1d'=2-(3-1)/2=1. Shifting the kernel by two shifts the entire transform by two, so its shift becomes three. The coefficient type remains kk in both cases.

The two sides at one positive covector

Difficulty: Intermediate.

At (0;dx)(0;dx) on ℝ\mathbb R, compare the simple shifts of k[0,∞)k_{[0,\infty)}, k{0}k_{\{0\}} and k(−∞,0)k_{(-\infty,0)}. Explain the degree in their localized relation.

Solution. The point sheaf and the closed positive half-line have shift 1/21/2. The open negative half-line has shift −1/2-1/2. The triangle k(−∞,0)→kℝ→k[0,∞)→+1k_{(-\infty,0)}\to k_\mathbb R\to k_{[0,\infty)}\xrightarrow{+1} has null middle term at the positive covector, hence k[0,∞)≃k(−∞,0)[1]k_{[0,\infty)}\simeq k_{(-\infty,0)}[1]. The difference of their simple shifts is exactly one. The open positive half-line instead is null at that covector and is not the object used here.

An object model does not compute every morphism

Difficulty: Advanced.

Does (19) alone prove that A↦AX⊗LGA\mapsto A_X\otimes^L G is fully faithful from Db(k)D^b(k) to Db(kX;p)D^b(k_X;p)? Specify the additional comparison needed.

Solution. No. An object decomposition supplies no computation of arbitrary derived morphisms between its coefficient factors. Full faithfulness requires the natural isomorphisms Hom⁡Db(k)(A,B[j])≃Hom⁡Db(kX;p)(AX⊗LG,BX⊗LG[j])\operatorname{Hom}_{D^b(k)}(A,B[j])\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{D^b(k_X;p)}(A_X\otimes^L G,B_X\otimes^L G[j]) for all bounded A,BA,B and all integers jj, compatible with identity and composition. Neither the existence of GG nor (19) states these comparisons. The coefficient-equivalence lesson treats that morphism normalization as an explicit separate prerequisite.

A test must meet the chosen covector

Difficulty: Advanced.

For the conormal of M={b=0}⊂ℝa×ℝbM=\{b=0\}\subset\mathbb R_a\times\mathbb R_b, fix p=(0;db)p=(0;db). Compare φ=b+a2\varphi=b+a^2, ψ=b−a2\psi=b-a^2, and h=bh=b. Which are transverse tests, and what fails for hh?

Solution. All three vanish at zero and have differential dbdb. The restrictions of the first two to MM have nonzero Hessians, so both are transverse. Their raw complexes are kk and k[−1]k[-1], and their indices are −1-1 and one. At simple shift 1/21/2 their corrected types are both kk. The restriction of hh is identically zero; its Hessian is zero, the graph and conormal tangents have a common nonzero aa-direction, and the transversality in (1) fails. Matching only the value and differential is insufficient.

Zero covectors retain a curved base submanifold

Difficulty: Intermediate.

On T*ℝ2T^*\mathbb R^2, with coordinates (a,b;α,β)(a,b;\alpha,\beta), inspect Λ={b=a2,α=−2aβ}\Lambda=\{b=a^2,\ \alpha=-2a\beta\}. Verify smoothness, conicity and the Lagrangian property, identify its zero-fibre base germ, and compare with the positive half of a cotangent fibre at its zero endpoint.

Solution. The free coordinates (a,β)(a,\beta) parameterize Λ\Lambda; projection to them is its smooth inverse, so the image is an embedded two-dimensional manifold. Positive covector dilation multiplies both α\alpha and β\beta and preserves its equations. Its canonical form pulls back to (−2aβ)da+βd(a2)=0(-2a\beta)\,da+\beta\,d(a^2)=0. Its symplectic form therefore vanishes, and its dimension is half the ambient dimension, so it is Lagrangian. Its zero covectors are exactly those with β=α=0\beta=\alpha=0, over the parabola M={b=a2}M=\{b=a^2\}; the full equations are TM*ℝ2T_M^*\mathbb R^2. The zero-covector theorem keeps this curved base rather than replacing it by a point or the entire zero section. By contrast {x=0,ξ≥0}⊂T*ℝ\{x=0,\xi\geq0\}\subset T^*\mathbb R has a boundary at its zero endpoint. It is not a smooth submanifold without boundary there, so it does not satisfy the theorem’s hypothesis and cannot be used to infer a full two-sided conormal germ.
