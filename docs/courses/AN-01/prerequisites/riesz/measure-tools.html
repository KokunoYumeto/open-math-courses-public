Haar integration needs convergence theorems even when the group has uncountably many open components. It also needs a precise meaning for a tensor product of Hilbert spaces. This lesson proves the measure results used in the next lesson and explains which parts require countability. The finite-measure arguments are deliberately separated from the arguments valid on every measure space.

The earlier programme lesson Hilbert spaces and compact operators, Theorems 2.1–2.3, 4.1 and 8.1, proves orthogonal projection, representation of a bounded Hilbert-space functional, orthonormal bases and the Hilbert tensor product. Those complete proofs are the Hilbert-space prerequisites below. Elementary set theory, including the axiom of choice, is assumed. No topology is needed until the following Haar lesson. The core course Measure and Integration uses Fremlin’s human text. The core Real Analysis I and Real Analysis II use J. Lebl’s open Basic Analysis. Our Lemma 6.1 supplies the one-variable integral identities used here. Basic references for measure theory are [Fremlin] and [Lebesgue].
