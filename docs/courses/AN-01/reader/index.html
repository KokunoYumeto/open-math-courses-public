The reader includes editable sources, reproducible illustrations and the prerequisite readings used by its proofs. Gaussian rescaling, both tangent directions of fiber normals, affine sections with closure after projection, convex propagation within a fiber, analytic boundary regularity at all normal frequencies, and smooth-data counterexamples are proved with worked geometric examples. The Fourier–Laplace range for every compact convex real carrier is constructed by Laplace contours, moment recovery and rotated boxes; examples explain infinite point derivatives and nonconvex-carrier limitations. Gaussian reconstruction supplies global derivative-growth representatives modulo analytic functions and the global regular-difference characterization of wavefront sets. Smooth extension at every finite order, square-variable descent with parameters, coordinate parity, exchange invariants and smooth flat majorants are constructed explicitly. Exact distributional extension orders are determined for powers with inverse-power oscillation, and a real phase giving an order-one extension is constructed for every continuously differentiable amplitude. Concentration and oscillatory limits retain exact point masses, periodic coefficients, radial center contributions and the dependence on two boundary parameters; a uniform gradient bound turns distributional convergence into local uniform convergence. Products and singular limits include coordinate principal values, quartic finite parts throughout a complex half-plane, logarithmic concentration on crossing axes, and exact overlap laws for moving Cauchy kernels. Primitives on intervals give exact derivative-order bounds, finite-measure endpoint extensions and weighted Taylor subtractions; exponential phases have the sharp extension threshold including equality. Reciprocal compression distinguishes principal values from concentrated mass; finite-difference moments represent every pole derivative, and smooth boundary coefficients determine all surviving point jets. Reflected endpoints produce explicit point derivatives; finite-order zeros permit smooth division on intervals, with a complete description of the kernel and sharp order bounds. Degenerate differential equations are classified by their admissible exponential branches, finite point-jet inverses and freely prescribed constants between isolated zeros. Coordinate-power kernels have arbitrary tangential distribution coefficients; simultaneous polynomial constraints reduce to finite jet equations. Directional uniqueness and parabolic boundary poles have sharp criteria and exact order bounds. Sublevel areas characterize planar concentration at nondegenerate zeros; logarithmic traces define cut boundary values, and polygonal logarithmic potentials converge to a circular curvature measure.
