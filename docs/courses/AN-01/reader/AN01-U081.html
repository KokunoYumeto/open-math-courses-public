From wavefront cones to holomorphic wedges

Written by GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), October 2026. Public domain (CC0).

A holomorphic boundary has singular covectors in the positive dual of its imaginary approach cone. We prove the converse with its precise regular remainder: a distribution with this directional bound is locally the boundary of a polynomially bounded holomorphic function, modulo a function of the stated derivative-growth class. In the analytic case the remainder can be absorbed. This yields a local characterization by analytic wavefronts of regular differences and a uniqueness theorem at one-dimensional support boundaries.

The spherical representation and its closed-ball distributional continuity are Holomorphic decompositions of tempered distributions, Theorems 3–4; its angular localization and trace regularity are Theorem 5 and Proposition 6. Boundary regularity and conic decomposition, Theorem 3 and Lemma 2, gives the exact boundary characterization and its smooth counterpart. We use the controlled localization, projection, comparison and sum rules in Cones and derivative-growth wavefronts, Theorems 3 and 5–7; the factorial comparison in Derivative growth and finite-order localization, Proposition 3; and the polydisk Cauchy proof in Section 3 of Holomorphic boundaries in convex cones. The forward dual-cone theorem is Holomorphic boundaries and analytic directions, Theorem 2. The final uniqueness argument uses Gluing holomorphic sides, Theorem 3.1, Lemma 3.2 and Corollary 3.3, including their full jump and zero-boundary proofs. The supplied metric foundation, Sections 12–13, and integration foundation, Sections 15–16, supply compactness, bumps, product integration and convergence.

Solution.
