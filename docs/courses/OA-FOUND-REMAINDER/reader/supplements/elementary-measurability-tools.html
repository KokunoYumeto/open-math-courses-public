1. Separable Hilbert space measurability

  3. fff is the pointwise norm limit of a sequence of measurable functions with finite range.

A separable metric space has a countable base of balls, with centres in a countable dense set and positive rational radii. Each open set is the union of a subfamily of that countable base. Its inverse image is therefore measurable, proving (2).

The basis theorem and Parseval identity are proved in Hilbert spaces and compact operators. The proof above contains the measurability argument rather than assuming a Pettis theorem.

2. The monotone class theorem for sets

An algebra of subsets of XXX contains XXX and is closed under complements and finite unions. A monotone class is closed under countable increasing unions and countable decreasing intersections. If A\mathcal AA is an algebra, the smallest monotone class C\mathcal CC containing A\mathcal AA is σ(A)\sigma(\mathcal A)σ(A).

Proof. The sigma algebra σ(A)\sigma(\mathcal A)σ(A) is a monotone class, so C⊆σ(A)\mathcal C\subseteq\sigma(\mathcal A)C⊆σ(A). The class D={B⊆X:Bc∈C} \mathcal D=\{B\subseteq X:B^c\in\mathcal C\} D={B⊆X:Bc∈C} is a monotone class: complementation interchanges increasing unions and decreasing intersections. It contains A\mathcal AA, so minimality gives C⊆D\mathcal C\subseteq\mathcal DC⊆D. Thus C\mathcal CC is closed under complements.

Fix A∈AA\in\mathcal AA∈A. The class {B⊆X:A∩B∈C}\{B\subseteq X:A\cap B\in\mathcal C\}{B⊆X:A∩B∈C} is a monotone class and contains A\mathcal AA, since the algebra is closed under finite intersections. It consequently contains C\mathcal CC. Now fix B∈CB\in\mathcal CB∈C. The class {A⊆X:A∩B∈C}\{A\subseteq X:A\cap B\in\mathcal C\}{A⊆X:A∩B∈C} is again a monotone class; the preceding conclusion says that it contains A\mathcal AA, so it contains C\mathcal CC. Therefore C\mathcal CC is closed under finite intersections, hence under finite unions by complements. In particular, if a class of measurable sets is monotone and contains an algebra generating the domain sigma algebra, it contains every measurable set. This is the exact set-class argument used to extend commutation from generating indicator multipliers to all indicator multipliers.

4. Interval and arc step functions

Finite linear combinations of bounded interval indicators are dense in L1(R)L^1(\mathbb R)L1(R) for Lebesgue measure. Finite linear combinations of arc indicators are dense in L1(T)L^1(\mathbb T)L1(T) for normalized Lebesgue measure.

Proof. Proposition 3.1(4) of Haar measure on locally compact groups proves that compactly supported continuous functions are dense in L1L^1L1 of a Radon measure. Lebesgue measure is Radon: the interval-cover construction gives open outer approximations with arbitrarily small excess measure, and, for a finite-measure set, first cutting off a small tail outside [−R,R][-R,R][−R,R] and then taking the complement of an open outer approximation to its complement inside [−R,R][-R,R][−R,R] gives a compact inner approximation. Open sets are countable disjoint unions of intervals, so their inner regularity also follows by retaining finitely many slightly shortened bounded intervals. The normalized circle measure is the image of Lebesgue measure on [0,1][0,1][0,1]; compact inner approximations pass to their compact images, and outer regularity follows by applying inner regularity to complements in this finite-measure compact space. For a compactly supported continuous ggg on R\mathbb RR, choose a bounded interval [−R,R][-R,R][−R,R] containing its support. On this interval ggg is uniformly continuous. Indeed, if uniform continuity failed, there would be pairs xn,ynx_n,y_nxn​,yn​ whose distance tends to zero but whose value difference is bounded away from zero; a convergent subsequence of xnx_nxn​, and hence of yny_nyn​ to the same limit, contradicts continuity. Partition [−R,R][-R,R][−R,R] into intervals of sufficiently small length and give the step function on each interval the value of ggg at one endpoint. The uniform approximation error on [−R,R][-R,R][−R,R] tends to zero, and both functions vanish outside it. Their L1L^1L1 distance is at most 2R2R2R times that error. Endpoint values affect no integral. This proves the first density assertion. For the circle, apply the same argument to the continuous periodic representative on [0,1][0,1][0,1]; the partition intervals become arcs, and the L1L^1L1 error is at most the uniform error. Density of continuous functions proves the second assertion. □\square□

For a translated interval indicator, the L1L^1L1 difference is the measure of its symmetric difference and is at most twice the translation distance; the same bound holds for arc indicators and sufficiently small circle distance. The density just proved and the fact that translation is an L1L^1L1 isometry then prove continuity of translation on every L1L^1L1 function. Reflection, translation and nonzero scalar dilation preserve or scale Lebesgue measure by the interval-cover construction. In the rational-translation proof, the two-variable map (t,x)↦(x−t,x)(t,x)\mapsto(x-t,x)(t,x)↦(x−t,x) preserves product Lebesgue measure: Tonelli integrates first in ttt, and reflection followed by translation changes that inner integral to integration in y=x−ty=x-ty=x−t. The same argument applies to normalized circle measure. The exact Tonelli and Fubini theorems, including their sigma-finite support condition, are proved in Sections 4 and 5 of the linked Haar-measure chapter; the real line and circle meet that condition.
