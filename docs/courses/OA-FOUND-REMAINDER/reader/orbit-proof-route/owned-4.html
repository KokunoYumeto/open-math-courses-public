The regular commutant in every covariant representation

The standard-form calculation in lesson 75 has the right answer for an arbitrary normal covariant representation, but changing representation is not a unitary change of the original Hilbert space. Finite-dimensional representations can even have different dimensions. We first show that two faithful normal representations become unitarily equivalent after one sufficiently large amplification. The standard commutant formula survives that amplification, and a rank-one slice removes the added Hilbert factor. A nonfaithful representation is then handled by its quotient algebra; this also exposes a faithfulness qualification in the source's following corollary.

A nonfaithful representation and the source qualification

If ρ\rhoρ is normal and unital but not faithful, its kernel is an ultraweakly closed central ideal zMzMzM. Covariance makes zzz invariant under α\alphaα. The action therefore descends to M/zMM/zMM/zM, which ρ\rhoρ represents faithfully as the concrete algebra ρ(M)\rho(M)ρ(M). Applying (E9) to this quotient proves the same displayed commutant identity for the original ρ\rhoρ, without a faithfulness condition. The abstract algebra represented by its regular crossed product is, however,

Pρ ≅ (M/zM)⋊G,(E10)P_\rho\ \cong\ (M/zM)\rtimes G, \tag{E10}Pρ​ ≅ (M/zM)⋊G,(E10)

by the faithful regular-model comparison of lesson 15. The two unitaries CCC and WWW from lesson 76 use only the implementation VVV and (E9); they now give

Pρ′ ≅ ρ(M)′⋊Ad⁡VG.(E11)P_\rho'\ \cong\ \rho(M)'\rtimes_{\operatorname{Ad}V}G. \tag{E11}Pρ′​ ≅ ρ(M)′⋊AdV​G.(E11)

For a faithful ρ\rhoρ, (E10) is M⋊GM\rtimes GM⋊G, so (E10)–(E11) recover both source isomorphisms of Corollary X.1.22(i). If “normal representation” is read literally to include a nonfaithful unital one, its first displayed isomorphism needs the quotient in (E10). Thus the first isomorphism requires either faithfulness or the quotient displayed in (E10).

Problem. Why is the summation in (E1) countable even when K2K_2K2​ is nonseparable?

Solution. It represents one fixed normal positive functional, for which Theorem 10.1 supplies a square-summable sequence; OA-MOD-SC-02 is an alternative. The cyclic decomposition of the entire representation may have an arbitrarily large index set III, and the later κ\kappaκ-amplification absorbs that index set. No countable family of functionals is chosen to distinguish all of MMM.
