Results on isometric immersions of surfaces, with their assumptions

The course proves the statements below; each link leads to the statement with its assumptions and its proof. The lessons are self-checked by the writing AI; independent mathematical review is not complete.

  • Curvature, Hessians and immersions under affine changes of coordinates — Lemma 1.2. For the pull-back g' = M^T (g o Phi) M of a metric by an affine map Phi(x) = c + Mx: the curvature is K o Phi, the covariant Hessian of z o Phi is M^T (H o Phi) M, |d(z o Phi)| = |dz| o Phi, and F o Phi is an isometric immersion if F is.
  • The height equation of an isometric immersion — Theorem 2.1. For an isometric immersion F of a plane domain with metric g into R^3 and a unit vector e, the height z = <F, e> satisfies Hess_g z = <n, e> II, |dz|^2 + <n, e>^2 = 1 and det Hess_g z = K det g (1 - |dz|^2).
  • Curvature of dX^2 + f^2 dY^2 — Lemma 3.1. The metric dX^2 + f(X, Y)^2 dY^2 with f > 0 has the curvature -f_XX / f.
  • A metric with a given small curvature — Proposition 3.2. For every smooth function K_0 on the square (-1, 1)^2 with |K_0| <= 1/1000 there is a smooth f with |f - 1| < 1/1000 such that dX^2 + f^2 dY^2 has the curvature K_0.
  • A boundary point with a second fundamental form of rank one — Proposition 4.2. If the curvature is negative in a round disk and zero on its boundary, and an isometric immersion of a neighbourhood is a graph over a plane, then at some point of the boundary circle the second fundamental form has rank one.
  • A symmetric form of rank one in a rotated frame — Lemma 2.1. For a symmetric bilinear form B of rank one on the plane and an orthonormal basis r_1, r_2 whose first line makes the angle alpha with the kernel, |alpha| < pi/2: B(r_2, r_2) is not zero and |B(r_1, r_2) / B(r_2, r_2)| = |tan alpha|.
  • Placement of the patches — Lemma 3.1. There are pairwise disjoint squares c_n + lambda_n R_n [-4, 4]^2, with rotations from a finite set, outside the closed disks D_j and inside the doubled disks, such that for every boundary point of every D_j and every rotation of the set, squares with that rotation lie in arbitrarily small disks around the point.
  • The assembled metric (assuming the patch obstruction) — Proposition 4.3. Assuming (P): the sum of the background metric and the pushed-forward perturbations is a smooth metric on (-1, 1)^2; its pull-back to each patch is a model metric plus a perturbation without admissible height; its curvature is negative in every disk D_j and zero on the boundary circles.
  • A smooth metric with no local isometric immersion (assuming the patch obstruction) — Theorem 5.1. Assuming (P): there is a smooth metric on (-1, 1)^2 such that no open neighbourhood of the origin has a smooth isometric immersion into R^3.
  • The Taylor series of the metric at the origin (assuming the patch obstruction) — Corollary 6.1. Assuming (P): the metric of Theorem 5.1 has the same derivatives of all orders at the origin as the Euclidean metric.
  • The curvature tensor of a metric on a plane domain — Lemma 2.1. In dimension two, R_kji^m = K (delta^m_k g_ji - delta^m_j g_ki), where R is formed from the Christoffel symbols and K is the curvature.
  • Commutation of third covariant derivatives of a function — Lemma 2.2. For a function z on a plane domain with a metric, the covariant derivative T of the covariant Hessian satisfies T_ijk - T_ikj = K (g_ik z_j - g_ij z_k).
  • The drift identity for a solution of the height equation — Theorem 3.1. For a solution z of the height equation with H_yy not zero, q = H_xy/H_yy, D = d_x - q d_y and G = E/H_yy^2: Dq - q Gamma^x_DD - Gamma^y_DD = K_y G + K rho_1 with an explicit rho_1, and Dq - P_{z_x} q - P_{z_y} = K_y G + K (rho_1 - rho_2).
  • The two leading terms of a high derivative of a composite function — Lemma 4.1. For l >= 3, the l-th derivative of F(y, p(y)) is sum F_{p_i} p_i^(l) + l sum (d/dy F_{p_i}) p_i^(l-1) plus terms in which all derivatives of p have order at most l - 2 and total order at most l.
  • The equation for a high derivative of a solution of the height equation — Theorem 5.1. For l >= 3, u = d_y^l z satisfies D^2 u + a u_yy + B Du + ((l + 1) K_y G + K rho) u_y = R_l with a = K G, B = -P_{z_x} - 2 l q_y and a remainder R_l in which all derivatives of z have order at most l.
  • The differentiated height equation in flow coordinates — Theorem 6.2. In coordinates t, s in which D = d_t, u = d_y^l z satisfies u_tt + A u_ss + B u_t + C u_s = R_l with A = G_1 K, G_1 = G sigma^2 > 0, and C = (l + 1) A_s + K (sigma rho + G sigma sigma_s - (l + 1) (G_1)_s).
