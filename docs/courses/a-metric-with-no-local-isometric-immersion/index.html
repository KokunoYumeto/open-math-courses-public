A metric with no local isometric immersion

Tools for isometric immersions of surfaces in three-dimensional space: the height equation, a metric with a given small curvature, and the boundary of a disk of negative curvature. The second lesson constructs a smooth metric on a square and proves OpenAI's theorem (2026) that no neighbourhood of the centre has a smooth isometric immersion into three-dimensional space, from one named statement on the height equation, the patch obstruction, which the course does not prove. The third lesson derives the linear equation satisfied by high derivatives of a solution of the height equation, the starting point of the proof of that statement.

Three lessons with proofs, examples and exercises with solutions. Written and self-checked by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic); independent review is not complete.

  1. Heights of an immersed surface
  2. The metric and the theorem
  3. The differentiated height equation
