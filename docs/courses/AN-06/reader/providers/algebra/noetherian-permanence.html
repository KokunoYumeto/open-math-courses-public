Noetherian polynomial rings and finite-type algebras

Written by GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI). Self-checked by the writing AI. Original exposition: CC0.

An ideal may contain infinitely many equations while being generated by finitely many of them. We prove that this finiteness survives adjoining finitely many variables, imposing relations and inverting elements. Rings in this reading are commutative and have a unit; the zero ring is allowed. Induction on natural numbers and ordinary set-theoretic choice are our foundational conventions.

Theorem. If every ideal of RRR has a finite generating set, every ideal of R[X]R[X]R[X], of every finite-type RRR-algebra and of every localization S−1RS^{-1}RS−1R has a finite generating set. In particular this holds in C[X1,…,Xn]\mathbb C[X_1,\ldots,X_n]C[X1​,…,Xn​] for every finite nnn.

1. Polynomial, quotient and fraction constructions

A polynomial is a finitely supported sequence of coefficients. Addition adds its entries and multiplication convolves them. Associativity follows by expanding a coefficient as the same finite sum over triples of indices; the other ring identities follow in the same way. Substitution into commuting ring elements respects these operations. In a domain, the top coefficient of a product of two nonzero polynomials is nonzero. Its degree is therefore the sum of their degrees. Applying this one variable at a time proves that a polynomial ring in finitely many variables over a domain is again a domain.

2. Finite generation, chains and two indices

For a1,…,am∈Ra_1,\ldots,a_m\in Ra1​,…,am​∈R, the ideal they generate consists of all sums ∑rjaj\sum r_ja_j∑rj​aj​. The empty list generates the zero ideal. Call RRR Noetherian when each ideal has such a finite list.

The union of an ascending sequence of ideals is an ideal: any two elements already occur together in some member of the sequence, where closure under addition and ring multiplication holds. If that union has finitely many generators, one member contains all of them, so every later member equals the union. Conversely, suppose an ideal has no finite generating list. Starting with the zero ideal, repeatedly select an element of the given ideal outside the ideal generated by the elements selected so far. Each selection is possible by the supposition and strictly enlarges the generated ideal. This contradicts stabilization of ascending sequences. Hence finite generation of all ideals is equivalent to the ascending chain condition.

5. Imposing relations and inverting elements

Let RRR be Noetherian. For an ideal JJJ in R/IR/IR/I, its inverse image under the quotient map is an ideal of RRR. A finite generating set for that inverse image maps to a generating set for JJJ: lift an element of JJJ, express its lift as a combination of generators, and apply the quotient map.
