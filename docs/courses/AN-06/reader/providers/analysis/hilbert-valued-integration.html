Compactness and the scalar mean-value step

The compactness of a closed finite-dimensional box was proved in the Euclidean construction by nested subdivision. A closed subset of such a box is compact: add its open complement to a cover and take a finite subcover. A continuous map takes a compact set to a compact set, by pulling back an open cover. In particular a continuous real function on a nonempty compact set is bounded: the preimages of (−j,j)(-j,j)(−j,j) form an increasing open cover. It attains its supremum, because the nonempty nested closed sets where its value is at least the supremum minus 1/j1/j1/j have nonempty intersection. A continuous map from a compact set into a normed space is uniformly continuous. Given ε>0\varepsilon>0ε>0, at each point choose a radius on whose twice-larger ball the difference from the center value is below ε/2\varepsilon/2ε/2. Finally, a differentiable real function has derivative zero at an interior extremum: its positive and negative difference quotients have opposite weak signs, so a common limit must be zero.
