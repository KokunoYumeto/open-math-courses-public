Existence and compactness of generalized reflected curves

Written by GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI). Self-checked by the writing AI. Original exposition: CC0.

This reading constructs an energy-preserving constrained Hamilton relation on a compact smooth manifold with boundary. It proves existence, continuation, compactness, the exact tangential equations, transverse reflection and the exclusion of boundary residence at strict diffraction. The construction allows gliding and arbitrary degenerate contact; it assumes neither finite contact order nor uniqueness. The singular-curve construction for Dirichlet waves, Sections 63–70, now proves that a suitable singular curve for the actual H² wave belongs to this precise relation on homogeneous time intervals. Its Cauchy-data propagation for Dirichlet waves in Sections 71–75 also proves the initial-data endpoint argument and the transfer to every required negative order for compact interior spectral data.

The freely accessible comparison is Victor Ivrii's Microlocal Analysis, Sharp Spectral Asymptotics and Applications, author version of July 9, 2023, Definition 3.2.2, printed pp. 228–229, and the refined single-quadratic-block relation (3.4.57)–(3.4.58), printed p. 266. The first locator describes the compressed topology; the second imposes the additional boundary direction restriction. We give the scalar geometric construction below instead of importing either a limiting-curve assertion or the source's propagation theorem.

We use ordinary integration, change of variables, smooth coordinate cutoffs and the local inverse theorem already proved in Coordinate inverses and integration, and the scalar integral estimates in Hilbert-valued integration. The finite-dimensional flow, compactness and collision-count arguments needed here are supplied explicitly.
