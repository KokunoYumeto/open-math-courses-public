Open Mathematics CoursesCourse contents (Global uniqueness for isotropic elasticity)

Results on the elastic boundary map, the augmented system, planar transport, exact solutions and the null conic, with their assumptions

The course proves the statements below; each link leads to the statement with its assumptions and its proof. The lessons are self-checked by the writing AI; independent mathematical review is not complete.

  • Positive elastic energy — Lemma 1.1. For real mu > 0 and 3 lambda + 2 mu > 0 and every real symmetric 3x3 matrix A, lambda (tr A)^2 + 2 mu |A|^2 >= min(2 mu, 3 lambda + 2 mu) |A|^2. No hypothesis.
  • Korn's identity with zero boundary values — Lemma 2.1. For phi in H^1_0(Omega; R^3), the integral of |grad phi|^2 equals twice the integral of |e(phi)|^2 minus the integral of (div phi)^2. No hypothesis.
  • The Dirichlet problem for the Lamé system — Proposition 2.3. For v in H^1(Omega; R^3) there is exactly one weak solution u of L u = 0 with u - v in H^1_0, depending only on the class of v modulo H^1_0, with ||u||_{H^1} <= C ||v||_{H^1}. No hypothesis.
  • The displacement-to-traction map — Definition 3.1. The bounded symmetric map Lambda from the trace space H^1/H^1_0 to its dual given by the energy form of the solutions of the Dirichlet problem. No hypothesis.
  • Global uniqueness for isotropic elasticity (OpenAI) — Theorem 4.1. On a bounded connected domain in R^3 with smooth boundary, smooth moduli with mu > 0 and 3 lambda + 2 mu > 0 are determined by the displacement-to-traction map; proved in the seventh lesson. Uses Hypothesis (BD) (boundary determination of all derivatives of the moduli, Tan-Liu 2023, Theorem 1.2); Hypothesis (ER) (interior elliptic regularity for systems, with uniform constants for smooth families, Dyatlov's notes, Section 15.1); Hypothesis (CE) (the Carleman estimate with a convexified linear weight, Salo-Tzou 2009, Lemma 2.1 with s = -1); Hypothesis (HF) (invertible solutions of d/dz-bar V = A V over a whole disk, Forstneric 2009, Theorem 1.1).
  • Gluing functions with vanishing jets — Lemma 2.2. If H is smooth on R^3 with all derivatives zero on the boundary of Omega, then H on the closure of Omega extended by zero is smooth. No hypothesis.
  • A common exterior extension of the moduli — Lemma 3.1. Two pairs with the same boundary map have smooth extensions to R^3 satisfying the positivity conditions, equal outside Omega and equal to one constant pair outside a ball. Uses Hypothesis (BD) (boundary determination of all derivatives of the moduli, Tan-Liu 2023, Theorem 1.2).
  • Transfer of solutions — Proposition 4.1. Every smooth solution of L_1 u_1 = 0 on a ball containing the closure of B_r has a smooth solution u_2 of L_2 u_2 = 0 on the ball that equals u_1 outside the closure of Omega. Uses Hypothesis (BD) (boundary determination of all derivatives of the moduli, Tan-Liu 2023, Theorem 1.2); Hypothesis (ER) (interior elliptic regularity for systems, with uniform constants for smooth families, Dyatlov's notes, Section 15.1).
  • The augmented system — Lemma 1.1. L u = R(u, div u) and div R(u,d) - S(u,d) = P(div u - d) for all smooth pairs (u, d); so L u = 0 gives R(u, div u) = 0 and S(u, div u) = 0. No hypothesis.
  • The normalized augmented operator — Lemma 1.2. The operator (m^-1 R, l^-1 (S - 2 m^-1 grad m . R)) is the Laplacian times the identity plus lower-order terms; for two pairs equal outside Omega the difference has order at most one with coefficients supported in the closure of Omega. No hypothesis.
  • A supported Carleman estimate — Proposition 3.1. For U in C^infinity_c(B; C^4) and small h = 1/tau, ||E_tau^-1 U||_{H^1_h} <= C h ||E_tau^-1 cal-L U||_{H^-1_h}, with E_tau = exp(tau theta . x) and theta null. Uses Hypothesis (CE) (the Carleman estimate with a convexified linear weight, Salo-Tzou 2009, Lemma 2.1 with s = -1).
  • Solvability with an exponential phase — Corollary 4.1. The conjugated Lamé operator A_theta has, for every f in L^2(B), a distributional solution z in L^2(B) with ||z|| <= C ||f||. Uses Hypothesis (CE) (the Carleman estimate with a convexified linear weight, Salo-Tzou 2009, Lemma 2.1 with s = -1).
  • Interior estimates at the scale 1/tau — Lemma 5.1. Solutions of A_theta z = f satisfy the sum over j <= 2 of h^j ||grad^j z||_{L^2(B')} <= C(||z||_{L^2(B)} + h^2 ||f||_{L^2(B)}). Uses Hypothesis (ER) (interior elliptic regularity for systems, with uniform constants for smooth families, Dyatlov's notes, Section 15.1).
  • The Cauchy transform — Lemma 1.1. For bounded f supported in a disk of radius delta, |T f| <= 2 delta sup |f|, T f is continuous and d/dz-bar T f = f in the sense of distributions. No hypothesis.
  • Regularity for d/dz-bar — Lemma 1.2. Locally square-integrable solutions of d/dz-bar W = b W + g with smooth b, g are smooth. No hypothesis.
  • Local frames — Lemma 2.1. Every point has a disk on which d/dz-bar V = A V has an invertible solution V with |V - Id| <= 1/2. No hypothesis.
  • Frames depending on a parameter — Lemma 2.2. For smooth A on a disk times an interval, there are locally in the parameter invertible solutions of d/dz-bar V = A V on any smaller disk, smooth in all variables. Uses Hypothesis (HF) (invertible solutions of d/dz-bar V = A V over a whole disk, Forstneric 2009, Theorem 1.1).
  • Inhomogeneous planar equations — Lemma 2.3. d/dz-bar v = A v + F has smooth solutions on smaller disks, vanishing for parameters where F vanishes outside a compact set. Uses Hypothesis (HF) (invertible solutions of d/dz-bar V = A V over a whole disk, Forstneric 2009, Theorem 1.1).
  • Supported planar transport — Lemma 3.1. Compactly supported L^2 solutions of the homogeneous planar equation vanish; supported solutions satisfy Sobolev estimates; if supported solutions of the inhomogeneous equation exist for a dense set of parameters, they exist for all, are unique and depend smoothly on the parameters. No hypothesis.
  • Conjugation by the exponential phase — Lemma 1.1. E_tau^-1 R(E_tau a, E_tau b) = R(a,b) + tau R^theta(a,b), and similarly for S and P, with explicit first-order operators R^theta, S^theta, P^theta. No hypothesis.
  • Compatibility of the leading operators — Lemma 1.2. theta . R^theta(a,b) = P^theta(theta . a), and div R^theta + theta . R - S^theta = P(theta . a) + P^theta(div a - b). No hypothesis.
  • Exact solutions with prescribed leading terms (OpenAI) — Proposition 3.1. Every local frame of the constrained transport equation on a cylinder is realized, after a cutoff, as the leading displacement and divergence of exact solutions u_tau of L u = 0 on B_5r, with E_tau^-1 (u_tau, div u_tau) converging in L^2(B_4r) and weighted first derivatives O(tau). Uses Hypothesis (ER) (interior elliptic regularity for systems, with uniform constants for smooth families, Dyatlov's notes, Section 15.1); Hypothesis (CE) (the Carleman estimate with a convexified linear weight, Salo-Tzou 2009, Lemma 2.1 with s = -1); Hypothesis (HF) (invertible solutions of d/dz-bar V = A V over a whole disk, Forstneric 2009, Theorem 1.1).
  • The transport normal form — Lemma 1.1. In the variables p = sqrt(m) a, s = -(k / 2 sqrt(m)) b - g . p / 2, the constrained transport equation becomes D p = theta s, D s = Q_theta . p + T_theta s with T_theta = D log(k/l) and an explicit Q_theta linear in theta. No hypothesis.
  • A parametrization of the null conic — Lemma 2.1. [z0 : z1] -> [(z0^2 - z1^2, i(z0^2 + z1^2), 2 z0 z1)] is a bijection of the complex projective line onto the conic of null directions, with explicit inverse charts. No hypothesis.
  • Matching of the transports (OpenAI) — Proposition 4.1. There is a unique smooth field G(x, [theta]) of endomorphisms of E_theta with D_theta G = M_2 G - G M_1 and G = Id for |x| > 2r; it is holomorphic in the direction. Uses Hypothesis (BD) (boundary determination of all derivatives of the moduli, Tan-Liu 2023, Theorem 1.2); Hypothesis (ER) (interior elliptic regularity for systems, with uniform constants for smooth families, Dyatlov's notes, Section 15.1); Hypothesis (CE) (the Carleman estimate with a convexified linear weight, Salo-Tzou 2009, Lemma 2.1 with s = -1); Hypothesis (HF) (invertible solutions of d/dz-bar V = A V over a whole disk, Forstneric 2009, Theorem 1.1).
  • Holomorphic functions and sections over the null conic — Lemma 1.1. Holomorphic functions on the conic are constant, null vectors span C^3, and every holomorphic section of the bundle H of planes theta-perp is zero. No hypothesis.
  • A frame of H and the quotient by the null line — Lemma 1.2. h1 = (z0, i z0, z1), h2 = (-z1, i z1, z0) frame H, theta = z0 h1 + z1 h2, the form pi = -z1 xi1 + z0 xi2 identifies H/N with the trivial bundle, and holomorphic endomorphism fields of H vanishing on N are zero. No hypothesis.
  • Rigidity of the comparison (OpenAI) — Proposition 2.1. The comparison field is the identity, so the transport matrices of the two media agree on E_theta for every null theta. Uses Hypothesis (BD) (boundary determination of all derivatives of the moduli, Tan-Liu 2023, Theorem 1.2); Hypothesis (ER) (interior elliptic regularity for systems, with uniform constants for smooth families, Dyatlov's notes, Section 15.1); Hypothesis (CE) (the Carleman estimate with a convexified linear weight, Salo-Tzou 2009, Lemma 2.1 with s = -1); Hypothesis (HF) (invertible solutions of d/dz-bar V = A V over a whole disk, Forstneric 2009, Theorem 1.1).
  • Finite-type uniqueness — Lemma 3.1. If d_i d_j eta = sum_a B_ij^a d_a eta + delta_ij q on a connected open set in R^n, n >= 2, and eta vanishes on a nonempty open subset, then eta = q = 0. No hypothesis.

Reading order

Seven lessons. The first defines the boundary map and states the theorem; the second reduces the comparison to solutions that differ only inside the domain; the third and fourth prove the analytic tools (a Carleman estimate for the augmented system, and planar transport equations), and can be read on their own; the fifth constructs exact solutions; the sixth compares the two media; the seventh proves the theorem. Four results from the literature are used as named hypotheses and are stated where they are used.
