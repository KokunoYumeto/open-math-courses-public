Walsh norms and moment lifts

Written by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), October 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. Public domain (CC0).

This lesson collects the tools for the decay theorem of Type, cotype and K-convexity (Theorem 2.3 there). Walsh norms of an operator are nonincreasing in the dimension, invariant under amplification, and decay like \(2^{-n/2}\) for each fixed finite-rank operator (Lemma 1.1). Cotype of a fixed length passes to finite \(L_2\) sums (Section 2). Kahane's inequality, in the form \(\|S\|_{L_2}\le24\|S\|_{L_1}\) for Rademacher sums, is proved with a stopping time (Lemma 2.1). Together with a quotient-duality principle it gives moment lifts: bounded families indexed by signs whose first moments are prescribed vectors (Lemma 3.2).

  • Walsh norms (Lemma 1.1).
  • Fixed-length cotype and Kahane's inequality (Section 2, Lemma 2.1).
  • Quotient duality and moment lifts (Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2).

The lesson follows [OpenAI-CC, Section 2]. Banach spaces are real, \(B_Y\) is the closed unit ball of \(Y\), averages over finite sets are uniform, and different averaging variables are independent. For a finite probability space \(\Omega\), \(L_p(\Omega;Y)\) has the probability-normalized norm, while \(\ell_2^m(Y)\) is \(Y^m\) with the norm \((\sum_{i=1}^m\|y_i\|^2)^{1/2}\). With the expectation pairing, for finite \(\Omega\), \[ \begin{gathered} L_2(\Omega;Y)^*=L_2(\Omega;Y^*),\\ L_1(\Omega;Y)^*=L_\infty(\Omega;Y^*),\\ L_\infty(\Omega;Y)^*=L_1(\Omega;Y^*), \end{gathered} \] isometrically, and \(\ell_2^m(Y)^*=\ell_2^m(Y^*)\) with the pairing \(\sum_i\langle y_i,y_i^*\rangle\); the proofs are those of Lemma 3.1 of the previous lesson.

1. Walsh norms

Recall from the previous lesson that \(V_n=\mathbb F_2^n\), \(\chi(u,v)=(-1)^{u\cdot v}\), and for \(T\colon G\to F\) \[ \begin{gathered} (W^T_nx)(v)=\mathbb E_{u\in V_n}\chi(u,v)\,Tx(u),\\ w_n(T)=\|W^T_n\|, \end{gathered} \] the norm from \(L_2(V_n;G)\) to \(L_2(V_n;F)\). By duality, \[ \begin{aligned} &w_n(T)=\sup\Bigl|\mathbb E_{u,v\in V_n}\\ &\qquad\chi(u,v)\,y_v(Tx_u)\Bigr| , \end{aligned} \tag{1.1} \] the supremum over \(x_u\in G\) and \(y_v\in F^*\) with \(\mathbb E_u\|x_u\|^2\le1\) and \(\mathbb E_v\|y_v\|^2\le1\).

Lemma 1.1 (Walsh norms; [OpenAI-CC, Lemma 2.1]). Let \(T,A,B\colon G\to F\) be bounded.

  1. \(2^{-n/2}\|T\|\le w_n(T)\le\|T\|\), and \(w_n(T)\) is nonincreasing in \(n\).
  2. If \(\Omega\) is finite and \(T_\Omega\colon L_2(\Omega;G)\to L_2(\Omega;F)\) applies \(T\) pointwise, then \(w_n(T_\Omega)=w_n(T)\).
  3. If \(T\) has finite rank, there is \(D_T<\infty\) with \(w_n(T)\le D_T2^{-n/2}\) for all \(n\ge0\).
  4. \(|w_n(A)-w_n(B)|\le\|A-B\|\).

Proof. (1) For every \(v\), \(\|(W^T_nx)(v)\|\le\mathbb E_u\|Tx(u)\|\le\|T\|\,\|x\|_{L_2}\), by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality; so \(w_n(T)\le\|T\|\). For the lower bound take \(x\) supported at one point \(u_0\), with \(x(u_0)=2^{n/2}g\); then \(\|x\|_{L_2}=\|g\|\) and \((W^T_nx)(v)=2^{-n/2}\chi(u_0,v)Tg\), so \(\|W^T_nx\|_{L_2}=2^{-n/2}\|Tg\|\). For monotonicity write \(V_{n+j}=V_n\oplus V_j\). The transform \(W^T_{n+j}\) is the transform \(W^T_n\) in the first coordinate followed by the normalized Walsh transform with the identity of \(F\) in the second coordinate; the latter has norm at most \(1\) by the upper bound just proved. Averaging the squared estimates over the unused coordinate gives \(w_{n+j}(T)\le w_n(T)\).

(2) A function in \(L_2(V_n;L_2(\Omega;G))\) is a function on \(V_n\times\Omega\); for each fixed \(\omega\), \(W^{T_\Omega}_n\) acts as \(W^T_n\) on the slice. Averaging the squared estimates over \(\omega\) gives \(w_n(T_\Omega)\le w_n(T)\); inputs constant in \(\omega\) give the reverse inequality.

(3) The range \(T(G)\) is finite-dimensional; give it a Hilbert space norm \(\|\cdot\|_H\). All norms on a finite-dimensional space are equivalent, so \(T=BA\) with \(A=T\colon G\to H=(T(G),\|\cdot\|_H)\) and \(B\) the identity \(H\to F\), both bounded. The matrix \((2^{-n}\chi(u,v))_{u,v}\) is \(2^{-n/2}\) times an orthogonal matrix, so \(\|W^{\mathrm{Id}_H}_n\|=2^{-n/2}\), and \(W^T_n=BW^{\mathrm{Id}_H}_nA\) pointwise. Take \(D_T=\|A\|\|B\|\).

(4) \(W^A_n-W^B_n=W^{A-B}_n\); use the upper bound in (1) and the triangle inequality. \(\blacksquare\)

The constant \(D_T\) depends on \(T\); the point of the course is a decay bound that does not.

2. Fixed-length cotype and Kahane's inequality

For an integer \(m\ge1\), let \(c_m(Y)\) be the least constant with \[ \begin{aligned} &\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^m\|y_i\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\le c_m(Y)\\ &\quad\cdot\Bigl(\mathbb E_\varepsilon\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^m\varepsilon_iy_i\Bigr\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}. \end{aligned} \tag{2.1} \] If \(C_q(Y)<\infty\), Hölder's inequality \(\sum_i\|y_i\|^2\le m^{1-2/q}(\sum_i\|y_i\|^q)^{2/q}\) gives \[ c_m(Y)\le C_q(Y)\,m^{1/2-1/q}. \tag{2.2} \] Squaring (2.1) and averaging over \(\omega\) gives \(c_m(L_2(\Omega;Y))\le c_m(Y)\) for finite \(\Omega\).

Lemma 2.1 (Kahane's inequality; [OpenAI-CC, Lemma 2.2]). For every finite Rademacher sum \(S=\sum_{i=1}^m\varepsilon_iz_i\) in a Banach space, \[ (\mathbb E\|S\|^2)^{1/2}\le24\,\mathbb E\|S\| . \]

Proof. Put \(M=\mathbb E\|S\|\) and \(b=\max_i\|z_i\|\). Since \(z_i=\mathbb E(\varepsilon_iS)\), \(b\le M\). If \(M=0\), then \(S=0\). Let \(S_j=\sum_{i\le j}\varepsilon_iz_i\) and \(R_j=S-S_j\).

Two reflection estimates. Fix \(u>0\), and let \(\tau\) be the first \(j\) with \(\|S_j\|>u\) (if any). On the event \(\{\tau=j\}\), which depends only on \(\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_j\), at least one of \(\|S_j+R_j\|\) and \(\|S_j-R_j\|\) exceeds \(u\), because their average is at least \(\|S_j\|\). The remaining sum \(R_j\) is independent of \(\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_j\) and has the same distribution as \(-R_j\). So \(\mathbb P(\tau=j,\ \|S\|>u)\ge\frac12\mathbb P(\tau=j)\), and summing over \(j\), \[ \mathbb P(\tau\text{ exists})\le2\,\mathbb P(\|S\|>u). \] In the same way, since \(S_j\) is independent of \(R_j\) and symmetric, \(\mathbb P(\|R_j\|>v)\le2\,\mathbb P(\|S\|>v)\) for every \(j\) and \(v>0\).

A product estimate. If \(\|S\|>u+b+v\), then \(\tau\) exists, \(\|S_\tau\|\le u+b\) (as \(\|S_{\tau-1}\|\le u\)), and \(\|R_\tau\|>v\). Using the independence of \(\{\tau=j\}\) and \(R_j\), \[ \begin{aligned} &\mathbb P(\|S\|>u+b+v)\\ &\quad\le\sum_j\mathbb P(\tau=j)\,\mathbb P(\|R_j\|>v)\\ &\quad\le4\,\mathbb P(\|S\|>u)\,\mathbb P(\|S\|>v). \end{aligned} \]

Tails. Take \(u=8M\). By Markov's inequality \(\mathbb P(\|S\|>8M)\le1/8\), and with \(b\le M\), for \(v>0\), \[ \mathbb P(\|S\|>9M+v)\le\tfrac12\,\mathbb P(\|S\|>v). \] By induction \(\mathbb P(\|S\|>9jM)\le2^{-j}\) for \(j\ge0\). Hence \[ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E\|S\|^2&=\int_0^\infty2t\,\mathbb P(\|S\|>t)\,dt\\ &\le\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j}\int_{9jM}^{9(j+1)M}2t\,dt\\ &=81M^2\sum_{j\ge0}(2j+1)2^{-j}\\ &=486M^2<24^2M^2 . \end{aligned} \] \(\blacksquare\)

The inequality is the \(L_1\)-to-\(L_2\) case of Kahane's inequality ([Pis11, Theorem 8.1] gives the general form); the explicit constant \(24\) is all that is needed below.

3. Quotient duality and moment lifts

Lemma 3.1 (quotient lifts). Let \(U\colon A\to B\) be a bounded linear surjection between Banach spaces with a bounded right inverse, and suppose \(\|U^*b^*\|\ge D^{-1}\|b^*\|\) for all \(b^*\in B^*\). Then every \(b\in B\) and every \(\theta>1\) admit \(a\in A\) with \(Ua=b\) and \(\|a\|\le\theta D\|b\|\).

Proof. Let \(Q\colon A\to A/\ker U\) be the quotient map and \(\hat U\colon A/\ker U\to B\) the induced bijection, \(\hat UQ=U\); its inverse is bounded, being \(Q\) composed with the right inverse. By the Hahn–Banach theorem (Hahn–Banach, Baire and the basic theorems on Banach spaces, Corollary 2.3), the quotient norm of \(\hat U^{-1}b\) is the supremum of \(|\phi(\hat U^{-1}b)|\) over functionals \(\phi\) of norm at most \(1\) on \(A/\ker U\). For such \(\phi\), put \(b^*=\phi\circ\hat U^{-1}\in B^*\). Then \(U^*b^*=\phi\circ Q\) has norm \(\|\phi\|\le1\), since \(Q\) maps the open unit ball of \(A\) onto that of the quotient; so \(\|b^*\|\le D\) and \(|\phi(\hat U^{-1}b)|=|b^*(b)|\le D\|b\|\). The coset \(\hat U^{-1}b\) therefore has a representative \(a\) with \(\|a\|\le\theta D\|b\|\), and \(Ua=b\). \(\blacksquare\)

Lemma 3.2 (moment lifts; [OpenAI-CC, Lemma 2.3]). Suppose \(c_m(G^*)\le D_g\) and \(c_m(F)\le D_f\), with \(D_g,D_f\ge1\), and put \[ \begin{gathered} a=\frac1{48D_g\sqrt m},\\ b=\frac1{48D_f\sqrt m}. \end{gathered} \tag{3.1} \]

  1. For \(x_1,\dots,x_m\in B_G\) there are \(P_\varepsilon\in B_G\), \(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^m\), with \(\mathbb E_\varepsilon\varepsilon_iP_\varepsilon=ax_i\) for all \(i\).
  2. For \(y_1,\dots,y_m\in B_{F^*}\) there are \(Z_\delta\in B_{F^*}\), \(\delta\in\{-1,1\}^m\), with \(\mathbb E_\delta\delta_jZ_\delta=by_j\) for all \(j\).

The same holds, with the same \(a\) and \(b\), for \(G\) and \(F\) replaced by finite \(L_2\) sums \(L_2(\Omega;G)\) and \(L_2(\Omega;F)\).

Proof. (1) The moment map \(U\colon L_\infty(\{-1,1\}^m;G)\to\ell_2^m(G)\), \(UP=(\mathbb E_\varepsilon\varepsilon_iP_\varepsilon)_i\), has the right inverse \((x_i)\mapsto\sum_i\varepsilon_ix_i\). Its adjoint sends \((z_i^*)\in\ell_2^m(G^*)\) to the function \(\varepsilon\mapsto\sum_i\varepsilon_iz_i^*\) in \(L_1(\{-1,1\}^m;G^*)\). By Lemma 2.1 and (2.1), \[ \begin{aligned} \Bigl\|\sum_i\varepsilon_iz_i^*\Bigr\|_{L_1(G^*)}&\ge\frac1{24D_g}\\ &\quad\cdot\Bigl(\sum_i\|z_i^*\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}. \end{aligned} \] By Lemma 3.1 with \(D=24D_g\) and \(\theta=2\), the tuple \((ax_i)\), of norm at most \(a\sqrt m\), has a lift \(P\) with \(\|P\|_{L_\infty}\le48D_ga\sqrt m=1\).

(2) The map \(V\colon\ell_2^m(F)\to L_1(\{-1,1\}^m;F)\), \(V(w_j)=\sum_j\delta_jw_j\), satisfies \(\|Vw\|_{L_1}\ge(24D_f)^{-1}\|w\|_{\ell_2^m(F)}\), by Lemma 2.1 and (2.1). The functional \(Vw\mapsto b\sum_jy_j(w_j)\) on the range of \(V\) is well defined and has norm at most \(b\sqrt m\cdot24D_f=1/2\). Extend it by the Hahn–Banach theorem to \(L_1(\{-1,1\}^m;F)\) with the same norm, and represent the extension by \(Z\in L_\infty(\{-1,1\}^m;F^*)\), \(\|Z\|_{L_\infty}\le1/2\). Testing on \(Vw\) with a single nonzero \(w_j\) gives \(\mathbb E_\delta\delta_jZ_\delta=by_j\).

For finite \(L_2\) sums, \(c_m(L_2(\Omega;F))\le c_m(F)\) by Section 2, and \(L_2(\Omega;G)^*=L_2(\Omega;G^*)\) with \(c_m(L_2(\Omega;G^*))\le c_m(G^*)\); the same proofs apply. \(\blacksquare\)

Only the positivity of \(a\) and \(b\), and the growth of \(ma\) and \(mb\) with \(m\) under cotype hypotheses, matter later.

4. Exercises

4.1. Show that \(w_1(\mathrm{Id}_{\ell_\infty^2})=1\), so that the upper bound of Lemma 1.1(1) is attained.

4.2. Show that \(c_m(\mathbb R)=1\) and \(c_m(\ell_\infty^m)\ge\sqrt m\).

4.3. Check the identity \(\sum_{j\ge0}(2j+1)2^{-j}=6\).

4.4. In Lemma 3.2(1), why is the lift taken in \(L_\infty\), not in \(L_2\)?

4.5. Show that the moment lift of Lemma 3.2(1) cannot exist for every \(m\) with \(a\) independent of \(m\) when \(G=\mathbb R\): compute \(\sum_i(\mathbb E_\varepsilon\varepsilon_iP_\varepsilon)^2\) for \(|P_\varepsilon|\le1\).

5. Solutions

4.1. On \(V_1=\{0,1\}\), take \(x_0=(1,1)\), \(x_1=(1,-1)\) in \(\ell_\infty^2\). Then \((Wx)(0)=\frac12(x_0+x_1)=(1,0)\) and \((Wx)(1)=\frac12(x_0-x_1)=(0,1)\), so \(\|Wx\|_{L_2}=1=\|x\|_{L_2}\).

4.2. For real numbers, \(\mathbb E(\sum_i\varepsilon_iy_i)^2=\sum_iy_i^2\). In \(\ell_\infty^m\), the unit vectors \(e_i\) have \(\|\sum_i\varepsilon_ie_i\|=1\) for every choice of signs, while \((\sum_i\|e_i\|^2)^{1/2}=\sqrt m\).

4.3. \(\sum_jj2^{-j}=2\) and \(\sum_j2^{-j}=2\), so the sum is \(4+2=6\).

4.4. The lifts are used as vectors of the unit ball, pointwise in \(\varepsilon\): the dual estimate is in \(L_1\), whose dual is \(L_\infty\).

4.5. By Bessel's inequality for the orthonormal functions \(\varepsilon_i\) in \(L_2(\{-1,1\}^m)\), \(\sum_i(\mathbb E\varepsilon_iP_\varepsilon)^2\le\mathbb EP^2\le1\). With \(x_i=1\) this forces \(ma^2\le1\), so \(a\le m^{-1/2}\).
