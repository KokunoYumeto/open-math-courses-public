Open Mathematics CoursesCourse contents (A translational tile with no periodic tiling in dimension three)

Results on tilings, the arithmetic word rule, tiling equations, stacking and the passage to three dimensions, with their assumptions

The course proves the statements below; each link leads to the statement with its assumptions and its proof. The lessons are self-checked by the writing AI; independent mathematical review is not complete.

  • Elementary facts on tilings — Lemma 1.2. Tiles and fully periodic tiling sets are preserved by translations and isomorphisms; A (+) H = G for a subgroup H exactly when A meets every coset once; fully periodic sets are finite unions of cosets of a finite-index subgroup. No hypothesis.
  • Newman's theorem — Theorem 2.1. Every tiling set of Z by a finite tile is invariant under a translation by some p >= 1. No hypothesis.
  • Subgroups of Z^d — Lemma 3.2. A subgroup of Z^d of index M contains M Z^d; a subgroup containing d linearly independent vectors has finite index. No hypothesis.
  • Unit-cube thickening — Proposition 3.3. A tiling set of Z^d by T is an almost-everywhere tiling set of R^d by T + [0,1]^d, fully periodic if invariant under a finite-index subgroup. No hypothesis.
  • A tile in Z^3 with no periodic tiling (OpenAI) — Theorem 4.1. A finite T in Z^3 tiles Z^3 with no tiling set invariant under a finite-index subgroup, and T + [0,1]^3 tiles R^3 almost everywhere with no tiling set invariant under a lattice of full rank; proved in the last lesson. No hypothesis.
  • Global affine approximation — Lemma 2.1. An array with the line rule (p > 200) agrees with a nonzero affine form An + Bm + C over F_p wherever the form is nonzero. No hypothesis.
  • Vertical nondegeneracy — Lemma 3.1. The columns are nonconstant exactly when B != 0, and then every positive vertical period is divisible by p. No hypothesis.
  • The vertical-period obstruction — Proposition 4.1. No array with the line rule and no constant column has a positive vertical period. No hypothesis.
  • The last-nonzero-digit model — Lemma 5.1. The array W(n,m) = f(m), with f the last nonzero digit in base p, satisfies the line rule and every column takes every value. No hypothesis.
  • A cyclic finite group — Lemma 1.2. V = Z/p^4 x prod (Z/r_i^2 x Z/a_i x Z/b_i) is cyclic. No hypothesis.
  • Invariance tiles — Lemma 3.1. For a channel i and a shift sigma there is a tile F with A (+) F = G exactly when c_i(b - sigma) = c_i(b) for all bases b, for every graph A. No hypothesis.
  • Exclusion of simultaneous activity — Lemma 4.1. For a cycle of channels with chosen labels, finitely many tiles are tiled by a graph exactly when at every x one of the labels is inactive. No hypothesis.
  • Activation fibre criterion — Lemma 3.1. A graph tiles with an activation tile exactly when the batch maps are bijections at every target base. No hypothesis.
  • Ordinary activation — Lemma 4.1. In every common solution every ordinary channel has an active label at every point. No hypothesis.
  • Seed activation — Lemma 4.2. In every common solution each seed is active at some point. No hypothesis.
  • No fully periodic common solution — Proposition 5.1. No common solution of the system is invariant under a subgroup of finite index. No hypothesis.
  • Block inverse — Lemma 2.1. The map (l, delta) -> (C(k - l) + e_delta, T(x_1 - h_1)(k - l)) is a bijection from K x Delta onto P x K. No hypothesis.
  • A system with a common solution and no periodic one (OpenAI) — Theorem 6.2. Finitely many finite tiles in Z^2 x V, V finite cyclic, have a common solution but no fully periodic common solution. No hypothesis.
  • Partitions with full difference sets — Lemma 1.1. For every s and every finite set of primes there are a prime q outside it and a partition of Z/q into s sets E with E - E = Z/q. No hypothesis.
  • Stacking — Proposition 2.1. Finite tiles F_1, ..., F_s have a common solution exactly when the stacked tile tiles H x Z/q, and full periodicity passes in both directions. No hypothesis.
  • One tile with a cyclic finite coordinate — Corollary 3.1. A finite tile of Z^2 x Z/Q has a tiling set but no fully periodic one. No hypothesis.
  • Rigid representatives — Lemma 1.1. For some m >= 2 there is a transversal modulo m whose points other than the marked one have every difference d with |d| <= m outside m Z^3. No hypothesis.
  • Existence of tilings — Proposition 3.1. The tile T tiles Z^3 and its thickening tiles R^3 almost everywhere. No hypothesis.
  • Grid centers — Lemma 4.2. In a periodic almost-everywhere tiling by the two constituent shapes, the centers form a translate of m Z^3. No hypothesis.
  • No fully periodic tiling of R^3 — Proposition 5.1. No almost-everywhere tiling of R^3 by the thickening of T has a tiling set invariant under a lattice of full rank. No hypothesis.

Reading order

Seven lessons. The first sets up tilings and periodicity, proves Newman's theorem and states the theorem; the second proves the obstruction for the arithmetic word rule; the third, fourth and fifth build the tiling equations in Z^2 x V, exclude periodic solutions and construct a common solution; the sixth stacks the system into one tile; the seventh passes to Z^3 and R^3 and completes the proof. Everything used is proved in the course apart from basic algebra, linear algebra and Lebesgue measure from the core courses; the planar periodicity theorem is stated as background and not used.
