# Positivity restricts the index

Two different positivity arguments control the index. Positive traces on a sequence of recursively constructed projections exclude most numbers below four. At infinite index, averaging in the semifinite basic construction makes the variational constant collapse to zero.

We assume [Going up and down the Jones tower](towers-and-tunnels.md), [Finite bases, bounded vectors and a positive-operator inequality](finite-bases-and-positive-index.md), and the tracial Hilbert-space and semifinite-trace prerequisites of the first lesson. Basic references are [Jones], [Wenzl], [Pimsner–Popa] and [Anantharaman–Popa].

## Quantum integers and recursively constructed projections

Suppose \(N\subseteq M\) has finite index \(d\). Set \(\delta=\sqrt d\) and, using the Jones projections in its tower, put \(U_i=\delta e_i\). Shift the generator labels in this section so that the first generator is \(U_1\). The relations become

\[
U_i^*=U_i,\quad U_i^2=\delta U_i,
\quad U_iU_{i+1}U_i=U_i,
\quad U_iU_j=U_jU_i\quad(|i-j|\geq2).
\]

Define real numbers

\[
[0]=0,\qquad [1]=1,\qquad
[n+1]=\delta[n]-[n-1].
\]

The brackets here denote numbers, not subfactor indices. Let \(A_n\) be the algebra generated by \(1,U_1,\ldots,U_{n-1}\), inside the tracial tower. Whenever the denominators are nonzero, define

\[
f_1=1,\qquad
f_{n+1}=f_n-\frac{[n]}{[n+1]}f_nU_nf_n.
\]

These are the Jones–Wenzl projections before the first zero denominator.

**Lemma 7.1.** For \(n\geq1\), if \([2],\ldots,[n]\) are nonzero, then \(f_n\) is a self-adjoint projection, lies in \(A_n\), and satisfies

\[
\begin{gathered}
U_if_n=f_nU_i=0\quad(1\leq i<n),\\
f_nf_{n-1}=f_n\quad(n\geq2),\\
\tau(f_n)=\frac{[n+1]}{\delta^n}.
\end{gathered}
\]

**Proof.** We prove the assertions inductively. The base case is \(f_1=1\), with trace \([2]/\delta=1\). For \(n=1\), \(U_1^2=\delta U_1\) gives \(f_2=1-\delta^{-1}U_1\), a projection annihilating \(U_1\).

For the general step, put \(Q=f_nU_nf_n\). Write

\[
f_n=f_{n-1}-c f_{n-1}U_{n-1}f_{n-1},
\qquad c=\frac{[n-1]}{[n]}.
\]

The operator \(U_n\) commutes with \(f_{n-1}\), whose generators have indices at most \(n-2\). Using \(f_nf_{n-1}=f_n\) and the adjacent relation gives

\[
Q^2=(\delta-c)Q=\frac{[n+1]}{[n]}Q,
\]

and, by the same expansion without the leftmost \(f_n\),

\[
U_nf_nU_nf_n=\frac{[n+1]}{[n]}U_nf_n.
\]

When \([n+1]\ne0\), these identities make
\(f_{n+1}=f_n-([n]/[n+1])Q\) a self-adjoint projection, annihilating \(U_n\). The earlier \(U_i\) annihilate both \(f_n\) and \(Q\), so they also annihilate \(f_{n+1}\). Multiplication by \(f_n\) fixes it.

The Markov trace gives

\[
\tau(f_nU_nf_n)=\tau(U_nf_n)=\delta^{-1}\tau(f_n),
\]

because the newest \(e_n\) has expectation \(d^{-1}1\) onto the preceding factor. Thus

\[
\tau(f_{n+1})
=\tau(f_n)\left(1-\frac{[n]}{\delta[n+1]}\right)
=\tau(f_n)\frac{[n+2]}{\delta[n+1]}
=\frac{[n+2]}{\delta^{n+1}}.
\]

This proves the induction. Notice that the projection calculation itself is algebraic; the trace is what forces its trace value to be nonnegative. \(\square\)

## The restriction below four

**Theorem 7.2 — Jones index restriction.** The index of a II₁ subfactor belongs to

\[
\left\{4\cos^2\frac\pi r:r=3,4,5,\ldots\right\}
\cup[4,\infty].
\]

**Proof.** Infinite index already belongs to the displayed extended interval. Suppose \(1\leq d<4\). Write \(\delta=2\cos\theta\) with \(0<\theta\leq\pi/3\). The sine addition formula gives

\[
[n]=\frac{\sin(n\theta)}{\sin\theta}.
\]

If \(\pi/\theta\) is not an integer, let \(r\) be the smallest positive integer with \(r\theta>\pi\). Then \([1],\ldots,[r-1]\) are positive, while \([r]<0\). Lemma 7.1 constructs the projection \(f_{r-1}\) using only the positive denominators \([2],\ldots,[r-1]\), but assigns it trace

\[
\tau(f_{r-1})=[r]/\delta^{r-1}<0.
\]

This contradicts positivity of the trace. Therefore \(\theta=\pi/r\) for an integer \(r\geq3\), giving the discrete values. \(\square\)

This proves a restriction. Existence at each discrete value needs a separate construction; the theorem alone does not supply it. The diagonal-corner example of the second lesson already realizes every finite value at least four.

**Corollary 7.3.** A proper II₁ inclusion has index at least two. At a discrete value \(d=4\cos^2(\pi/r)\), the projection \(f_{r-1}\) vanishes in the tracial Jones tower.

**Proof.** The first discrete values are \(1\) for \(r=3\) and \(2\) for \(r=4\); index one is equality of the two factors. For the second assertion all the denominators needed for \(f_{r-1}\) are positive, and its trace is \([r]/\delta^{r-1}=0\). Faithfulness of the trace forces the projection to vanish. \(\square\)

## An averaging lemma in infinite trace

We now address the infinite-index part of the positive-operator characterization. The argument uses an inclusion \(P\subseteq Q\) where \(P\) is a finite factor, \(Q\) is semifinite with a faithful normal trace \(T\), and

\[
P'\cap Q=\mathbb C,
\qquad T(p)=\infty\quad(0\ne p\in\mathcal P(P)).
\]

For a finite partition \(1=\sum_jp_j\) in \(P\), write
\(\Phi(X)=\sum_jp_jXp_j\).

**Lemma 7.4 — small pinching.** If \(0\ne X\in Q_+\cap L^2(Q,T)\), then for every \(\varepsilon>0\) there is such a partition with

\[
\|\Phi(X)\|_{2,T}^2<\varepsilon\|X\|_{2,T}^2.
\]

**Proof.** The closed convex hull in \(L^2(Q,T)\) of \(\{uXu^*:u\in\mathcal U(P)\}\) has a unique vector of least norm. All its elements remain bounded positive operators of norm at most \(\|X\|\). To justify this for a Hilbert-space limit, choose an ultraweakly convergent subnet in the bounded positive ball and test with \(Q\cap L^1(Q,T)\cap L^2(Q,T)\), which is dense in \(L^2\). Trace pairings identify the ultraweak limit with the Hilbert-space limit. The least-norm vector is invariant under all these conjugations, so belongs to \(P'\cap Q=\mathbb C\). No nonzero scalar lies in \(L^2(Q,T)\), because \(T(1)=\infty\). Thus the least-norm vector is zero.

Consequently some \(u\in\mathcal U(P)\) satisfies

\[
\operatorname{Re}\langle X,uXu^*\rangle_{2,T}
\leq\tfrac12\|X\|_{2,T}^2.
\]

Indeed, otherwise every finite convex combination would have its pairing with \(X\) larger than that fixed positive bound and could not approach zero. Approximate \(u\) in operator norm by a finite-spectrum unitary \(w\in P\). Its approximation can be chosen to give

\[
\operatorname{Re}\langle X,wXw^*\rangle_{2,T}
\leq\tfrac34\|X\|_{2,T}^2,
\]

using \(\|wXw^*-uXu^*\|_2\leq2\|w-u\|\|X\|_2\). Let \((p_j)\) be the spectral partition of \(w\). The corresponding pinching is the orthogonal projection onto the block-diagonal subspace. The diagonal blocks cancel in \(X-wXw^*\), so

\[
\|X-wXw^*\|_2^2
\leq4\bigl(\|X\|_2^2-\|\Phi(X)\|_2^2\bigr).
\]

The previous pairing bound gives \(\|X-wXw^*\|_2^2\geq\frac12\|X\|_2^2\). Hence

\[
\|\Phi(X)\|_2^2\leq\tfrac78\|X\|_2^2.
\]

The same argument applies inside each nonzero corner \(p_jPp_j\subseteq p_jQp_j\). Its relative commutant is \(\mathbb Cp_j\): a full-corner comparison transfers the commutant from the factor inclusion. Explicitly, choose a finite family of partial isometries \(v_a\in P\), including \(v_0=p_j\), with \(v_a^*v_a\leq p_j\) and orthogonal \(v_av_a^*\) summing to one. An element \(z\) commuting with \(p_jPp_j\) extends by \(\sum_av_a z v_a^*\) to an element commuting with \(P\): check its matrix blocks using \(v_a^*xv_b\in p_jPp_j\) for \(x\in P\). The extension is scalar, and its \(p_j\)-corner is \(z\). Each such corner identity has infinite \(T\)-trace by hypothesis.

Refine every block by its new spectral partition. The squared \(L^2\)-norms of the orthogonal diagonal blocks add, so each refinement reduces their total by a factor at most \(7/8\). After finitely many refinements the factor is smaller than \(\varepsilon\). \(\square\)

## Infinite index gives variational constant zero

**Theorem 7.5.** For any II₁ inclusion, including infinite index,

\[
[M:N]^{-1}
=\sup\{c\geq0:E_N(x)\geq cx\text{ for all }x\in M_+\}
=\inf_{0\ne x\in M_+}
\frac{\|E_N(x)\|_2^2}{\|x\|_2^2},
\]

where \(\infty^{-1}=0\).

**Proof.** The finite-index case is Corollary 4.6. Assume the index is infinite. If \(N'\cap M\) is infinite dimensional, it has nonzero projections \(p\) of arbitrarily small \(\tau\)-trace. Since \(p\) commutes with \(N\), bimodularity gives \(E_N(p)\in Z(N)=\mathbb C\), and trace preservation gives \(E_N(p)=\tau(p)1\). Therefore

\[
\frac{\|E_N(p)\|_2^2}{\|p\|_2^2}=\tau(p)\longrightarrow0.
\]

Suppose next that \(N'\cap M=\mathbb C\). In the basic construction \(B\), we have

\[
M'\cap B=\mathbb C,
\qquad \operatorname{Tr}(1)=\infty,
\qquad \operatorname{Tr}(e_N)=1.
\]

The relative-commutant assertion follows by conjugating \(N'\cap M\) with the tracial \(J\). Every nonzero projection of \(M\) has infinite \(\operatorname{Tr}\)-trace by projection comparison, as in Exercise 1.5. Lemma 7.4, applied to \(X=e_N\), gives a partition \(1=\sum_jp_j\) in \(M\) with

\[
\sum_j\operatorname{Tr}((p_je_Np_j)^2)<\varepsilon.
\]

Trace cyclicity and the compression identity give

\[
\operatorname{Tr}((p_je_Np_j)^2)
=\tau(E_N(p_j)^2)=\|E_N(p_j)\|_2^2.
\]

Since \(\sum_j\|p_j\|_2^2=1\), at least one nonzero \(p_j\) has ratio \(\|E_N(p_j)\|_2^2/\|p_j\|_2^2<\varepsilon\).

Finally, if \(N'\cap M\) is finite dimensional but not scalar, choose a partition into minimal projections. The local-index partition formula at infinite index forces some corner \(Np\subseteq pMp\) to have infinite index. Its relative commutant is scalar, so the preceding argument applies there. Put \(t=\tau(p)\), let \(\alpha:N\to Np\) be \(\alpha(n)=np\), and let \(E_p:pMp\to Np\) preserve the normalized corner trace. Trace pairings show that, for \(x\in pMp\),

\[
E_N(x)=t\,\alpha^{-1}(E_p(x)).
\]

Thus the global squared-norm ratio for positive \(x\) is \(t\) times the corner ratio, and again has infimum zero.

If \(E_N(x)\geq cx\) for all positive \(x\), trace pairing with \(x\) yields \(\|E_N(x)\|_2^2\geq c\|x\|_2^2\), exactly as in Theorem 3.5. Since that infimum is zero, no positive \(c\) is possible. The constant zero always works. \(\square\)

## Exercises

**Exercise 7.1 — introductory.** Compute \([2],[3],[4]\) as polynomials in \(\delta\), and the first three values of \(d=4\cos^2(\pi/r)\).

**Solution.** The recurrence gives \([2]=\delta\), \([3]=\delta^2-1\), \([4]=\delta^3-2\delta\). The values for \(r=3,4,5\) are \(1,2,(3+\sqrt5)/2\).

**Exercise 7.2 — intermediate.** Show directly why \(d=3/2\) cannot be a II₁ subfactor index.

**Solution.** Here \(\delta=\sqrt{3/2}\), \([3]=1/2>0\), while \([4]=\delta(3/2-2)=-\delta/2<0\). The projection \(f_3\) is defined using positive denominators \([2],[3]\), but its trace would be \([4]/\delta^3<0\), which is impossible.

**Exercise 7.3 — intermediate.** For a positive matrix block operator \(X\), explain the factor four in the pinching estimate in Lemma 7.4.

**Solution.** On a block \(p_iXp_j\), conjugation by the finite-spectrum unitary multiplies it by \(z_i\overline z_j\), where \(|z_i|=|z_j|=1\). The difference multiplier has absolute value at most two and hence squared absolute value at most four. The diagonal blocks have difference multiplier zero. Orthogonality of the blocks in tracial \(L^2\) gives the estimate.

**Exercise 7.4 — advanced.** An inclusion has infinite index and scalar relative commutant. Why is a small value of \(\operatorname{Tr}(e_N)\) not the reason the variational infimum vanishes?

**Solution.** That trace is fixed at one. The vanishing comes from conjugating and pinching this fixed finite-trace projection by elements of \(M\), whose nonzero projections have infinite basic-construction trace. Irreducibility makes the Hilbert-space orbit average zero. The resulting partition contains a projection with arbitrarily small expectation-to-vector norm ratio.
