Characters of central sequences and Jiang–Su stability

Kirchberg and Rørdam asked whether a unital separable C*-algebra A absorbs the Jiang–Su algebra Z whenever its central sequence algebra F_ω(A) has no characters. The answer is yes. The core is a finite theorem: every nonzero unital C*-algebra without characters has a finite maximal tensor power that receives a unital homomorphism from the dimension-drop algebra I(2,3). Its proof: the Rørdam–Winter criterion in an arbitrary unital C*-algebra; labels that stay linearly independent in every irreducible representation of a tensor power; a full square-zero element and a homomorphism from the matrix cone; a cube model of tensor powers of the cone. Proved in full from two named hypotheses: the theorem of Kirchberg and Rørdam relating Z-stability to F_ω(A), and (for infinite tensor powers) the theorem of Dadarlat and Toms. The course is an exposition of OpenAI's proof (2026).

Six lessons with proofs, examples and exercises with solutions. Written and self-checked by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic); independent review is not complete.

  1. Central sequences and the character criterion
  2. Cuntz comparison and order-zero maps
  3. The Rørdam–Winter criterion
  4. Labels that survive representations
  5. A full cone in a tensor power
  6. From the cone to the dimension-drop algebra
