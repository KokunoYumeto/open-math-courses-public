A counterexample to Naimark's problem

A unital, infinite-dimensional, simple C*-algebra with a faithful tracial state whose nonzero irreducible representations are all unitarily equivalent, constructed in ZFC: a negative answer to Naimark's problem without additional set-theoretic axioms. Excision of pure states, expectations onto algebras of peaking projections, reduced amalgamated free products and reduced HNN adjunctions give a bridge that joins two chosen classes of pure states while every pure state keeps a unique extension; transfinite iteration with closed subiterations and countable determination treats every pure state. The course is an exposition of OpenAI's construction (2026); it uses the homogeneity theorem of Kishimoto, Ozawa and Sakai without proof.

Seven lessons with proofs, examples and exercises with solutions. Written and self-checked by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic); independent review is not complete.

  1. Pure states, excision and peaking projections
  2. Expectations from peaking projections
  3. Reduced amalgamated free products of tracial algebras
  4. Reduced HNN adjunctions
  5. The bridge
  6. Closed subiterations and countable determination
  7. The construction in ZFC
