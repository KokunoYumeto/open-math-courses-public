Random sampling in expanding graphs

Written by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), October 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. Public domain (CC0).

The pieces produced in Splitting a graph at a scale have order between \(D^{0.90}\) and \(D^{1.02}\) and cut expansion of order \(D^{0.90}\). Two random operations on such a piece are needed later: dividing its edges into a few classes that each keep a fixed fraction of the cut expansion, and choosing a random set of vertices that still induces a graph with good cut expansion. This lesson proves both. The second is the delicate one: a sampled set has far too many subsets to control each cut separately, and the proof approximates every possible bad cut by a cut from a small family fixed in advance.

All probability spaces in this lesson are finite. Logarithms are to base two and \(\ln\) is the natural logarithm. Throughout, \(D\) is a large real number, and \[ \sigma=D^{0.90},\qquad\tau=D^{0.74}. \] Statements "for all sufficiently large \(D\)" hold with a threshold that depends only on the constants fixed in the statement, uniformly over all graphs and orders allowed there.

1. Tail bounds

Lemma 1.1 (Markov's inequality). For a random variable \(X\ge0\) and \(a>0\), \(\Pr(X\ge a)\le\mathbb EX/a\).

Proof. \(X\ge a\mathbf 1_{\{X\ge a\}}\); take expectations. \(\square\)

A binomial random variable is a sum of independent indicator variables with a common success probability; its mean is the number of indicators times that probability.

Lemma 1.2 (Chernoff bounds). If \(X\) is binomial with mean \(\mu\), then \[ \begin{gathered} \Pr\Bigl(X\le\frac\mu2\Bigr)\le e^{-\mu/8},\\ \Pr(X\ge2\mu)\le e^{-\mu/3}. \end{gathered} \]

Proof. Let \(X=X_1+\dots+X_m\) with independent indicators of probability \(\pi\), so \(\mu=m\pi\). For real \(t\), independence gives \[ \mathbb Ee^{tX}=\prod_{i=1}^m\bigl(1+\pi(e^t-1)\bigr)\le e^{\mu(e^t-1)}, \] using \(1+y\le e^y\). With \(t=\ln2\) and Lemma 1.1 applied to \(e^{tX}\), \[ \begin{aligned} \Pr(X\ge2\mu)&\le e^{\mu(2-1)-2\mu\ln2}\\ &=e^{-\mu(2\ln2-1)} . \end{aligned} \] With \(t=-\ln2\), the event \(X\le\mu/2\) is the event \(e^{tX}\ge e^{t\mu/2}\), so \[ \begin{aligned} \Pr\Bigl(X\le\frac\mu2\Bigr)&\le e^{\mu(\frac12-1)+\frac\mu2\ln2}\\ &=e^{-\mu\frac{1-\ln2}2} . \end{aligned} \] Finally \(2\ln2-1>0.38>1/3\) and \((1-\ln2)/2>0.15>1/8\). \(\square\)

Together with the union bound, \(\Pr(\bigcup_iE_i)\le\sum_i\Pr(E_i)\), these are the only probabilistic tools of the course.

2. Splitting the edges

Lemma 2.1 (edge splitting). Fix \(c>0\) and an integer \(l\ge1\). For all sufficiently large \(D\): if a graph \(P\) of order \(r\) with \(D^{0.90}\le r\le D^{1.02}\) has cut expansion at least \(h_0\ge cD^{0.90}\), then \(E(P)\) can be partitioned into \(l\) spanning subgraphs, each of cut expansion at least \(h_0/(2l)\).

Proof. Put every edge into one of \(l\) classes, independently and uniformly. Fix \(U\subseteq V(P)\) with \(u=|U|\) and \(1\le u\le r/2\), and let \(e=|\partial_PU|\ge h_0u\). In a fixed class the number of edges of \(\partial_PU\) is binomial with mean \(e/l\ge h_0u/l\). By Lemma 1.2 it is less than \(h_0u/(2l)\) with probability at most \(e^{-h_0u/(8l)}\). There are at most \(r^u\) sets \(U\) of size \(u\), so the probability that some class fails at some cut is at most \[ l\sum_{u\ge1}\rho^u,\qquad\rho=re^{-h_0/(8l)} . \] Since \(r\le D^{1.02}\) and \(h_0\ge cD^{0.90}\), the ratio \(\rho\) tends to zero uniformly, and for large \(D\) the sum \(l\rho/(1-\rho)\) is less than one. Some assignment therefore succeeds. A cut is controlled by its smaller side, so each class, taken on all of \(V(P)\), has cut expansion at least \(h_0/(2l)\). \(\square\)

3. Sampling the vertices

For \(A,B\subseteq V(P)\) disjoint, \(e_P(A,B)\) is the number of edges of \(P\) with one end in \(A\) and the other in \(B\). For \(V'\subseteq V(P)\), \(P[V']\) is the subgraph induced on \(V'\).

Lemma 3.1 (vertex sampling). Fix \(c'>0\) and \(p\in(0,1)\). Let \(P\) be a graph of order \(r\) with \(D^{0.90}\le r\le D^{1.02}\) and cut expansion at least \(h=c'D^{0.90}\). Choose every vertex independently with probability \(p\), forming \(V'\). Then, with probability \(1-o(1)\) as \(D\to\infty\), uniformly in \(P\): \[ \begin{gathered} |V'|\le2pr,\\ |N_P(v)\cap V'|\ge\frac{ph}2\quad(v\in V(P)),\\ P[V']\text{ has cut expansion at least }\tau . \end{gathered} \]

Proof. Size and degrees. By Lemma 1.2 of Routing in graphs with cut expansion, every vertex has degree at least \(h\). So \(|N_P(v)\cap V'|\) is binomial with mean at least \(ph\), and Lemma 1.2 bounds the probability that it is at most \(ph/2\) by \(e^{-ph/8}\). Also \(|V'|\) is binomial with mean \(pr\). The first two assertions fail with probability at most \[ e^{-pr/3}+re^{-ph/8}=o(1). \]

A family of witness sets, fixed before sampling. Put \[ M=\frac{16}p\log r,\qquad s=\Bigl\lceil\frac{4rM}h\Bigr\rceil . \] For large \(D\), \(M/h<1\) and \(s\le r\). Let \(\mathcal U\) be the family of all unions \(\bigcup_{v\in S}N_P(v)\) of neighbourhoods in \(P\), over sets \(S\subseteq V(P)\) with \(|S|\le s\). Then \[ |\mathcal U|\le\sum_{t=0}^s\binom rt\le(s+1)r^s . \]

Claim 1. With probability \(1-o(1)\), every \(U\in\mathcal U\) with \(\min\{|U|,r-|U|\}\ge ph/8\) satisfies \[ e_P(U\cap V',\,V'\setminus U)\ge\frac{p^2}4|\partial_PU| . \]

Fix such a \(U\) and put \(e=|\partial_PU|\); cut expansion gives \(e\ge ph^2/8\). Colour the edges of \(\partial_PU\) greedily so that edges of one colour share no vertex: an edge meets fewer than \(2r\) other edges, so \(2r\) colours suffice, and the colour classes are matchings. Fix this colouring before sampling. Classes with fewer than \(e/(4r)\) edges contain fewer than \(e/2\) edges in total. In a class with \(m\ge e/(4r)\) edges, the edges have pairwise disjoint ends, so the events that both ends of an edge are sampled are independent, each of probability \(p^2\). The number of edges of the class with both ends in \(V'\) is binomial with mean \(p^2m\), and by Lemma 1.2 it is less than \(p^2m/2\) with probability at most \[ e^{-p^2m/8}\le e^{-p^2e/(32r)}\le e^{-p^3h^2/(256r)} . \] If no large class fails, at least \(\frac{p^2}2\cdot\frac e2\) edges of \(\partial_PU\) have both ends sampled, and each of them joins \(U\cap V'\) to \(V'\setminus U\). The union bound over the at most \(2r|\mathcal U|\) pairs of a set and a class bounds the failure probability by \[ 2r(s+1)r^se^{-p^3h^2/(256r)} . \] Here \(h^2/r\ge c'^2D^{0.78}\), while \(r/h\le D^{0.12}/c'\) and \(M=O(\log D)\), so the logarithm of \(2r(s+1)r^s\) is \(O(D^{0.12}\log^2D)\), with constants depending on \(p\) and \(c'\). The bound is \(o(1)\), which proves Claim 1.

Approximating a bad cut. Fix an outcome satisfying the size and degree bounds and Claim 1. Suppose that some \(A\subseteq V'\) satisfies \[ \begin{gathered} 0<a=|A|\le\frac{|V'|}2,\\ e_P(A,V'\setminus A)<\tau a . \end{gathered} \] We derive a contradiction for large \(D\); take \(D\) so large that \(\tau<ph/4\).

Step 1: \(a>ph/4\). Otherwise a vertex of \(A\) has at least \(ph/2\) sampled neighbours, of which at most \(a-1<ph/4\) lie in \(A\); so it has more than \(ph/4\) neighbours in \(V'\setminus A\), and \(e_P(A,V'\setminus A)>aph/4>\tau a\).

Step 2: few vertices of \(A\) look outward. Let \(A_{\rm good}\) be the set of \(v\in A\) with \(|N_P(v)\cap A|\ge ph/4\). A vertex of \(A\setminus A_{\rm good}\) has more than \(ph/4\) neighbours in \(V'\setminus A\), so \[ |A\setminus A_{\rm good}|\le\frac{4\tau a}{ph}. \]

Step 3: a small hitting set. With \(V'\) and \(A\) fixed, choose a random set \(S\subseteq A\), containing each vertex independently with probability \(q=M/h\). For \(v\in A_{\rm good}\), the probability that \(S\) misses \(N_P(v)\cap A\) is at most \[ (1-q)^{ph/4}\le e^{-pM/4}=e^{-4\log r}\le r^{-4}, \] so with probability at least \(1-r^{-3}\ge7/8\), the set \(S\) meets the neighbourhood of every vertex of \(A_{\rm good}\). Let \(T(S)\) be the number of vertices of \(V'\setminus A\) with a neighbour in \(S\). Then \(\mathbb E|S|=aM/h\) and \[ \begin{aligned} \mathbb ET(S)&\le\frac Mh\sum_{w\in V'\setminus A}|N_P(w)\cap A|\\ &=\frac Mhe_P(A,V'\setminus A)<\frac{\tau Ma}h . \end{aligned} \] By Lemma 1.1, \(|S|>4aM/h\) and \(T(S)>4\tau Ma/h\) each have probability at most \(1/4\). The three failure probabilities add up to less than one, so there is a set \(S\subseteq A\) with \[ \begin{gathered} |S|\le\frac{4aM}h,\qquad T(S)\le\frac{4\tau Ma}h,\\ S\cap N_P(v)\ne\varnothing\quad(v\in A_{\rm good}). \end{gathered} \] This auxiliary choice only proves that \(S\) exists; Claim 1 was established for the whole family \(\mathcal U\) in advance.

Step 4: comparison. Put \(U=\bigcup_{v\in S}N_P(v)\). Since \(|S|\le4rM/h\le s\), \(U\in\mathcal U\). Every vertex of \(A_{\rm good}\) has a neighbour in \(S\) and so lies in \(U\); every vertex of \((U\cap V')\setminus A\) is counted by \(T(S)\). Hence \[ \delta=\bigl|(U\cap V')\,\triangle\,A\bigr|\le\frac{4\tau a}{ph}+\frac{4\tau Ma}h . \] Since \(\tau M/h=O(D^{-0.16}\log D)\), we have \(\delta=o(a)\). Both sides of the cut of \(U\) in \(P\) are large: \[ \begin{aligned} |U|&\ge a-\delta,\\ r-|U|&\ge|V'\setminus U|\\ &\ge|V'\setminus A|-\delta\ge a-\delta , \end{aligned} \] and for large \(D\) both are at least \(a/2>ph/8\). Claim 1 and cut expansion give \[ e_P(U\cap V',V'\setminus U)\ge\frac{p^2}4h\frac a2=\frac{p^2ha}8 . \] On the other hand, moving one vertex of \(V'\) from one side to the other changes the number of crossing edges by at most \(r-1\). Turning \(A\) into \(U\cap V'\) takes \(\delta\) such moves, so \[ e_P(U\cap V',V'\setminus U)<\tau a+r\delta . \] Dividing by \(ha\), the right side becomes at most \[ \begin{aligned} &\frac\tau h+O\Bigl(\frac{\tau Mr}{h^2}\Bigr)\\ &\quad=O(D^{-0.16})+O(D^{-0.04}\log D), \end{aligned} \] which is less than \(p^2/8\) for large \(D\). This contradiction shows that no such \(A\) exists.

A set \(X\subseteq V'\) with \(|X|>|V'|/2\) is handled through its complement in \(V'\). So \(P[V']\) has cut expansion at least \(\tau\), and the three assertions hold with probability \(1-o(1)\). Every \(o(1)\) above depends only on \(D\), once \(p\) and \(c'\) are fixed. \(\square\)

A direct union bound over all subsets of \(V'\) would fail. The edges leaving a set \(A\) may end at few outside vertices, each joined to \(A\) by many edges, and then the probability that few of them survive the sampling is only about \(e^{-pha/r}\), far more than \(r^{-a}\). The witness family has \(e^{O(D^{0.12}\log^2D)}\) members, each controlled with failure probability \(e^{-\Omega(D^{0.78})}\), and every bad cut is close to one of them; this is where the gap between the exponents \(0.78\) and \(0.12\) is used.

4. Exercises

4.1 (easy). Prove \(1+y\le e^y\) for all real \(y\).

4.2 (easy). Show that a greedy colouring of the edges of a simple graph of order \(r\) uses at most \(2r-3\) colours, with every colour class a matching.

4.3 (medium). Let \(X\) be binomial with mean \(\mu\). Show that \(\Pr(X\ge3\mu)\le e^{-\mu}\).

4.4 (medium). In Step 3, the vertices of \(S\) are chosen inside \(A\). Explain why choosing them in all of \(V(P)\) would not work with the same estimate for \(T(S)\).

5. Solutions

4.1. The function \(e^y-1-y\) has derivative \(e^y-1\), negative for \(y<0\) and positive for \(y>0\), so its minimum is its value \(0\) at \(y=0\).

4.2. An edge \(xy\) meets at most \(r-2\) other edges at \(x\) and at most \(r-2\) at \(y\). When it is coloured, at most \(2r-4\) colours are excluded, so one of \(2r-3\) colours is free.

4.3. With \(t=\ln3\), the bound on \(\mathbb Ee^{tX}\) and Lemma 1.1 give \[ \begin{aligned} \Pr(X\ge3\mu)&\le e^{\mu(3-1)-3\mu\ln3}\\ &=e^{-\mu(3\ln3-2)}, \end{aligned} \] and \(3\ln3-2>1.29>1\).

4.4. The estimate \(\mathbb ET(S)\le\frac Mhe_P(A,V'\setminus A)\) counts, for each outside vertex \(w\), its neighbours that could enter \(S\). If \(S\) could contain vertices outside \(A\), a vertex of \(V'\setminus A\) could have many neighbours outside \(A\), and \(T(S)\) would not be controlled by the few edges leaving \(A\).
