Open Mathematics CoursesCourse contents (The Erdős–Gallai cycle decomposition theorem)

Results on path and cycle decompositions, disjoint representatives, routing, expanding pieces, folding and the linear bound, with their assumptions

The course proves the statements below; each link leads to the statement with its assumptions and its proof. The lessons are self-checked by the writing AI; independent mathematical review is not complete.

  • Parity of end counts — Lemma 1.1. In a decomposition into paths and cycles, the number of paths ending at a vertex has the parity of its degree; every odd vertex is an end of a path. No hypothesis.
  • Lovász's decomposition theorem — Theorem 2.1. Every graph decomposes into at most floor(sigma/2) paths and cycles, sigma the number of vertices of positive degree; so a graph with n vertices needs at most floor(n/2). No hypothesis.
  • Paths with at most two ends at each vertex — Corollary 3.1. Every graph with n vertices decomposes into at most n paths, every vertex being an end of at most two of them. No hypothesis.
  • Even graphs decompose into cycles — Lemma 4.1. A graph whose degrees are all even has a decomposition into cycles. No hypothesis.
  • Haxell's condition — Theorem 1.1. If, for every nonempty set I of indices, the union of the hypergraphs indexed by I (edges of size at most s) has a matching with more than (2s-1)(|I|-1) edges, then the family has pairwise disjoint representatives. No hypothesis.
  • Basic facts on cut expansion — Lemma 1.2. A graph of order r >= 2 with cut expansion h > 0 has minimum degree at least h, satisfies h < r, and is connected.
  • Routing theorem — Theorem 2.1. If h >= 128(l^2(k+b)+z), demands with at most k ends at a vertex, teams of at most b demands and forbidden sets of at most z vertices are served by edge-disjoint paths of length at most l, with disjoint inner vertices within each team.
  • Length of the routing paths — Corollary 2.2. The length bound of the routing theorem satisfies l <= 108 (r/h) (log r)^2.
  • Vertex expansion gives a long cycle — Lemma 1.1. For large u: if every vertex set U with 0 < |U| <= u/2 has at least alpha |U| external neighbours, alpha = 1/(32 log^2 u), then the graph has a cycle longer than alpha^2 u/100.
  • Splitting at a scale — Lemma 2.1. For large D, a graph of order N and average degree at most D splits into at most N/(2D^0.01) cycles of length at least D^1.01 and overlapping boxes of order at most D^1.02 and total order at most (1 + 2/log D)N; each box splits into pieces of cut expansion D^0.90 and a residual of average degree at most D^0.95.
  • Chernoff bounds — Lemma 1.2. A binomial variable with mean mu is at most mu/2 with probability at most e^(-mu/8), and at least 2 mu with probability at most e^(-mu/3). No hypothesis.
  • Edge splitting — Lemma 2.1. For large D, a graph of order between D^0.90 and D^1.02 with cut expansion h_0 >= c D^0.90 splits into l spanning subgraphs of cut expansion at least h_0/(2l).
  • Vertex sampling — Lemma 3.1. In such a graph with cut expansion c' D^0.90, a p-random vertex set has at most 2pr vertices, gives every vertex at least ph/2 sampled neighbours and induces cut expansion at least D^0.74, with probability 1 - o(1).
  • Small residue — Lemma 1.1. For large D, a graph of order r between D^0.90 and D^1.02 with a spanning subgraph of cut expansion c D^0.90 decomposes into at most 9r cycles and edges and at most three residual graphs on disjoint vertex sets of total order at most eta r.
  • Pair folding — Lemma 1.1. If the folding sets have at least max(8, a^2) vertices of degree at most a and every vertex has at most a neighbours in each, pairings exist such that, after deleting at most 2 sum m_i edges, the quotient is simple with unique representatives.
  • Batch resolution — Lemma 2.1. Batches with active sets in expanding routers have quotients of order |W| - sum floor(|X|/2); any cycle-and-edge partitions of the quotients lift to the batch edges and some router edges at an extra cost of 8 times the total active order.
  • Choice of a prefix — Lemma 1.1. A nonnegative, finitely supported, nonzero sequence has a positive term S_i such that the sum of S_j over j <= i + K is at most 4(K+1) S_i. No hypothesis.
  • The Erdős–Gallai cycle decomposition theorem (OpenAI) — Theorem 7.1. The edges of every graph with n vertices can be partitioned into at most Cn cycles and single edges, for an absolute constant C. No hypothesis.
  • Eulerian graphs (second part under GGKO) — Corollary 7.2. Every Eulerian graph with n vertices decomposes into at most Cn cycles; under the named statement (GGKO), into at most Delta/2 + delta n cycles when all large cuts are dense.
