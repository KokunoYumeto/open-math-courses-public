\[ |\partial^I f(x,t_2)-\partial^I f(x,t_1)|\le C_I|t_2-t_1|. \tag{PL6.1} \]

It is uniformly Cauchy as \(t_1,t_2\uparrow1\). Denote its continuous limit by \(f_I(x)\). The exact exterior heat factor is also directly checkable. Original (A.32) defines

\[ a_K=1+h,\qquad \mathcal H(Z)=\frac1{\Gamma(1+h)}\int_0^\infty e^{-v}v^h(1+Zv)^{-h}\,dv, \qquad Z\ge0. \tag{PL6.2} \]

\[ \mathcal H^{(m)}(Z)=\frac{(-1)^m(h)_m}{\Gamma(1+h)} \int_0^\infty e^{-v}v^{h+m}(1+Zv)^{-h-m}\,dv, \quad \sup_{Z\ge0}|\mathcal H^{(m)}(Z)|\le(h)_m(1+h)_m. \tag{PL6.3} \]

The dominating function is the displayed integrand with its last factor replaced by one; it is integrable at zero since \(h+m>-1\) and at infinity by exponential decay. Gamma integration by parts gives \(\Gamma(1+h+m)/\Gamma(1+h)=(1+h)_m\). Dominated convergence proves smooth right derivatives at zero of all orders, where equality is \(\mathcal H^{(m)}(0)=(-1)^m(h)_m(1+h)_m\). No convergent Taylor series is asserted.

The finite Taylor remainder can be retained exactly. For every integer \(n\ge0\) and \(Z\ge0\), the fundamental theorem of calculus and (PL6.3) give

\[ \begin{split} \mathcal H(Z)&=\sum_{m=0}^n\frac{(-1)^m(h)_m(1+h)_m}{m!}Z^m+R_n(Z),\\ R_n(Z)&=\frac1{n!}\int_0^Z(Z-s)^n\mathcal H^{(n+1)}(s)\,ds,\\ |R_n(Z)|&\le\frac{(h)_{n+1}(1+h)_{n+1}}{(n+1)!}Z^{n+1}. \end{split} \tag{PL6.3a} \]

LC4. The actual potential sum and its residual

Take \(U_0=(u^{[0]},p^{[0]})\) to be the actual initialized field, with its potential \(\mathcal A_0\) and angular coefficient \(\mathcal B_0\). It is already defined throughout \(q<q_{\rm big}\), so no additional initialization cutoff is needed on \(q<q_*\). For j≥1 retain exactly

\[ Z_j=(A_j,B_j^{\rm vec},p_j),\qquad \Delta u_j=\operatorname{curl}A_j+B_j^{\rm vec},\qquad (u^{[J]},p^{[J]})=U_0+\sum_{j=1}^J(\Delta u_j,p_j), \tag{LC4.1} \]

where \(A_j\) contains the wave and azimuthal mean potentials, \(B_j^{\rm vec}\) is the direct angular mean field, and \(p_j\) is the entire pressure difference. The exact telescoping pressure formula is PL0.3. PL1–PL2 give

\[ |Z_j|_m+|\Delta u_j|_m \le C_{j,m}q^{g_j-\ell_m}(1+|\log q|)^{P_{j,m}},\qquad g_j=hj/10,\quad\ell_m=2A+(m+1)(1+3h/2). \tag{LC4.2} \]

The physical derivative costs include the carrier frequency, the full auxiliary graph and the cylindrical frame. The finite J is fixed for this estimate. Consequently PL2.4–PL2.5 have actual inputs and yield

\[ \begin{split} |\mathscr R(u^{[j]},p^{[j]})|_m &\le C_{j,m}q^{h\sigma_j-K_m^R}(1+|\log q|)^{P_{j,m}}+E_{j,m},\\ |(u^{[j]},p^{[j]})|_m &\le C_{j,m}q^{-K_m^U}(1+|\log q|)^{P_{j,m}},\\ K_m^R&=2A+1/2+m(1+3h/2),\qquad K_m^U=\max\{2A+m,\ell_{m+1}\}, \end{split} \tag{LC4.3} \]

where \(E_{j,m}\le C_{j,m,N}q^N\) for every fixed j,m,N. These exponents are independent of j. Outside the correction shell the finite fields equal the base; its stress is zero there, so Z5 supplies the residual bound. This proves the required uniformity including the axis on every \(0\le X\le X_1<\infty\).

For one smooth cutoff χ, one on [0,1/2] and zero on [1,∞), PL3–PL4 bound every complete cut increment by

uniformly in \(a>0\). This includes \(a\chi'(aq)\nabla q\times A_j\); no transition term is discarded. Choose \(a_1^{-1}<q_*\), \(a_{j+1}\ge2a_j\), so that for m≤j and \(0<q\le a_j^{-1}\), \(\widehat C_{j,m}(1+|\log q|)^{\widehat P_{j,m}}q^{g_j/2}\le2^{-j}\). Every finite list is possible because \(g_j>0\); PL4 proves the required logarithmic limit. The same choice can include the cut potentials and direct fields themselves, using their weaker derivative bounds from LC4.2. Now set

\[ \begin{split} \mathcal A&=\mathcal A_0+\sum_{j\ge1}\chi(a_jq)A_j,\\ \mathcal Be_\theta&=\mathcal B_0e_\theta+\sum_{j\ge1}\chi(a_jq)B_j^{\rm vec},\\ p&=p^{[0]}+\sum_{j\ge1}\chi(a_jq)p_j,\qquad u=\operatorname{curl}\mathcal A+\mathcal Be_\theta. \end{split} \tag{LC4.5} \]

Only finitely many terms survive on a neighborhood with q bounded below. Thus these are actual smooth fields on \(\Omega_* =\{(x,t):\tau>0,\ q<q_*\}\). Curl has zero divergence and \(\operatorname{div}(\mathcal Be_\theta)=r^{-1}\partial_\theta\mathcal B=0\). The axis representatives in LC0.2–LC0.4 are smooth, and every added correction is supported away from the axis for fixed q. This proves all of Theorem 3.1(i).

For J≥max{1,m} and \(q<(2a_J)^{-1}\), every first cutoff is one and the finite comparison is

\[ |(u,p)-(u^{[J]},p^{[J]})|_m \le2^{-J}q^{g_{J+1}/2-\ell'_m}. \tag{LC4.6} \]

\[ g_{J+1}/2-\ell'_r\ge N+K_r^U+1,\qquad h\sigma_J-K_m^R\ge N+1. \tag{LC4.7} \]

These are possible since \(g_j\) and \(h\sigma_j\) tend to infinity. For this fixed J, take q small enough to absorb the finitely many logarithms and constants in LC4.3 into \(q^{-1}\). Put \(v=u^{[J]}\), \(e=u-v\), \(\pi=p-p^{[J]}\). The exact residual difference is

\[ \mathscr R(v+e,p^{[J]}+\pi)-\mathscr R(v,p^{[J]}) =\partial_te-\Delta e+\nabla\pi+(v\cdot\nabla)e+(e\cdot\nabla)v+(e\cdot\nabla)e. \tag{LC4.8} \]

After m derivatives, its norm is bounded by \(C_m(|e|_{m+2}+|\pi|_{m+1}+|v|_{m+1}|e|_{m+1}+|e|_{m+1}^2)\), by the full finite product rule. LC4.6–LC4.7 make every term \(O(q^N)\). The finite-state residual in LC4.3 also is \(O(q^N)\), including its retained \(E_{J,m}\). Hence

\[ |\partial_x^\alpha\partial_t^b\mathscr R(u,p)| \le C_{\alpha,b,N,X_1}q^N\qquad(0\le X\le X_1,\ q\downarrow0) \tag{LC4.9} \]

for every finite \(X_1\) and every \(\alpha,b,N\). One finite comparison, rather than a sum of infinitely many residual errors, proves the claim. This is the full flat-residual part of Theorem 3.1(iii).

LC8. Five exercises with complete solutions

Exercise 1. The original coordinate map and its second derivatives

For fixed \(\nu>0\), prove that the original map from \((q_\nu,\eta_\nu)\) to \((z_\nu,t)\) is invertible on \(q_\nu>0,|\eta_\nu|<1\). Compute its first derivatives, retain the derivatives of \(X_\nu\), and give exact second derivatives of \(q_\nu\). Keep the physical viscosity in every spatial derivative.

Solution. Throughout this exercise write \(q=q_\nu,\eta=\eta_\nu,X=X_\nu\) in the displayed calculations; these remain the original physical variables. Put \(L=1-2h\eta^2\), \(D=1/2-h\). The map is

\[ z_\nu=\sqrt\nu q^D\eta,\qquad t=1-q(1-\eta^2). \tag{LC8.1} \]

In the row order \((z_\nu,t)\) and column order \((q,\eta)\), its full matrix and determinant are

\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{\partial(z_\nu,t)}{\partial(q,\eta)} =\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt\nu Dq^{D-1}\eta&\sqrt\nu q^D\\ -(1-\eta^2)&2q\eta\end{pmatrix},\\ \det=\sqrt\nu q^D\{2D\eta^2+1-\eta^2\} =\sqrt\nu q^D L>0. \end{gathered} \tag{LC8.2} \]

For fixed \(\tau=1-t>0\), the equation \(q^{1-2h}-\tau q^{-2h}=z_\nu^2/\nu\) has derivative \((1-2h)q^{-2h}+2h\tau q^{-2h-1}>0\), with limits minus infinity and plus infinity. Thus there is exactly one root. It satisfies \(q\ge\tau\), and \(\eta^2=1-\tau/q<1\). This proves global uniqueness as well as the local inverse supplied by (LC8.2). Its exact derivatives are

\[ \begin{aligned} q_{z_\nu}&=\nu^{-1/2}q^{1-D}\frac{2\eta}{L},&q_t&=-\frac1L,\\ \eta_{z_\nu}&=\nu^{-1/2}q^{-D}\frac{1-\eta^2}{L},& \eta_t&=\frac{D\eta}{qL},\\ X_{r_\nu}&=\frac{r_\nu}{\nu q},&X_t&=\frac{X}{qL},\\ X_{z_\nu}&=-\nu^{-1/2}\frac{2\eta Xq^{-D}}L,& q_{r_\nu}&=\eta_{r_\nu}=0. \end{aligned} \tag{LC8.3} \]

For any real \(\lambda\) and smooth \(F\), the full chain rules are

\[ \begin{aligned} \partial_{z_\nu}(q^\lambda F(\eta)) &=\nu^{-1/2}q^{\lambda-D} \frac{2\lambda\eta F+(1-\eta^2)F'}L,\\ \partial_t(q^\lambda F(\eta)) &=q^{\lambda-1}\frac{-\lambda F+D\eta F'}L. \end{aligned} \tag{LC8.4} \]

Apply them to each first derivative in (LC8.3). In particular, differentiating \(2\eta/L\) gives \(2(1+2h\eta^2)/L^2\), and differentiating \(-1/L\) gives \(-4h\eta/L^2\). Hence

\[ \begin{aligned} q_{z_\nu z_\nu} &=\nu^{-1}q^{1-2D} \frac{4(1-D)\eta^2L+2(1-\eta^2)(1+2h\eta^2)}{L^3},\\ q_{z_\nu t} &=\nu^{-1/2}q^{-D} \frac{-2(1-D)\eta L+2D\eta(1+2h\eta^2)}{L^3},\\ q_{tt}&=-\frac{4hD\eta^2}{qL^3}. \end{aligned} \tag{LC8.5} \]

The mixed derivative obtained by differentiating \(-1/L\) in \(z_\nu\) is \(-4h\nu^{-1/2}q^{-D}\eta(1-\eta^2)/L^3\). It equals the middle line because \(D=1/2-h\); expanding its numerator retains and combines exactly those two terms. Thus the mixed derivatives agree. Repeated use of (LC8.4) proves all higher derivative identities in PL3.5 with smooth coefficient functions on the closed interval \([-1,1]\), since \(L\ge1-2h>0\).

At \(\tau=0,z_\nu\ne0\), the same map has \(q=(|z_\nu|/\sqrt\nu)^{1/D}>0\) and \(\eta=\operatorname{sgn}z_\nu\). Equations (LC8.2)–(LC8.5) remain one-sided smooth there. This is the boundary used in LC5; it makes no regularity assertion at \(q=0\).

Exercise 2. Every cutoff term and the two preserved velocity moments

Take one actual physical increment \(\Delta u=\operatorname{curl}A+b e_\theta\), with \(b\) independent of \(\theta\). For \(f=\chi(aq_\nu)\), compute the exact cut velocity and its divergence. Then specialize to its mean potential \(\Psi e_\theta\), and prove preservation of both zero velocity moments. Include the wave part.

Solution. Since \(q_\nu\) depends on \(z_\nu,t\) only,

\[ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{curl}(fA)+fb e_\theta &=f\Delta u+a\chi'(aq_\nu)q_{\nu,z_\nu} (A_r e_\theta-A_\theta e_r),\\ q_{\nu,z_\nu}&=\nu^{-1/2} \frac{2\eta_\nu q_\nu^{1-D}}{1-2h\eta_\nu^2}. \end{aligned} \tag{LC8.6} \]

The added term follows from \(e_z\times e_r=e_\theta\), \(e_z\times e_\theta=-e_r\), and \(e_z\times e_z=0\). In Cartesian components, equality of mixed derivatives gives \(\operatorname{div}(\nabla f\times A)=-\nabla f\cdot\operatorname{curl}A\). This cancels the extra term in \(\operatorname{div}(f\operatorname{curl}A)=\nabla f\cdot\operatorname{curl}A\). Also \(\operatorname{div}(fb e_\theta)=r_\nu^{-1}\partial_\theta(fb)=0\). Thus the complete velocity in (LC8.6) is exactly divergence-free. This proof includes the extra curl term rather than discarding it.

For the mean potential, write the original components as

\[ \beta=-\partial_{z_\nu}\Psi,\qquad v=b,\qquad \gamma=(\partial_{r_\nu}+r_\nu^{-1})\Psi. \tag{LC8.7} \]

The cut components are exactly

\[ \beta^f=f\beta-a\chi'(aq_\nu)q_{\nu,z_\nu}\Psi, \qquad v^f=fv,\qquad\gamma^f=f\gamma. \tag{LC8.8} \]

The last identity uses \(\partial_{r_\nu}f=0\). Every potential is compactly supported away from the radial axis at fixed \(q_\nu>0\), with smooth zero extension. Since \(f\) is independent of radius and the auxiliary torus variable, Haar averaging and integration give

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_0^\infty r_\nu^2\langle v^f\rangle_Y\,dr_\nu &=f\int_0^\infty r_\nu^2\langle v\rangle_Y\,dr_\nu=0,\\ \int_0^\infty r_\nu\langle\gamma^f\rangle_Y\,dr_\nu &=f\,[r_\nu\langle\Psi\rangle_Y]_{0}^{\infty}=0. \end{aligned} \tag{LC8.9} \]

For a wave potential, every cylindrical component has angular multiplier \(kmp_\gamma\), as in the finite-cycle lesson. Here \(kp_\gamma\ne0\) is a fixed integer for each label, and \(m\ne0\) is an integer, so their product is a nonzero integer. Its coefficient and the cutoff are independent of \(\theta\). The full cylindrical curl differentiates that multiplier but never changes it; the frame connection terms in the component formula retain the same multiplier. Equation (LC8.6) also retains it. All cylindrical component angular means are therefore zero. The wave part adds nothing to either moment in (LC8.9).

In a band these physical moments retain the exact multipliers \(\nu^2Q^{3/2-A}\) and \(\nu^{3/2}Q^{1-A}\) from FC9.3. No cancellation uses a change of viscosity. The two preserved velocity moments do not assert that the nonlinear residual defects vanish on cutoff transition regions. Those regions are included in the complete residual estimate PL5.

Exercise 5. All terminal derivatives of the exterior swirl and pressure

The integral defining \(\mathcal H\) in PL6.2 gives \(\mathcal H^{(b)}(0)=(-1)^b(h)_b(1+h)_b\) for every nonnegative integer \(b\). Since \(\partial_t=-\partial_\tau\), all signs cancel in the swirl derivative, giving

\[ \left.\partial_t^bK_\nu\right|_{t=1^-} =C_\infty4^b\nu^{1+h+b}r_\nu^{-d-2b}(h)_b(1+h)_b. \tag{LC8.19} \]

This includes \(b=0\), with both rising factorials equal to one. Every term in the differentiated pressure integrand is integrable on \([r_\nu,\infty)\): PL6.7 bounds it by a constant times \(\rho^{-2d-2b-1}\), and \(d>0\). Dominated convergence and the full binomial product rule therefore give

\[ \begin{aligned} \left.\partial_t^b p_{{\rm ext},\nu}\right|_{t=1^-} ={}&-\frac{C_\infty^2 4^b\nu^{2+2h+b}} {(2d+2b)r_\nu^{2d+2b}}\\ &\times\sum_{i=0}^{b}\binom bi (h)_i(1+h)_i(h)_{b-i}(1+h)_{b-i}. \end{aligned} \tag{LC8.20} \]

The denominator comes from the actual integral \(\int_{r_\nu}^{\infty}\rho^{-2d-2b-1}d\rho =r_\nu^{-2d-2b}/(2d+2b)\); the upper endpoint vanishes because \(d+b>0\). For \(b=0\), radial differentiation gives exactly \(\partial_{r_\nu}p_{{\rm ext},\nu}=K_\nu(r_\nu,0)^2/r_\nu\), preserving the centrifugal cancellation.

For the first swirl derivative, (LC8.19) gives \(\partial_tK_\nu=C_\infty4h(1+h)\nu^{2+h}r_\nu^{-d-2}\) at the boundary. Applying the full swirl Laplacian to its boundary value gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \nu(\partial_{r_\nu}^2+r_\nu^{-1}\partial_{r_\nu}-r_\nu^{-2}) (C_\infty\nu^{1+h}r_\nu^{-d}) &=C_\infty\nu^{2+h}\{d(d+1)-d-1\}r_\nu^{-d-2}\\ &=C_\infty\nu^{2+h}4h(1+h)r_\nu^{-d-2}. \end{aligned} \tag{LC8.21} \]

The second equality uses \(d=1+2h\) and retains the separate second-derivative, first-derivative and frame contributions in the first line. This verifies the original heat equation at the boundary. The full equation for \(\tau\ge0\) is already proved from the integral in PL6.4–PL6.6. These are smooth one-sided derivatives.
