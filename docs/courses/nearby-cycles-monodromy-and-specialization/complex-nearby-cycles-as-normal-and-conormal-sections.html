Nearby cycles, monodromy and specialization

Complex nearby cycles as normal and conormal sections

A complex normal line has two useful sections. A normal vector on which the defining function has derivative one computes nearby cycles. The conormal covector given by that derivative computes vanishing cycles. The second assertion depends on the Fourier convention and on including the endpoint of a closed ray. We prove both comparisons, then deduce constructibility and the support bound for an arbitrary holomorphic function.

Let kk be a commutative ring of finite global dimension. Manifolds are complex analytic, Hausdorff and countable at infinity. An object of Dw-ℂ-cb(kX)D^b_{w\text{-}\mathbb C\text{-}c}(k_X) has locally constant cohomology on a locally finite complex analytic stratification. Its coefficient modules may be infinite. The perfect constructible subcategory additionally requires perfect stalk complexes. No Noetherian hypothesis is imposed on kk.

We retain the cycle normalization from Nearby cycles and the two monodromy triangles:

ϕf(F)=Cone⁡(i−1F⟶ψf(F))[−1].(1) \phi_f(F)=\operatorname{Cone}\bigl(i^{-1}F\longrightarrow\psi_f(F)\bigr)[-1]. \qquad\text{(1)}

Thus the vanishing object here already contains the source’s shift by −1-1. The real covector associated to a complex covector is its real part. On a normal complex line the pairing is Re⁡(vξ)\operatorname{Re}(v\xi), without a conjugate. The Fourier transform uses the closed kernel Re⁡(vξ)≤0\operatorname{Re}(v\xi)\leq0.

The normal-deformation comparison for weak real constructibility is proved in Nearby cycles through the normal deformation. Here the additional complex geometry is essential. The proof uses the bounded specialization estimate, Fourier test and whole-complex descent theorems in the precise forms stated below.

Complex constructibility survives specialization

under the canonical normal/cotangent identification. In adapted holomorphic coordinates (u,y)(u,y), with Y={u=0}Y=\{u=0\}, write (α,β)(\alpha,\beta) for their complex covectors. The coordinates and signs of that identification are

(v,y;α,β)∈T*E↔(α,y;−v,β)∈T*L↔((0,y;α,0);v,β)∈NL(T*X).(3) (v,y;\alpha,\beta)\in T^*E \longleftrightarrow(\alpha,y;-v,\beta)\in T^*L \longleftrightarrow \bigl((0,y;\alpha,0);v,\beta\bigr)\in N_L(T^*X). \qquad\text{(3)}

The second map uses the symplectic normal identification K([w])(a)=Re⁡Ω(w,a)K([w])(a)=\operatorname{Re}\Omega(w,a); its inverse is induced by −H-H with the Hamiltonian convention used earlier. The analytic normal-cone argument in Analytic normal cones through complex deformation applies to the analytic set Λ\Lambda and the analytic submanifold LL. A dual halfspace has a closed-ray polar

Let τ:E→Y\tau:E\to Y and τ∨:E∨→Y\tau^\vee:E^\vee\to Y be the bundle projections. Set

U={ξ∈ℂ:Re⁡ξ>0},R={v∈ℂ:Im⁡v=0,Re⁡v≥0}.(11) U=\{\xi\in\mathbb C:\operatorname{Re}\xi>0\}, \qquad R=\{v\in\mathbb C:\operatorname{Im}v=0, \ \operatorname{Re}v\geq0\}. \qquad\text{(11)}

The cohomology of μYF\mu_YF is locally constant along the UU fibres. A smooth product identification U≃ℝ2U\simeq\mathbb R^2, chosen to take 11 to the origin, and the whole-complex cylinder descent give

s′−1μYF≃Rτ*∨Rℋom(kU×Y,μYF).(12) s'^{-1}\mu_YF\simeq R\tau^\vee_*R\mathcal Hom(k_{U\times Y},\mu_YF). \qquad\text{(12)}

The internal Hom for this open coefficient means ordinary sections over the open halfspace, pushed into the ambient bundle. It does not mean compactly supported cohomology.

In the real pairing Re⁡(vξ)\operatorname{Re}(v\xi), the positive polar of UU is RR. Indeed, write v=u+iwv=u+iw and ξ=a+ib\xi=a+ib. Then

Re⁡(vξ)=ua−wb,a>0,b∈ℝ.(13) \operatorname{Re}(v\xi)=ua-wb, \qquad a>0,\quad b\in\mathbb R. \qquad\text{(13)}

Nonnegativity for every such a,ba,b forces w=0w=0 and u≥0u\geq0, and these conditions suffice. The zero vector passes every inequality and must be included.

The open-convex-cone test SH02-FS-SECTIONS, formula FS13, now gives

Rτ*∨Rℋom(kU×Y,G∧)≃Rτ*Rℋom(kR×Y,G).(14) R\tau^\vee_*R\mathcal Hom(k_{U\times Y},G^\wedge) \simeq R\tau_*R\mathcal Hom(k_{R\times Y},G). \qquad\text{(14)}

To obtain an isomorphism of sheaves on YY, apply the natural Fourier test to the restriction over every open base subset and its restriction maps. Its proof applies the inverse Fourier equivalence to both Hom arguments; the inverse image of the open-cone coefficient is its closed-polar coefficient. The inverse Fourier shifts and dual orientation lines cancel in this test. The real rank of the complex line remains two; (14) introduces no further shift or orientation factor.

The slit and the cover have the same section complex

Let O=ℂ\RO=\mathbb C\setminus R. This slit plane is contained in ℂ*\mathbb C^*, and its inverse image has the distinguished open strip

S={w∈ℂ:0<Re⁡w<1}.(15) S=\{w\in\mathbb C:0<\operatorname{Re}w<1\}. \qquad\text{(15)}

The restriction p|S:S→Op|_S:S\to O is a homeomorphism. Write E*=ℂ*×YE^*=\mathbb C^*\times Y and j:E*↪Ej:E^*\hookrightarrow E; the cover pp has target E*E^*. Extension by zero from SS to the cover, proper-support direct image along pp, and then j!j_! give a coefficient morphism on EE

α:kO×Y⟶L=j!p!kℂw×Y,tr⁡∘α:kO×Y⟶kE.(16) \alpha:k_{O\times Y}\longrightarrow L=j_!p_!k_{\mathbb C_w\times Y}, \qquad \operatorname{tr}\circ\alpha: k_{O\times Y}\longrightarrow k_E. \qquad\text{(16)}

The trace is the composite of the punctured-target counit, extended by j!j_!, with the open-inclusion counit j!kE*→kEj_!k_{E^*}\to k_E. On E*E^* it sums finitely supported sheet coefficients. Consequently tr⁡∘α\operatorname{tr}\circ\alpha is the ordinary open-extension inclusion. Both two-term complexes below are objects on EE, with terms in degrees −1,0-1,0:

B=[kO×Y⟶kE],K=[L→trkE],B⟶K=(α,id).(17) B=[k_{O\times Y}\longrightarrow k_E], \qquad K=[L\xrightarrow{\operatorname{tr}}k_E], \qquad B\longrightarrow K=(\alpha,\mathrm{id}). \qquad\text{(17)}

Open–closed localization identifies B≃kR×YB\simeq k_{R\times Y}, in degree zero. Applying contravariant internal Hom to (17) gives

Rτ*Rℋom(K,G)⟶Rτ*Rℋom(kR×Y,G).(18) R\tau_*R\mathcal Hom(K,G) \longrightarrow R\tau_*R\mathcal Hom(k_{R\times Y},G). \qquad\text{(18)}

The left side equals ϕℓ(G)\phi_\ell(G). This follows from the coefficient definition in the monodromy lesson and ordinary positive-conic contraction to ee. The coefficient KK, the target GG, and their internal Hom have the needed positive conicity; the lifted positive action preserves SS, so it also preserves the coefficient map in (17).

We show that (18) is an isomorphism by comparing the Hom triangles of the two complexes in (17). Their kEk_E terms have the identity map. On the other terms, ordinary composition and the cover adjunction identify the map with restriction

Rq*p−1(G|v≠0)⟶R(τ|O×Y)*(G|O×Y).(19) Rq_*p^{-1}(G|_{v\ne0}) \longrightarrow R(\tau|_{O\times Y})_*(G|_{O\times Y}). \qquad\text{(19)}

It is restriction from the entire cover to SS. The whole cover and the open strip are each a product with a contractible real two-dimensional fibre. Their cohomology is vertically locally constant. Apply the cylinder descent contract, identifying SS with ℝ2\mathbb R^2, and evaluate both sides at w=1/2w=1/2. The two evaluation maps commute with restriction. Both are isomorphisms, so (19) is an isomorphism.

The evaluation point lies above v=−1v=-1, which belongs to OO. In contrast, v=1v=1 lies on the removed ray. Evaluation at w=0w=0 from (9) is related to evaluation at w=1/2w=1/2 by transport in the contractible cover, rather than by pretending that the normal section 11 lies in the slit. This distinction allows arbitrary monodromy on the punctured normal line.

The map between the two Hom triangles is now an isomorphism on both their other terms. Their fibre term (18) is an isomorphism too. Combining (12), (14), (18), and the normal vanishing comparison gives

ϕf(F)≃ϕℓ(νYF)≃Rτ*Rℋom(kR×Y,νYF)≃s′−1μYF.(20) \phi_f(F)\simeq\phi_\ell(\nu_YF) \simeq R\tau_*R\mathcal Hom(k_{R\times Y},\nu_YF) \simeq s'^{-1}\mu_YF. \qquad\text{(20)}

The coefficient map (17) fixes the slit branch used in this comparison. Formula (20) retains exactly the normalization (1). It does not append a further Fourier, real-rank or complex-orientation shift. Changing the lift is governed by deck transport; no trivialization of monodromy on the entire punctured line has been assumed.

Constructibility and support for a critical function

For a regular fibre, (5), (10), and (20), followed by holomorphic ordinary inverse image along s,s′s,s', prove weak complex constructibility of both cycles. If FF has perfect stalks, both bundle objects have perfect stalks and ordinary inverse image retains them. This proves perfect complex constructibility as well.

Now allow any holomorphic f:X→ℂf:X\to\mathbb C. Its zero set YY can be singular. Use the closed graph and the coordinate projection

g:X↪ℂt×X,g(x)=(f(x),x),H=g*F,Z={t=0}≃X.(21) g:X\hookrightarrow\mathbb C_t\times X, \quad g(x)=(f(x),x),\qquad H=g_*F, \quad Z=\{t=0\}\simeq X. \qquad\text{(21)}

The graph is proper and holomorphic. The operation theorem makes HH weakly, or perfectly, complex constructible as appropriate. The target coordinate tt has a regular fibre ZZ, so the results just proved apply to ψt(H)\psi_t(H) and ϕt(H)\phi_t(H). With a:Y↪Za:Y\hookrightarrow Z the closed inclusion, the actual proper-on-support comparisons from Proper pushforwards of nearby and vanishing cycles give

ψt(H)≃a*ψf(F),ϕt(H)≃a*ϕf(F).(22) \psi_t(H)\simeq a_*\psi_f(F), \qquad \phi_t(H)\simeq a_*\phi_f(F). \qquad\text{(22)}

These are objects supported on a(Y)a(Y). Refine their analytic stratifications in ZZ compatibly with the analytic subset YY. Their restrictions are locally constant on the resulting strata of YY; ordinary stalk restriction retains the perfect condition. Thus both cycles are weakly complex constructible on YY, and are perfect constructible when FF is. This argument does not assign a smooth normal line to a singular fibre.

The selected contract SH02-MO-MICROLOCAL-SUPPORT gives

supp⁡(μYF)⊂TY*X∩SS⁡(F)(23) \operatorname{supp}(\mu_YF) \subset T_Y^*X\cap\operatorname{SS}(F) \qquad\text{(23)}

for a smooth YY. Here and below support is the closed support of the cohomology sheaves. For a regular fibre, pulling (23) back along s′s' and using (20) gives the closed bound

supp⁡(ϕf(F))⊂{y∈f−1(0):(y;dfy)∈SS⁡(F)}.(24) \operatorname{supp}(\phi_f(F)) \subset\{y\in f^{-1}(0):(y;df_y)\in\operatorname{SS}(F)\}. \qquad\text{(24)}

For a critical function use (21)–(22). At (0,x)(0,x), the covector dtdt restricts to the graph as dfxdf_x. More explicitly, graph transpose pullback sends (c,ξ)(c,\xi) to cdfx+ξc\,df_x+\xi. The proper direct-image estimate SH02-MO-PROPER-PUSH, formula MO8, implies

((0,x);dt)∈SS⁡(g*F)⇒(x;dfx)∈SS⁡(F).(25) ((0,x);dt)\in\operatorname{SS}(g_*F) \ \Longrightarrow\ (x;df_x)\in\operatorname{SS}(F). \qquad\text{(25)}

Apply the regular support bound to t,Ht,H and use (22). It gives (24) for arbitrary ff, including dfx=0df_x=0. Closedness matters: the preimage of the closed set SS⁡(F)\operatorname{SS}(F) under y↦(y;dfy)y\mapsto(y;df_y) is closed, so the assertion bounds closed support and not merely individual nonzero stalks.

Finally, if p∉SS⁡(F)p\notin\operatorname{SS}(F), choose an open cotangent neighborhood VV disjoint from microsupport. For every point xx and every local holomorphic function hh with h(x)=0h(x)=0 and (x;dhx)∈V(x;dh_x)\in V, restriction of microsupport to the domain of hh and (24) give

ϕh(F)x=0.(26) \phi_h(F)_x=0. \qquad\text{(26)}

This proves the uniform forward holomorphic test. Quadratic cycles and the holomorphic microsupport test proves the reverse implication using generic microlocal models, transfer of the test to those models, and the quadratic calculation.

The cotangent sign and the two scaling actions

Difficulty: Advanced.

Solution. The complex symplectic form is dα∧du+dβ∧dyd\alpha\wedge du+d\beta\wedge dy. A normal representative has components (δu,δβ)=(v,β)(\delta u,\delta\beta)=(v,\beta); a tangent vector to LL has components (δα,δy)=(a,b)(\delta\alpha,\delta y)=(a,b). Pairing the normal representative first gives −av+βb-av+\beta b. Taking real parts is exactly the real symplectic normal map, so its complex covector on LL is −vdα+βdy-v\,d\alpha+\beta\,dy. Normal scaling multiplies v,βv,\beta at fixed (α,y)(\alpha,y); transported to T*ET^*E, it changes the base vv, so it is not cotangent dilation there. Multiplying ambient input covectors in (4) by λ\lambda, however, changes (α,β)(\alpha,\beta) to (λα,λβ)(\lambda\alpha,\lambda\beta) at fixed (v,y)(v,y). This proves the actual output cotangent dilation property. The analytic normal cone and the isotropy theorem supply the other hypotheses of the complex constructibility criterion.

A uniform test near the zero section

Difficulty: Advanced.

For F=kXF=k_X with nonzero kk, prove the forward test near any nonzero cotangent covector. Show that no neighborhood of a zero covector can have every holomorphic test vanish, by choosing a constant function. Keep the normalization (1).

Solution. The microsupport of kXk_X is the zero section. A small cotangent neighborhood of a nonzero covector can be chosen disjoint from that section. Formula (24), applied on the domain of each holomorphic test, makes its vanishing stalk zero whenever its derivative belongs to that neighborhood. At a zero covector (x;0)(x;0), take h=0h=0 on a neighborhood of xx. Its punctured inverse image is empty, so ψh(F)=0\psi_h(F)=0, and (1) gives ϕh(F)=F\phi_h(F)=F, with nonzero stalk kk in degree zero. Thus that test belongs to every neighborhood of (x;0)(x;0) and prevents uniform vanishing. This verifies the zero-function phenomenon directly without asserting the reverse criterion for general weakly complex constructible objects.

What has been established

The normal and conormal comparisons give weak and perfect complex cycle constructibility for every holomorphic function, the closed vanishing support bound, and the uniform forward test, using the stated prerequisite theorems. Quadratic cycles and the holomorphic microsupport test proves the reverse criterion and its coefficient model. Vanishing cycles as positive real support gives the local-support comparison, including critical functions and singular zero fibres.
