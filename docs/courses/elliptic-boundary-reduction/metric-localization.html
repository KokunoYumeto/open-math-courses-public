Localizing symbols with moving metrics

A symbol can be measured by a different ellipsoid at every point. Those ellipsoids may be anisotropic, their coefficients need not vary smoothly, and the weight may be discontinuous. We construct a locally finite cover, a partition of unity, and smooth representatives of the metric and weight. The resulting localization and reconstruction estimates make no uncertainty, symplectic, or temperateness assumption; later operator theorems require their own additional hypotheses.

We use finite-dimensional differential calculus, compactness, Lebesgue volume under linear changes of variables, and the maximality principle for partially ordered sets. The supporting arguments are collected in Metric and topological foundations. The covering and partition proofs appear here. A freely accessible treatment of the wider calculus is [Lerner].

1. Data and symbol seminorms

Let EE be a real vector space of finite dimension d≥1d\geq1. At every X∈EX\in E, let gXg_X be a positive-definite quadratic form on EE. Write

BX(t)={Y:gX(Y−X)<t2}. B_X(t)=\{Y:g_X(Y-X)<t^2\}.

We assume that there are r*>0r_*>0 and Cg≥1C_g\geq1 such that

gX(Y−X)≤r*2⇒Cg−1gX(T)≤gY(T)≤CggX(T)(T∈E).(1) g_X(Y-X)\leq r_*^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad C_g^{-1}g_X(T)\leq g_Y(T)\leq C_g g_X(T) \quad(T\in E). \tag{1}

This is slow variation. A positive function m:E→(0,∞)m:E\to(0,\infty) is an admissible weight for this local discussion if, after possibly decreasing r*r_*,

gX(Y−X)≤r*2⇒Cm−1m(X)≤m(Y)≤Cmm(X)(2) g_X(Y-X)\leq r_*^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad C_m^{-1}m(X)\leq m(Y)\leq C_m m(X) \tag{2}

for some Cm≥1C_m\geq1. In this lesson, “locally admissible” means exactly (2). We impose neither global temperateness nor an uncertainty inequality. Neither continuity of X↦gXX\mapsto g_X nor continuity of mm is required by (1)–(2).

For a smooth complex-valued function aa, define

|a|k,g(X)=supgX(Tj)≤1|Dka(X)[T1,…,Tk]|,pk(a;m,g)=supX|a|k,g(X)m(X).(3) |a|_{k,g}(X)= \sup_{g_X(T_j)\leq1} \left|D^k a(X)[T_1,\ldots,T_k]\right|, \qquad p_k(a;m,g)=\sup_X\frac{|a|_{k,g}(X)}{m(X)}. \tag{3}

For k=0k=0, the numerator is |a(X)||a(X)|. The symbol space S(m,g)S(m,g) consists of the smooth functions for which every pkp_k is finite. We also write p≤k=max⁡j≤kpjp_{\leq k}=\max_{j\leq k}p_j. These norms of multilinear derivatives avoid making the definition depend on a preferred basis. Replacing them with the maximum of derivatives in a gXg_X-orthonormal basis gives equivalent seminorms, with constants depending only on d,kd,k.

For clarity about conventions, the usual one-sided slow-variation assumption is sufficient. Suppose gX(Y−X)≤cg_X(Y-X)\leq c implies gY≤CgXg_Y\leq Cg_X. If gX(Y−X)≤c/Cg_X(Y-X)\leq c/C, then gY(X−Y)≤cg_Y(X-Y)\leq c; applying the same assumption with X,YX,Y exchanged gives gX≤CgYg_X\leq Cg_Y. Thus (1) results by shrinking the radius. We do not add a stronger regularity assumption by using its symmetric form.

2. A locally finite cover of moving ellipsoids

Theorem 2.1 (Ellipsoid cover). Choose any

0<r<r*4(1+Cg),R1=(1+Cg)r,R2=2R1. 0<r<\frac{r_*}{4(1+C_g)},\qquad R_1=(1+C_g)r, \qquad R_2=2R_1.

There is an at most countable family of centers XνX_\nu such that:

  1. the balls BXν(r)B_{X_\nu}(r) are pairwise disjoint;
  2. the balls BXν(R1)B_{X_\nu}(R_1) cover EE;
  3. the larger balls BXν(R2)B_{X_\nu}(R_2) form a locally finite family;
  4. no point belongs to more than

N=⌈Cgd(1+R2/r)d⌉(4) N=\left\lceil C_g^d\left(1+R_2/r\right)^d\right\rceil \tag{4}

of those larger balls.

The particular numerical radii are only one convenient choice. All constants are independent of the centers and of the eccentricities of the ellipsoids.

Proof. Order the collections of pairwise disjoint balls BX(r)B_X(r) by inclusion. A chain has its union as an upper bound, since any two balls in the union lie together in one member of the chain. The maximality principle gives a maximal collection. Every ball in it is a nonempty Euclidean open set. Choose a rational-coordinate point in each ball, in any fixed linear coordinates; disjointness makes these chosen points distinct. The collection is at most countable.

Maximality means that BX(r)B_X(r) meets a selected ball BXν(r)B_{X_\nu}(r) for every XX. Let YY belong to the intersection. Applying (1) with centers XX and XνX_\nu at YY gives

gXν≤CggY≤Cg2gX. g_{X_\nu}\leq C_g g_Y\leq C_g^2g_X.

The triangle inequality in the norm gXν1/2g_{X_\nu}^{1/2} therefore gives

gXν(X−Xν)1/2≤gXν(X−Y)1/2+gXν(Y−Xν)1/2<(Cg+1)r=R1. g_{X_\nu}(X-X_\nu)^{1/2} \leq g_{X_\nu}(X-Y)^{1/2}+g_{X_\nu}(Y-X_\nu)^{1/2} <(C_g+1)r=R_1.

This proves the covering assertion.

Fix XX, and consider centers with X∈BXν(R2)X\in B_{X_\nu}(R_2). Since R2<r*R_2<r_*, (1) yields

Cg−1gX≤gXν≤CggX. C_g^{-1}g_X\leq g_{X_\nu}\leq C_g g_X.

Consequently the disjoint smaller balls BXν(r)B_{X_\nu}(r) all lie in the gXg_X-ball centered at XX of radius Cg(R2+r)\sqrt{C_g}(R_2+r). Each has volume at least Cg−d/2rdvXC_g^{-d/2}r^d v_X, where vXv_X is the volume of the unit ball of gXg_X. The containing ball has volume Cgd/2(R2+r)dvXC_g^{d/2}(R_2+r)^d v_X. Dividing these two quantities bounds every finite subcollection by the expression in (4), and hence bounds the entire collection. This proves the multiplicity assertion without any global comparison to the Euclidean metric.

For local finiteness we need more than a pointwise multiplicity estimate. Choose 0<δ<r*0<\delta<r_*, and consider all larger balls meeting BX(δ)B_X(\delta). At an intersection point YY, (1) first compares gYg_Y with gXg_X, then with gXνg_{X_\nu}. Thus

Cg−2gX≤gXν≤Cg2gX. C_g^{-2}g_X\leq g_{X_\nu}\leq C_g^2g_X.

The centers lie within gXg_X-distance δ+CgR2\delta+C_gR_2 of XX. Their disjoint smaller balls lie in the fixed ball of radius δ+Cg(R2+r)\delta+C_g(R_2+r), and each has volume at least Cg−drdvXC_g^{-d}r^dv_X. Volume comparison bounds the number meeting this neighborhood by a finite number. The family is therefore locally finite. ∎

The same centers at every permissible radius. For every 0<R≤r*0<R\leq r_*, the family {BXν(R)}\{B_{X_\nu}(R)\} is locally finite and has multiplicity at most ⌈Cgd(1+R/r)d⌉\lceil C_g^d(1+R/r)^d\rceil; it covers whenever R≥R1R\geq R_1. These assertions hold for the same centers, not a new selection for each RR. In the last two paragraphs of the proof replace R2R_2 by RR; the only radius condition used in the metric comparison is R≤r*R\leq r_*. The covering assertion follows by inclusion from the cover at R1R_1.

In particular, given 0<ε<10<\varepsilon<1, choose r=r*ε/[8(1+Cg)]r=r_*\sqrt\varepsilon/[8(1+C_g)]. The same centers cover at every radius satisfying εr*2<R2\varepsilon r_*^2<R^2, and their multiplicity for all R2<r*2R^2<r_*^2 is bounded by the one constant

Indeed, R1=r*ε/8R_1=r_*\sqrt\varepsilon/8, which is smaller than every radius in that covering range; the multiplicity estimate follows by using R≤r*R\leq r_*.

3. A partition of unity with uniform derivatives

Theorem 3.1 (Uniform partition). With the centers above, there are smooth nonnegative functions ϕν\phi_\nu, compactly supported in BXν(R2)B_{X_\nu}(R_2), such that

∑νϕν=1,supX,ν|ϕν|k,g(X)≤Ak(k≥0).(5) \sum_\nu\phi_\nu=1, \qquad \sup_{X,\nu}|\phi_\nu|_{k,g}(X)\leq A_k \quad(k\geq0). \tag{5}

4. Localizing and reconstructing symbols

Theorem 4.1 (Analysis and reconstruction). If a∈S(m,g)a\in S(m,g), the pieces aν=ϕνaa_\nu=\phi_\nu a are supported in BXν(R2)B_{X_\nu}(R_2), and for every kk

supνsupX|aν|k,gXν(X)m(Xν)≤Ckp≤k(a;m,g).(7) \sup_\nu\sup_X\frac{|a_\nu|_{k,g_{X_\nu}}(X)}{m(X_\nu)} \leq C_k p_{\leq k}(a;m,g). \tag{7}

Conversely, suppose smooth functions bνb_\nu are supported in these balls and satisfy

supνsupX|bν|k,gXν(X)/m(Xν)≤Lkfor every k.(8) \sup_\nu\sup_X |b_\nu|_{k,g_{X_\nu}}(X)/m(X_\nu)\leq L_k \quad\text{for every }k. \tag{8}

Then b=∑νbνb=\sum_\nu b_\nu is a smooth symbol and

pk(b;m,g)≤NCmCgk/2Lk.(9) p_k(b;m,g)\leq NC_m C_g^{k/2}L_k. \tag{9}

Proof. On the support of a piece, (1)–(2) compare the metric and weight at XX with their values at the center. Apply the product rule and (5) to obtain (7). For reconstruction, local finiteness makes the sum smooth. At every point at most NN derivative tensors occur. A direction of gXg_X-length at most one has gXνg_{X_\nu}-length at most Cg\sqrt{C_g}. Estimate each tensor by (8), multiply by the weight-comparison constant CmC_m, and add the at most NN contributions. This gives exactly (9). ∎

This result is a quantitative interface: a later operator estimate can be proved on compactly supported symbols measured in one fixed ellipsoid, and then combined using a separately justified summability estimate. Local bounded overlap alone does not justify summing the images of pieces under a nonlocal operator.

The reconstruction assertion also holds for the same centers and any support radius 0<R<r*0<R<r_*, with the multiplicity constant at that radius replacing NN. The all-radius part of Theorem 2.1 supplies local finiteness and multiplicity, and (1)–(2) still compare center data with point data. Thus the proof of (9) applies verbatim at the new radius. In the ε\varepsilon-parameter version, NεN_\varepsilon gives a uniform bound over all such radii.

5. Smooth metrics and weights with the same symbol space

Theorem 5.1 (Smooth representatives). There is a positive smooth function MM such that

Cm−1m≤M≤Cmm,M∈S(m,g).(10) C_m^{-1}m\leq M\leq C_m m, \qquad M\in S(m,g). \tag{10}

There is also a smooth field of positive-definite forms GXG_X such that

Cg−1gX≤GX≤CggX,(11) C_g^{-1}g_X\leq G_X\leq C_g g_X, \tag{11}

and, for every kk,

|DXkGX(V,W)[T1,…,Tk]|≤BkgX(V)gX(W)∏j=1kgX(Tj).(12) \left|D_X^kG_X(V,W)[T_1,\ldots,T_k]\right| \leq B_k\sqrt{g_X(V)g_X(W)}\prod_{j=1}^k\sqrt{g_X(T_j)}. \tag{12}

Here the same notation denotes a quadratic form and its symmetric bilinear form. The symbol spaces S(M,G)S(M,G) and S(m,g)S(m,g) agree, with equivalent seminorms.

Proof. Define

M(X)=∑νϕν(X)m(Xν),GX=∑νϕν(X)gXν.(13) M(X)=\sum_\nu\phi_\nu(X)m(X_\nu),\qquad G_X=\sum_\nu\phi_\nu(X)g_{X_\nu}. \tag{13}

Both sums are locally finite. On the support of ϕν\phi_\nu, (1)–(2) apply between XνX_\nu and XX. Since the coefficients are nonnegative and sum to one, weighted averaging immediately proves (10) without its derivative assertion, and proves (11). In particular the average form is positive definite.

Differentiating (13) differentiates only ϕν\phi_\nu, since the center values are constants. At a given point there are at most NN nonzero terms, and (5) bounds the differentiated coefficients. It follows that |M|k,g≤NAkCmm|M|_{k,g}\leq NA_kC_m m. For the metric, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for the positive form gXνg_{X_\nu}, followed by (1), gives

|gXν(V,W)|≤gXν(V)gXν(W)≤CggX(V)gX(W). |g_{X_\nu}(V,W)| \leq\sqrt{g_{X_\nu}(V)g_{X_\nu}(W)} \leq C_g\sqrt{g_X(V)g_X(W)}.

Together with (5) this proves (12) with Bk=NAkCgB_k=NA_kC_g. Finally (10)–(11) compare each defining seminorm in either direction. Thus equality and equivalence of the symbol spaces follow directly from (3), without differentiating the weight or the metric. ∎

The new metric is itself slowly varying. Indeed, if GX(Y−X)≤r*2/CgG_X(Y-X)\leq r_*^2/C_g, then gX(Y−X)≤r*2g_X(Y-X)\leq r_*^2; inserting (11) on both sides of (1) compares GYG_Y and GXG_X with constant Cg3C_g^3. The new weight is locally comparable on these balls by (2) and (10). Thus regularization keeps us within the same class of local data.

Corollary 5.2 (Recognition of weights). For fixed gg and two locally admissible weights m1,m2m_1,m_2, the inclusion S(m1,g)⊂S(m2,g)S(m_1,g)\subset S(m_2,g) as sets holds if and only if m1/m2m_1/m_2 is bounded. The reverse implication follows from (3). For the forward implication, construct M1M_1 by (10). It lies in S(m1,g)S(m_1,g), hence in S(m2,g)S(m_2,g); its zeroth seminorm gives M1≤Cm2M_1\leq Cm_2, and (10) then gives the claimed ratio bound. In particular, equality of these spaces is equivalent to comparability of the weights in both directions.

6. Completeness, products and reciprocals

The pointwise differential inequalities in this section are local statements: for fixed kk, they apply to complex-valued CkC^k functions on a neighborhood of the point, measured in any fixed positive-definite form QQ. In particular the product rule gives |ab|k,Q(X)≤∑j=0k(kj)|a|j,Q(X)|b|k−j,Q(X). |ab|_{k,Q}(X)\leq\sum_{j=0}^k\binom{k}{j}|a|_{j,Q}(X)|b|_{k-j,Q}(X). No global symbol membership is needed for this inequality or for the pointwise reciprocal bound (18). Global smoothness and symbol seminorms are imposed only for the Fréchet-space and symbol-membership conclusions.

Proposition 6.1. The seminorms (3) make S(m,g)S(m,g) a Fréchet space. For locally admissible weights m1,m2m_1,m_2, multiplication is continuous and satisfies

Proof. At any fixed point XX, (1)–(2) give a neighborhood on which the metric is comparable to gXg_X and the weight is bounded above and bounded away from zero. If (an)(a_n) is Cauchy in every seminorm, its coordinate derivatives of every order have uniform limits on compact sets. These limits are the derivatives of a single smooth function: on a coordinate box apply the fundamental theorem of calculus to ana_n along each coordinate segment, pass to the uniform limit, and repeat for each already obtained derivative. 7. Approximation on compact sets

Proposition 7.1. Every a∈S(m,g)a\in S(m,g) is the limit in Cloc∞C^\infty_{\mathrm{loc}} of a sequence aN∈Cc∞(E)a_N\in C_c^\infty(E) bounded in every seminorm of S(m,g)S(m,g).

Proof. First Cc∞(E)⊂S(m,g)C_c^\infty(E)\subset S(m,g), by the compact-set comparison in Proposition 6.1. Enumerate the partition and set aN=∑ν≤Nϕνaa_N=\sum_{\nu\leq N}\phi_\nu a. Each sum has compact support. The proof of (7)–(9) gives bounds independent of NN. On any compact set only finitely many partition supports meet it; after all these indices have been included, aN=aa_N=a on a neighborhood of that compact set. This proves the asserted convergence. ∎

This is deliberately a bounded approximation statement. It does not assert convergence in the symbol seminorms. For the constant Euclidean metric and m=1m=1, the symbol a=1a=1 has p0(1−b;1,g)≥1p_0(1-b;1,g)\geq1 for every compactly supported bb. Thus Cc∞C_c^\infty need not be dense in the Fréchet topology. Later extension theorems must specify which topology their continuity uses.

8. An anisotropic model

On E=ℝxn×ℝξnE=\mathbb R_x^n\times\mathbb R_\xi^n, take arbitrary real δ,q\delta,q, take ρ≤1\rho\leq1, and set

⟨ξ⟩=(1+|ξ|2)1/2,g(x,ξ)(y,η)=⟨ξ⟩2δ|y|2+⟨ξ⟩−2ρ|η|2,m(x,ξ)=⟨ξ⟩q.(16) \langle\xi\rangle=(1+|\xi|^2)^{1/2},\qquad g_{(x,\xi)}(y,\eta) =\langle\xi\rangle^{2\delta}|y|^2 +\langle\xi\rangle^{-2\rho}|\eta|^2, \qquad m(x,\xi)=\langle\xi\rangle^q. \tag{16}

9. Exercises and solutions

Problem 1: an unsmoothed weight. For the Euclidean metric on ℝd\mathbb R^d, let m(X)=2+𝟏{X1≥0}m(X)=2+\mathbf1_{\{X_1\geq0\}}. Verify the hypotheses and identify S(m,g)S(m,g). Explain what the regularization theorem supplies.

Solution. The metric comparison constant is one at every radius. The weight lies between two and three, so any pair of points satisfies (2) with Cm=3/2C_m=3/2. Formula (3) gives 13sup⁡|Dka|≤pk(a;m,g)≤12sup⁡|Dka|\frac13\sup|D^ka|\leq p_k(a;m,g)\leq\frac12\sup|D^ka|. Thus the space consists exactly of smooth functions with all derivatives bounded, with its usual topology. Theorem 5.1 constructs a positive smooth representative comparable to the discontinuous weight. It does not claim to make the original weight continuous or to approximate its jump uniformly by continuous functions.

Problem 2: the missing local-finiteness argument. The intervals (2−j,3⋅2−j−1)(2^{-j},3\cdot2^{-j-1}), j≥1j\geq1, have multiplicity one. Are they locally finite in ℝ\mathbb R? Which part of Theorem 2.1 prevents the analogous failure?

Solution. They are pairwise disjoint, but every neighborhood of zero meets infinitely many. They are therefore not locally finite. In Theorem 2.1, slow variation compares every center whose large ball meets a fixed small neighborhood with the metric at that neighborhood’s center. It gives a common positive lower bound for the volumes of the disjoint small balls, inside one bounded ellipsoid. That neighborhood-volume argument, rather than the pointwise bound alone, proves local finiteness.

Problem 3: a matrix symbol with a nonconstant scale. For a weight mm, let MM be its smooth representative and let B∈S(1,g;ℂr×r)B\in S(1,g;\mathbb C^{r\times r}) satisfy sup⁡X∥B(X)∥≤1/2\sup_X\|B(X)\|\leq1/2. Show that A=M(I+B)A=M(I+B) belongs to S(m,g)S(m,g) and that A−1∈S(m−1,g)A^{-1}\in S(m^{-1},g).

Solution. Product estimate (14) gives the symbol bound for AA. For each XX, the geometric series ∑j≥0(−B(X))j\sum_{j\geq0}(-B(X))^j converges in operator norm, multiplies I+B(X)I+B(X) to the identity, and has norm at most two. Hence ∥A(X)−1∥≤2/M(X)≤2Cm/m(X)\|A(X)^{-1}\|\leq2/M(X)\leq2C_m/m(X). The matrix form of (15) now bounds every derivative of the inverse. Differentiating the infinite geometric series is unnecessary and would require a separate convergence argument.

Problem 4: a distinction in approximation. Give an approximating sequence for the constant symbol one which is bounded in S(1,g)S(1,g), converges locally smoothly, and fails to converge in p0p_0, for Euclidean gg.

Solution. Choose ψ∈Cc∞\psi\in C_c^\infty equal to one near the origin and set aN(X)=ψ(X/N)a_N(X)=\psi(X/N). Derivatives of order kk have supremum at most N−ksup⁡|Dkψ|N^{-k}\sup|D^k\psi|, so the sequence is bounded in every seminorm. On each compact set it is eventually identically one. But each aNa_N vanishes outside a compact set, so p0(aN−1;1,g)≥1p_0(a_N-1;1,g)\geq1. This explicitly separates the two topologies used in Proposition 7.1.
