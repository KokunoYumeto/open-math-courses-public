Fourier decomposition and enabled graphs

Written by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), October 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. Public domain (CC0).

A Fourier transform in the abelian normal subgroup \(V\) of \(G_{\mathrm{sur}}=V\rtimes\Gamma\) turns the left regular representation of \(G_{\mathrm{sur}}\) into a field of representations \(\pi_\omega\) on \(\ell^2(\Gamma)\), one for each point \(\omega\) of the compact dual \(\Omega\) of \(V\). In \(\pi_\omega\), the elements of \(V\) act by multiplication by functions of the pattern \(X_\omega\), and \(\Gamma\) acts by right translations. The norm of every element of \(C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}})\) is the supremum of its norms in the representations \(\pi_\omega\), and its trace is the average of a diagonal coefficient [OpenAI-IT, Section 5.1]. The operator \(T\) of the construction becomes, in \(\pi_\omega\), the Laplacian \(dI-\operatorname{Adj}(G_\omega)\) of the enabled graph \(G_\omega\): the subgraph of the Cayley graph \(X\) whose edges are allowed by the decoded pattern at both ends. Copies whose pattern is the prototype are isolated expanders in \(G_\omega\). On the rest of the graph, edges are missing in a controlled way, and this lesson proves the two deficit estimates that lead to the spectral gap of the next lesson [OpenAI-IT, Lemmas 5.2 and 5.3].

The data and notation of Codes and the lamp module are fixed: the prime \(p\), the decoder \(\mathcal J\), the codes \(C_P\) and prototypes \(w_P\), the module \(V\) with \(v_*\), the group \(G_{\mathrm{sur}}\), the dual \(\Omega\) with its measure \(m\), and the patterns \(X_\omega(h)=\omega(hv_*)\). The constant \(c=\frac\eta2\min\{\kappa,1\}\) and the other data come from Labelled expanders and cycle spaces, Proposition 2.1.

1. The Fourier decomposition

For \(v\in V\) let \(\chi_v(\omega)=e^{2\pi i\,\omega(v)/p}\), reading \(\omega(v)\in\mathbb F_p\) as an integer in \(\{0,\ldots,p-1\}\). These functions are continuous, \(\chi_v\chi_{v'}=\chi_{v+v'}\), \(\overline{\chi_v}=\chi_{-v}\), and \(\int_\Omega\chi_v\,dm=0\) for \(v\ne0\) (Exercise 4.4 of the previous lesson). So the \(\chi_v\) are orthonormal in \(L^2(\Omega,m)\). They span a dense subspace: their span contains the indicator of every cylinder set, since the indicator of \(\{\omega(b)=x\}\) is \(\frac1p\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p}e^{2\pi i\,a(\omega(b)-x)/p}\) and products of such indicators are again in the span; and finite combinations of indicators of cylinder sets are dense in \(L^2(\Omega,m)\) [Measure and Integration, Section 254].

The group \(\Gamma\) acts on \(\Omega\) by \((g\omega)(v)=\omega(g^{-1}v)\), so that \(\chi_v(g\omega)=\chi_{g^{-1}v}(\omega)\) and \(X_{h^{-1}\omega}(1)=\omega(hv_*)=X_\omega(h)\).

The representations \(\pi_\omega\). For \(\omega\in\Omega\), define unitary operators on \(\ell^2(\Gamma)\) by \[ \begin{gathered} \bigl(\pi_\omega(v,1)\xi\bigr)(h)=\chi_v(h^{-1}\omega)\,\xi(h),\\ \bigl(\pi_\omega(0,g)\xi\bigr)(h)=\xi(hg), \end{gathered} \] and \(\pi_\omega(v,g)=\pi_\omega(v,1)\pi_\omega(0,g)\). A direct check on \((v,g)(v',g')=(v+gv',gg')\) shows that \(\pi_\omega\) is a unitary representation of \(G_{\mathrm{sur}}\) (Exercise 4.1). We extend \(\pi_\omega\) linearly to the group ring \(\mathbb CG_{\mathrm{sur}}\).

Proposition 1.1 (Fourier decomposition; [OpenAI-IT, Section 5.1, (17) to (19)]). Let \(\lambda\) be the left regular representation of \(G_{\mathrm{sur}}\) on \(\ell^2(G_{\mathrm{sur}})\).

  1. There is a unitary \(U:\ell^2(G_{\mathrm{sur}})\to L^2(\Omega,\ell^2(\Gamma))\) such that \(U\lambda(x)U^*\) acts on square-integrable fields \(\Xi\) by \((U\lambda(x)U^*\Xi)(\omega)=\pi_\omega(x)\Xi(\omega)\), for every \(x\in\mathbb CG_{\mathrm{sur}}\); and \(U\delta_{(0,1)}\) is the constant field \(\omega\mapsto\delta_1\).
  2. For \(x\in\mathbb CG_{\mathrm{sur}}\), \(\|\lambda(x)\|=\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}\|\pi_\omega(x)\|\).
  3. Every \(\pi_\omega\) extends to a representation of \(C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}})\), again written \(\pi_\omega\), with \(\pi_\omega(\lambda(x))=\pi_\omega(x)\) for \(x\in\mathbb CG_{\mathrm{sur}}\), and \(\|b\|=\sup_\omega\|\pi_\omega(b)\|\) for every \(b\in C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}})\).
  4. For every \(b\in C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}})\), the function \(\omega\mapsto\langle\pi_\omega(b)\delta_1,\delta_1\rangle\) is continuous, and \[ \tau(b)=\int_\Omega\langle\pi_\omega(b)\delta_1,\delta_1\rangle\,dm(\omega). \]

Proof. (1) Define \(U\) on the basis by \[ U\delta_{(v,h)}=\bigl(\omega\mapsto\chi_v(h\omega)\,\delta_{h^{-1}}\bigr). \] For fixed \(h\), the functions \(\omega\mapsto\chi_v(h\omega)=\chi_{h^{-1}v}(\omega)\), \(v\in V\), are again all the characters, so the images of the \(\delta_{(v,h)}\) with fixed \(h\) form an orthonormal basis of the fields supported on the coordinate \(h^{-1}\), and images with different \(h\) are orthogonal. Hence \(U\) extends to a unitary. For \(x=(u,1)\): \(\lambda(u,1)\delta_{(v,h)}=\delta_{(u+v,h)}\), whose image is \(\omega\mapsto\chi_u(h\omega)\chi_v(h\omega)\delta_{h^{-1}}\), and this is \(\pi_\omega(u,1)\) applied to \(\chi_v(h\omega)\delta_{h^{-1}}\), by the definition of \(\pi_\omega\) at the coordinate \(h^{-1}\). For \(x=(0,g)\): \(\lambda(0,g)\delta_{(v,h)}=\delta_{(gv,gh)}\), whose image is \(\omega\mapsto\chi_{gv}(gh\omega)\delta_{h^{-1}g^{-1}}=\chi_v(h\omega)\delta_{h^{-1}g^{-1}}\), and \(\pi_\omega(0,g)\) moves \(\delta_{h^{-1}}\) to \(\delta_{h^{-1}g^{-1}}\). Both sides being linear and bounded, the formula holds for all \(x\). The last statement is the case \(v=0\), \(h=1\).

(2) For a field \(\Xi\), \[ \begin{aligned} &\|U\lambda(x)U^*\Xi\|^2=\int\|\pi_\omega(x)\Xi(\omega)\|^2\,dm\\ &\quad\le\sup_\omega\|\pi_\omega(x)\|^2\,\|\Xi\|^2 , \end{aligned} \] so \(\|\lambda(x)\|\le\sup_\omega\|\pi_\omega(x)\|\). Conversely, let \(\omega_0\in\Omega\) and \(\epsilon>0\), and choose finitely supported unit vectors \(\xi,\zeta\in\ell^2(\Gamma)\) with \(|g(\omega_0)|>\|\pi_{\omega_0}(x)\|-\epsilon\), where \(g(\omega)=\langle\pi_\omega(x)\xi,\zeta\rangle\). This \(g\) is a finite combination of the continuous functions \(\omega\mapsto\chi_v(h^{-1}\omega)\), so some cylinder set \(O\ni\omega_0\) satisfies \(|g(\omega)-g(\omega_0)|<\epsilon\) on \(O\), and \(m(O)>0\). The fields \(\mathbf1_O\xi\) and \(\mathbf1_O\zeta\) have norm \(m(O)^{1/2}\), and \[ \begin{aligned} &|\langle U\lambda(x)U^*(\mathbf1_O\xi),\mathbf1_O\zeta\rangle|\\ &\quad=\Bigl|\int_Og\,dm\Bigr|\ge(|g(\omega_0)|-\epsilon)\,m(O) . \end{aligned} \] Hence \(\|\lambda(x)\|\ge\|\pi_{\omega_0}(x)\|-2\epsilon\).

(3) By (2), \(\|\pi_\omega(x)\|\le\|\lambda(x)\|\), so \(\lambda(x)\mapsto\pi_\omega(x)\) extends by continuity to \(C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}})\), the closure of \(\lambda(\mathbb CG_{\mathrm{sur}})\). Both \(b\mapsto\|b\|\) and \(b\mapsto\sup_\omega\|\pi_\omega(b)\|\) are \(1\)-Lipschitz in \(b\) and agree on a dense set, so they agree.

(4) For \(x=\sum_yx_y\,y\) in the group ring, \(\tau(\lambda(x))=x_{(0,1)}\). On the other hand \(\langle\pi_\omega(v,g)\delta_1,\delta_1\rangle=\chi_v(\omega)\) if \(g=1\) and \(0\) otherwise, so \[ \begin{aligned} &\int\langle\pi_\omega(x)\delta_1,\delta_1\rangle\,dm\\ &\quad=\sum_vx_{(v,1)}\int\chi_v\,dm=x_{(0,1)} . \end{aligned} \] For general \(b\), approximate in norm by \(\lambda(x_k)\); the integrands converge uniformly in \(\omega\), by (3), and \(\tau\) is norm continuous. A uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous. \(\square\)

So questions about elements of \(C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}})\) can be answered one point \(\omega\) at a time, and \(\bigoplus_\omega\pi_\omega\) is a faithful representation.

2. The operator and its enabled graphs

For a set \(A\subseteq\mathbb F_p\), the function \(\omega\mapsto\mathbf1_A(\omega(v_*))\) is \(\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p}c_a\chi_{av_*}\) with \(c_a=\frac1p\sum_{x\in A}e^{-2\pi i\,ax/p}\), as in Section 1. For \(s\in S^{\pm1}\) let \[ \begin{gathered} f_s=\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p}c_{s,a}\,(av_*,1)\in\mathbb CG_{\mathrm{sur}},\\ A_s=\{x\in\mathbb F_p:s\in\mathcal J(x)\}, \end{gathered} \] with the coefficients \(c_{s,a}\) of \(A_s\). By the definition of \(\pi_\omega\), \(\pi_\omega(f_s)\) is multiplication by \[ \begin{aligned} h\mapsto{}&\mathbf1_{A_s}\bigl((h^{-1}\omega)(v_*)\bigr)\\ &=\mathbf1\bigl[s\in\mathcal J(X_\omega(h))\bigr], \end{aligned} \] a projection. For \(s\in S^{\pm1}\) let \(u_s=(0,s)\) if \(s\in S\), and \(u_s=(0,t)^{-1}\) if \(s=t^{-1}\) with \(t\in S\). Define \[ T=d\cdot1-\sum_{s\in S^{\pm1}}f_s\,u_s\,f_{s^{-1}}\ \in\ \mathbb CG_{\mathrm{sur}}, \] and regard it as an element of \(C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}})\). The adjoint of \(f_su_sf_{s^{-1}}\) is \(f_{s^{-1}}u_{s^{-1}}f_s\), because the \(f_s\) are self-adjoint, so \(T\) is self-adjoint.

Definition 2.1 (enabled graphs; [OpenAI-IT, Definition 5.1]). For \(\omega\in\Omega\), the allowed labels at \(h\in\Gamma\) are the elements of \(\mathcal J(X_\omega(h))\). The enabled graph \(G_\omega\) has vertex set \(\Gamma\), and the edge of \(X\) from \(h\) to \(hs\) is enabled when \(s\) is allowed at \(h\) and \(s^{-1}\) is allowed at \(hs\). A copy \(P\) is valid for \(\omega\) if \(X_\omega|_P=w_P\). Let \(U_\omega\) be the set of elements of \(\Gamma\) that lie in no valid copy.

The condition is symmetric under reversing the edge, so \(G_\omega\) is an undirected subgraph of the simple graph \(X\); every vertex has degree at most \(d\), since \(\mathcal J\) returns a \(d\)-element set or the empty set.

Lemma 2.2 (the operator in \(\pi_\omega\)). \(\pi_\omega(T)=dI-\operatorname{Adj}(G_\omega)\), a self-adjoint operator with \(0\le\pi_\omega(T)\le2dI\).

Proof. By the definition of \(\pi_\omega\) and the formula for \(\pi_\omega(f_s)\), for \(\xi\in\ell^2(\Gamma)\), \[ \begin{aligned} &\bigl(\pi_\omega(f_su_sf_{s^{-1}})\xi\bigr)(h)\\ &\quad=\mathbf1\bigl[s\in\mathcal J(X_\omega(h))\bigr]\\ &\qquad\cdot\mathbf1\bigl[s^{-1}\in\mathcal J(X_\omega(hs))\bigr]\,\xi(hs). \end{aligned} \] Summing over \(s\in S^{\pm1}\) gives the sum of \(\xi\) over the enabled neighbours \(hs\) of \(h\), each counted once because \(X\) is simple. The adjacency operator of a graph with degrees at most \(d\) has norm at most \(d\) (Exercise 4.3), which gives the bounds. \(\square\)

Valid copies. Let \(P\) be valid for \(\omega\). The allowed labels at \(h\in P\) are exactly \(J_P(h)\), the labels of the edges of \(P\) at \(h\). So every edge of \(P\) is enabled, and these \(d\) edges use up the allowed labels at each vertex of \(P\): no other enabled edge touches \(P\). Since \(P\) is connected, it is a whole connected component of \(G_\omega\), with exactly its own edges. Distinct valid copies \(P\ne Q\) are disjoint: if they shared a vertex, both would be the component of that vertex, so they would have the same vertex set; but with the root \(R\) at a shared vertex, both centers are at distance one, and Lemma 3.1 of Thin triangles and simultaneous overlaps puts \(P\cap Q\) in a ball with fewer than \(\eta|P|<|P|\) vertices. Consequently \(G_\omega\) has no edge between \(U_\omega\) and its complement.

3. Deficits

For a set \(B\) of vertices of a copy \(P\), let \(e_{\omega,P}(B)\) be the number of enabled edges of \(P\) with both ends in \(B\). The quantity \(d|B|-2e_{\omega,P}(B)\) counts the missing incidences of \(P\)-edges at \(B\): each vertex of \(B\) has \(d\) edges in \(P\), and each enabled edge of \(P\) inside \(B\) uses two of these incidences.

Lemma 3.1 (deficit inside an invalid copy; [OpenAI-IT, Lemma 5.2]). If \(P\) is not valid for \(\omega\), then every set \(B\) of vertices of \(P\) satisfies \[ d|B|-2e_{\omega,P}(B)\ge c|B| .\tag{3.1} \]

Proof. Let \(n=|P|\) and \(M=\{h\in P:X_\omega(h)\ne w_P(h)\}\). By Lemma 3.1 of the previous lesson, \(X_\omega|_P\in C_P\), and \(w_P\in C_P\); their difference is a nonzero codeword, so \(|M|>\eta n\) by (1.1) of that lesson. At a vertex \(h\in M\), the decoder returns the empty set or a \(d\)-element set different from \(J_P(h)\), so at least one edge of \(P\) at \(h\) is not enabled. Hence, with \(b=|B|\), \[ \begin{gathered} db-2e_{\omega,P}(B)\ge|M\cap B|,\\ db-2e_{\omega,P}(B)\ge|\partial_PB| , \end{gathered} \] where \(\partial_PB\) is the set of edges of \(P\) with exactly one end in \(B\); the second holds because every such edge uses an incidence at \(B\) that no edge inside \(B\) uses. If \(n-b\ge\eta n/2\), then \(\min\{b,n-b\}\ge\frac\eta2b\), and the expansion of \(\Theta_i\) (Proposition 2.1 of the lesson on labelled expanders) gives \(|\partial_PB|\ge\kappa\frac\eta2b\ge cb\). Otherwise \(|M\cap B|\ge|M|-(n-b)\), which is larger than \(\eta n-\frac{\eta n}2\ge\frac\eta2b\ge cb\). \(\square\)

Lemma 3.2 (deficit on the remaining graph; [OpenAI-IT, Lemma 5.3]). For every \(\omega\in\Omega\) and every finite set \(Y\subseteq U_\omega\), let \(A\) be the graph on \(Y\) whose edges are the enabled edges with both ends in \(Y\). Then \[ d|Y|-2|E(A)|\ge\frac c2|Y| .\tag{3.2} \]

Proof. Let \(b_1(A)=\dim Z_1(A;\mathbb F_2)\) be the cycle rank of \(A\). Euler's identity for graphs, \(|E(A)|=|Y|-\#\pi_0(A)+b_1(A)\), where \(\#\pi_0(A)\) is the number of components, shows that, with \(\beta=\frac d2-1-\frac c4\), it suffices to prove \[ b_1(A)\le\beta|Y| .\tag{3.3} \]

Eliminating copy coordinates. Regard \(\mathcal L=Z_1(A;\mathbb F_2)\) as a subspace of \(Z_1(X;\mathbb F_2)\), and use the coordinates \(\operatorname{pr}_P\) of the direct sum (3.1) of the lesson on labelled expanders. A basis of \(\mathcal L\) has finitely many nonzero coordinates, so only finitely many copies \(P_1,\ldots,P_N\) have \(\operatorname{pr}_{P_j}\ne0\) on \(\mathcal L\). Fix a group vertex \(R\), and order them so that the distances \(d(c_{P_j},R)\) do not increase. Let \(\mathcal L_j=\{z\in\mathcal L:\operatorname{pr}_{P_l}z=0\text{ for }l<j\}\). Then \(\mathcal L_1=\mathcal L\), \(\mathcal L_{N+1}=0\), and \(\operatorname{pr}_{P_j}\) maps \(\mathcal L_j\) onto a space with kernel \(\mathcal L_{j+1}\), so \[ \dim\mathcal L=\sum_{j=1}^N\dim\operatorname{pr}_{P_j}(\mathcal L_j).\tag{3.4} \]

Localizing. Fix \(j\), put \(P=P_j\), and let \(B_P\) be the ball of Lemma 3.1 of the lesson on thin triangles for the root \(R\), which contains every vertex that \(P\) shares with \(P_{j+1},\ldots,P_N\); it spans a tree in \(P\). Let \(D_P\) be the graph with the vertices of \(B_P\) and of \(Y\cap P\), the edges of the tree \(B_P\), and the edges of \(A\) that lie in \(P\). We claim that \(\operatorname{pr}_P(\mathcal L_j)\subseteq Z_1(D_P;\mathbb F_2)\). Let \(z\in\mathcal L_j\), so \(z=\sum_{l\ge j}\operatorname{pr}_{P_l}z\). An edge \(e\) of \(\operatorname{pr}_Pz\) that is not in \(A\) is not in \(z\), so it occurs in some \(\operatorname{pr}_{P_l}z\) with \(l>j\); its ends are shared by \(P\) and \(P_l\), so they lie in \(B_P\), and \(e\) is an edge of the tree \(B_P\). Every other edge of \(\operatorname{pr}_Pz\) is an edge of \(A\) in \(P\). Hence \[ \dim\operatorname{pr}_P(\mathcal L_j)\le b_1(D_P).\tag{3.5} \]

Bounding \(b_1(D_P)\). If \(P\) is valid, it is a component of \(G_\omega\) disjoint from \(U_\omega\supseteq Y\), so \(D_P\) is the tree \(B_P\) and \(b_1(D_P)=0\). Let \(P\) be invalid, of type \(i\), with \(\sigma=\sigma_i\) and \(r\le\sigma/16\) the radius of \(B_P\). Delete the vertices of \(B_P\) from \(D_P\), and let \(C\) run over the components of what is left; their vertex sets partition the set \(Y_P=(Y\cap P)\setminus B_P\). Let \(l_C\) be the number of edges of \(D_P\) joining \(C\) to \(B_P\), and \(a_C=\max\{0,l_C-1\}\). Since \(B_P\) is a connected tree, counting vertices and edges gives \[ \begin{aligned} &b_1(D_P)\\ &\quad=\sum_C\bigl(b_1(C)+a_C\bigr). \end{aligned}\tag{3.6} \] The edges of \(C\) are all the enabled edges of \(P\) between its vertices, by the definition of \(A\). If \(C\) contains a cycle, it has at least \(\sigma\) vertices, and Lemma 3.1 applied to its vertex set gives \(2|E(C)|\le(d-c)|C|\), so \[ \begin{aligned} &b_1(C)=|E(C)|-|C|+1\\ &\quad\le\bigl(\beta-\tfrac c4+\tfrac1\sigma\bigr)|C| ; \end{aligned}\tag{3.7} \] if \(C\) is a tree, \(b_1(C)=0\) and (3.7) holds as well, its right side being positive. For the attachments, take two edges joining \(C\) to \(B_P\), with ends \(v,v'\) in \(C\) and \(t,t'\) in \(B_P\). A shortest path from \(v\) to \(v'\) in \(C\), the two edges, and the path from \(t'\) to \(t\) in the tree form a cycle of \(P\) (if \(v=v'\) or \(t=t'\), the corresponding path is empty; both cannot happen, as \(P\) is simple). The tree path has length at most \(2r\), so \(d_C(v,v')\ge\sigma-2r-2\), and since \(r\le\sigma/16\) and \(\sigma>16\), \[ d_C(v,v')>\frac\sigma2 .\tag{3.8} \] So the attachment ends in \(C\) are distinct, and if \(l_C\ge2\), the balls of radius \(\lfloor\sigma/4\rfloor\) in \(C\) about them are pairwise disjoint; each contains more than \(\sigma/4\) vertices, an initial piece of a shortest path to another attachment end. Hence \[ a_C\le\frac4\sigma|C| ,\tag{3.9} \] which is immediate if \(l_C\le1\). Combining (3.6), (3.7) and (3.9), and using \(\frac5\sigma<\frac c4\) (as \(\sigma>20/c\)), \[ b_1(D_P)\le\beta|Y_P| ;\tag{3.10} \] for a valid copy both sides vanish.

Disjoint charges. For \(j<l\), the center of \(P_l\) is no farther from \(R\) than that of \(P_j\), so \(P_j\cap P_l\subseteq B_{P_j}\) by Lemma 3.1 of the lesson on thin triangles. So the sets \(Y_{P_j}\) are pairwise disjoint subsets of \(Y\). Summing (3.5) and (3.10) over \(j\) in (3.4) gives (3.3). \(\square\)

4. Exercises

4.1 (medium). Check that the formulas defining \(\pi_\omega\) give a representation: \(\pi_\omega(0,g)\pi_\omega(v,1)\pi_\omega(0,g)^{-1}=\pi_\omega(gv,1)\).

4.2 (easy). Show that \(v_*\ne0\) in \(V\), using Lemma 2.1 of the previous lesson and a copy containing \(1\). Then compute \(\tau(f_s)\) in two ways: as the coefficient of the identity in \(f_s\), and by the trace formula of Proposition 1.1(4); check that both give \(|A_s|/p\).

4.3 (easy). Show that the adjacency operator of a graph whose degrees are at most \(d\) has norm at most \(d\) on \(\ell^2\) of its vertex set.

4.4 (easy). In Lemma 3.1, why is \(|\partial_PB|\le db-2e_{\omega,P}(B)\) even when some edges of \(P\) inside \(B\) are not enabled?

5. Solutions

4.1. For \(\xi\in\ell^2(\Gamma)\), \[ \begin{aligned} &\bigl(\pi_\omega(0,g)\pi_\omega(v,1)\pi_\omega(0,g^{-1})\xi\bigr)(h)\\ &\quad=\bigl(\pi_\omega(v,1)\pi_\omega(0,g^{-1})\xi\bigr)(hg)\\ &\quad=\chi_v(g^{-1}h^{-1}\omega)\,\xi(h), \end{aligned} \] and \(\chi_v(g^{-1}h^{-1}\omega)=\chi_{gv}(h^{-1}\omega)\) because \((g^{-1}\eta)(v)=\eta(gv)\). This is \((\pi_\omega(gv,1)\xi)(h)\). Together with \(\pi_\omega(v,1)\pi_\omega(v',1)=\pi_\omega(v+v',1)\) and \(\pi_\omega(0,g)\pi_\omega(0,g')=\pi_\omega(0,gg')\), this gives the multiplication rule of \(V\rtimes\Gamma\).

4.2. Let \(P\) be a copy containing \(1\). If \(\mathbf e_1\in W_0\), then \(\mathbf e_1\in W_0\cap\mathbb F_p^P=C_P^\perp\), so \(\langle\mathbf e_1,w_P\rangle=w_P(1)=0\); but all coordinates of \(w_P\) are nonzero. So \(v_*\ne0\). The coefficient of the identity \((0,1)\) in \(f_s\) is \(c_{s,0}=\frac1p|A_s|\). By the trace formula of Proposition 1.1(4), \(\tau(f_s)=\int\mathbf1[\omega(v_*)\in A_s]\,dm\); the value \(\omega(v_*)\) is uniformly distributed on \(\mathbb F_p\), by translation invariance as in Lemma 3.1 of the previous lesson (it is a surjective additive function of \(\omega\), as \(v_*\ne0\)), so the integral is \(|A_s|/p\).

4.3. Each edge \(\{x,y\}\) contributes \(\xi(y)\overline{\zeta(x)}+\xi(x)\overline{\zeta(y)}\) to \(\langle\operatorname{Adj}\xi,\zeta\rangle\), and by \(ab\le\frac12(a^2+b^2)\) the absolute value of this contribution is at most half of \(|\xi(x)|^2+|\xi(y)|^2+|\zeta(x)|^2+|\zeta(y)|^2\). Every vertex lies on at most \(d\) edges, so \(|\langle\operatorname{Adj}\xi,\zeta\rangle|\le\frac d2(\|\xi\|^2+\|\zeta\|^2)\), which is at most \(d\) for unit vectors.

4.4. The \(db\) incidences of \(P\)-edges at \(B\) include one for each edge of \(\partial_PB\) and two for each enabled edge of \(P\) inside \(B\); these are different incidences. So \(db\ge|\partial_PB|+2e_{\omega,P}(B)\).
