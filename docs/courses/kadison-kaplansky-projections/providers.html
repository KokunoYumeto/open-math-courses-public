Open Mathematics CoursesCourse contents (A torsion-free counterexample to the Kadison–Kaplansky conjecture)

Results on separated spectral bands, the tower, voltage covers and their Cayley graphs, the nilpotent voltage group and the random realization, with their assumptions

The course proves the statements below; each link leads to the statement with its assumptions and its proof. The lessons are self-checked by the writing AI; independent mathematical review is not complete.

  • A torsion-free counterexample to the Kadison-Kaplansky conjecture (OpenAI) — Theorem 1.1. There are a finitely generated torsion-free group G_proj and a projection e in C_r*(G_proj) with 0 < tau(e) < 1/2. Proved in lessons 2 to 18. No hypothesis.
  • Reduced subgroup inclusion — Lemma 2.1. For a subgroup Lambda of a countable group K, lambda_Lambda(x) -> lambda_K(x) extends to an isometric unital *-homomorphism C_r*(Lambda) -> C_r*(K) preserving the canonical traces. No hypothesis.
  • Torsion in free products — Lemma 3.2. A free product of torsion-free groups is torsion-free, and finitely many finitely generated factors give a finitely generated group. No hypothesis.
  • Scalar compression (OpenAI) — Proposition 4.1. A projection p in M_N(C_r*(Lambda)) with 0 < tau_(Lambda,N)(p) < 1/2 gives a projection e in C_r*(Lambda * F_N) of the same trace. No hypothesis.
  • The matrix construction (OpenAI) — Theorem 5.1. There are a finitely generated torsion-free group Gamma and a self-adjoint T in M_N(C Gamma) with a separated spectral projection p_0 in M_N(C_r*(Gamma)) of unnormalized trace in (0, 1/2). Proved in the last lesson. No hypothesis.
  • Separated spectral projections — Lemma 1.1. The projection onto a separated part of the spectrum of T = T* lies in every unital C*-algebra containing T, commutes with the commutant of T, and is a contour integral of the resolvent. No hypothesis.
  • The identity trace of equivariant operators — Lemma 2.1. Tr(A*A) = Tr(AA*) for equivariant A; the trace is additive, monotone and basis independent; equivariant projections of trace zero vanish; injective equivariant maps and equivalences compare traces. No hypothesis.
  • Compression of a separated upper space — Lemma 3.1. Cutting A = A* with a separated upper space E by a projection J with ||(1-J)E|| = eps <= 1/2 gives an operator within a + 12 eps of JAJ with an upper space equivalent to E and the same gap. No hypothesis.
  • A contour estimate — Lemma 4.2. If ||A - B|| <= u < r_0 <= b/2 and B has the eigenvalue 1 on F and spectrum in [-2, 1-b] elsewhere, the contour projection F_A satisfies ||F_A - F|| <= u/(r_0 - u) and is equivalent to F when u < r_0/2. No hypothesis.
  • Cardinalities of the layers — Lemma 2.1. |A_i| = d 2^(H_i), |B_i| = 2^(H_i), and all stream lengths at layer i are at most H_i. No hypothesis.
  • Degrees — Lemma 3.1. A vertex of B_k has D_ik = D 2^(|i|-|k|) slots for the pair (i,k), between D/2 and 2D, and d|A_i| = D_ik |B_k|. No hypothesis.
  • Coefficients and spills (OpenAI) — Lemma 6.1. The injections and spills can be chosen so that, for every family, side and nonzero functional zeta, every value of zeta M_a restricted to an initial segment of the inputs occurs for at most 0.55 n_sl slots, and every value of the full functional for at most 10^-3 n_sl slots. For large l.
  • One edge per slot — Lemma 3.1. The edge equations have exactly one solution for every vertex and slot, from either side, so every slot carries exactly one edge. No hypothesis.
  • Folding and the alphabet — Lemma 4.1. Both towers are folded, and the number m_l of positive labels satisfies log2(2 m_l) = 11 l + O(log l). No hypothesis.
  • Independent matching clusters (OpenAI) — Lemma 5.1. With uniform permutations at the marked block, the edges of a pair form independent uniform matchings within clusters. No hypothesis.
  • Uniform gap of the mean incidence (OpenAI) — Lemma 4.1. The mean incidence of every adjacent pair contracts functions of mean zero by a factor less than 0.8. Uses Lemma 6.1 of the lesson on streams and slots.
  • The local lemma, asymmetric form — Lemma 3.1. If each event is independent of the family of its non-neighbours and P(E_z) <= x_z prod over neighbours (1 - x_z') with 0 <= x_z < 1, all events are avoided with probability at least prod (1 - x_z) > 0. No hypothesis.
  • Expansion of the realized incidence (OpenAI) — Proposition 5.1. The tables can be chosen so that the normalized incidence of every adjacent pair maps normalized constants to normalized constants and has norm at most 1 - 1/800 off the constants. For large l.
  • Recovery of prefixes (OpenAI) — Lemma 1.1. Two nonbacktracking paths with the same word have equal stream prefixes at corresponding positions, of length growing with the radius. No hypothesis.
  • The tower (OpenAI) — Proposition 4.1. After deleting a fraction at most eps_l -> 0 of every layer, the tower Y has expanding incidences, folded labels from at most m_l letters, recovery of long paths from their words, and no short cyclically nonbacktracking closed paths. For large l.
  • The geometric conclusion (OpenAI) — Theorem 3.1. Under the girth condition, every component of the voltage cover embeds in the Cayley graph of Gamma, the indexed copies have nested overlap balls, the remaining edges form a forest, and Gamma is torsion-free. For large l.
  • Embeddings of the components (OpenAI) — Proposition 7.1. Every label map from a component of the cover to the Cayley graph is injective on vertices and formal edges. No hypothesis.
  • Short chain fillings (OpenAI) — Lemma 1.1. The chain of every closed walk of length n in the coned Cayley graph is a sum of at most 12n chains of closed walks of length at most four. No hypothesis.
  • Thin triangles from linear chain fillings — Lemma 2.1. In a connected graph in which the chain of every closed walk of length n is a sum of at most 12n chains of closed walks of length at most four, geodesic triangles are 10^6-thin. No hypothesis.
  • One ball for all earlier overlaps (OpenAI) — Proposition 4.1. In a well-order of the indexed copies, each copy has one ball containing all its vertices shared with earlier copies, on which its edges form a forest, of size at most 2^(C_bullet H_i). No hypothesis.
  • Assigned domains and the residual forest (OpenAI) — Proposition 5.2. The new vertices D_Q of the copies are disjoint, and the Cayley edges outside the copies' own domains form a forest. No hypothesis.
  • The incidence count — Proposition 6.1. Pairs of an indexed copy and one of its vertices correspond bijectively and equivariantly to pairs of a group element and a vertex of the tower. No hypothesis.
  • Torsion-freeness (OpenAI) — Proposition 4.1. The group Gamma defined by the closed walks of the cover is finitely generated and torsion-free. No hypothesis.
  • The rank bound — Lemma 1.1. A finite connected metric graph of total length at most Cg and girth greater than g has first Betti number at most max(1, ceil(20C))^2. No hypothesis.
  • The matrix detector — Lemma 3.1. A nontrivial reduced word of length less than m is not an identity on upper unitriangular integer m x m matrices; its entries are polynomials of degree at most m-1, also after fixed factors. No hypothesis.
  • Grading, Haar measure and integer points — Lemma 2.1. The affine group has integer polynomial laws, dilation automorphisms scaling the cocycle by y^m, Lebesgue Haar measure, and torsion-free nilpotent integer points. No hypothesis.
  • The fiber representations — Lemma 5.1. Fourier transform in the central coordinate decomposes the regular representation of F into twisted representations u_t of the two factors, commuting with twisted right operators. No hypothesis.
  • Schwartz idempotents (OpenAI) — Lemma 1.1. For each sign there is a Schwartz function J on the quotient group with J *_eps J = J, J^(*eps) = J and ||J||_2 = 1. Uses the Hermite expansion of the index theory course.
  • Nonzero discrete twisted projections (OpenAI) — Proposition 3.1. For 0 < |theta| < theta_0, the lattice samples of a rescaled Schwartz idempotent give a projection p_theta in C_r*(L, theta c) with 0 < tr(p_theta) <= C|theta|^(n_f). No hypothesis.
  • Absorption of the finite factor (OpenAI) — Lemma 3.1. Tensoring u_t with the finite magnetic representation R gives N copies of u_(t - delta), preserving norms and multiplying identity traces by N. No hypothesis.
  • Uniform weighted trace bound (OpenAI) — Proposition 6.1. w_i N t^(n_f) (delta - t)^(n_f) tends to zero uniformly in the active layers i, so the weighted fiber trace mu_l tends to zero. For large l.
  • The model band and its trace (OpenAI) — Lemma 2.1. The model operator has eigenvalue 1 on layerwise constant vectors of the active layers tensored with p(t), the rest of its spectrum below 1 - chi, identity trace equal to that of p(t), and deletion loss at most sqrt(eps_l). No hypothesis.
  • Realization of the model (OpenAI) — Theorem 3.1. For every eta > 0 there are voltages and finitely many matrix weights on the edges of Y such that the cover has the girth of Theorem 3.1 of the voltage cover lesson and its weighted adjacency is within eta of the compressed model. For large l.
  • Finite norm tests (OpenAI) — Lemma 4.1. Coordinate boxes in F with small boundary under bounded right translations test the regular norm of convolution matrices up to C x^(-B) R. No hypothesis.
  • Empirical averages (OpenAI) — Lemma 5.1. Expectations of at most C(1 + log q) finite operator distributions have M-term empirical averages within any fixed epsilon in the regular norm. For large l.
  • The expectation of one draw (OpenAI) — Proposition 4.1. For every epsilon there is a finite distribution of one weight and one voltage per block whose expectation is within epsilon of h_i h_k p(t) in every fiber. For large l.
  • The alphabet and the voltage coordinates — Lemma 5.1. The matrix weights number O(d^(1/32) log d), so refined labels number o(dD); voltages have coordinates at most 2^(C H_i) and wide independent unitriangular coordinates. For large l.
  • The Schwartz-Zippel bound — Lemma 1.1. A nonzero polynomial of degree D vanishes at independent uniform points of sets of size at least s with probability at most D/s. No hypothesis.
  • Walks without singleton edges (OpenAI) — Lemma 3.1. Closed walks of length 2l without singleton edges and with supports of first Betti number at most b number at most (72(2b+2)^2 Delta)^l. No hypothesis.
  • A simultaneous choice (OpenAI) — Proposition 5.1. One assignment of weights and voltages gives nontrivial holonomy to all short cyclically nonbacktracking closed walks and keeps every block within epsilon sqrt(dD_ik) of its mean. For large l.
  • The cover projection and its exact fiber trace (OpenAI) — Proposition 2.1. The weighted adjacency of the cover has a separated upper projection whose fiber traces equal those of p(t). For large l.
  • The weighted size (OpenAI) — Proposition 3.1. The layer-weighted identity trace of the cover projection is at most 96 mu_l. For large l.
  • A nonzero upper band (OpenAI) — Proposition 4.3. The weighted Cayley adjacency T has spectrum in (-inf, 1 - chi + 4 alpha] and [1 - 3 alpha, 1 + 3 alpha], with a nonzero upper projection in M_N(C_r*(Gamma)). For large l.
  • The matrix projection (OpenAI) — Theorem 6.1. The construction gives Theorem 5.1 of the first lesson: 0 < tau_(Gamma,N)(p_0) < 1/2. No hypothesis.

Reading order

Eighteen lessons. The first reduces the theorem to a matrix with a separated spectral band and the second proves the spectral and trace tools; lessons 3 to 7 build and prune the tower; lessons 8 to 10 prove the geometry of the voltage cover, its Cayley graph and torsion-freeness; lessons 11 to 14 build the nilpotent voltage group, its Schwartz idempotents and the magnetic factor; lessons 15 to 17 realize the model operator on the cover with random voltages; the last lesson transfers the band and bounds its trace. Van Kampen diagrams and plane maps are used as named statements with free locators.
