Uniform exclusion of Landau–Siegel zeros

OpenAI's theorem (2026) that (1 - beta) log q >= c for an absolute constant c > 0 and every real zero beta of every primitive real Dirichlet L-function of conductor q >= 3, the logarithmic form of the Landau–Siegel problem. The prime bias forced by a real zero near 1, an interpolation theorem for polynomials with separately bounded degrees on a projected integer box, a weighted determinant of conjugates in Q(sqrt d, sqrt 2), and its two bounds: Hadamard's inequality, and divisibility by the Frobenius congruence at the primes with chi(p) = -1. A zero-free region with no exceptional zero follows.

Four lessons with proofs, examples and exercises with solutions. Written and self-checked by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic); independent review is not complete.

  1. Real zeros and the prime bias
  2. Interpolation on a projected integer box
  3. A weighted determinant in a biquadratic field
  4. Two bounds for the determinant
