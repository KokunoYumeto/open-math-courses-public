Results on the energy of planar configurations and the linear programming bound, with their assumptions

The course proves the statements below; each link leads to the statement with its assumptions and its proof. The lessons are self-checked by the writing AI; independent mathematical review is not complete.

  • The energy of a lattice — Lemma 1.1. A lattice of covolume one in the plane has density one, and its energy for any nonnegative interaction is the sum of the interaction over the nonzero lattice vectors, finite or not.
  • The linear programming bound for configurations of density one — Theorem 3.1. If a continuous even function with suitable decay has a nonnegative Fourier transform, then for every locally finite planar configuration of centred density one the lower limit of the averaged sums of the function over pairs is at least the transform at zero minus the function at zero.
  • The bound and lattices — Proposition 4.1. The linear programming bound is at most the energy of every lattice of covolume one, with equality exactly when the function touches the interaction at the nonzero lattice points and its transform vanishes at the nonzero dual lattice points.
  • Gaussian optimality from a sharp pair — Theorem 4.2. If a sharp pair exists for a Gaussian interaction, every configuration of density one has at least the energy of the triangular lattice for it.
  • The moment problem on the unit interval — Theorem 2.3. A sequence all of whose iterated differences alternate in sign is the sequence of moments of exactly one finite positive Borel measure on the unit interval.
  • Completely monotone functions as mixtures — Theorem 3.1. A completely monotone function on the closed half-line is the integral of the functions v^t against a finite positive measure on the unit interval, of total mass the value at zero and without mass at zero.
  • From Gaussians to completely monotone interactions — Theorem 4.1. A planar configuration of density one that has at least the energy of the triangular lattice for every Gaussian interaction has at least its energy for every completely monotone interaction of the squared distance, in the extended nonnegative reals.
  • Universal optimality from sharp Gaussian pairs — Theorem 5.1. If a sharp pair exists for every Gaussian, the triangular lattice has the least energy among planar configurations of density one for every completely monotone interaction of the squared distance.
  • Residues of the triangular form — Lemma 1.1. Every integer of the form j^2 + j l + l^2 is congruent to 0, 1, 3, 4, 7 or 9 modulo 12.
  • The dual of the triangular lattice — Lemma 1.2. The dual lattice of the triangular lattice of covolume one is the lattice turned by a right angle.
  • The Fourier transform of a planar Gaussian — Lemma 3.1. The Fourier transform of exp(-pi beta |x|^2) on the plane is exp(-pi |xi|^2 / beta) divided by beta.
  • Gaussian duality — Theorem 4.1. If the normalized statement holds for every parameter k at least 2.36, there is a radial sharp pair for every Gaussian.
  • The Fourier coefficients of the sine product — Proposition 1.1. The product of the squares of 2 sin(pi (s - a)/12) over a = 0, 1, 3, 4, 7, 9 is a trigonometric polynomial with frequencies l/6, l from -6 to 6, and coefficients 5, -1 + i sqrt 3, 1 + i sqrt 3, -2, (-1 + i sqrt 3)/2, -1 - i sqrt 3, 1 for l = 0 to 6 and their conjugates for negative l.
  • The sine product at its zeros — Proposition 1.2. At each of its real zeros the sine product has a double zero with leading coefficient above 7/4 and relative next coefficient below 53/25 in absolute value; both are given in closed form for the six residue classes.
  • Cardinal functions — Theorem 2.2. For a summable coefficient list the cardinal function is entire, real on the real axis, has value and derivative at each node determined by the two coefficients of that node, and is the sum of a multiple of the sine product and the integral of exponentials with frequencies between -1 and 1 against a density of integral at most 36 times the sum of the absolute values of the coefficients.
  • Uniform bounds for cardinal functions — Corollary 2.3. The j-th derivative of a cardinal function is bounded on the real axis by pi^j (25 |C| + 36 W), with W the sum of the absolute values of the pole coefficients, wherever the nonzero coefficients sit.
  • The Fourier transform of a planar Gaussian with a complex parameter — Proposition 1.2. For a complex number gamma with positive real part, the Fourier transform of exp(-pi gamma |x|^2) on the plane is exp(-pi |xi|^2 / gamma) divided by gamma.
  • Fourier pairs from cardinal functions — Theorem 3.2. For a cardinal function p there is an entire function K, real on the real axis and decaying exponentially on the half-line with all derivatives, such that exp(-pi h s) p(s) and exp(-pi h s) K(s), with s = (sqrt 3 / 2)|x|^2 and h = 2/5, are Fourier transforms of each other on the plane.
  • The coupled pair — Corollary 3.3. For two coefficient lists the functions H_1 = p_1 + K_{p_2} and H_2 = p_2 + K_{p_1} give an admissible radial function exp(-pi h s) H_1(s) with Fourier transform exp(-pi h s) H_2(s); if they satisfy the contact and sign conditions, the normalized statement holds.
  • The norm of the jet operator — Proposition 4.1. The operator S that sends a coefficient list to minus the jet list of its transformed function maps into summable lists, with the sum of absolute values at most 4000 times that of the input list.
  • The transformed kernel on two frequency ranges — Lemma 1.1. For frequencies of absolute value at most 5/6 the kernel decays at the rate 0.70; between 5/6 and 1 it is smaller in size and decays at the rate 0.187 + 2.48 (1 - |t|).
  • Refined bounds for the transformed function — Theorem 3.1. The transformed function of a cardinal function and its derivatives are bounded on the positive half-line by a term with the rate 0.70 plus a term with the rate 0.187 whose part coming from the pole coefficients carries a factor 1/s.
  • The tail bound for the jet operator — Theorem 3.2. The sum of the absolute values of the output of the jet operator at the nodes n >= r is at most U_r(w, c) = 800 (w + c) e^(-0.70 r)/(1 - e^(-0.70)) + 5.3 (2.54 w/r + 2c) e^(-0.187 r)/(1 - e^(-0.187)), where w bounds the pole coefficients and c the constant entry of the list.
  • The first rule of Fejér — Theorem 1.2. For even N the rule with the points cos(pi (v + 1/2)/N) and the weights o_v gives the average over [-1, 1] of every polynomial of degree at most N - 1; the weights are at least 1/(N(N - 1)) and add up to 1.
  • The error of the rule for holomorphic functions — Proposition 2.1. For a function holomorphic and bounded by A on the closed disc of twice the radius of the interval, the average over the interval and the rule differ by at most 4A 2^(-N).
  • The quadrature error for the spectral integrals — Theorem 3.3. With 384 points on each of the six frequency intervals, the values and scaled derivatives y^a p^(a)(s)/a! and y^a K^(a)(s)/a! for a list with one unit entry at a node up to 168, for 0 <= s <= 168 and |y| <= 3/2, differ from their quadrature forms by less than 10^-31.
  • The jet matrix and its quadrature form — Lemma 1.1. On lists that vanish beyond the node 168, every entry of the matrix of the jet operator at the nodes up to 168 differs from its quadrature form by less than 2 x 10^-31.
  • The residual of the reference columns — Lemma 3.1. The reference columns satisfy X_1 - D X_2 = g_1 - D^512 g_1 and X_2 - D X_1 = g_2 - D^512 g_2 on the nodes up to 168.
  • Norm bounds for the finite matrices (computed) — Proposition 4.1. The matrix D^64 has column-sum norm below 0.004, the two finite geometric sums have norms adding up to less than 36, and the columns of the two reference matrices have total l^1 norm below 2.3 (columns 1 to 5) and 5.6 (columns 6 to 10). Established by a computation in ball arithmetic; the program is printed in the lesson.
  • The size of the reference lists — Corollary 4.2. For every parameter k at least 2.36 the two reference lists have total l^1 norm below 4.8 and constant entries of total absolute value below 0.02, and D^512 has norm below 0.004^8.

Reading order

Eight lessons so far, with complete proofs; one statement of the eighth lesson, a set of bounds for explicitly defined finite matrices, is established by a computation with rigorous error bounds, and the lesson prints the program. The first three prove the universal optimality of the triangular lattice from one named hypothesis, the normalized statement of the third lesson. The fourth to eighth lessons begin the proof of that statement, the long part of OpenAI's proof: they reduce it to two lists of numbers that solve a linear system and satisfy two sign conditions. The solution of the system and the sign conditions are not yet in the course.
