Results on pretrees, with their assumptions

The course proves the statements below; each link leads to the statement with its assumptions and its proof. The lessons are self-checked by the writing AI; independent mathematical review is not complete.

  • Pretree — Definition 2.1. A set with a ternary betweenness relation satisfying four axioms that hold for the vertices of a tree.
  • Four points in a row — Lemma 2.3. Three implications that place four points of a pretree in a row.
  • Intervals along a chain — Lemma 3.1. The interval between the ends of a finite sequence of points lies in the union of the intervals between consecutive points.
  • Finite pretrees and finite trees — Theorem 4.1. Every finite pretree is a set of vertices of a finite tree with the betweenness of the tree.
  • Cycles of adjacent points — Lemma 4.3. Distinct points of a pretree that are cyclically adjacent are pairwise adjacent.
  • Directions — Proposition 5.2. A linear subset of a pretree with two chosen points has exactly one direction in which the first precedes the second.
  • Intervals are ordered — Corollary 5.3. A closed interval of a pretree is an arc, ordered from one end to the other, and its ends are determined by it.
  • Uniqueness of medians — Proposition 6.2. Three points of a pretree have at most one median.
  • Half-open intervals — Lemma 1.2. In a dense pretree, two half-open intervals with the same closed end that are equal have the same open end.
  • Complete and dense does not imply median — Proposition 3.2. A tripod with a half-open segment next to the centre removed on one leg is a complete dense pretree in which three points have no median and an interval has a cut that is not made at a point.
  • Ends of half-open intervals — Lemma 4.1. In a dense median pretree, if the half-open interval from x to y lies in the closed interval from x to z, then y lies in it.
  • Cuts are made at points — Theorem 4.3. In a complete dense median pretree, every partition of a closed interval into two arcs is made at a point of the interval.
  • Intervals have no gaps — Corollary 4.4. In a complete dense median pretree every nonempty subset of a closed interval has a least upper bound in the interval.
  • Gates — Lemma 1.1. In a tree, the path from a vertex to a point of a given path passes through the gate of the vertex, and the path between two vertices with different gates runs through both gates.
  • From weakly to strongly dense — Proposition 2.2. In a median pretree the medians of triples from a weakly dense set form a strongly dense set.
  • Cantor's theorem — Theorem 3.1. Any two nonempty countable dense totally ordered sets without ends are order isomorphic.
  • Order type of intervals — Theorem 4.1. In a complete separable median pretree every interval with distinct ends is order isomorphic to the real interval [0,1].
  • Intervals are arcs — Proposition 3.1. Under the assumptions of the theorem, every interval of the pretree with distinct ends is homeomorphic to the real interval [0,1], by an order isomorphism.
  • Small intervals — Proposition 4.2. Every neighbourhood of a point contains the intervals from that point to all points of a smaller neighbourhood.
  • Countable base — Proposition 4.4. The sets H(p,q) with p, q in a countable strongly dense set form a subbase of the topology, so the space is metrizable.
  • Dendrites from pretrees — Theorem 5.1. A compact space with a countable dense subset carrying a complete dense median pretree structure with open sets H(x,y) is a dendrite, and the pretree intervals are its only arcs.
  • Flows towards points — Lemma 2.2. A flow is the flow towards a point a exactly when it flows from every other point to a.
  • Adding a point for a flow — Proposition 3.1. For a flow on a pretree, adding one terminal point whose betweenness is given by the flow yields a pretree in which the flow is the trace of the flow towards the new point.
  • The pretree of a pretree and its flows — Theorem 4.1. A pretree together with the set of all its flows is a pretree in which every flow is terminal and is the trace of the flow towards itself.
  • Cofinal arcs — Lemma 2.3. Directed arcs with a common final segment on which their orders agree have the same flow.
  • The flow of a directed arc — Proposition 2.4. The relation defined by a directed arc is a flow; on the arc it is the order, and from a point of the arc it reaches exactly the later points of the arc.
  • Arcs with the same flow — Lemma 2.6. Two directed arcs with a common point and the same flow are cofinal.
  • Restriction to a full subset — Lemma 3.1. If the part of a directed arc inside a full subset is endless, the flow of the arc restricts on the subset to the flow of that part.
  • Completeness and suprema — Theorem 4.3. A pretree is complete if and only if every directed arc has a supremum.
  • Intervals between flows — Lemma 1.1. Two different flows have a point of the pretree between them; the interval between the flows towards two points is the closed interval between the points.
  • Betweenness of flows — Definition 2.1. A flow r lies between flows p and q if r is the flow towards a point between p and q, or r is not a flow towards a point and no point lies both between p and r and between r and q.
  • The flows form a pretree — Theorem 3.1. The set of flows of a pretree with this betweenness is a pretree.
  • Medians of flows — Theorem 4.1. Every three flows have a median; when no point and none of the three flows serves, it is the flow that goes where two of the three go.
  • Completeness — Theorem 5.2. Every arc in the pretree of flows is an interval.
  • The completion — Corollary 5.3. Every pretree embeds, with its betweenness, in the complete median pretree of its flows.
  • Star — Definition 2.1. A set of at least two points of a pretree that is full and is maximal among the sets with no betweenness relation among their points.
  • The flow of a star — Theorem 3.1. From each point the flow of a star runs along the interval to the nearest point of the star; this is a flow, and the star is the set of points from which it runs nowhere.
  • Classes and type of a flow — Lemma 4.2. The arcs along which a flow runs fall into classes, each endless or ending in one point; the type of the flow counts the classes of each kind.
  • Three kinds of flows — Theorem 5.1. Every flow on a nonempty pretree is exactly one of: the flow towards a point, the flow of a star, the flow of an endless directed arc; the type decides which.
  • Points of the pretree are not adjacent — Proposition 1.1. Between the flows towards two different points of a pretree there is always a further flow in the completion.
  • Adjacent points of the completion — Theorem 2.3. Two points of the completion are adjacent exactly when one is the flow towards a point a and the other is a different flow that runs nowhere from a; so exactly one of them is a point of the pretree.
  • Neighbours and finite intervals — Corollary 2.4. A flow of type (m,n) that is not a flow towards a point has n neighbours; a finite interval of the completion alternates between points of the pretree and other flows.
  • Neighbours and branches — Theorem 3.2. The neighbours in the completion of a point a of the pretree correspond one to one to the branches at a, the classes of the relation that a is not between two points.
  • Finite intervals are chains — Lemma 1.2. A finite interval of a pretree is a chain of points, each adjacent to the next, in the order of the interval.
  • Discrete median pretrees and trees — Theorem 2.1. A pretree with finite intervals in which every three points have a median is the vertex set of exactly one tree; its edges join the adjacent pairs.
  • Discrete pretrees in trees — Theorem 3.2. Every pretree with finite intervals is a set of vertices of a tree, with the betweenness of the tree, in which each further vertex is joined to at least three points of the pretree.
  • Completion of a finite pretree — Proposition 4.1. The completion of a finite pretree is the vertex set of a finite tree in which every edge joins a point of the pretree to a flow that is not a flow towards a point.
  • The quotient pretree — Theorem 2.2. For an equivalence relation with full classes on a pretree, the set of classes is a pretree: a class lies between two others if one of its points lies between them.
  • Intervals map onto intervals — Proposition 3.1. The quotient map sends the interval between two points onto the interval between their classes.
  • A quotient of a quotient — Proposition 3.3. A full relation on a quotient pulls back to a full relation on the pretree, with the same quotient.
  • Quotients of median pretrees — Theorem 4.1. A quotient of a median pretree by a full relation is median, and the quotient map preserves medians.
  • Quotients of complete pretrees — Theorem 4.2. A quotient of a complete pretree by a full relation is complete.
