Results on pretrees, with their assumptions

The course proves the statements below; each link leads to the statement with its assumptions and its proof. The lessons are self-checked by the writing AI; independent mathematical review is not complete.

  • Pretree — Definition 2.1. A set with a ternary betweenness relation satisfying four axioms that hold for the vertices of a tree.
  • Four points in a row — Lemma 2.3. Three implications that place four points of a pretree in a row.
  • Intervals along a chain — Lemma 3.1. The interval between the ends of a finite sequence of points lies in the union of the intervals between consecutive points.
  • Finite pretrees and finite trees — Theorem 4.1. Every finite pretree is a set of vertices of a finite tree with the betweenness of the tree.
  • Cycles of adjacent points — Lemma 4.3. Distinct points of a pretree that are cyclically adjacent are pairwise adjacent.
  • Directions — Proposition 5.2. A linear subset of a pretree with two chosen points has exactly one direction in which the first precedes the second.
  • Intervals are ordered — Corollary 5.3. A closed interval of a pretree is an arc, ordered from one end to the other, and its ends are determined by it.
  • Uniqueness of medians — Proposition 6.2. Three points of a pretree have at most one median.
  • Half-open intervals — Lemma 1.2. In a dense pretree, two half-open intervals with the same closed end that are equal have the same open end.
  • Complete and dense does not imply median — Proposition 3.2. A tripod with a half-open segment next to the centre removed on one leg is a complete dense pretree in which three points have no median and an interval has a cut that is not made at a point.
  • Ends of half-open intervals — Lemma 4.1. In a dense median pretree, if the half-open interval from x to y lies in the closed interval from x to z, then y lies in it.
  • Cuts are made at points — Theorem 4.3. In a complete dense median pretree, every partition of a closed interval into two arcs is made at a point of the interval.
  • Intervals have no gaps — Corollary 4.4. In a complete dense median pretree every nonempty subset of a closed interval has a least upper bound in the interval.
  • Gates — Lemma 1.1. In a tree, the path from a vertex to a point of a given path passes through the gate of the vertex, and the path between two vertices with different gates runs through both gates.
  • From weakly to strongly dense — Proposition 2.2. In a median pretree the medians of triples from a weakly dense set form a strongly dense set.
  • Cantor's theorem — Theorem 3.1. Any two nonempty countable dense totally ordered sets without ends are order isomorphic.
  • Order type of intervals — Theorem 4.1. In a complete separable median pretree every interval with distinct ends is order isomorphic to the real interval [0,1].
  • Intervals are arcs — Proposition 3.1. Under the assumptions of the theorem, every interval of the pretree with distinct ends is homeomorphic to the real interval [0,1], by an order isomorphism.
  • Small intervals — Proposition 4.2. Every neighbourhood of a point contains the intervals from that point to all points of a smaller neighbourhood.
  • Countable base — Proposition 4.4. The sets H(p,q) with p, q in a countable strongly dense set form a subbase of the topology, so the space is metrizable.
  • Dendrites from pretrees — Theorem 5.1. A compact space with a countable dense subset carrying a complete dense median pretree structure with open sets H(x,y) is a dendrite, and the pretree intervals are its only arcs.
