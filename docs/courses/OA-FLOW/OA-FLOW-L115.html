The dual-center kernel through central overlap

A frequency survives every fixed corner precisely when the corresponding dual automorphism fixes the crossed-product center. We first prove a general overlap theorem for an integrable action with eigenunitaries. We then construct that setting by regular stabilization, check its full normal maps and bounded orbit domain, and transport the conclusion back. The direct absorption proof \(\Gamma(\alpha)+\operatorname{Sp}(\alpha)=\operatorname{Sp}(\alpha)\) and its closed-subgroup deduction give a second route to the subgroup property.

The group is arbitrary locally compact Hausdorff abelian and the von Neumann algebra and representation Hilbert spaces are arbitrary. There is no countability, separability, factor, finite-Haar or original-action integrability assumption.

An abstract integrable principal-eigenspace theorem

Theorem. Let \(\beta\) be an integrable normal point-ultraweakly continuous action of \(G\) on a nonzero von Neumann algebra \(P\). Let \(\mu:H\to\mathcal U(P)\) be a strongly continuous unitary representation satisfying

\[ \beta_s(\mu_p)=p(s)\mu_p\qquad(s\in G,\ p\in H). \tag{DK4} \] Put \(B=P^\beta\) and \(\delta_p=\operatorname{Ad}(\mu_p)|_{Z(B)}\). Then

\[ \Gamma(\beta)=\{p\in H:\delta_p=\operatorname{id}_{Z(B)}\}. \tag{DK5} \] The claim does not assume that the eigenunitaries belong to \(B\).

Proof. For \(b\in B\), the scalar factors in \(\beta_s(\mu_p b\mu_p^*)\) cancel. Thus \(\mu_p\) normalizes \(B\), and also \(Z(B)\). Conjugation is normal by the vector-series test in CP6. The representation law gives an action \(\delta\) of \(H\). It is point-ultraweakly continuous: for a vector functional of \(\mu_p z\mu_p^*\), split the difference into the two changing vectors \(\mu_p^*\xi,\mu_p^*\eta\); strong continuity and the bound \(\|z\|\) control both terms. A summable normal vector series has a uniformly small tail, so the conclusion holds for every normal functional. The center is a von Neumann algebra, since commutation with each fixed bounded element is an ultraweakly closed condition. Consequently the complete L113 normal abelian-action theorem applies to this center and its actual norm-continuous part.

Write \(P_p=\{x:\beta_s(x)=p(s)x\text{ for all }s\}\). The product \(x\mu_p^*\) is fixed whenever \(x\in P_p\); conversely \(b\mu_p\in P_p\) for \(b\in B\). Fixed multiplication is ultraweakly continuous, with inverse multiplication by \(\mu_p^*\). Thus

\[ P_p=B\mu_p \quad\hbox{as ultraweakly closed linear spaces}. \tag{DK6} \] The reflected singleton theorem SS3 identifies this actual eigenoperator space with the positive-label singleton spectral space, including its zero element.

For a nonzero central projection \(e\in Z(B)\), the corner's \(p\)-eigenspace is precisely \(eP_pe=P_p\cap ePe\): fixed compression preserves the eigenphase, and an element already in the corner is its own compression. Since \(e\) and \(\delta_p(e)\) are central in \(B\),

\[ eP_pe=B e\delta_p(e)\mu_p,\qquad eP_pe\ne\{0\}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ e\delta_p(e)\ne0. \tag{DK7} \] Indeed \(\mu_pe=\delta_p(e)\mu_p\). If \(k=e\delta_p(e)=0\), the entire right-hand space is zero. If \(k\ne0\), then \(k\mu_p=e(k\mu_p)e\) is an actual \(p\)-eigenoperator; unitarity gives \(\|k\mu_p\|=\|k\|=1\). This proves both nonzero implications.

If \(\delta_p\) fixes the center, every nonzero central \(e\) has \(e\delta_p(e)=e\). Its corner therefore detects \(p\). GCC's complete central-corner intersection puts \(p\) in \(\Gamma(\beta)\).

If \(\delta_p\ne\operatorname{id}\), L113 displacement supplies \(0\ne e\in\operatorname{Proj}(Z(B))\) and an open neighborhood \(U\ni p\) such that \(e\delta_q(e)=0\) for all \(q\in U\). Formula (DK7) makes every such corner eigenspace zero. The compression proof above makes \(\beta^e\) integrable. The direct open-localization theorem of L114 then gives \((ePe)_0^{\beta^e}(U)=0\), where the subscript means the ultraweakly closed span of filters with positive Fourier support in \(U\). Choose a compactly supported plateau \(f\) with \(f(p)=1\) and support in \(U\). Every \((\beta^e)_f(y)\) belongs to that zero space, so \(f\) is in the action annihilator while \(f(p)=1\). Its hull therefore omits \(p\). Thus \(p\notin\operatorname{Sp}(\beta^e)\), hence \(p\notin\Gamma(\beta)\). This proves (DK5). \(\square\)

The open-localization step is essential: the absence of one eigenoperator alone would not exclude a limit frequency from an arbitrary action spectrum.

Stabilization preserves the Connes spectrum

Let \(\mathcal K=L^2(G)\), and define the right regular representation and its conjugation action by

$$ (R_s\xi)(r)=\xi(r+s),\qquad \rho_s=\operatorname{Ad}R_s. \tag{K15} $$

On

$$ \overline M=M\ \overline\otimes\ B(\mathcal K) \tag{K16} $$

consider

$$ \overline\alpha_s=\alpha_s\otimes\operatorname{id}, \qquad \widetilde\alpha_s=\alpha_s\otimes\rho_s. \tag{K17} $$

The normal tensor action exists on the entire spatial tensor product by NCF1/3. In a faithful standard representation, the implementation from NCF2 makes it point-ultraweakly continuous; NCF3 transports that property normally to every faithful representation. The original Hilbert space need not itself implement \(\alpha\). The fixed algebra of \(\overline\alpha\) is

$$ \overline M^{\overline\alpha} =F\ \overline\otimes\ B(\mathcal K),\qquad Z(\overline M^{\overline\alpha})=Z(F)\otimes1. \tag{K18} $$

NCF1 proves the first identity in (K18) by all matrix entries, at arbitrary Hilbert dimension. Here is the center calculation. An element central in \(F\bar\otimes B(\mathcal K)\) commutes with every second-factor matrix unit. Commutation with each diagonal matrix unit makes all off-diagonal entries zero; commutation with \(|e_i\rangle\langle e_j|\) makes all diagonal entries equal to some \(z\in F\). Thus the element is \(z\otimes1\). Commutation with \(a\otimes1\), for \(a\in F\), makes \(z\in Z(F)\). Conversely every such tensor is central, proving the second identity. A basis exists by the earlier Hilbert proof used in NCF1; it is not presumed countable. Also \(\mathcal K\ne0\): a nonzero compact Haar bump has positive finite squared norm by HR3/6.

For every nonzero \(c\in\operatorname{Proj}(Z(F))\), the action on the corresponding central corner is \(\alpha^c\otimes\operatorname{id}\). Its Fourier filter is \((\alpha^c)_f\otimes\operatorname{id}\), so its action annihilator and spectrum equal those of \(\alpha^c\). For precision, the normal filter identity is checked entrywise against matrix vector tests: AT5 makes each entry the filtered entry, so all entries agree. A bounded matrix slice is normal by CP6; NCF1 constructs the normal tensor automorphisms, and AT5 constructs their normal integrated filter. No tensor extension of an arbitrary bounded map is presumed. If the base filter vanishes, every matrix entry of its amplification vanishes, so the amplified filter is zero. If it does not vanish, test a nonzero base output tensored with a rank-one projection. The two action annihilators are equal, hence their hull spectra are equal. Since central fixed corners suffice by the earlier GCC proof,

$$ \Gamma(\overline\alpha)=\Gamma(\alpha). \tag{K19} $$

Equivalently, a rank-one projection on the second tensor factor is fixed, has central support one in (K18), and cuts the stabilized action back to \(\alpha\).

The regular tensor action is integrable

Use the complete locally determined Haar convention of HR8–9 for \(L^\infty(G)\), and the canonical finite-exponent \(L^2(G)\) comparison with the outer regular Radon convention. The multiplier representation on \(\mathcal K\) is the actual ND multiplication-algebra proof. No global-null-set identification of the two raw measures is asserted. Formula (K15) gives

$$ \rho_s(M_h)=M_{h(s+\,\cdot\,)}. \tag{K23} $$

$$ \int_G\rho_s(M_h)\,ds =\left(\int_Gh(r)\,dr\right)1. \tag{K24} $$

The integral in (K24) is an extended-positive orbit value, not an operator-norm Bochner integral. \[ \int_G\langle\rho_s(M_h)\xi,\xi\rangle\,ds =\int_G\int_G h(r+s)|\xi(r)|^2\,dr\,ds =\left(\int_Gh\right)\|\xi\|_2^2. \tag{DK8} \] Changing representatives changes only null sections on this carrier. The dual-center kernel is exactly the Connes spectrum

Finally use (K22) and the identification (K29):

$$ \boxed{ \Gamma(\alpha) =\ker\!\left( \widehat\alpha:H\longrightarrow \operatorname{Aut}Z(M\rtimes_\alpha G) \right).} \tag{K43} $$

Thus (DK5) applied to the verified regular system gives exactly (K42)–(K43), rather than a statement for a different-sign dual action. Connes frequencies absorb the action spectrum

Let \(G\) be a locally compact abelian group, \(H=\widehat G\), and let \(\alpha:G\to\operatorname{Aut}(M)\) be a point-ultraweakly continuous action on a nonzero von Neumann algebra. Put \(F=M^\alpha\). Recall from GCC's complete definition that

$$ \Gamma(\alpha) =\bigcap_{\substack{0\ne e\in\operatorname{Proj}(F)}} \operatorname{Sp}(\alpha^e). \tag{K1} $$

We prove

$$ \boxed{\Gamma(\alpha)+\operatorname{Sp}(\alpha) =\operatorname{Sp}(\alpha).} \tag{K2} $$

The inclusion from right to left follows from \(0\in\Gamma(\alpha)\). For the other inclusion, take

$$ p\in\Gamma(\alpha),\qquad q\in\operatorname{Sp}(\alpha), \tag{K3} $$

and let \(U\) be any open neighborhood of \(p+q\). Continuity of addition first supplies neighborhoods \(V_0\ni p,W_0\ni q\) with \(V_0+W_0\subset U\). The actual LCH compact-neighborhood shrinking proved in H0 gives \(V\ni p\) and \(W\ni q\) with compact closures contained in those neighborhoods. Consequently

$$ \overline V+\overline W\subset U. \tag{K4} $$

Local spectral detection supplies a nonzero \(x\in M\) satisfying

$$ \operatorname{Sp}_\alpha(x)\subset\overline W. \tag{K5} $$

Let \(s_r(a)\) denote the right support of \(a\), and form

$$ e=\bigvee_{t\in G}s_r(\alpha_t(x)). \tag{K6} $$

The join is nonzero, and translation merely reindexes its terms. Hence

$$ 0\ne e\in F. \tag{K7} $$

Because \(p\in\operatorname{Sp}(\alpha^e)\), local detection inside \(eMe\) gives

$$ 0\ne y\in eMe,\qquad \operatorname{Sp}_\alpha(y)\subset\overline V. \tag{K8} $$

Some translate of \(x\) acts nontrivially on \(y\). Indeed, if \(\alpha_t(x)y=0\) for every \(t\), then \(s_r(\alpha_t(x))y=0\) for every \(t\). GCC TOOLS proves both support implications: \(ab=0\) if and only if \(s_r(a)b=0\), and the join projects onto the closed span of the support ranges. The range of \(y\) is perpendicular to each such range, hence to their closed span. Thus the join in (K6) gives \(ey=0\), contrary to \(ey=y\ne0\). Choose \(t\) with

$$ z=\alpha_t(x)y\ne0. \tag{K9} $$

The support join is fixed because automorphisms preserve the least right-support characterization and projection joins, and the group translation permutes the terms; these are precisely GCC TOOLS. The nonzero \(y\) is supplied by the compact local-detection formulation in the opening, inside the normal reduced action. Orbit invariance of vector spectra and the reflected GL6 product theorem yield

$$ \operatorname{Sp}_\alpha(z) \subset\overline W+\overline V \subset U. \tag{K10} $$

The sum of the two compact closures in (K10) is compact, so GL6's general closure causes no extra term here. The spectrum of \(z\ne0\) is nonempty by GL2 essentiality and LF6's empty-hull theorem. Thus every neighborhood of \(p+q\) contains the spectrum of a nonzero vector. The local detection criterion gives \(p+q\in\operatorname{Sp}(\alpha)\), proving (K2).

The Connes spectrum is a closed subgroup

The set \(\Gamma(\alpha)\) is closed because it is an intersection of closed action spectra. It contains \(0\): the unit of every nonzero fixed corner is a nonzero zero-frequency vector. It is symmetric because

$$ \operatorname{Sp}_\alpha(x^*) =-\operatorname{Sp}_\alpha(x), \tag{K11} $$

so every reduced action spectrum in (K1) is symmetric.

It remains to prove closure under addition. Fix \(p,r\in\Gamma(\alpha)\) and a nonzero \(e\in\operatorname{Proj}(F)\). Every nonzero projection fixed by \(\alpha^e\) is also fixed by \(\alpha\), so

$$ p\in\Gamma(\alpha^e),\qquad r\in\operatorname{Sp}(\alpha^e). \tag{K12} $$

Apply (K2) to the reduced action:

$$ p+r\in\operatorname{Sp}(\alpha^e). \tag{K13} $$

Since \(e\) was arbitrary, (K1) puts \(p+r\) in \(\Gamma(\alpha)\). Therefore

$$ \boxed{\Gamma(\alpha)\text{ is a closed subgroup of }H.} \tag{K14} $$

The same proof works without a countability or separability assumption. Neighborhoods and support joins are nets and arbitrary joins, respectively.

Problem. In the proof of (K14), why is it insufficient to use (K2) only for the original action?

Solution. From \(p,r\in\Gamma(\alpha)\subset\operatorname{Sp}(\alpha)\), the original absorption law gives only \(p+r\in\operatorname{Sp}(\alpha)\). Membership in \(\Gamma(\alpha)\) requires membership in every reduced action spectrum. Applying the same absorption law to each \(\alpha^e\), as in (K12)--(K13), supplies exactly those cornerwise conclusions. \(\square\)

Frequencies and central projection overlap

The upper panel concerns an arbitrary locally compact Hausdorff abelian group \(G\), its dual \(H\), and an integrable point-ultraweakly continuous action \(\beta\) on a nonzero von Neumann algebra \(P\). Orbit averages for an arbitrary locally compact group

The Fourier and Connes-spectrum arguments above concern LCA groups. Orbit averaging and inheritance of integrability also have a formulation for any locally compact Hausdorff group. Fix such a group \(G\), a point-ultraweakly continuous action \(\alpha\) on \(M\), and left Haar measure \(m\). Use the Haar and modular conventions \[ m(Et)=\Delta_G(t)m(E). \tag{L115G1} \] No second countability, sigma compactness, separable predual or countably decomposable algebra is assumed. Let \(F=M^\alpha\).

Theorem (whole positive cone and bounded finite domains). For every compact \(K\subseteq G\), the compact orbit average \(A_K:M\to M\) is bounded and normal, positive, and satisfies \(A_K(1)=m(K)1\). There is a faithful normal operator-valued weight \[ T_\alpha:M_+\longrightarrow\widehat F_+, \qquad \widehat{\omega|_F}(T_\alpha(x)) =\sup_K\omega(A_K(x)) =\int_G\omega(\alpha_s(x))\,dm(s) \quad(\omega\in M_*^+). \tag{L115G2} \] The supremum is over all compact subsets, ordered by inclusion. Infinite values and the infinite spectral projection of an extended-positive element are retained. The output is fixed under \(\alpha\), while the input obeys \[ T_\alpha(\alpha_t(x))=\Delta_G(t)^{-1}T_\alpha(x). \tag{L115G3} \] Define \[ F_{T_\alpha}=\{x\in M_+:\sup_K\|A_K(x)\|<\infty\},\qquad N_{T_\alpha}=\{a\in M:a^*a\in F_{T_\alpha}\},\qquad m_{T_\alpha}=\operatorname{span}F_{T_\alpha} =\operatorname{span}N_{T_\alpha}^*N_{T_\alpha}. \tag{L115G4} \] Here \(F_{T_\alpha}\) is exactly the set where \(T_\alpha\) has a bounded value in \(F_+\). The following conditions are equivalent:

  1. \(N_{T_\alpha}\) is weak-operator dense in \(M\), that is, \(T_\alpha\) is semifinite and \(\alpha\) is integrable.
  2. \(m_{T_\alpha}\) is ultraweakly dense in \(M\).
  3. There is an increasing net of positive contractions in \(F_{T_\alpha}\), converging strongly to \(1\).

Proof of the whole-cone construction. For each \(\omega\in M_*\), AT-3 makes \(s\mapsto\omega\alpha_s\) norm continuous. On a compact set its image is compact and therefore separable in the metric of \(M_*\). The compact integral in AT-5 exists in the predual, and its adjoint defines \[ \omega(A_K(x))=\int_K\omega(\alpha_s(x))\,dm(s). \] Thus \(A_K\) is normal, \(\|A_K\|\leq m(K)\), and has the stated positivity and value at one. For \(x\geq0\) the operators \(A_K(x)\) increase with \(K\).

The nonnegative continuous function \(s\mapsto\omega(\alpha_s(x))\) has integral equal to the supremum of its compact integrals. This follows from the compact localization in HR-2 and HR-5: its integral is the supremum over compact continuous functions below it, each with compact support; conversely every compact restriction is bounded above by the whole integral. This statement works for an infinite integral and for either the outer regular Haar convention or its complete locally determined counterpart in HR-9. It introduces no unrestricted product-measurability assertion.

Define \(m_x(\omega)=\sup_K\omega(A_K(x))\) on \(M_*^+\). Each compact term is continuous there. A common larger compact set handles any two compact indices, so this supremum is additive, positively homogeneous and norm lower semicontinuous, including infinite values. Hence \(m_x\in\widehat M_+\), by EP-4. The complete spectral representation EP-2–3 writes it as a positive affiliated operator on its finite-support subspace together with its complementary infinite projection.

For \(t\in G\), testing on every \(\omega\geq0\) gives \[ \widehat{\omega\alpha_t}(m_x) =\int_G\omega(\alpha_{ts}(x))\,dm(s) =\widehat\omega(m_x). \] This is left translation of the integration variable. Thus \(\alpha_t\) fixes \(m_x\), including its full form domain. The unique spectral pair and its bounded truncations \((H_x\wedge n)e_x+n(1-e_x)\) are consequently fixed by every \(\alpha_t\), by FF's spectral transport. The finite and infinite projections and all spectral projections lie in \(F\). The same pair therefore defines a unique element \(T_\alpha(x)\in\widehat F_+\) whose bounded truncations give (L115G2). Every positive normal functional on \(F\) has a positive normal extension to \(M\), by EP-1's vector-series extension. Testing those extensions proves uniqueness as an element of \(\widehat F_+\).

Additivity and positive homogeneity in \(x\) follow at each compact average and then at the directed supremum. If \(x_i\uparrow x\) is a bounded increasing positive net, normality of \(A_K\) and interchange of two independent suprema give \[ \sup_i\sup_K\omega(A_K(x_i)) =\sup_K\sup_i\omega(A_K(x_i)) =\sup_K\omega(A_K(x)). \tag{L115G5} \] Positive normal extensions from \(F\) prove order normality in \(\widehat F_+\). For \(b\in F\), compact averages satisfy \(A_K(b^*xb)=b^*A_K(x)b\). The full bounded-conjugation and supremum rule of EP-4 gives \(T_\alpha(b^*xb)=b^*T_\alpha(x)b\), including singular \(b\) and all infinite values. Thus this is an operator-valued weight.

If \(T_\alpha(x)=0\), every nonnegative continuous function \(s\mapsto\omega(\alpha_s(x))\) has zero Haar integral. A positive value would give a positive lower bound on a nonempty open neighborhood, which has positive Haar measure by HR-6. Its value at the identity is therefore zero for every \(\omega\in M_*^+\), so \(x=0\). This proves faithfulness without a countable family of states.

Right translation is different from the output calculation. For every compact \(K\), the convention (L115G1) gives \[ A_K(\alpha_t(x)) =\Delta_G(t)^{-1}A_{Kt}(x). \tag{L115G6} \] The sets \(Kt\) run through all compact sets. Taking their extended-positive supremum proves (L115G3). In the LCA case \(\Delta_G=1\), recovering the input invariance used above.

If the norms of \(A_K(x)\) have a finite common bound, WF-2 gives their bounded strong supremum in \(M\). It represents \(m_x\) and is fixed, hence belongs to \(F_+\). Conversely a bounded value of \(T_\alpha(x)\) bounds every \(A_K(x)\), by EP-3's bounded-element test. This proves the finite-cone identity in (L115G4); EP-6 supplies its hereditary cone, left ideal and polarization identities. Semifiniteness is a further property, not a consequence of the whole-cone construction.

Proof of the density equivalences. The cutoff argument in L114 uses only a hereditary finite cone and bounded spectral calculus; here are those steps for \(F_{T_\alpha}\). Let \(q\) be the join of all projections belonging to that cone, as constructed in PC-1. For \(a\in F_{T_\alpha}\) and \(\epsilon>0\), \[ p_\epsilon(a)=1_{[\epsilon,\infty)}(a) \leq\epsilon^{-1}a, \] so \(p_\epsilon(a)\in F_{T_\alpha}\) and the support of \(a\) is at most \(q\). If \(z\in N_{T_\alpha}\), apply this to \(z^*z\) to obtain \(z(1-q)=0\). The set of operators satisfying that equation is weak-operator closed. Condition 1 therefore forces \(q=1\). Condition 2 also forces \(q=1\), because every member of \(m_{T_\alpha}=\operatorname{span}F_{T_\alpha}\) satisfies the same equation and the set is ultraweakly closed.

Index the entire cone \(F_{T_\alpha}\) by its operator order; it is directed since \(a+b\) is an upper bound. Put \[ e_a=a(1+a)^{-1}=1-(1+a)^{-1}. \tag{L115G7} \] These are positive contractions in \(F_{T_\alpha}\), since \(e_a\leq a\). They increase with \(a\): if \(0<A\leq B\) are bounded invertible positives, \(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}\geq1\), so its inverse is at most one and \(B^{-1}\leq A^{-1}\). Apply this with \(A=1+a\), \(B=1+b\). Their strong supremum \(E\leq1\) exists by WF-2. For every projection \(p\in F_{T_\alpha}\), \(e_{np}=n(1+n)^{-1}p\), so \(E\geq p\). If \(q=1\), this implies \(E=1\): positivity makes \(E\) the identity on each \(pH\), hence on the closed span defining their join. Thus either condition 1 or 2 implies condition 3.

Finally any net \(e_i\) as in condition 3 has \(e_i^2\leq e_i\), so \(e_i\in N_{T_\alpha}\). For \(x\in M\), the left-ideal property gives \(xe_i\in N_{T_\alpha}\), whence \(e_i x e_i\in m_{T_\alpha}\). These nets converge strongly to \(x\) and have norms at most \(\|x\|\); the normal vector-series argument CP-6 makes them ultraweakly convergent as well. The net \(xe_i\to x\) proves condition 1, and \(e_i x e_i\to x\) proves condition 2. This also gives controlled approximants for every positive \(x\). All arguments use nets, not sequences. The zero algebra is included, with its identity and every domain equal to zero. \(\square\)

Integrability passes through invariant subalgebras, products and fixed corners

Theorem. For the general-group notion of integrability just proved, each of the following is sufficient for an action \(\alpha\) on \(M\) to be integrable:

  1. A globally invariant unital von Neumann subalgebra \(B\subseteq M\) has integrable restricted action.
  2. The action is normally conjugate to \(\alpha^{(1)}\overline\otimes\alpha^{(2)}\) on \(M_1\overline\otimes M_2\), and at least one factor action is integrable.
  3. The action is normally conjugate to the restriction of an integrable action \(\beta\) on \(N\) to \(eNe\), for a fixed projection \(e\in N^\beta\).

Normal conjugacy means a unital normal coefficient isomorphism with normal inverse intertwining the actions. None of these statements imposes a countability condition. They do not assert that a cocycle perturbation of an integrable action is integrable.

Proof of invariant-subalgebra inheritance. The restriction is point ultraweakly continuous: a positive normal functional on \(B\) extends normally to \(M\), by EP-1, and arbitrary normal functionals are linear combinations of such functionals. Compact averages in \(B\) agree with those in \(M\) on every element of \(B\), because these extensions test both predual integrals. By the preceding theorem choose positive contractions \(e_i\in F_{T_{\alpha|_B}}\) increasing strongly to one. Strong convergence is representation independent on this bounded increasing net, by normality and WF-2. Each \(e_i\) has bounded ambient compact averages with the same norm bound.

For \(a\in M_+\), \[ 0\leq e_i a e_i\leq\|a\|e_i^2\leq\|a\|e_i, \qquad 0\leq A_K(e_i a e_i)\leq\|a\|A_K(e_i). \tag{L115I1} \] Hence \(e_i a e_i\in F_{T_\alpha}\); the bound is uniform in \(K\) for each fixed \(i\), as required. The nets converge strongly and ultraweakly to \(a\), with norm at most \(\|a\|\). Thus \(m_{T_\alpha}\) is ultraweakly dense. The proved density criterion gives integrability of \(\alpha\).

Construction of the whole product action and its inheritance. Take faithful standard-form representations of \(M_1,M_2\). Their canonical implementing unitary groups \(U_1(s),U_2(s)\) are strongly continuous on the whole Hilbert spaces by NR-1. On simple Hilbert tensors \(U_1(s)\otimes U_2(s)\) is strongly continuous. Finite sums of such tensors are dense, and these operators have norm one, so the tensor unitary group is strongly continuous everywhere. Its conjugation gives the required normal continuous product action.

The normal tensor maps used here can also be verified without an abstract tensor-functor assumption. For a normal isomorphism \(\gamma:A\to C\) with normal inverse, ND.TENSOR extends \(\gamma\otimes\mathrm{id}_{B(K)}\) to a whole normal isomorphism \(A\overline\otimes B(K)\to C\overline\otimes B(K)\), with its whole normal inverse. If \(B_0\subseteq B(K)\) is a von Neumann algebra, restrict that extension to \(A\overline\otimes B_0\). Normality sends its ultraweakly dense algebraic tensor product into \(C\overline\otimes B_0\); the inverse does the same in the reverse direction. This gives a normal isomorphism onto the latter whole algebra. Applying the Hilbert-space flip supplies the corresponding extension in the other factor. The two extensions commute, first on elementary tensors and then everywhere by normality. Their composition is the product automorphism. The arbitrary-basis matrix proof NCF-1 likewise supplies the whole normality used in these restrictions. Thus the spatial unitary implementation and the normal tensor maps agree on the entire product.

If \(M_2\ne0\), the invariant unital subalgebra \(M_1\overline\otimes\mathbb C1\) is normally isomorphic to \(M_1\) by \(x\mapsto x\otimes1\), with normal inverse on its image. Compact averages satisfy \[ A_K^{\alpha^{(1)}\otimes\alpha^{(2)}}(x\otimes1) =A_K^{\alpha^{(1)}}(x)\otimes1. \tag{L115I2} \] Testing normal functionals proves the equality; the inclusion has its whole normality from the preceding construction. If the first factor is integrable, part 1 therefore proves product integrability. Use the flip if the second factor is integrable. If either factor algebra is zero, the product is zero and is integrable.

Proof of fixed-corner inheritance, with its full domains. Let \(T_\beta:N_+\to\widehat{N^\beta}_+\), and put \(F=N^\beta\). For \(e\in F\), the corner fixed algebra is \(eFe\). Every compact average of the restricted action satisfies \[ A_K^e(eae)=eA_K^\beta(a)e\qquad(a\in N_+). \tag{L115I3} \] For a normal corner functional \(\omega\), its normal extension \(z\mapsto\omega(eze)\) tests the equality. Taking directed suprema in the full extended cone, with EP-4, gives \[ T_{\beta|_{eNe}}(eae)=eT_\beta(a)e \quad\text{in }\widehat{eFe}_+. \tag{L115I4} \] For a positive element of \(eNe\) its ambient average is supported in \(e\), so the ambient and corner bounded-element tests agree exactly. In particular \[ \begin{aligned} F_{T_{\beta|_{eNe}}}&=F_{T_\beta}\cap(eNe)_+,\\ N_{T_{\beta|_{eNe}}}&=N_{T_\beta}\cap eNe,\\ m_{T_{\beta|_{eNe}}}&=e\,m_{T_\beta}\,e =m_{T_\beta}\cap eNe. \end{aligned} \tag{L115I5} \] For the last equality use EP-6's \(F\)-bimodule property and its finite-positive polarization: compression of each ambient finite positive element is corner finite, and every corner finite positive is already ambient finite. The reverse intersection identity follows from \(x=exe\) for \(x\in eNe\).

If \(\beta\) is integrable, \(m_{T_\beta}\) is ultraweakly dense. Normal compression by \(e\) sends its approximating nets to \(e m_{T_\beta}e\), which is therefore ultraweakly dense in \(eNe\). The preceding density criterion proves corner integrability. For \(e=0\), these identities give precisely the zero domains and zero weight, so no nonzero-corner assumption is concealed. This extends the earlier fixed-corner proof from LCA to arbitrary locally compact groups.

Normal conjugacy. If \(\psi:M\to N\) is a normal isomorphism with normal inverse and \(\psi\alpha_s=\beta_s\psi\), normal functional tests give \[ A_K^\beta(\psi(x))=\psi(A_K^\alpha(x)),\qquad T_\beta(\psi(x))=\widehat\psi(T_\alpha(x)). \tag{L115I6} \] Here \(\widehat\psi\) is the whole extended-positive transport proved in EP-4 and FF, including the infinite projection and full form domain. Norms of bounded positive averages are unchanged by CF-6, applied to the isomorphism and its inverse. Hence \(\psi\) carries each of the three domains in (L115G4) bijectively onto the corresponding domain for \(\beta\), and transports their density. Integrability is consequently invariant under normal conjugacy. This completes parts 2 and 3 as stated. \(\square\)

Applying the last paragraph to that normal map transports the dual orbit averages and their complete bounded finite domains. This application concerns the dual actions; it does not identify the original-action orbit averages of a cocycle perturbation.

The arbitrary-group formulation here uses the compact-average construction and the modular input factor \(\Delta_G(t)^{-1}\); the preceding Fourier and Connes-spectrum conclusions retain their LCA hypotheses.

The exact multiplier domains of the regular action

The compact compressions used above prove that the regular action is integrable. Its bounded domain also has an exact intersection with the multiplication algebra, including complex functions. That equality requires a reverse argument: a decomposition of a multiplication operator into positive integrable operators need not remain inside the multiplication algebra.

Let \(G\) be an arbitrary LCH abelian group with its fixed Haar normalization, let \(\mathcal K=L^2(G)\), and let \[ (R_s\xi)(r)=\xi(r+s),\qquad \rho_s=\operatorname{Ad}R_s. \tag{RD1} \] Use the concrete multiplication representation \(h\mapsto M_h\) from the regular-integrability proof. Its \(L^\infty\) classes use the complete locally determined Haar measure of HR9. Finite-exponent \(L^p\) classes have the isometric outer-regular representatives proved there. The group is not assumed sigma compact or first countable.

Write \(T_\rho\) for the full normal orbit weight constructed in L114, and put \[ \begin{aligned} F_\rho&=\{a\in B(\mathcal K)_+:T_\rho(a)\text{ is bounded}\},\\ \mathfrak n_\rho&=\{x:T_\rho(x^*x)\text{ is bounded}\},\qquad \mathfrak p_\rho=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}F_\rho. \end{aligned} \tag{RD2} \] The weight takes values in the entire extended positive cone of the fixed algebra. In particular, a value \(+\infty\,1\) below denotes the extended positive element with infinite-part projection \(1\).

Theorem. For every \(h\in L^\infty(G)\), \[ \begin{aligned} M_h\in\mathfrak p_\rho &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad h\in L^1(G),\\ M_h\in\mathfrak n_\rho &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad h\in L^2(G). \end{aligned} \tag{RD3} \] For \(h\geq0\), including nonintegrable \(h\), \[ T_\rho(M_h)=\left(\int_G h\,dm\right)1. \tag{RD4} \] All intersections in (RD3) are inside the specified multiplication representation.

Positive functions and the full extended value. For a compact \(K\subset G\), the function \(h1_K\) is bounded and integrable. Formula (K24), proved with normal vector tests and qualified Haar product integration, gives \[ T_\rho(M_{h1_K})=\left(\int_K h\,dm\right)1. \tag{RD5} \] The projections \(q_K=M_{1_K}\), directed by inclusion of all compact sets, increase strongly to \(1\), as already proved before (K24a). Thus \(M_{h1_K}=M_{h^{1/2}}q_KM_{h^{1/2}}\) increases strongly to \(M_h\). Normality of the full extended weight gives (RD4) by taking these directed suprema.

Here the scalar identity \(\int h=\sup_K\int_Kh\) holds even when the integral is infinite. To spell out the use of HR9, compact inner regularity holds for each locally measurable level set. For a nonnegative simple function below \(h\), approximate the finitely many level sets by compact subsets and take their finite union. This makes its full integral the supremum of its compactly restricted integrals. Taking the supremum over simple functions proves the identity for \(h\). When one level set has infinite measure, choose compact subsets with arbitrarily large finite measure instead. This argument concerns the locally determined convention and does not assert global inner regularity of every Borel set for a different raw Haar measure.

It follows that \(F_\rho\) meets the positive multiplication operators exactly in \(L^1(G)_+\cap L^\infty(G)\). Decomposing the real and imaginary parts of an integrable complex \(h\) into positive and negative parts proves \[ L^1(G)\cap L^\infty(G) \ \subseteq\ \{h:M_h\in\mathfrak p_\rho\}. \tag{RD6} \]

A uniform coefficient bound for the reverse implication. Suppose \(x=M_h\in\mathfrak p_\rho\). The gauge and constant-one estimate (IP6)–(IP8) in the complete integrable-domain proof give a finite number \[ Q=q_\rho(x)= \inf_{x=\sum_j c_j a_j,\ a_j\in F_\rho} \sum_j |c_j|\,\|T_\rho(a_j)\|, \qquad \int_G|\omega(\rho_s(x))|\,ds\leq Q\|\omega\| \quad(\omega\in B(\mathcal K)_*). \tag{RD7} \] The bound is uniform over normal functionals, not just a separate integrability assertion for each one. It holds for this possibly nonpositive \(x\).

Take the positive compact cutoffs \(k_U\) of L113, (D24): \[ k_U\in C_c(G),\quad k_U\geq0,\quad \int k_U=1,\quad \operatorname{supp}k_U\subset U, \qquad \xi_U=k_U^{1/2}\in L^2(G). \tag{RD8} \] The index \(U\) runs through all relatively compact identity neighborhoods, ordered by reverse inclusion. The vector \(\xi_U\) has norm one. Its vector functional is normal by CP6; Cauchy–Schwarz bounds its norm by one, and evaluation at the identity attains one. Since \(\rho_s(M_h)=M_{h(s+\,\cdot\,)}\), define the actual coefficient \[ c_U(s)=\langle \rho_s(M_h)\xi_U,\xi_U\rangle =\int_G k_U(r)h(s+r)\,dr . \tag{RD9} \] It is continuous by the already established normal-action continuity. Equations (RD7)–(RD9) give \[ c_U\in L^1(G),\qquad \|c_U\|_1\leq Q \quad\text{for every }U. \tag{RD10} \] The integral in (RD9) depends only on the multiplication class of \(h\): translations preserve compact-local null sets, and \(k_U\) has compact support.

Recovering every compact test. Fix \(f\in C_c(G)\). Haar substitution \(t=s+r\) and scalar Fubini give \[ \begin{aligned} \int_G f(s)c_U(s)\,ds &=\int_G h(t)\,(k_U*f)(t)\,dt,\\ (k_U*f)(t)&=\int_G k_U(r)f(t-r)\,dr . \end{aligned} \tag{RD11} \] Only the product of the two compact supports is needed before the substitution; afterward \(t\) lies in their compact sum. On the finitely many open sigma compact cosets meeting those sets, \(h\) has a bounded measurable representative in the completed sigma-finite Haar restriction. HR3/5/9 supply a Borel representative and the qualified Radon-product Fubini rule. Changing that representative on a compact-local null set changes none of these integrals. No global product-Borel identity or sigma compact exhaustion of \(G\) is used.

By L24's translation theorem, \[ \begin{aligned} \|k_U*f-f\|_1 &\leq \int_G k_U(r)\|L_r f-f\|_1\,dr\\ &\leq \sup_{r\in U}\|L_r f-f\|_1\longrightarrow0, \qquad (L_rf)(t)=f(t-r). \end{aligned} \tag{RD12} \] The first inequality follows by the scalar triangle inequality and the same finite-carrier Tonelli rule; \(\int k_U=1\). Norm continuity at zero proves the last limit along the neighborhood net. Because \(h\) is bounded, (RD11)–(RD12) imply \[ \int f c_U\longrightarrow\int f h. \] Combining this with (RD10) yields the compact-test inequality \[ \left|\int_G f h\,dm\right|\leq Q\|f\|_\infty \qquad(f\in C_c(G)). \tag{RD13} \] This limit is a pairing identity. It assumes neither pointwise convergence of \(c_U\) nor an arbitrary-net version of Fatou's lemma.

Compact phase tests give global integrability. Fix a compact \(K\subset G\). Set \[ g_K(r)= \begin{cases} \overline{h(r)}/|h(r)|,&r\in K,\ h(r)\ne0,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \qquad |g_K|\leq1 . \tag{RD14} \] This is a compactly supported \(L^1\) class in the locally determined convention, and \(\int h g_K=\int_K|h|\). The finite-exponent identification in HR9 and HR3's density proof give \(a_n\in C_c(G)\) with \(\|a_n-g_K\|_1\to0\). A sequence is legitimate here because only approximation in a normed function space is involved; it is not a countable neighborhood base of \(G\).

Clip the approximants radially: \[ f_n=\frac{a_n}{\max(1,|a_n|)}\in C_c(G),\qquad \|f_n\|_\infty\leq1,\qquad \|f_n-g_K\|_1\leq2\|a_n-g_K\|_1\longrightarrow0. \tag{RD15} \] Indeed, where \(|a_n|>1\), the displacement caused by clipping is \(|a_n|-1\leq|a_n-g_K|\); elsewhere it is zero. The triangle inequality proves the displayed pointwise bound and its integral.

Apply (RD13) to \(f_n\). Boundedness of \(h\) permits the \(L^1\) limit in the pairing, giving \[ \int_K|h|\,dm=\lim_n\int h f_n\,dm\leq Q . \tag{RD16} \] The middle terms can be complex; the conclusion follows from their absolute-value bound \(Q\) and their real nonnegative limit. Taking the supremum over all compact \(K\), with the scalar compact-inner formula proved above, gives \[ \|h\|_1\leq q_\rho(M_h)<\infty. \tag{RD17} \] This proves the reverse inclusion in (RD3). For its square-integrable assertion, use \(M_h^*M_h=M_{|h|^2}\) and (RD4). Its orbit value is bounded exactly when \(\int |h|^2<\infty\). This completes both domain equalities. \(\square\)

Solved check. Why is mere integrability of every \(c_U\) insufficient for the last argument?

Solution. The bound in (RD13) must be independent of \(U\) to survive the limit. That uniform bound comes from (RD7) and the unit norm of every vector functional. On \(G=\mathbb R\), for example, the functions \(1_{[-n,n]}\) are individually integrable and converge pointwise to \(1\), but their integrals are \(2n\), and \(1\notin L^1(\mathbb R)\). The proof uses the common bound \(Q\) and compact test pairings, rather than drawing an integrability conclusion from pointwise convergence.

The exact intersection \(\mathfrak p_\rho\cap L^\infty(G)=L^1(G)\cap L^\infty(G)\) occurs in Takesaki, Theory of Operator Algebras II, XI.2, proof of Theorem 2.7(iv), printed 336. The argument here proves its complex reverse implication through uniform normal coefficients and compact phase tests. The earlier Haar, normal-integration and bounded-domain lessons supply the complete local premises; the citation does not replace them.
