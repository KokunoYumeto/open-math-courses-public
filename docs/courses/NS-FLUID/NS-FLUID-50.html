Original clocks, weighted transport and exact corrections

Chen and Hou's stability construction changes both time and spatial scale, controls outgoing transport with weights, and corrects an approximate evolution so that its residual vanishes to the required order at the origin. This lesson proves those maps with every original factor, coupling, endpoint and correction term retained.

Part 1 calculates the physical clock and its exact remaining-time integral. Part 2 constructs a complete annular damping weight and keeps its cost in the nonlocal operator. Part 3 gives the full scalar and three-component analytic corrections, their time tails, and the return from independent components to a temperature field. Part 4 solves the original frozen transport model, including every mixed derivative and both homogeneous and inhomogeneous Sobolev norms.

Five solved exercises calculate a clock with a logarithmic correction, an explicit annular damping exponent, a nonzero analytic correction tail, every derivative norm of a quadratic Gaussian, and all three forced correction coefficients with their actual gradient defect.

Each part identifies the original author-source version and precise passages. Corrections to the explanatory note and frozen model remain visible. These comparisons do not establish a failure of the main blowup theorem or certify its full computational stability argument.

QC1. The actual clock and its complete inverse

Write the original spatial point as \(\boldsymbol x=(x,y)\) in \(\mathbb H=\mathbb R\times[0,\infty)\), and keep \(\tau\) for rescaled time and \(t\) for physical time. Fix the source constants \(\bar c_l>0\), \(\bar c_\omega<0\), the actual initial vorticity scale \(A=C_\omega(0)>0\), and \(\delta\) with \(0\leq\delta<-\bar c_\omega\). Define both positive rate endpoints without replacing the original rate:

\[ a_-=-\bar c_\omega-\delta>0,\qquad a_+=-\bar c_\omega+\delta,\qquad -a_+\leq c_\omega(\tau)\leq-a_-,\quad \tau\geq0. \tag{QC1} \]

Take any continuous original rate \(c_\omega\) satisfying QC1. The full source scales and time are

\[ \begin{gathered} C_\omega(\tau)=A\exp\!\left(\int_0^\tau c_\omega(s)\,ds\right), \qquad C_l(\tau)=\exp(-\bar c_l\tau),\\ c_\theta(\tau)=\bar c_l+2c_\omega(\tau),\qquad C_\theta(\tau)=A^2 \exp\!\left(\int_0^\tau c_\theta(s)\,ds\right),\\ t(\tau)=\int_0^\tau C_\omega(s)\,ds, \qquad T=\int_0^\infty C_\omega(s)\,ds. \end{gathered} \tag{QC2} \]

Here \(C_l(0)=1\) is the original source value. The initial factor \(A\), and hence \(A^2\), are precisely the source's extension at825. Integrating QC1 over \([0,\tau]\), exponentiating and retaining \(A\) gives

\[ \begin{gathered} Ae^{-a_+\tau}\leq C_\omega(\tau)\leq Ae^{-a_-\tau}, \\ \frac{A(1-e^{-a_+\tau})}{a_+} \leq t(\tau)\leq \frac{A(1-e^{-a_-\tau})}{a_-},\\ \frac A{a_+}\leq T\leq\frac A{a_-}. \end{gathered} \tag{QC3} \]

The two exponential integrals are finite because both endpoints are strictly positive. The time derivative is exactly \(t'(\tau)=C_\omega(\tau)>0\). Thus \(t\) is a strictly increasing \(C^1\) bijection from \([0,\infty)\) onto \([0,T)\): continuity gives every intermediate value, strict increase gives uniqueness, and the defining improper integral gives the limit \(T\). Its inverse \(\tau(t)\) is \(C^1\) at every \(t<T\), with derivative \(1/C_\omega(\tau(t))\). In particular the complete space-time map and its inverse are

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbb H\times[0,\infty)&\longrightarrow\mathbb H\times[0,T), &(\boldsymbol y,\tau)&\longmapsto (C_l(\tau)\boldsymbol y,t(\tau)),\\ (\boldsymbol x,t)&\longmapsto (C_l(\tau(t))^{-1}\boldsymbol x,\tau(t)), &\det D_{(\boldsymbol y,\tau)} (C_l\boldsymbol y,t)&=C_l(\tau)^2C_\omega(\tau)>0. \end{aligned} \tag{QC4} \]

The determinant follows from the block triangular derivative: its spatial block is \(C_l I_2\), its lower-left block is zero and its last diagonal entry is \(C_\omega\). The moving spatial column \(C_l'(\tau)\boldsymbol y\) is retained and does not alter that determinant. Positive dilation preserves both the half-plane and its boundary. No point at \(t=T\) is in the inverse map's domain.

QC2. The exact remaining-time factor

For every \(\tau\geq0\), define

\[ q(\tau)=\int_0^\infty \exp\!\left(\int_\tau^{\tau+u}c_\omega(s)\,ds\right)du. \tag{QC5} \]

The integrand lies between \(e^{-a_+u}\) and \(e^{-a_-u}\). Changing variables \(s=\tau+u\) in the tail of the original time integral, and using \(C_\omega(\tau+u)=C_\omega(\tau) \exp(\int_\tau^{\tau+u}c_\omega)\), proves

\[ T-t(\tau)=C_\omega(\tau)q(\tau),\qquad \frac1{a_+}\leq q(\tau)\leq\frac1{a_-},\qquad q'(\tau)=-1-c_\omega(\tau)q(\tau). \tag{QC6} \]

The last identity follows by differentiating the first identity, whose two factors are \(C^1\); it requires no differentiation under an improper integral. Every constant in QC6 is attained by the continuous constant endpoint rates \(c_\omega=-a_+\) and \(c_\omega=-a_-\), respectively. The original scale therefore obeys the sharp two-sided remaining-time estimate

\[ a_-\big(T-t(\tau)\big)\leq C_\omega(\tau) \leq a_+\big(T-t(\tau)\big). \tag{QC7} \]

The upper and lower sides retain different constants. Replacing both by \(-\bar c_\omega\) is not an exact identity for the rate class QC1.

QC3. Spatial scales and the entire accumulated rate error

Put \(\Delta_t=T-t(\tau)>0\). Integrating the exponential envelopes from \(\tau\) to infinity yields

\[ \frac A{a_+}e^{-a_+\tau}\leq\Delta_t \leq\frac A{a_-}e^{-a_-\tau}. \tag{QC8} \]

Since \(C_l=e^{-\bar c_l\tau}\), all quantities are positive and raising each side to the indicated positive power gives

\[ \left(\frac{a_-\Delta_t}{A}\right)^{\bar c_l/a_-} \leq C_l(\tau) \leq\min\!\left\{1, \left(\frac{a_+\Delta_t}{A}\right)^{\bar c_l/a_+}\right\}, \tag{QC9} \]

and hence

\[ \max\!\left\{1, \left(\frac{a_+\Delta_t}{A}\right)^{-\bar c_l/a_+}\right\} \leq C_l(\tau)^{-1} \leq\left(\frac{a_-\Delta_t}{A}\right)^{-\bar c_l/a_-}. \tag{QC10} \]

The cap at one uses \(\tau\geq0\). The lower base in QC9 is at most one because \(\Delta_t\leq T\leq A/a_-\); thus these endpoint bounds are consistent at \(\tau=0\) as well.

There is also a full identity retaining the deviation of the original rate rather than only its bound. Set

\[ B(\tau)=\int_0^\tau(c_\omega(s)-\bar c_\omega)\,ds, \qquad |B(\tau)|\leq\delta\tau, \qquad \gamma=-\frac{\bar c_l}{\bar c_\omega}>0. \tag{QC11} \]

The logarithm of QC2 is \(\log(C_\omega/A)=\bar c_\omega\tau+B(\tau)\). Multiplying this equality by \(\gamma\), and retaining the entire \(B\) term, gives

\[ C_l(\tau) =\left(\frac{C_\omega(\tau)}A\right)^\gamma e^{-\gamma B(\tau)} =\left(\frac{\Delta_t}{Aq(\tau)}\right)^\gamma e^{-\gamma B(\tau)}. \tag{QC12} \]

For \(\delta=0\), the only rate is \(c_\omega=\bar c_\omega\). Then \(B=0\), \(q=1/(-\bar c_\omega)\), and every displayed clock formula specializes to its exact constant-rate value.

For \(\delta>0\), the two permitted constant rates \(c_\omega=\bar c_\omega+\delta=-a_-\) and \(c_\omega=\bar c_\omega-\delta=-a_+\) give, respectively,

\[ C_l(\tau)= \left(\frac{a_-\Delta_t}{A}\right)^{\bar c_l/a_-}, \qquad C_l(\tau)= \left(\frac{a_+\Delta_t}{A}\right)^{\bar c_l/a_+}. \tag{QC13} \]

Since \(a_-<-\bar c_\omega<a_+\), dividing the first expression by \(\Delta_t^\gamma\) tends to zero as \(\Delta_t\downarrow0\), and dividing the second by it tends to infinity. This proves exactly why a bounded rate error alone does not give a uniform multiplicative comparison with the central power. These examples are clocks in QC1, not asserted PDE solutions or counterexamples to the source theorem.

Even the factor \(q\) need not converge in this rate class. For the specified rate \(c_\omega(\tau)=\bar c_\omega+\delta\sin\tau\),

\[ \begin{gathered} C_\omega(\tau)=A\exp\big(\bar c_\omega\tau+ \delta(1-\cos\tau)\big),\\ q(\tau)=\int_0^\infty \exp\big(\bar c_\omega u+ \delta(\cos\tau-\cos(\tau+u))\big)du. \end{gathered} \tag{QC14} \]

The integrand makes \(q\) continuous and \(2\pi\)-periodic by dominated convergence, with dominating function \(e^{-a_-u}\). If \(q\) were constant, QC6 would force \(c_\omega=-1/q\) to be constant, contrary to \(\delta>0\). A nonconstant continuous periodic function cannot have a limit at infinity: two different values repeat along two sequences of times tending to infinity. This proves the asserted nonconvergence directly.

QC4. Every field, derivative and measure factor

Keep the original source fields \(\widetilde\omega,\widetilde\theta,\widetilde{\boldsymbol u}\) in the rescaled half-plane. The identity \(c_\theta=\bar c_l+2c_\omega\), together with the actual initial factors in QC2, gives

\[ C_\theta(\tau)=\frac{C_\omega(\tau)^2}{C_l(\tau)}. \tag{QC15} \]

At \(\boldsymbol y=C_l(\tau)^{-1}\boldsymbol x\), the full inverse source transformation is therefore

\[ \begin{aligned} \omega_{\rm phy}(\boldsymbol x,t(\tau)) &=C_\omega(\tau)^{-1}\widetilde\omega(\boldsymbol y,\tau),\\ \theta_{\rm phy}(\boldsymbol x,t(\tau)) &=C_l(\tau)C_\omega(\tau)^{-2} \widetilde\theta(\boldsymbol y,\tau),\\ \boldsymbol u_{\rm phy}(\boldsymbol x,t(\tau)) &=C_l(\tau)C_\omega(\tau)^{-1} \widetilde{\boldsymbol u}(\boldsymbol y,\tau). \end{aligned} \tag{QC16} \]

For any original multi-index \(\alpha=(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\) of order \(k=\alpha_1+\alpha_2\), spatial differentiation at fixed time multiplies each field by \(C_l^{-k}\). Thus the exact derivative formulas retain factors \(C_\omega^{-1}C_l^{-k}\), \(C_\omega^{-2}C_l^{1-k}\) and \(C_\omega^{-1}C_l^{1-k}\), in the order of QC16. In particular the original temperature gradient has the full factor \(C_\omega^{-2}\); its spatial scale cancels only after both original factors are displayed.

For \(1\leq p<\infty\), the substitution \(\boldsymbol x=C_l\boldsymbol y\) has Jacobian \(C_l^2\). Taking the \(p\)-th root of the resulting integral proves

\[ \begin{aligned} \|D^\alpha\omega_{\rm phy}(t(\tau))\|_{L^p(\mathbb H)} &=C_\omega^{-1}C_l^{2/p-k} \|D^\alpha\widetilde\omega(\tau)\|_{L^p(\mathbb H)},\\ \|D^\alpha\theta_{\rm phy}(t(\tau))\|_{L^p(\mathbb H)} &=C_\omega^{-2}C_l^{1+2/p-k} \|D^\alpha\widetilde\theta(\tau)\|_{L^p(\mathbb H)},\\ \|D^\alpha\boldsymbol u_{\rm phy}(t(\tau))\|_{L^p(\mathbb H)} &=C_\omega^{-1}C_l^{1+2/p-k} \|D^\alpha\widetilde{\boldsymbol u}(\tau)\|_{L^p(\mathbb H)}. \end{aligned} \tag{QC17} \]

All time-dependent factors on the right are evaluated at \(\tau\). The identities also hold with infinite values when the fields are not integrable. For \(p=\infty\), the bijective spatial dilation preserves the essential supremum, so the three factors are respectively \(C_\omega^{-1}C_l^{-k}\), \(C_\omega^{-2}C_l^{1-k}\), and \(C_\omega^{-1}C_l^{1-k}\). No finite-energy conclusion is inferred from bounded vorticity alone.

QC5. Exact receiving estimate for the source's stated error sizes

Let \(M=\|\bar\omega\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb H)}<\infty\) and suppose a rescaled field has the actual error \(\|\widetilde\omega(\tau)-\bar\omega\|_\infty\leq\epsilon_\omega\) at every time. The triangle inequality and its reverse give \((M-\epsilon_\omega)_+\leq\|\widetilde\omega(\tau)\|_\infty \leq M+\epsilon_\omega\), where \((r)_+=\max\{r,0\}\). Multiplying QC16 by \(\Delta_t\), using QC6 and retaining both endpoints, gives

\[ \frac{(M-\epsilon_\omega)_+}{a_+} \leq (T-t(\tau))\|\omega_{\rm phy}(t(\tau))\|_\infty \leq\frac{M+\epsilon_\omega}{a_-}. \tag{QC18} \]

This is a direct algebraic receiving estimate. When \(M>\epsilon_\omega\), its left coefficient is positive and it gives the exact lower rate for the supplied field. Without that strict inequality it does not claim a positive lower bound.

At time zero \(\widetilde\omega(0)=A\omega_{\rm phy}(0)\). Writing \(M_0=\|\widetilde\omega(0)\|_\infty\), any nonzero initial field satisfies \(A=M_0/\|\omega_{\rm phy}(0)\|_\infty\). Substitution into QC3 proves the more precise initial-data form

\[ \frac{M_0}{a_+\|\omega_{\rm phy}(0)\|_\infty} \leq T\leq \frac{M_0}{a_-\|\omega_{\rm phy}(0)\|_\infty}. \tag{QC19} \]

There is no division by a zero initial norm: QC19 is stated only for a nonzero initial field, while QC3 holds in every case in QC1–QC2.

The source's stated \(E_*=5\cdot10^{-6}\) gives exactly

\[ \begin{gathered} \delta=100E_*=\frac1{2000},\qquad \epsilon_\omega=200E_*=\frac1{1000},\\ a_-=-\bar c_\omega-\frac1{2000},\qquad a_+=-\bar c_\omega+\frac1{2000}. \end{gathered} \tag{QC20} \]

Thus QC18–QC19 supply the exact comparison receiving those stated errors, with the original \(\bar c_\omega\) still present. WT1. All components of the original equation

Use the source's perturbation notation, in which \(\omega,\theta\) are perturbations of \(\bar\omega,\bar\theta\), and \(c_\omega=u_x(0)\) is the perturbation of the rate. Retain the source's fixed derivative anchors, so the perturbation of \(c_l\) is zero and that of \(c_\theta\) is \(2u_x(0)\). On the original half-plane let

\[ \begin{gathered} \boldsymbol x=(x,y),\quad \boldsymbol u=(u,v)=\nabla^\perp(-\Delta)^{-1}\omega, \quad \bar{\boldsymbol u}=(\bar u,\bar v) =\nabla^\perp(-\Delta)^{-1}\bar\omega,\\ \eta=\theta_x,\quad \xi=\theta_y,\quad W=(\omega,\eta,\xi)^T,\quad b=\bar c_l\boldsymbol x+\bar{\boldsymbol u},\quad \bar c_\theta=\bar c_l+2\bar c_\omega. \end{gathered} \tag{WT1} \]

The stream function and boundary conditions are those of the source; the velocity here is its exact Biot–Savart velocity, not the separately represented numerical stream function. The full equation is

\[ (\partial_t+b\cdot\nabla)W=M W+G, \qquad G=G_{\rm nl}+G_{\rm quad}+G_{\rm res}, \tag{WT2} \]

with every matrix entry retained:

\[ M=\begin{pmatrix} \bar c_\omega&1&0\\ 0&2\bar c_\omega-\bar u_x&-\bar v_x\\ 0&-\bar u_y&2\bar c_\omega+\bar u_x \end{pmatrix}. \tag{WT3} \]

The nonlocal part is the following full vector, not a numerical bound:

\[ G_{\rm nl}=\begin{pmatrix} -u\bar\omega_x-v\bar\omega_y+u_x(0)\bar\omega\\ -u_x\bar\theta_x-v_x\bar\theta_y -u\bar\theta_{xx}-v\bar\theta_{xy}+2u_x(0)\bar\theta_x\\ -u_y\bar\theta_x-v_y\bar\theta_y -u\bar\theta_{xy}-v\bar\theta_{yy}+2u_x(0)\bar\theta_y \end{pmatrix}. \tag{WT4} \]

The full nonlinear and original residual vectors are

\[ \begin{gathered} G_{\rm quad}=\begin{pmatrix} -u\omega_x-v\omega_y+u_x(0)\omega\\ -u\eta_x-v\eta_y-u_x\eta-v_x\xi+2u_x(0)\eta\\ -u\xi_x-v\xi_y-u_y\eta-v_y\xi+2u_x(0)\xi \end{pmatrix},\\ \bar F_\omega=-b\cdot\nabla\bar\omega +\bar\theta_x+\bar c_\omega\bar\omega, \qquad \bar F_\theta=-b\cdot\nabla\bar\theta+\bar c_\theta\bar\theta,\\ G_{\rm res}=(\bar F_\omega, \partial_x\bar F_\theta, \partial_y\bar F_\theta)^T. \end{gathered} \tag{WT5} \]

To verify WT2, insert the full barred-plus-perturbation fields into the source equations. The vorticity equation has transport \(-b\cdot\nabla\omega\), the local terms \(\bar c_\omega\omega+\eta\), the first entry of WT4, the first entry of WT5 and the barred residual. These exhaust its products. For the temperature equation differentiate once in \(x\): the derivative of transport contributes \(-\bar c_l\eta-\bar u_x\eta-\bar v_x\xi\), and \(\bar c_\theta\eta-\bar c_l\eta=2\bar c_\omega\eta\). Differentiating its perturbation transport and rate products gives exactly the second entries of WT4–WT5. Differentiate instead in \(y\): the corresponding local entries are \(-\bar u_y\eta+(\bar c_\theta-\bar c_l-\bar v_y)\xi\). The exact divergence identity \(\bar v_y=-\bar u_x\) gives the third row of WT3; all other differentiated products are the third entries of WT4–WT5. Thus no term from the source's two spatial directions is discarded.

WT2. The complete componentwise weight map

On any region excluding the origin let \(\varphi_i>0\) be \(C^1\) time-independent weights, and put \(D_\varphi=\operatorname{diag}(\varphi_1,\varphi_2,\varphi_3)\), \(Z=D_\varphi W\). Multiplication is invertible there. The product rule proves the exact conjugated equation

\[ (\partial_t+b\cdot\nabla)Z =\left(D_\varphi M D_\varphi^{-1} +\operatorname{diag}(d_1,d_2,d_3)\right)Z +D_\varphi G, \qquad d_i=\frac{b\cdot\nabla\varphi_i}{\varphi_i}. \tag{WT6} \]

In particular the three off-diagonal coefficients are exactly \(\varphi_1/\varphi_2\), \(-\bar v_x\varphi_2/\varphi_3\) and \(-\bar u_y\varphi_3/\varphi_2\). They cannot be ignored when choosing different weights for the components.

WT3. A constructed weight with the source's exact plateaus

Retain \(0<R_1<R_2\) and work in the original positive quadrant \(x,y\geq0\), away from its origin. Let

\[ \begin{gathered} a=R_1/2,\quad d=2R_2,\quad Q=\int_a^d h(s)\,ds,\\ h(r)=\begin{cases} \exp\!\left(-\dfrac1{(r-a)(d-r)}\right),&a<r<d,\\ 0,&r\leq a\text{ or }r\geq d, \end{cases} \end{gathered} \tag{WT7} \]

and, for \(r=|\boldsymbol x|\), set

\[ \varphi_0(\boldsymbol x)=1-\frac1{2Q}\int_a^r h(s)\,ds. \tag{WT8} \]

The oriented integral in WT8 is zero for \(r\leq a\), since its integrand vanishes there. It equals \(Q\) for \(r\geq d\). The positive integrand on \((a,d)\) gives \(0<Q<d-a\), and consequently

\[ \varphi_0=1\ (r\leq R_1/2),\qquad \varphi_0=\tfrac12\ (r\geq2R_2),\qquad \tfrac12\leq\varphi_0\leq1,\qquad \nabla\varphi_0=-\frac{h(r)}{2Q}\frac{\boldsymbol x}{r}. \tag{WT9} \]

All derivatives of the exponential in WT7 tend to zero at either endpoint: each derivative is that exponential times a finite rational function of \(r-a\) and \(d-r\); an exponential of a negative reciprocal dominates every such power. Thus \(h\), its integral and \(\varphi_0\) are smooth. The last function is constant in a full ball about the origin, so the radial definition is smooth there too. For \(R_1\leq r\leq R_2\) and \(x_i>0\), WT9 has \(\partial_i\varphi_0<0\). At the corresponding coordinate axis that derivative is zero, which is the exact boundary behavior.

Retain actual positive outgoing constants \(c_1,c_2\) in \(b_1(\boldsymbol x)\geq c_1x\) and \(b_2(\boldsymbol x)\geq c_2y\), and set \(c_*=\min(c_1,c_2)>0\). The constants are not replaced by the source's rounded illustrative decimals. Then

\[ b\cdot\boldsymbol x\geq c_1x^2+c_2y^2\geq c_*r^2, \qquad d_0:=\frac{b\cdot\nabla\varphi_0}{\varphi_0} =-\frac{h(r)}{2Q\varphi_0}\frac{b\cdot\boldsymbol x}{r} \leq0. \tag{WT10} \]

This retains the two individual coordinate contributions before taking their lower bound. On the closed annulus \(R_1\leq r\leq R_2\), the concave quadratic \((r-a)(d-r)\) attains its minimum at an endpoint. The second endpoint value minus the first is \((R_2-R_1)(R_2-R_1/2)>0\). Therefore

\[ \begin{gathered} h(r)\geq h_*:= \exp\!\left(-\frac1{(R_1/2)(2R_2-R_1)}\right)>0,\\ d_0\leq-C_*,\qquad C_*:=\frac{c_*R_1h_*}{2Q} >\frac{c_*R_1}{2(2R_2-R_1/2)} \exp\!\left(-\frac1{(R_1/2)(2R_2-R_1)}\right)>0. \end{gathered} \tag{WT11} \]

The complete integral \(Q\) remains in the stronger coefficient; the following explicit lower bound uses only \(Q<d-a\). The inequality is valid at the axes as well, because WT10 uses the radial dot product rather than strict negativity of both partials.

WT4. Strong local damping without deleting the remaining equation

For fixed \(\alpha_i<0\) and real \(\beta_i\), retain the original two-term radial factors

\[ \psi_i(r)=r^{\alpha_i}+r^{\beta_i},\qquad \varphi_i=\psi_i\varphi_0^m,\qquad m\geq0, \qquad d_{s,i}= \frac{\alpha_i r^{\alpha_i}+\beta_i r^{\beta_i}} {r^{\alpha_i}+r^{\beta_i}} \frac{b\cdot\boldsymbol x}{r^2}. \tag{WT12} \]

Differentiation gives \(d_i=d_{s,i}+m d_0\). The common bounded factor cancels in every same-point ratio \(\varphi_i/\varphi_j=\psi_i/\psi_j\), so the off-diagonal matrix does not acquire an unrecorded dependence on \(m\). The original weighted spaces are unchanged as sets, with the explicit norm comparison

\[ 2^{-m}\max_i\|\psi_i W_i\|_\infty \leq\max_i\|\varphi_i W_i\|_\infty \leq\max_i\|\psi_i W_i\|_\infty. \tag{WT13} \]

This follows directly by multiplying \(2^{-m}\leq\varphi_0^m\leq1\). In particular the factor is identically one near the origin. It does not repair a missing vanishing jet required by a singular \(\psi_i\).

On the closed quadrant annulus define the exact three row quantities

\[ \begin{aligned} \rho_1&=\bar c_\omega+d_{s,1}+\psi_1/\psi_2,\\ \rho_2&=2\bar c_\omega-\bar u_x+d_{s,2} +|\bar v_x|\psi_2/\psi_3,\\ \rho_3&=2\bar c_\omega+\bar u_x+d_{s,3} +|\bar u_y|\psi_3/\psi_2,\qquad K=\max_{R_1\leq r\leq R_2,\ x,y\geq0}\max_i\rho_i. \end{aligned} \tag{WT14} \]

When the barred velocity is \(C^1\) on this annulus, all functions are continuous and the denominators are positive. Compactness makes \(K\) finite. For any prescribed local damping \(\kappa>0\), the fully specified choice

\[ m=\frac{\max\{0,K+\kappa\}}{C_*} \tag{WT15} \]

makes every diagonal-plus-absolute-off-diagonal row sum of the exact matrix in WT6 at most \(-\kappa\) throughout the annulus. Indeed, that row sum is \(\rho_i+m d_0\leq K-mC_*\); WT15 gives \(-\kappa\) when \(K+\kappa\geq0\), and when it is negative, \(m=0\) and \(K<-\kappa\). This proves the full local matrix statement with unequal base weights and without discarding the \(\eta\)-to-\(\omega\) or either cross-gradient coupling.

Here is its precise receiving estimate. Let \(X(t)\) be an actual characteristic, \(X'=b(X)\), which stays in the annulus on \([t_0,t_1]\). For a differentiable solution of the complete WT2, put \(z(t)=Z(X(t),t)\). Its equation is exactly WT6 evaluated on this path. The upper right derivative of \(n(t)=\max_i|z_i(t)|\) satisfies

\[ D^+ n(t)\leq-\kappa n(t) +\max_i|\varphi_i(X(t))G_i(X(t),t)|. \tag{WT16} \]

To prove this finite-dimensional statement, at a nonzero maximizing component multiply its differential equation by its sign. The diagonal term is its diagonal coefficient times \(n\), and each off-diagonal term is at most its absolute coefficient times \(n\). At a maximizing zero all components vanish, so the derivative of its absolute value is bounded by the forcing norm. For a maximum of finitely many differentiable absolute values, its upper right derivative is at most the maximum of these derivatives among the maximizing components: any nonmaximizing component has a strict value gap and cannot become a maximizer over sufficiently short times. WT15 now gives WT16. Multiplication by \(e^{\kappa t}\) and integration yields

\[ \begin{aligned} \max_i|\varphi_i(X(t))W_i(X(t),t)| &\leq e^{-\kappa(t-t_0)} \max_i|\varphi_i(X(t_0))W_i(X(t_0),t_0)|\\ &\quad+\int_{t_0}^t e^{-\kappa(t-s)} \max_i\Bigl|\varphi_i(X(s))\\ &\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\cdot (G_{{\rm nl},i}+G_{{\rm quad},i}+G_{{\rm res},i})(X(s),s)\Bigr|\,ds. \end{aligned} \tag{WT17} \]

Equivalently, the integrating-factor comparison follows by integrating the almost-everywhere differential inequality for the locally Lipschitz function \(n\). JC1. The original quadratic defect and its exact projection

Work on \(\mathbb H=\mathbb R\times[0,\infty)\) with original coordinates \(\boldsymbol x=(x,y)\), \(r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\). Derivatives at the boundary origin are derivatives of functions smooth up to that boundary. Retain the source operator

\[ \mathcal L f=-\overline V_1\partial_x f -\overline V_2\partial_y f+\bar c f+\overline F P(f), \qquad P(f)=\int_{\mathbb R^2_{++}}f(\boldsymbol z)\bar p(\boldsymbol z) \,d\boldsymbol z. \tag{JC1} \]

The functional is used on its actual domain of convergence. In the source model it is bounded on the receiving space. The local coefficients obey

\[ \overline V_1=c_1x+O(r^2),\qquad \overline V_2=c_2y+O(r^2),\qquad \overline F=\alpha xy+O(r^3),\qquad \alpha=\partial_{xy}\overline F(0). \tag{JC2} \]

The last expansion follows from the source's oddness in \(x\), its quadratic vanishing, and Taylor's formula. Keep all coefficients and assume the differentiability needed in each following formula; \(C^{3,1}\) represented spatial functions suffice for the residual estimates, as the note itself observes at1518–1523.

Let \(\mathcal Y\) consist of \(C^{2,1}\) functions whose derivatives of order less than two vanish at zero and whose only possibly nonzero second derivative there is the mixed derivative. Set

\[ Jf=\partial_{xy}f(0),\qquad \mathcal X=\{f\in\mathcal Y:Jf=0\}. \tag{JC3} \]

Every \(f\in\mathcal Y\) has \(f=Jf\,xy+O(r^3)\), and every \(f\in\mathcal X\) is \(O(r^3)\). Choose an actual compactly supported \(\chi\in\mathcal Y\) with \(J\chi=1\). Then

\[ \Pi f=f-\chi Jf,\qquad J\Pi=0,\qquad \Pi^2=\Pi, \qquad\operatorname{im}\Pi=\mathcal X, \qquad\ker\Pi=\operatorname{span}\{\chi\}. \tag{JC4} \]

These identities follow by applying \(J\chi=1\), not by discarding a small coefficient. The exact maps \(f\mapsto(\Pi f,Jf)\) and \((h,z)\mapsto h+z\chi\) are inverse linear maps between \(\mathcal Y\) and \(\mathcal X\oplus\mathbb R\). Thus the quadratic obstruction is the actual one-dimensional quotient \(\mathcal Y/\mathcal X\), with its supplied section and projection.

For \(f\in\mathcal Y\) of sufficient regularity, Leibniz's rule gives

\[ J\mathcal L f=\lambda Jf+\alpha P(f),\qquad \lambda=\bar c(0)-\partial_x\overline V_1(0) -\partial_y\overline V_2(0) =\bar c(0)-c_1-c_2. \tag{JC5} \]

For example, \(\partial_{xy}(\overline V_1 f_x)(0)\) has the four terms \(\overline V_{1,xy}f_x\), \(\overline V_{1,x}f_{xy}\), \(\overline V_{1,y}f_{xx}\), and \(\overline V_1f_{xxy}\). Only the second survives, giving \(c_1Jf\). The four terms from \(\overline V_2f_y\) similarly give \(c_2Jf\). The four terms from \(\bar c f\) give \(\bar c(0)Jf\), and the functional is constant in space, so the final term gives \(\alpha P(f)\). The same product rules show that \(\mathcal L f\) has zero value, zero gradient and zero pure second derivatives at the origin. These claims use the diagonal first derivative of \(\overline V\) in JC2.

The full operator \(\mathcal L_0=\mathcal L-\alpha\chi P\) consequently satisfies \(J\mathcal L_0f=\lambda Jf\). For an adequately regular solution of \(\partial_tW_1=\mathcal L_0W_1-R\) with all derivatives through order two of \(R\) zero at the origin, the lower derivatives evolve by a homogeneous finite linear system; zero initial derivatives remain zero by uniqueness for that system. After those derivatives vanish, the mixed derivative obeys \((JW_1)'=\lambda JW_1\). Thus initial cubic vanishing is preserved. This is an exact statement about the regular evolution; existence and global stability of the source PDE are not inferred from this jet identity.

JC2. A constructed correction with its full stream function

Take an even \(C^\infty\) compactly supported cutoff \(\kappa\), equal to one in a neighborhood of zero. Write \(K(x,y)=\kappa(x)\kappa(y)\) and define

\[ \Phi=-\frac{xy^3}{6}K(x,y),\qquad \chi=(-\Delta)\Phi=\Delta\left(\frac{xy^3}{6}K(x,y)\right). \tag{JC6} \]

The complete expression, including every transition term, is

\[ \begin{aligned} \chi={}&xy\kappa(x)\kappa(y) +\frac{y^3}{3}\kappa'(x)\kappa(y) +\frac{xy^3}{6}\kappa''(x)\kappa(y)\\ &+xy^2\kappa(x)\kappa'(y) +\frac{xy^3}{6}\kappa(x)\kappa''(y). \end{aligned} \tag{JC7} \]

Near zero \(\chi=xy\) exactly. It is odd in \(x\), compactly supported and smooth up to the original boundary. Its original velocity, with \(\nabla^\perp=(-\partial_y,\partial_x)\), is

\[ \boldsymbol u(\chi)=\nabla^\perp\Phi =\left( \frac{x\kappa(x)}6[3y^2\kappa(y)+y^3\kappa'(y)], -\frac{y^3\kappa(y)}6[\kappa(x)+x\kappa'(x)] \right). \tag{JC8} \]

This identification with the original Dirichlet inverse can be proved directly. For two points \(\boldsymbol x,\boldsymbol z\) in the open half-plane, its Green function is \((2\pi)^{-1}\log(|\boldsymbol x-\boldsymbol z^*|/ |\boldsymbol x-\boldsymbol z|)\), where \(\boldsymbol z^*=(z_1,-z_2)\). The identity \(-\Delta_{\boldsymbol z}[-(2\pi)^{-1}\log|\boldsymbol x-\boldsymbol z|] =\delta_{\boldsymbol x}\) follows from its radial derivative \(-1/(2\pi\rho)\) on a circle of radius \(\rho\) about \(\boldsymbol x\). Its outward flux is \(-1\), so the negative Laplacian has mass one. On the inner boundary of the punctured half-plane the outward normal points toward the center; that reversal gives the corresponding positive term in Green's identity. The reflected term is harmonic inside the half-plane and cancels the boundary value. Integrate Green's identity against the compactly supported \(\Phi\): the outer boundary contributes zero, and on \(y=0\) both \(\Phi\) and the Green function vanish. Removing a small circle and taking its radius to zero gives \((-\Delta)^{-1}(-\Delta\Phi)=\Phi\). This proves JC8 for the original inverse, with its boundary condition fixed.

In particular, for the source functional \(P(f)=u_x(f)(0)=-\partial_{xy}(-\Delta)^{-1}f(0)\), JC8 gives

\[ P(\chi)=0,\qquad \boldsymbol u(\chi)=(xy^2/2,-y^3/6)\text{ near zero}, \qquad\nabla\boldsymbol u(\chi)(0)=0. \tag{JC9} \]

The functional has an explicit bounded domain adequate for the example below. Let \(f\) be a smooth function supported in the closed half-ball of radius \(R\), whose extension to the whole plane is odd in each coordinate and smooth, and set \(\|f\|_{2,R}=\sup_{0<r\leq R}|f|/r^2\). Then

\[ P(f)=-\frac4\pi\int_{\mathbb R^2_{++}} \frac{z_1z_2}{|\boldsymbol z|^4}f(\boldsymbol z) \,d\boldsymbol z, \qquad |P(f)|\leq\frac{R^2}{\pi}\|f\|_{2,R}. \tag{JC9a} \]

To justify the derivative, extend \(f\) oddly across the boundary. The whole-plane fundamental solution is \(N(\boldsymbol z)=-(2\pi)^{-1}\log|\boldsymbol z|\). In \(\partial_{xy}(N*f)(0)=\int N\partial_{xy}f\), integrate by parts twice outside a circle of radius \(\rho\). The omitted interior integral is \(O(\rho^2|\log\rho|)\). The two boundary terms are bounded by constants times \(\rho^2|\log\rho|\) and \(\rho^2\), since \(f=O(r^2)\), \(\nabla f=O(r)\), \(N=O(|\log\rho|)\) and \(\nabla N=O(\rho^{-1})\). All tend to zero. The resulting kernel is \(\partial_{xy}N=z_1z_2/(\pi|\boldsymbol z|^4)\), which is absolutely integrable against \(f\) at zero. Odd reflection gives the Dirichlet potential on the half-plane; oddness in both coordinates makes the whole-plane integral four times the quadrant integral. The original velocity adds its minus sign. Finally, polar coordinates give \(\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos\vartheta\sin\vartheta\,d\vartheta=1/2\) and \(\int_0^Rr\,dr=R^2/2\), proving the bound in JC9a. Thus the model example uses the actual bounded Biot–Savart functional, including its sign and domain, rather than an unspecified scalar map.

It cannot satisfy the subsequent required mixed derivative \(+1\). The sign in JC6 is the one explicitly present in Part I v4 at2260 and Part II v3 at1150. JC4. Complete weighted estimates and the infinite time tail

For a scalar function with zero derivatives through order two at zero, Taylor's integral formula on the original half-ball gives

\[ f(\boldsymbol x)=\frac12\int_0^1(1-s)^2 D^3f(s\boldsymbol x)[\boldsymbol x,\boldsymbol x,\boldsymbol x]\,ds, \qquad \sup_{0<r\leq R}r^{-3}|f(\boldsymbol x)| \leq\frac16\sup_{r\leq R}\|D^3f(\boldsymbol x)\|_{\rm op}. \tag{JC15} \]

The third differential contains all four original terms \(x^3f_{xxx}+3x^2yf_{xxy}+3xy^2f_{xyy}+y^3f_{yyy}\). The integral of \((1-s)^2/2\) is \(1/6\). For \(C^{2,1}\) functions use the second-order integral remainder and the Lipschitz constant of the Hessian; it gives the same coefficient. The constant \(1/6\) is attained by \(f=x^3/6\) on the positive \(x\)-axis when the norm of its third differential is one.

Writing \(M_3(f;R)=\sup_{r\leq R}\|D^3f\|_{\rm op}\), JC12–JC15 give both complete bounds

\[ \begin{aligned} \|r^{-3}E_2(t)\|_{L^\infty(0<r\leq R)} &\leq\tfrac16\big(M_3(E;R)+|a'|M_3(\chi;R) +|a|M_3(\mathcal L\chi;R)\big),\\ \|r^{-3}E_2(t)\|_{L^\infty(0<r\leq R)} &\leq\tfrac16\big(M_3(\Pi E;R) +|a|M_3(\Pi\mathcal L\chi;R)\big). \end{aligned} \tag{JC16} \]

The first keeps the derivative explicitly; the second retains its entire quadratic correction inside \(\Pi E\). Away from zero the pointwise bound is

\[ r^{-3}|E_2|\leq r^{-3}|E|+|a'|r^{-3}|\chi| +|a|r^{-3}|\mathcal L\chi|, \]

with the same three terms.

For an actual negative \(\lambda=-\gamma<0\), a defect supported in \([0,T_0]\), and \(|e(t)|\leq\varepsilon\), the full original integral in JC12 gives

\[ \begin{gathered} |a(t)|\leq e^{-\gamma t}|e_0| +\frac{\varepsilon}{\gamma} e^{-\gamma\max(t-T_0,0)} (1-e^{-\gamma\min(t,T_0)}),\\ |a'(t)|\leq\gamma|a(t)|+|e(t)|, \qquad a(t)=e^{-\gamma(t-T_0)}a(T_0)\quad(t\geq T_0). \end{gathered} \tag{JC17} \]

Each formula follows by integrating the positive exponential kernel on \([0,\min(t,T_0)]\). The correction is not truncated at \(T_0\). No numerical value such as the source's claimed rate below \(-5\) is assumed here in place of its still-unchecked parameter enclosure.

For the actual first-corrected approximation \(\widehat W^{(1)}\), let \(\boldsymbol E=(\partial_t-\mathcal L)\widehat W^{(1)}\), \(\boldsymbol F=J\boldsymbol E\), and set \(\boldsymbol a'=\Gamma\boldsymbol a-\boldsymbol F\), with initial value chosen to match the three target jets. Direct expansion gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \boldsymbol E_2 &=(\partial_t-\mathcal L)(\widehat W^{(1)}+B\boldsymbol a)\\ &=\boldsymbol E+B\boldsymbol a'-\mathcal L B\boldsymbol a\\ &=(I-BJ)\boldsymbol E+(B\Gamma-\mathcal L B)\boldsymbol a, \qquad J\boldsymbol E_2=0. \end{aligned} \tag{JC27} \]

This is exactly the algebra performed in Part II at1284–1303, before its split into the numerical velocity, velocity error and profile-velocity error. In particular it accounts for \(\boldsymbol a'\); it does not use the note's incomplete bound. The full original functions in each parenthesis are still needed for the quantitative estimates. Their jets alone are not those estimates.

Keeping the source relation \(\bar c_\omega=\bar c_l/2+\bar u_x(0)\), put \(r_1=-2\bar c_l+\bar c_\omega\) and \(r_2=-2\bar c_l+2\bar c_\omega-\bar u_x(0)\). Then \(r_2-r_1=\bar c_l/2\), with all original rates retained, and

\[ e^{\Gamma t}= \begin{pmatrix} e^{r_1t}&\displaystyle\int_0^t e^{r_1(t-s)}e^{r_2s}\,ds&0\\ 0&e^{r_2t}&0\\0&0&e^{r_2t} \end{pmatrix},\qquad \int_0^t e^{r_1(t-s)}e^{r_2s}\,ds =\frac{2}{\bar c_l}(e^{r_2t}-e^{r_1t}). \tag{JC28} \]

Differentiate the integral entry to obtain \(h'=r_1h+e^{r_2t}\), \(h(0)=0\). This verifies the full matrix equation and identity initial value, proving JC28. The forced solution is exactly \(\boldsymbol a(t)=e^{\Gamma t}\boldsymbol a(0) -\int_0^t e^{\Gamma(t-s)}\boldsymbol F(s)\,ds\), so the first component retains both \(F_1\) and the transfer from \(F_2\). For \(\bar c_l>0\) and \(\bar c_\omega<0\), the original formulas give \(r_1<0\) and \(r_2=-3\bar c_l/2+\bar c_\omega<0\). This proves decay of this finite correction matrix in that class. It neither estimates the full infinite-dimensional operator nor supplies the source's numerical claim that its particular rates are below \(-5\).

JC8. The exact return from independent components to a temperature

Retain the original \(b=\bar c_l(x,y)+\bar{\boldsymbol u}\), \(\bar c_\theta=\bar c_l+2\bar c_\omega\), \(c_\omega=u_x(0)\) and \(h=-\boldsymbol u\cdot\nabla\bar\theta+2c_\omega\bar\theta\). The last two linear equations in Part II935–944, including all nonlocal terms, are precisely

\[ \begin{aligned} (\partial_t+b\cdot\nabla)\eta &=(\bar c_\theta-b_{1,x})\eta-b_{2,x}\xi+h_x,\\ (\partial_t+b\cdot\nabla)\xi &=-b_{1,y}\eta+(\bar c_\theta-b_{2,y})\xi+h_y. \end{aligned} \tag{JC29} \]

For example, \(h_x=-\boldsymbol u_x\cdot\nabla\bar\theta -\boldsymbol u\cdot\nabla\bar\theta_x+2c_\omega\bar\theta_x\); the \(y\) derivative retains the corresponding three terms. Thus the displayed compact form preserves the original full functions.

Define the actual incompatibility \(D=\partial_x\xi-\partial_y\eta\). Differentiate the second equation in \(x\) and subtract the \(y\) derivative of the first. The transport differentiation contributes

\[ -b_{1,x}\xi_x-b_{2,x}\xi_y+b_{1,y}\eta_x+b_{2,y}\eta_y. \]

Differentiating the matrix products contributes

\[ \begin{gathered} -b_{1,y}\eta_x+b_{2,x}\xi_y +(\bar c_\theta-b_{2,y})\xi_x-(\bar c_\theta-b_{1,x})\eta_y,\\ (-b_{1,xy}+b_{1,xy})\eta+(-b_{2,xy}+b_{2,xy})\xi=0. \end{gathered} \]

The forcing contributes \(h_{xy}-h_{xy}=0\). Hence

\[ (\partial_t+b\cdot\nabla)D =(\bar c_\theta-\operatorname{div}b)D =(2\bar c_\omega-\bar c_l)D, \qquad D(t,X(t))=e^{(2\bar c_\omega-\bar c_l)t}D(0,X(0)). \tag{JC30} \]

The characteristic formula holds on each existing characteristic interval beginning at zero; it follows by a scalar integrating factor. Here \(\operatorname{div}b=2\bar c_l\) uses the exact divergence of the original barred velocity. Thus the independent-component evolution preserves the class of compatible gradients. No choice of a correction coefficient is presumed to do so by itself: for the source's exact correction,

\[ D(B\boldsymbol a) =a_3\left(xK_*+\frac{x^2}{2}\partial_xK_*\right) -a_2\left(xK_*+xy\partial_yK_*\right). \tag{JC31} \]

The local leading term is \((a_3-a_2)x\); the two distinct cutoff derivatives also remain. Even \(a_2=a_3\) does not cancel them throughout the support. The exact Duhamel sum, with compatible initial data, obeys the full linear equations and therefore JC30. This proves its return to the gradient class without asserting compatibility of every auxiliary component.

For completeness construct the original temperature, rather than only showing zero incompatibility. On the half-plane, for \(D=0\) put

\[ \begin{gathered} \theta(t,x,y)=q(t)+\int_0^x\eta(t,s,0)\,ds +\int_0^y\xi(t,x,s)\,ds,\\ q(t)=e^{\bar c_\theta t}q(0) +\int_0^t e^{\bar c_\theta(t-s)}h(s,0,0)\,ds. \end{gathered} \tag{JC32} \]

The oriented first integral includes negative \(x\). Direct differentiation gives \(\theta_y=\xi\) and \(\theta_x=\eta(t,x,0)+\int_0^y\xi_x(t,x,s)ds=\eta\). Applying both derivatives to \(Q=\theta_t+b\cdot\nabla\theta-\bar c_\theta\theta-h\) and using JC29 gives \(Q_x=Q_y=0\). The half-plane is connected, so \(Q\) is constant in space. The original symmetries give \(b(0,0)=0\); at the origin the ODE for \(q\) gives \(Q=0\). This proves the full original linearized temperature equation with its specified initial constant and boundary origin. Two reconstructed temperatures with the same gradient differ by a spatial constant, whose evolution is exactly \(q'=\bar c_\theta q\); fixing \(q(0)\) therefore gives uniqueness. This is the actual inverse map from the compatible enlarged system to temperature, including its constant.

The nonlinear terms in WT5 have the same exact consequence. Replace \(b\) by \(b+\boldsymbol u\), the scalar growth by \(\bar c_l+2\bar c_\omega+2c_\omega(t)\), and \(h\) by \(h+\bar F_\theta\). Their divergence is still \(2\bar c_l\), and the same expansion gives

\[ (\partial_t+(b+\boldsymbol u)\cdot\nabla)D =(2\bar c_\omega+2c_\omega(t)-\bar c_l)D. \]

The integrating factor is the exponential of the full time integral of this coefficient. This propagates compatibility through the complete perturbation equation without discarding its profile residual.

The local omissions in the correction note have exact repairs already visible in the current main papers. The remaining work is to check their actual represented profiles, full nonlocal estimates and numerical residual bounds. No main-theorem failure follows from the local omissions established here.

Part 4. Exact derivatives, singular weights and both Sobolev norms in the original transport model

Human sources: Jiajie Chen and Thomas Y. Hou, Part I, arXiv:2210.07191v4, original TeX1851–1938, and the analytic correction note, arXiv:2607.15256v1, original TeX315–358. Part I1935 and the note354 say that decay of the pure \(x\) derivative in the displayed model needs order at least fourteen. The exact calculation below gives order eight for strict \(L^2\) decay in Part I's specified model, with order seven neutral. It also proves the full mixed-derivative and coupled equations, and the exact distinction between a homogeneous derivative norm and the inhomogeneous Sobolev norm. These are statements about the original displayed constant-coefficient model; no nonlocal term of the full Euler/Boussinesq problem is thereby controlled.

LT1. Complete original solutions and their supports

Retain the original quadrant \(Q=(0,\infty)^2\), coordinates \((x,y)\), time \(t\geq0\), and coefficients \(a_1,a_2>0\) and real \(a_3\). The source's model is

\[ \begin{aligned} \omega_t+(a_1x\partial_x+a_2y\partial_y)\omega&=-\omega+\eta,\\ \eta_t+(a_1x\partial_x+a_2y\partial_y)\eta&=a_3\eta, \qquad (a_1,a_2,a_3)=(1/2,11/2,1/2). \end{aligned} \tag{LT1} \]

The triple on the right is exactly the model printed at1855; it is not an exact enclosure for the full source profile. We retain general coefficients while deriving the maps and then substitute that triple. Take \(\omega_0,\eta_0\in C_c^\infty(Q)\). Extending oddly in \(x\) gives the source symmetry without imposing an artificial boundary value at \(y=0\) on the full PDE. This initial class already contains nonzero examples sufficient to test every asserted necessity below. The same explicit formulas apply to other initial functions whenever the indicated norms and derivatives exist.

Let

\[ \begin{gathered} X(t;s,r)=(e^{a_1t}s,e^{a_2t}r),\qquad A_t(x,y)=(e^{-a_1t}x,e^{-a_2t}y),\\ H(t)=\int_0^t e^{-(t-s)}e^{a_3s}\,ds =\begin{cases} (e^{a_3t}-e^{-t})/(a_3+1),&a_3\ne-1,\\ te^{-t},&a_3=-1. \end{cases} \end{gathered} \tag{LT2} \]

Both maps preserve the quadrant, and each is the inverse of the other at fixed time. Their Jacobians are respectively \(e^{(a_1+a_2)t}\) and \(e^{-(a_1+a_2)t}\). Along the first map the second equation is \(\dot\eta=a_3\eta\); the first then has the integrating factor \(e^t\). Therefore the unique smooth solution with these initial values is

\[ \eta(t,x,y)=e^{a_3t}\eta_0(A_t(x,y)),\qquad \omega(t,x,y)=e^{-t}\omega_0(A_t(x,y))+H(t)\eta_0(A_t(x,y)). \tag{LT3} \]

Uniqueness follows along every characteristic from the two scalar ODEs, whose initial footpoints cover the quadrant. Direct differentiation also verifies the full two equations, including the \(+\eta\) coupling. The support of each transported initial field is precisely the image of its support under \(X(t;\cdot)\). The vorticity support is contained in the union of its two such images; cancellation can make that inclusion strict. At every finite time the supports remain compact and avoid both axes. No boundary integral or far-field contribution is lost in subsequent integrations by parts.

LT2. Every mixed derivative and original measure factor

For integers \(i,j\geq0\), differentiation at fixed time gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \partial_x^i\partial_y^j\eta(t) &=e^{(a_3-ia_1-ja_2)t} (\partial_x^i\partial_y^j\eta_0)\circ A_t,\\ \partial_x^i\partial_y^j\omega(t) &=e^{-(ia_1+ja_2)t} \left[e^{-t}\partial_x^i\partial_y^j\omega_0 +H(t)\partial_x^i\partial_y^j\eta_0\right]\circ A_t. \end{aligned} \tag{LT4} \]

No mixed derivative is omitted. In the differentiated equations the transport contributes exactly \(-ia_1-ja_2\) to each scalar growth coefficient; its constant diagonal gradient generates no coupling between distinct derivative indices. Changing variables using the actual Jacobian in LT2 proves, for \(1\leq p<\infty\),

\[ \begin{aligned} \|\partial_x^i\partial_y^j\eta(t)\|_{L^p(Q)} &=e^{[a_3-ia_1-ja_2+(a_1+a_2)/p]t} \|\partial_x^i\partial_y^j\eta_0\|_{L^p(Q)},\\ \|\partial_x^i\partial_y^j\omega(t)\|_{L^p(Q)} &=e^{[-ia_1-ja_2+(a_1+a_2)/p]t} \|e^{-t}\partial_x^i\partial_y^j\omega_0 +H(t)\partial_x^i\partial_y^j\eta_0\|_{L^p(Q)}. \end{aligned} \tag{LT5} \]

For \(p=\infty\) the same formulas have measure contribution zero: composition with the bijection preserves the supremum. For any nonzero derivative the first equality proves strict decay exactly when its exponent is negative, neutral norm when it is zero, and strict growth when it is positive. A zero derivative stays identically zero and is not a witness for any strict threshold.

Specialize to the original triple in LT1 and \(p=2\). The exact formulas are

\[ \begin{gathered} \|\partial_x^i\partial_y^j\eta(t)\|_2^2 =e^{(7-i-11j)t}\|\partial_x^i\partial_y^j\eta_0\|_2^2,\\ \|\partial_x^k\eta(t)\|_2 =e^{(7-k)t/2}\|\partial_x^k\eta_0\|_2. \end{gathered} \tag{LT6} \]

In particular \(k=7\) is neutral and every \(k\geq8\) decays. For a concrete nonzero function in the stated original class let \(w(s)=e^{-1/s}\) for \(s>0\) and zero otherwise, and set \(b(s)=w(s-1)w(2-s)\), \(\eta_0(x,y)=b(x)b(y)\). This is smooth and supported in \([1,2]^2\). Its \(k\)-th pure \(x\) derivative is nonzero: otherwise \(b\) would be a polynomial of degree at most \(k-1\) on the connected positive half-line, and its vanishing on \((2,\infty)\) would force it to be zero everywhere, a contradiction. For \(k=0\) nonvanishing is immediate. Thus LT6 gives actual examples at every threshold, including strict decay with \(k=8<14\).

LT3. The entire homogeneous derivative norm and the coupled system

Specify the order-\(k\) homogeneous derivative seminorm by

\[ |f|_{\dot H^k(Q)}^2=\sum_{i+j=k} \|\partial_x^i\partial_y^jf\|_2^2. \tag{LT7} \]

This formula fixes every term and coefficient in the convention used here. On the compactly supported smooth class it is a norm: if all order-\(k\) derivatives vanish, its pure \(x\) derivative vanishes, and the polynomial argument just given on each horizontal line forces \(f=0\). For \(k=0\) it is the ordinary \(L^2\) norm. For \(a_2\geq a_1>0\), LT5 with \(p=2\) gives the full identity and bound

\[ \begin{aligned} |\eta(t)|_{\dot H^k}^2 &=\sum_{j=0}^k e^{[2a_3+a_1+a_2-2(k-j)a_1-2ja_2]t} \|\partial_x^{k-j}\partial_y^j\eta_0\|_2^2\\ &\leq e^{[2a_3+a_1+a_2-2ka_1]t}|\eta_0|_{\dot H^k}^2. \end{aligned} \tag{LT8} \]

All mixed derivatives have been retained in the sum. If \(a_2>a_1\) and \(\eta_0\ne0\), division by the largest exponential and the finite sum give

\[ \lim_{t\to\infty} e^{-[a_3+(a_1+a_2)/2-ka_1]t}|\eta(t)|_{\dot H^k} =\|\partial_x^k\eta_0\|_2>0. \tag{LT9} \]

The positivity follows on some horizontal slice from the same compact-support polynomial argument, then by continuity on a neighborhood of that slice. Thus the exponent in LT8 cannot be improved uniformly in time by introducing a fixed finite prefactor. For the source model it is \((7-k)/2\), and all homogeneous norms of order \(k\geq8\) decay. The mixed derivatives do not create an additional difficulty in this frozen model.

For the coupled vorticity put \(d_k=(a_1+a_2)/2-ka_1\). Apply LT4 termwise in the finite direct sum of \(L^2\) spaces. Every dilation factor is at most \(e^{d_kt}\); the triangle inequality in that direct sum gives

\[ |\omega(t)|_{\dot H^k} \leq e^{d_kt} \left(e^{-t}|\omega_0|_{\dot H^k} +H(t)|\eta_0|_{\dot H^k}\right). \tag{LT10} \]

Here \(H(t)\geq0\) for \(t\geq0\), directly from its integral, including the resonant value in LT2. For the exact triple in LT1, \(d_k=3-k/2\) and \(H(t)=\tfrac23(e^{t/2}-e^{-t})\). Consequently

\[ \begin{aligned} |\omega(t)|_{\dot H^k} &\leq e^{(2-k/2)t}|\omega_0|_{\dot H^k} +\frac23\left(e^{(7-k)t/2}-e^{(2-k/2)t}\right) |\eta_0|_{\dot H^k},\\ |\eta(t)|_{\dot H^k}&\leq e^{(7-k)t/2}|\eta_0|_{\dot H^k}. \end{aligned} \tag{LT11} \]

For \(k\geq8\), both right sides tend exponentially to zero, with the exact transfer coefficient \(2/3\) retained. In particular, their sum is at most \(e^{(7-k)t/2}(|\omega_0|_{\dot H^k} +\tfrac53|\eta_0|_{\dot H^k})\). This proves decay for the full two-component homogeneous norm without replacing the vorticity equation by the temperature-derivative equation.

LT4. Why this is not decay in the full unweighted Sobolev norm

Define the inhomogeneous norm by retaining all its original orders:

\[ \|f\|_{H^k(Q)}^2=\sum_{i+j\leq k} \|\partial_x^i\partial_y^j f\|_2^2. \tag{LT12} \]

For the source model the exact formula is

\[ \|\eta(t)\|_{H^k}^2 =\sum_{i+j\leq k}e^{(7-i-11j)t} \|\partial_x^i\partial_y^j\eta_0\|_2^2, \qquad \lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-7t}\|\eta(t)\|_{H^k}^2 =\|\eta_0\|_2^2. \tag{LT13} \]

Indeed, the zero-order term has exponent seven and every other term has a strictly smaller exponent. There are only finitely many terms, so passage to the displayed limit is immediate term by term. For every nonzero compactly supported initial \(\eta_0\), this norm grows at the exact leading rate \(e^{7t/2}\) for every finite \(k\). Increasing the derivative order cannot remove the zero-order term in an inhomogeneous norm. For the coupled system the sum of the two inhomogeneous norms is at least this growing temperature-derivative norm, so it cannot decay either.

The source's surrounding caution about establishing a full stability estimate remains relevant. The corrected statement is precise: the model's top homogeneous derivatives decay starting at order eight; its full unweighted inhomogeneous Sobolev norm does not decay for nonzero \(\eta_0\). Neither statement estimates the variable coefficients or nonlocal operators in the full source PDE.

LT5. Every energy-weight factor and the exact derivative correspondence

Retain arbitrary real \(\alpha,\beta\) and the original weight \(\phi(x,y)=x^{-\alpha}y^{-\beta}\) on \(Q\). For a compactly supported initial field as above every weighted integral is finite. Changing variables in LT3 proves the full energy identity

\[ \int_Q|\eta(t,x,y)|^2x^{-\alpha}y^{-\beta}\,dx\,dy =e^{[2a_3+(1-\alpha)a_1+(1-\beta)a_2]t} \int_Q|\eta_0(x,y)|^2x^{-\alpha}y^{-\beta}\,dx\,dy. \tag{LT14} \]

The factors arise separately from the square of the field, the Jacobian, and the two powers of the weight. Integrating by parts gives the same result: the \(x\)-transport contributes \(a_1(1-\alpha)\int|\eta|^2\phi\) to the energy derivative, the \(y\)-transport contributes \(a_2(1-\beta)\int|\eta|^2\phi\), and growth contributes \(2a_3\int|\eta|^2\phi\). Compact supports make the boundary terms zero. For broader classes those boundary limits must actually vanish before this integration-by-parts proof is used.

For the exact model, the exponent in LT14 is \(7-\alpha/2-11\beta/2\). With \(\beta=0\), the weighted energy is nonincreasing for \(\alpha\geq14\) and strictly decays for \(\alpha>14\), with neutrality at fourteen. Thus the printed number fourteen does arise for this energy-weight exponent. It is not the corresponding derivative order. The exact comparison is as follows.

For \(i,j\geq0\) set \(T_{ij}f=x^iy^j\partial_x^i\partial_y^jf\). The Euler transport operator \(L_b=a_1x\partial_x+a_2y\partial_y\) satisfies

\[ \begin{gathered} {}[L_b,\partial_x^i\partial_y^j] =-(ia_1+ja_2)\partial_x^i\partial_y^j, \qquad [L_b,T_{ij}]=0,\\ \|\partial_x^i\partial_y^jf\|_2^2 =\int_Q|T_{ij}f|^2x^{-2i}y^{-2j}\,dx\,dy. \end{gathered} \tag{LT15} \]

For the first identity, \([x\partial_x,\partial_x]=-\partial_x\) and induction gives the factor \(-i\); the \(y\) factor is proved in the same way, and cross-coordinate derivatives commute. Differentiating the multiplying monomial contributes \((ia_1+ja_2)x^iy^j\), canceling exactly that commutator. The norm identity is pointwise multiplication on \(Q\), followed by integration; no equivalence constant or omitted factor is involved. Hence \(T_{ij}\eta\) obeys the original scalar transport equation with growth \(a_3\), and LT14 with \(\alpha=2i,\beta=2j\) is exactly LT5 squared. This explains the factor of two while proving the actual intertwining map.

The image of this map can also be specified exactly. In one dimension let \(n\geq1\) and \(F\in C_c^\infty(0,\infty)\). Define

\[ I_nF(x)=\frac{(-1)^n}{(n-1)!} \int_x^\infty(s-x)^{n-1}F(s)\,ds. \tag{LT16} \]

Differentiating under the integral \(n\) times, with the lower endpoint contribution retained on the last step, gives \(\partial_x^n I_nF=F\). Above the support the integral is zero. Below its support expand the entire polynomial \((s-x)^{n-1}=\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{m}s^m(-x)^{n-1-m}\). Thus \(I_nF\) vanishes below the support exactly when \(\int_0^\infty s^mF(s)ds=0\) for every \(0\leq m<n\). These conditions are also necessary for \(F=\partial_x^nf\) with compactly supported \(f\), by integration by parts \(n\) times. The difference \(I_n\partial_x^nf-f\) has zero \(n\)-th derivative and vanishes above both supports; it is a polynomial that vanishes on an interval, hence zero. This proves both inverse identities on the specified image.

Apply this argument separately in \(x\) and \(y\). The map \(T_{ij}:C_c^\infty(Q)\to C_c^\infty(Q)\) is a bijection onto the subspace of functions \(G\) for which \(F(x,y)=x^{-i}y^{-j}G(x,y)\) obeys

\[ \int_0^\infty s^mF(s,y)\,ds=0\quad(0\leq m<i),\qquad \int_0^\infty r^nF(x,r)\,dr=0\quad(0\leq n<j), \tag{LT17} \]

at every value of the other coordinate. A family of conditions is empty when its index is zero. The inverse is \(I_i^xI_j^yF\), where \(I_0\) denotes the identity. If \(i,j\geq1\), its full formula is

\[ f(x,y)=\frac{(-1)^{i+j}}{(i-1)!(j-1)!} \int_x^\infty\int_y^\infty (s-x)^{i-1}(r-y)^{j-1}s^{-i}r^{-j}G(s,r)\,dr\,ds. \tag{LT18} \]

Fubini is valid on the finite supports. The moment conditions make the result vanish below each support as well as above it, so it lies in \(C_c^\infty(Q)\). The two derivatives recover \(F\), and multiplication recovers \(G\). Applying the one-dimensional inverse identities to an original \(f\) recovers that same \(f\), proving injectivity as well as surjectivity onto the stated subspace. This supplies the precise objects and inverse map behind LT15.

For a general field nonzero at \(y=0\), a factor \(y^{-\beta}\) in its squared energy does require \(\beta<1\) for local integrability near any boundary point where its value stays away from zero. Indeed, continuity supplies a positive lower bound on a small rectangle, and \(\int_0^d y^{-\beta}dy\) diverges when \(\beta\geq1\). In LT15 the weighted field is \(T_{ij}f\), which contains the actual factor \(y^j\); its square cancels \(y^{-2j}\) exactly. The boundary statement for the original undifferentiated field therefore does not forbid the derivative norm. This uses the proved map, not an identification of two different fields.

LT6. Original radial weights and exact anisotropic dilation

For completeness retain the actual original radial weight \(r^{-\gamma}\), \(r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\), with \(\gamma\geq0\). At \((x,y)=X(t;s,r_0)\), LT3 gives

\[ \frac{|\eta(t,X(t;s,r_0))|}{|X(t;s,r_0)|^\gamma} =e^{a_3t} \left(\frac{s^2+r_0^2}{e^{2a_1t}s^2+e^{2a_2t}r_0^2}\right)^{\gamma/2} \frac{|\eta_0(s,r_0)|}{(s^2+r_0^2)^{\gamma/2}}. \tag{LT19} \]

The symbol \(r_0\) is the second original coordinate of the initial point here, not its radius. Both anisotropic contributions remain in the denominator. If \(a_2\geq a_1\), it follows that

\[ \begin{aligned} \|r^{-\gamma}\eta(t)\|_\infty &\leq e^{(a_3-a_1\gamma)t}\|r^{-\gamma}\eta_0\|_\infty,\\ \|r^{-\gamma}\omega(t)\|_\infty &\leq e^{-a_1\gamma t} \left(e^{-t}\|r^{-\gamma}\omega_0\|_\infty +H(t)\|r^{-\gamma}\eta_0\|_\infty\right). \end{aligned} \tag{LT20} \]

For the original triple, strict scalar damping holds for \(\gamma>1\); retaining only quadratic vanishing permits \(1<\gamma\leq2\) near the origin, exactly as stated in the source. The full angular coefficient is \(a_3-\gamma(a_1x^2+a_2y^2)/(x^2+y^2)\), obtained by differentiating the original radial weight, with no averaged replacement of the two coordinates.

The first bound in LT20 has the exact operator norm on the compact smooth class used here. To prove the lower bound, fix \(s=1\) and let \(r_0>0\) decrease to zero. The multiplier in LT19 tends to \(e^{(a_3-a_1\gamma)t}\). Choose a smooth nonnegative bump \(\psi\) supported in a ball in \(Q\), equal to one at that point, and bounded by one; then \(\eta_0(s,r_0)=(s^2+r_0^2)^{\gamma/2}\psi(s,r_0)\) has weighted norm one and realizes the exact multiplier at the selected point. Its support avoids zero, so every real \(\gamma\) in the stated range yields a smooth field. Odd extension in \(x\) preserves the source symmetry. Taking the limit proves the lower bound, while LT20 proves the upper bound. No value on the excluded axis is needed to attain the supremum as a limit.

These computations complete the original frozen model, its full derivative system, all measure factors, the singular-weight comparison and the exact norm thresholds. Exercise 1. An exact clock with a logarithmic spatial correction

Use the original clock definitions with \(A=3\), \(\bar c_l=5\), \(\bar c_\omega=-2\) and \(c_\omega(\tau)=-2-(1+\tau)^{-1}\). Calculate the entire physical clock and both field scales, give an exact remaining-time formula, and determine the leading length scale as physical time approaches its endpoint. Solution. The rate is continuous and lies in \([-3,-2]\), so it also lies in the QC1 interval with \(\delta=1<2\). Its full accumulated deviation is \(B(\tau)=-\log(1+\tau)\). Direct integration gives

\[ \begin{gathered} C_\omega(\tau)=\frac{3e^{-2\tau}}{1+\tau},\qquad C_l(\tau)=e^{-5\tau},\qquad C_\theta(\tau)=\frac{9e^\tau}{(1+\tau)^2},\\ c_\theta(\tau)=1-\frac2{1+\tau},\qquad C_\theta=C_\omega^2/C_l. \end{gathered} \tag{EX1} \]

For the lower bound use \(\log(1+u/(1+\tau))\leq u/(1+\tau)\), proved by integrating \(1/(1+s)\leq1\) from zero to the nonnegative argument. The upper bound uses the ratio at most one. The limit also follows from these two bounds; no asymptotic formula for the special function is required.

Exercise 3. The exact nonlocal coefficient and the entire correction tail

\[ \begin{aligned} P(H)&=-\frac4\pi \int_0^{\pi/2}\cos^2\theta\sin^2\theta\,d\theta \int_0^2r\vartheta((4-r^2)/3)\,dr\\ &=-\frac14\cdot\frac54=-\frac5{16}. \end{aligned} \tag{EX12} \]

Indeed, \(\cos^2\theta\sin^2\theta=(1-\cos4\theta)/8\), so the angular integral is \(\pi/16\). For the radial integral put \(z=(4-r^2)/3\), retaining \(r\,dr=-3\,dz/2\). \[ P(\mathcal L\chi)=0,\qquad J\mathcal L\chi=-3,\qquad P(g^{(1)}(t))=-\frac{5\varepsilon}{16}\rho(t)t. \tag{EX13} \]

Here \(Jf=f_{xy}(0)\), and the middle equality uses the actual jet formula JC5. Put \(q_0(t)=1+(-3+\varepsilon)t\). Then \(Jg^{(1)}=\rho q_0\). Applying \(J\mathcal Lf=-3Jf+P(f)\), with the full nonlocal term, gives
