The complete original cascade averaging theorem

Every original local cascade in the stated small-parameter range can be written as an average of the original Euler operator. This lesson proves the whole construction from Terence Tao's arXiv:1402.0290v3, original navier-blowup.tex, lines 501–826. Every frequency, scale, rotation, dilation, Fourier coefficient and measure factor remains explicit.

The proof begins with the exact tensor observation at a closed frequency triangle. It constructs the full rotation fiber and its measure, a smooth extension and all Fourier multipliers, then the complete scale sum and the maps back to the original real annular fields. Source coefficient and index corrections are stated next to their full derivations.

The operator, Fourier and physical conventions are those of lesson 37. The local-cascade fields, exact pump and full real/complex composition formulas are proved in lesson 38. References to CC or to that lesson's EX formulas identify those proofs. All SP, RF, AF, AV and current EX formulas are proved below.

Five solved exercises calculate the full closing measure and its moments, the smallest possible recovery kernel, the exact retraction derivative, the sharp scale-weight class for the original pump and the stability of its actual original-viscosity flows. The averaging theorem is one ingredient of the later blowup construction; the finite-time dynamics still need their own proof.

SP1. Keep the original triangle, orientation and trilinear form

Let the original real frequencies satisfy

\[ \eta_1+\eta_2+\eta_3=0,\qquad H=|\eta_1\times\eta_2|>0,\qquad r_j=|\eta_j|>0. \tag{SP1} \]

No frequency is replaced by a unit vector. Define the auxiliary frame, retaining every original radius,

\[ n=\frac{\eta_1\times\eta_2}{H},\qquad w_j=\frac{\eta_j\times n}{r_j},\qquad E_j=\{Y\in\mathbb C^3:\eta_j\cdot Y=0\}. \tag{SP2} \]

Here a dot without a bar is the complex bilinear extension of the real Euclidean dot product. Norms of complex vectors and tensors use the Hermitian Euclidean norm. Closure of the triangle implies \(\eta_3\cdot n=0\). Each pair \(n,w_j\) is a real orthonormal basis of the original perpendicular plane, hence a complex basis of \(E_j\).

The rotation about the oriented original axis \(\eta_j\) is specified on this plane by

\[ \begin{gathered} R_{\eta_j}^{\theta}n=\cos\theta\,n+\sin\theta\,w_j,\qquad R_{\eta_j}^{\theta}w_j=-\sin\theta\,n+\cos\theta\,w_j,\\ R_{\eta_j}^{\theta}\eta_j=\eta_j. \end{gathered} \tag{SP3} \]

The second equality follows from \((\eta_j/r_j)\times((\eta_j/r_j)\times n)=-n\). These formulas define a real orthogonal determinant-one map on the full orthogonal basis and fix its angle and orientation exactly. They extend complex linearly, without any restriction on the magnitude of an input vector.

Retain the source trilinear expression and its actual angle function:

\[ \begin{gathered} \Lambda_\eta(Y_1,Y_2,Y_3) =(Y_1\cdot\eta_2)(Y_2\cdot Y_3) +(Y_2\cdot\eta_1)(Y_1\cdot Y_3),\\ \Theta_\eta(\gamma) =\Lambda_\eta(R_{\eta_1}^{\gamma_1}n, R_{\eta_2}^{\gamma_2}n,R_{\eta_3}^{\gamma_3}n). \end{gathered} \tag{SP4} \]

Both original summands and frequencies remain in every comparison below.

SP2. Calculate all eight coefficients from both summands

The exact scalar products are

\[ \begin{gathered} a=w_1\cdot\eta_2=-\frac{H}{r_1},\qquad b=w_2\cdot\eta_1=\frac{H}{r_2},\\ d_{23}=w_2\cdot w_3 =\frac{r_1^2-r_2^2-r_3^2}{2r_2r_3},\qquad d_{13}=w_1\cdot w_3 =\frac{r_2^2-r_1^2-r_3^2}{2r_1r_3}. \end{gathered} \tag{SP5} \]

For the first two identities, use the scalar triple product and \(n\cdot(\eta_1\times\eta_2)=H\); in particular \((\eta_1\times n)\cdot\eta_2=-H\). For the last two, the identity \((\eta_j\times n)\cdot(\eta_k\times n)=\eta_j\cdot\eta_k\) holds because \(n\) is unit and orthogonal to both frequencies. Squaring \(\eta_2+\eta_3=-\eta_1\) and \(\eta_1+\eta_3=-\eta_2\) gives the two full numerators.

Substitution of SP3 into both terms of SP4 gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \Theta_\eta(\gamma) ={}&a\sin\gamma_1\bigl(\cos\gamma_2\cos\gamma_3 +d_{23}\sin\gamma_2\sin\gamma_3\bigr)\\ &+b\sin\gamma_2\bigl(\cos\gamma_1\cos\gamma_3 +d_{13}\sin\gamma_1\sin\gamma_3\bigr). \end{aligned} \tag{SP6} \]

For each original angle, retain the exact expansions

\[ \cos\gamma=\frac12\sum_{\sigma=\pm1}e^{i\sigma\gamma}, \qquad \sin\gamma=\frac1{2i}\sum_{\sigma=\pm1}\sigma e^{i\sigma\gamma}. \]

The product of the two sine coefficients has the negative sign from \(i^{-2}=-1\). Consequently the complete Fourier series is

\[ \begin{gathered} \Theta_\eta(\gamma)=\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^3} c_\sigma(\eta)e^{i(\sigma_1\gamma_1+\sigma_2\gamma_2+\sigma_3\gamma_3)},\\ c_\sigma(\eta)=\frac1{8i} \left[ -\frac{H}{r_1}\sigma_1(1-d_{23}\sigma_2\sigma_3) +\frac{H}{r_2}\sigma_2(1-d_{13}\sigma_1\sigma_3) \right]. \end{gathered} \tag{SP7} \]

This is the source's general coefficient formula with its original geometric scalar products evaluated exactly.

To classify its zeros, keep SP7 and prove the following additional identity:

\[ c_\sigma(\eta)= \frac{H\sigma_1\sigma_2}{8i r_1r_2r_3} (\sigma_1r_1-\sigma_2r_2) \bigl[r_3+\sigma_3(\sigma_1r_1+\sigma_2r_2)\bigr]. \tag{SP8} \]

Indeed the constant part of \(8i c_\sigma/H\) is \((-\sigma_1r_2+\sigma_2r_1)/(r_1r_2)\), and its triple-sign part has numerator

\[ r_2d_{23}-r_1d_{13}=\frac{r_1^2-r_2^2}{r_3}. \]

Expanding the full right side of SP8 gives precisely those two terms. This proves an identity for the original coefficient; it does not discard either source summand.

Noncollinearity in SP1 implies the strict triangle inequalities

\[ |r_1-r_2|<r_3<r_1+r_2. \tag{SP9} \]

For example equality in \(|\eta_1+\eta_2|\leq r_1+r_2\) would make the two original vectors positively collinear, by equality in Cauchy–Schwarz. Equality in the reverse inequality would make them negatively collinear. Both contradict \(H>0\). The last bracket of SP8 is therefore nonzero for all eight sign triples: the absolute value of the signed sum of \(r_1,r_2\) is either \(r_1+r_2\) or \(|r_1-r_2|\), neither of which equals \(r_3\). The other factor vanishes precisely when \(r_1=r_2\) and \(\sigma_1=\sigma_2\). We have thus proved the exact condition

\[ \begin{gathered} \text{all eight }c_\sigma(\eta)\ne0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r_1\ne r_2,\\ r_1=r_2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad c_\sigma=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \sigma_1=\sigma_2. \end{gathered} \tag{SP10} \]

The radius \(r_3\) need not differ from both other radii.

SP3. Check the source base frequencies and provide a full neighborhood bound

At the actual source base point retain

\[ \eta_1^0=(0,1,0),\qquad \eta_2^0=(-1,-1,0),\qquad \eta_3^0=(1,0,0). \tag{SP11} \]

Then \(H=1\), \(n=(0,0,1)\), \((r_1,r_2,r_3)=(1,\sqrt2,1)\), \(a=-1\), \(b=1/\sqrt2\), \(d_{23}=-1/\sqrt2\), \(d_{13}=0\). The exact result is

\[ c_\sigma(\eta^0) =\frac1{8i} \left(-\sigma_1+\frac{\sigma_2}{\sqrt2} -\frac{\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3}{\sqrt2}\right), \qquad \min_\sigma|c_\sigma(\eta^0)|=\frac{\sqrt2-1}{8}. \tag{SP12} \]

For the minimum, when \(\sigma_1\sigma_3=1\) the last two terms cancel and the absolute numerator is one. When \(\sigma_1\sigma_3=-1\) it is \(|-\sigma_1+\sqrt2\,\sigma_2|\), whose two possible values are \(\sqrt2-1\) and \(\sqrt2+1\).

The original source's line 820 instead displays

\[ -\frac1{8i} \left(-\sigma_1+\frac{\sigma_2}{\sqrt2} +\frac{\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3}{\sqrt2}\right) +O(\epsilon_0). \tag{SP13} \]

Its limiting coefficient does not agree with its own general formula at lines 809–811. For example, at the base point and signs \((+,+,+)\), SP12 gives \(-1/(8i)\), while the displayed leading term SP13 gives \(-(\sqrt2-1)/(8i)\). This is a coefficient correction, not a failure of the required nonvanishing conclusion. Lines 814–815 also use a dot where the cross product in SP5 and source lines 810–811 is needed; a scalar dotted with \(\eta_j\) is not the expression being evaluated.

The nonvanishing can be made uniform with every neighborhood constant explicit. Suppose the original frequencies close as in SP1 and

\[ |\eta_j-\eta_j^0|\leq h,\qquad 0<h\leq\frac1{16}. \tag{SP14} \]

Triangle and reverse triangle inequalities give

\[ \begin{gathered} H\geq1-(1+\sqrt2)h-h^2,\qquad |r_1-r_2|\geq\sqrt2-1-2h,\\ r_1+r_2-r_3\geq\sqrt2-3h,\qquad r_3-|r_1-r_2|\geq2-\sqrt2-3h,\\ r_1r_2r_3\leq(1+h)^2(\sqrt2+h). \end{gathered} \tag{SP15} \]

For the first inequality, expand both perturbations in \(\eta_1\times\eta_2-\eta_1^0\times\eta_2^0\); the three errors have bounds \(\sqrt2 h,h,h^2\). For all remaining inequalities use \(|r_j-r_j^0|\leq h\), retaining the sign \(r_2>r_1\), which follows from its displayed positive lower gap. The two strict triangle gaps in SP15 are positive for \(h\leq1/16\), as is its bound for \(H\). The absolute value of the last SP8 bracket is at least the smaller of those two gaps, and the first factor is at least \(|r_1-r_2|\). Thus every coefficient obeys the original quantitative bound

\[ |c_\sigma(\eta)|\geq \frac{\bigl[1-(1+\sqrt2)h-h^2\bigr] (\sqrt2-1-2h)(2-\sqrt2-3h)} {8(1+h)^2(\sqrt2+h)}>0. \tag{SP16} \]

This estimate keeps all original frequencies; no rescaled triangle replaces them.

SP4. Construct the full tensor inverse for arbitrary input sizes

Let \(E=E_1\otimes_{\mathbb C}E_2\otimes_{\mathbb C}E_3\), of complex dimension eight. For signs \(\sigma_j=\pm1\), define

\[ v_{j,\sigma_j}=\frac{n-i\sigma_jw_j}{2},\qquad U_\sigma=v_{1,\sigma_1}\otimes v_{2,\sigma_2} \otimes v_{3,\sigma_3}. \tag{SP17} \]

For an arbitrary \(Y_j=\alpha_j n+\beta_jw_j\in E_j\), \(\alpha_j=n\cdot Y_j\) and \(\beta_j=w_j\cdot Y_j\) are its actual complex coefficients. SP3 gives

\[ R_{\eta_j}^{\theta_j}Y_j =\sum_{\sigma_j=\pm1} v_{j,\sigma_j}(\alpha_j+i\sigma_j\beta_j)e^{i\sigma_j\theta_j}. \tag{SP18} \]

To verify both components, sum the terms for \(Y_j=n\) and \(Y_j=w_j\). They are respectively \(\cos\theta_j n+\sin\theta_j w_j\) and \(-\sin\theta_j n+\cos\theta_j w_j\); linearity gives the full formula with its original \(\alpha_j,\beta_j\).

Define a functional on simple tensors by

\[ \ell_\sigma(Y_1\otimes Y_2\otimes Y_3) =\prod_{j=1}^3(\alpha_j+i\sigma_j\beta_j), \tag{SP19} \]

and extend linearly to \(E\). Each factor is linear in its own \(Y_j\), so the tensor functional is well-defined. For a basis candidate \(v_{j,\tau_j}\), the factor equals \((1+\sigma_j\tau_j)/2\). It follows that

\[ \ell_\sigma(U_\tau)=\delta_{\sigma,\tau},\qquad X=\sum_\sigma U_\sigma\ell_\sigma(X)\quad(X\in E). \tag{SP20} \]

Indeed the eight \(U_\sigma\) are linearly independent by these functionals; they form a basis in the eight-dimensional tensor space, proving the expansion. Multilinearity, SP18 and SP7 also prove

\[ \Lambda_\eta(R_{\eta_1}^{\theta_1}Y_1, R_{\eta_2}^{\theta_2}Y_2,R_{\eta_3}^{\theta_3}Y_3) =\sum_\sigma c_\sigma\ell_\sigma(Y_1\otimes Y_2\otimes Y_3) e^{i\sigma\cdot\theta}. \tag{SP21} \]

Here \(c_\sigma=\Lambda_\eta(v_{1,\sigma_1},v_{2,\sigma_2},v_{3,\sigma_3})\), because this is exactly the coefficient obtained by setting all \(\alpha_j=1,\beta_j=0\). Thus the coefficient in the actual rotated-input expression is the original SP7 coefficient, without an extra magnitude assumption.

When \(r_1\ne r_2\), define the actual tensor-valued kernel

\[ F_\eta(\theta)= \sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^3} \frac{U_\sigma e^{-i\sigma\cdot\theta}}{(2\pi)^3c_\sigma(\eta)}. \tag{SP22} \]

Integration uses the full Lebesgue measure \(d\theta_1d\theta_2d\theta_3\) on \([0,2\pi]^3\), not a probability measure. Since \(\int_{[0,2\pi]^3}e^{i(\tau-\sigma)\cdot\theta}d\theta=(2\pi)^3\delta_{\sigma,\tau}\), SP20–SP22 prove the complete original recovery identity

\[ \begin{aligned} Y_1\otimes Y_2\otimes Y_3 ={}&\int_{[0,2\pi]^3}F_\eta(\theta)\, \Lambda_\eta(R_{\eta_1}^{\theta_1}Y_1, R_{\eta_2}^{\theta_2}Y_2,R_{\eta_3}^{\theta_3}Y_3) \,d\theta. \end{aligned} \tag{SP23} \]

Every denominator, radius, orientation and input magnitude in this map is explicit.

The construction is smooth in every noncollinear closed triangle with \(r_1\ne r_2\): the cross product, positive square root, divisions by \(H,r_j,c_\sigma\), and finite exponential sum are smooth there. Differentiating these explicit formulas gives every local derivative. On a compact subset the same formulas bound all derivatives because their denominators have positive minima. SP16 provides a definite such lower bound near the original base frequencies.

For real \(\eta\), \(U_{-\sigma}=\overline{U_\sigma}\) and \(c_{-\sigma}=\overline{c_\sigma}\). Pairing the opposite signs in SP22 shows that \(F_\eta(\theta)\) lies in the real tensor product for real \(\theta\). Also

\[ \|v_{j,\sigma_j}\|=\frac1{\sqrt2},\quad \|U_\sigma\|=\frac1{2\sqrt2},\quad \|F_\eta(\theta)\|\leq \frac{2\sqrt2}{(2\pi)^3\min_\sigma|c_\sigma|}. \tag{SP24} \]

The first identity follows by the Hermitian norm of \(n-i\sigma_jw_j\); the second uses the tensor norm, and the third sums all eight original coefficients. In particular the integral in SP23 is a genuine finite-dimensional Bochner integral.

SP5. Prove the exact operator norms and the space defined by degeneracy

Keep the full Lebesgue \(L^2\) norm on the angle torus. Define the actual linear observation map

\[ T_\eta:E\longrightarrow L^2([0,2\pi]^3;\mathbb C), \qquad (T_\eta X)(\theta)=\sum_\sigma c_\sigma\ell_\sigma(X)e^{i\sigma\cdot\theta}. \tag{SP25} \]

For simple tensors this is exactly the rotated original trilinear expression SP21. Its range consists of a subspace of the eight indicated Fourier characters. The vectors \(U_\sigma\) are Hermitian orthogonal: in any coordinate where two signs differ, the corresponding inner product is \((n+i\sigma_jw_j)\cdot(n+i\sigma_jw_j)/4=0\). Each tensor has squared norm \(1/8\), hence

\[ \|X\|^2=\frac18\sum_\sigma|\ell_\sigma(X)|^2,\qquad \|T_\eta X\|_{L^2}^2=(2\pi)^3 \sum_\sigma|c_\sigma|^2|\ell_\sigma(X)|^2. \tag{SP26} \]

Orthogonality of the original angle characters proves the second identity, including its full measure factor. The singular values on the nonzero modes are exactly \(\sqrt8(2\pi)^{3/2}|c_\sigma|\).

For \(r_1\ne r_2\), define

\[ A_\eta g=\int_{[0,2\pi]^3}F_\eta(\theta)g(\theta)d\theta =\sum_\sigma U_\sigma\frac{\widehat g(\sigma)}{c_\sigma}, \quad \widehat g(\sigma)=\frac1{(2\pi)^3} \int_{[0,2\pi]^3}g(\theta)e^{-i\sigma\cdot\theta}d\theta. \tag{SP27} \]

This is bounded on the full angle \(L^2\) space: the eight coefficient functionals are bounded by Cauchy–Schwarz. Equations SP20 and SP25 give \(A_\eta T_\eta=I_E\), and \(T_\eta A_\eta\) is the orthogonal projection onto the span of the eight angle characters. Bessel's inequality, obtained directly by subtracting the finite orthogonal projection, and SP26 prove the exact norms

\[ \|T_\eta\|=\sqrt8(2\pi)^{3/2}\max_\sigma|c_\sigma|, \qquad \|A_\eta\|=\frac1{\sqrt8(2\pi)^{3/2}\min_\sigma|c_\sigma|}. \tag{SP28} \]

Equality in each bound is attained by an individual maximizing or minimizing mode. These are the full physical coefficient maps and their exact norms, not bounds on a replacement tensor.

At an original noncollinear triangle with \(r_1=r_2\), define instead

\[ \begin{gathered} E_{\rm lost}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C} \{U_\sigma:\sigma_1=\sigma_2\},\qquad E_{\rm seen}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C} \{U_\sigma:\sigma_1\ne\sigma_2\},\\ \mathcal R_{\rm seen}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C} \{e^{i\sigma\cdot\theta}:\sigma_1\ne\sigma_2\}. \end{gathered} \tag{SP29} \]

Each has complex dimension four. The exact SP10 zeros and dual expansion SP20 imply

\[ \ker T_\eta=E_{\rm lost},\qquad \operatorname{ran}T_\eta=\mathcal R_{\rm seen},\qquad E=E_{\rm lost}\oplus E_{\rm seen},\qquad E_{\rm lost}\perp E_{\rm seen}. \tag{SP30} \]

Construct \(F_\eta^+\) and \(A_\eta^+\) by the SP22 and SP27 formulas with only the four signs satisfying \(\sigma_1\ne\sigma_2\). Every retained denominator is nonzero. The same character integrals prove

\[ A_\eta^+T_\eta=P_{\rm seen} =\sum_{\sigma_1\ne\sigma_2}U_\sigma\ell_\sigma,\qquad T_\eta A_\eta^+=P_{\mathcal R_{\rm seen}}. \tag{SP31} \]

Both displayed projections are the actual orthogonal projections for the declared full norms. The restricted map \(T_\eta:E_{\rm seen}\to\mathcal R_{\rm seen}\) is an isomorphism with inverse \(A_\eta^+\), and the exact norm calculation SP28 holds after restricting both extrema to these four signs. This supplies the split exact sequence

\[ 0\longrightarrow E_{\rm lost} \xrightarrow{\ \iota\ }E \xrightarrow{\ T_\eta\ }\mathcal R_{\rm seen} \longrightarrow0, \tag{SP32} \]

whose right splitting is \(A_\eta^+|_{\mathcal R_{\rm seen}}\). Exactness follows from SP30; the composition with the splitting is the identity by SP31. Thus the failure of full recovery defines a precise four-dimensional space, with an exact recovery map for all remaining data.

All these subspaces and maps preserve real tensors. Complex conjugation takes \(U_\sigma\) to \(U_{-\sigma}\), preserving both sign conditions. For the lost space a real basis is given, for each \(\sigma=(+,+,+)\) and \((+,+,-)\), by

\[ U_\sigma+U_{-\sigma},\qquad \frac{U_\sigma-U_{-\sigma}}{i}. \tag{SP33} \]

Linear independence follows by the four distinct complex basis coefficients in SP20; these four real tensors span the conjugation-fixed part. The same construction from \((+,-,+)\) and \((+,-,-)\) gives a real basis of the seen space. Hence the original real observation also has rank four and a four-dimensional kernel. No original complex or real data have been discarded by calling this loss a notation issue.

RF1. Close the original frequencies by explicit rotations

Retain the original base vectors \(\xi_1^0=(0,1,0)\), \(\xi_2^0=(-1,-1,0)\), \(\xi_3^0=(1,0,0)\). For the current construction take the original parameter and input region

\[ 0<\epsilon_0\leq\frac1{1024},\qquad h=2\epsilon_0^3,\qquad |\xi_j-\xi_j^0|<h,\qquad r_j=|\xi_j|. \tag{RF1} \]

The vectors \(\xi_j\) need not close. Their original radii are never changed. Define

\[ \begin{gathered} b=\frac{r_2^2-r_1^2-r_3^2}{2r_1},\qquad a=\sqrt{r_3^2-b^2},\\ \eta_1=(0,r_1,0),\quad \eta_2=(-a,-r_1-b,0),\quad \eta_3=(a,b,0). \end{gathered} \tag{RF2} \]

The positive square root is well-defined. To see this and retain definite bounds, first \(|r_j-r_j^0|\leq h\), where \((r_1^0,r_2^0,r_3^0)=(1,\sqrt2,1)\). Since \(h<1/128\),

\[ \begin{gathered} |r_2^2-r_1^2-r_3^2| \leq(4+2\sqrt2+3h)h<7h,\qquad |b|<4h,\\ |r_3^2-b^2-1|\leq2h+17h^2<3h,\qquad a^2>1-3h>0,\qquad |a-1|<3h. \end{gathered} \tag{RF3} \]

The first bound expands the three square differences from their original values \(2,1,1\); the denominator \(2r_1\geq2(1-h)\) gives the second. The next follows from \(|r_3^2-1|\leq(2+h)h\) and \(b^2\leq16h^2\). The last follows by dividing \(|a^2-1|\) by \(a+1>1\). The definitions now give exactly

\[ \begin{gathered} \eta_1+\eta_2+\eta_3=0,\qquad|\eta_j|=r_j,\qquad H=|\eta_1\times\eta_2|=r_1a>0,\\ |\eta_1-\xi_1^0|\leq h,\qquad |\eta_3-\xi_3^0|<5h,\qquad |\eta_2-\xi_2^0|<6h. \end{gathered} \tag{RF4} \]

For \(\eta_2\), its squared norm is \(a^2+(r_1+b)^2=r_3^2+r_1^2+2r_1b=r_2^2\). Its displacement has components of absolute value at most \(3h,5h\), hence is less than \(\sqrt{34}h<6h\). Thus SP14–SP16 apply with their actual neighborhood radius \(6h=12\epsilon_0^3<1/16\). In particular the eight original spin coefficients are nonzero with the complete bound supplied there.

There is also an explicit original-field rotation. Write

\[ u_j=\frac{\xi_j}{r_j},\quad v_j=\frac{\eta_j}{r_j},\quad k_j=u_j\times v_j,\quad d_j=u_j\cdot v_j,\qquad Q_j=I+[k_j]_\times+\frac{[k_j]_\times^2}{1+d_j}, \tag{RF5} \]

where \([k]_\times x=k\times x\). The auxiliary unit directions retain their full original radii in this formula. By RF4, \(|u_j-v_j|\leq7h/(1-h)<8h\), so \(d_j=1-|u_j-v_j|^2/2>1-32h^2>0\). Every denominator in RF5 is positive. To verify the map exactly, put \(K=[k_j]_\times\). Then \(K^{\mathsf T}=-K\), \(K^2=k_jk_j^{\mathsf T}-|k_j|^2I\), \(|k_j|^2=1-d_j^2\), and \(K^3=-|k_j|^2K\). Put \(t=(1+d_j)^{-1}\). Expanding RF5 and its transpose gives

\[ Q_j^{\mathsf T}Q_j =I+(2t-1)K^2+t^2K^4 =I+\left[\frac{2}{1+d_j}-1 -\frac{1-d_j^2}{(1+d_j)^2}\right]K^2=I. \]

Also

\[ Ku_j=v_j-d_ju_j,\qquad K^2u_j=-(1-d_j^2)u_j, \qquad Q_ju_j=v_j. \]

The determinant is one: continuously move \(v_j\) to \(u_j\) along the shorter spherical arc, on which \(1+d_j>0\); RF5 remains orthogonal and reaches \(I\), and an orthogonal determinant cannot change between \(1\) and \(-1\) continuously. Consequently

\[ Q_j\in SO(3),\qquad Q_j\xi_j=\eta_j. \tag{RF6} \]

All these maps are smooth on the entire open region RF1 by their explicit formulas.

RF2. Identify the entire six-dimensional fiber

For the original fixed \(\xi=(\xi_1,\xi_2,\xi_3)\), define

\[ \begin{gathered} G_\xi:SO(3)^3\longrightarrow\mathbb R^3,\qquad G_\xi(R)=R_1\xi_1+R_2\xi_2+R_3\xi_3,\\ \Sigma_\xi=G_\xi^{-1}(0). \end{gathered} \tag{RF7} \]

Its full parametrization is

\[ \Phi_\xi(S,\theta)= \bigl(SR_{\eta_1}^{\theta_1}Q_1,\, SR_{\eta_2}^{\theta_2}Q_2,\, SR_{\eta_3}^{\theta_3}Q_3\bigr), \qquad (S,\theta)\in SO(3)\times(\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z)^3. \tag{RF8} \]

Each image lies in the fiber, because its three frequency images are \(S\eta_j\), whose sum is zero.

Conversely let \(R\in\Sigma_\xi\) and \(y_j=R_j\xi_j\). The original lengths are \(r_j\), the three vectors close, and

\[ y_1\cdot y_2=\eta_1\cdot\eta_2 =\frac{r_3^2-r_1^2-r_2^2}{2}. \tag{RF9} \]

Their Gram matrices agree, and their cross products are nonzero by RF4 and RF9. There is a unique \(S\in SO(3)\) sending all \(\eta_j\) to \(y_j\). Explicitly use the two oriented orthonormal frames whose first vectors are respectively \(\eta_1/r_1\) and \(y_1/r_1\), whose second vectors are the components of \(\eta_2\) and \(y_2\) perpendicular to those first vectors divided by their common positive length, and whose third vectors are the cross products of the first two. If these frame matrices are \(E\) and \(Y\), then \(S=YE^{\mathsf T}\). It sends the first two original vectors correctly by RF9, and the third by closure. Uniqueness follows because a proper rotation agreeing on two independent vectors also agrees on their cross product.

The map \(S^{-1}R_jQ_j^{-1}\) fixes \(\eta_j\); its restriction to the oriented perpendicular plane is therefore a unique planar rotation. Its angle determines \(\theta_j\) modulo \(2\pi\), as in SP3. This proves bijectivity of RF8. The frame formulas and the sine and cosine entries of these planar rotations give a smooth inverse valued in \(SO(3)\times(\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z)^3\); no global real angle branch is needed. The differential metric below is nonsingular, proving that this is a diffeomorphism of the entire fiber, not only a parametrized subset. In particular every frequency-closing rotation is included.

RF3. Specify the original group metric and Haar volume

On each \(SO(3)\), identify a tangent vector by its spatial angular velocity \(\alpha\in\mathbb R^3\): \(\dot R=[\alpha]_\times R\). The metric is \(|\alpha|^2\); use its product metric on \(SO(3)^3\). Both left and right translations preserve this metric, since their angular maps are rotations. Its Riemannian volume \(dV\) is thus invariant. Its exact total volume is

\[ \operatorname{vol}(SO(3))=8\pi^2,\qquad dR=\frac{dV(R)}{8\pi^2}, \tag{RF10} \]

where \(dR\) denotes probability Haar measure. For completeness use Euler coordinates \(R=R_{e_3}^{\alpha}R_{e_2}^{\beta}R_{e_3}^{\gamma}\), with \(0\leq\alpha,\gamma<2\pi\) and \(0<\beta<\pi\). At a point of the fiber put \(y_j=R_j\xi_j\). The full derivative and its Euclidean adjoint satisfy

\[ \begin{gathered} DG_\xi(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3) =\sum_{j=1}^3\alpha_j\times y_j,\\ DG_\xi DG_\xi^* =\sum_{j=1}^3(r_j^2I-y_jy_j^{\mathsf T}),\qquad J_\xi(R)=\sqrt{\det(DG_\xi DG_\xi^*)}. \end{gathered} \tag{RF11} \]

Exercise 1. Compute the original closing measure and its complete second moments

For a noncollinear closed reference triangle with original lengths \(r_j\), use the exact rotation-fiber measure RF17. Determine its total mass and the first and second moments of the actual rotated frequencies \(y_j=R_j\xi_j\).

Solution. Keep \(r_j>0\), the original reference vectors \(\eta_j\), and the complete delta measure

\[ d\mathcal M_\xi(R)= \delta(R_1\xi_1+R_2\xi_2+R_3\xi_3)\,dR_1dR_2dR_3, \qquad M_\xi=\frac1{8\pi r_1r_2r_3}. \tag{EX1} \]

The total mass is RF18. Retain both this original measure and the associated probability measure \(d\mathbb P_\xi=d\mathcal M_\xi/M_\xi\). Under the full parametrization RF8, RF17 gives exactly

\[ d\mathbb P_\xi=dS\, \frac{d\theta_1d\theta_2d\theta_3}{(2\pi)^3}, \qquad y_j=S\eta_j. \tag{EX2} \]

Thus the original frequencies depend only on the common Haar rotation; the three original spin angles remain independent uniform angles in this representation.

For any fixed vector \(a\), the vector \(\int Sa\,dS\) is fixed by every rotation, by left Haar invariance. A vector fixed by rotations by \(\pi\) around two perpendicular coordinate axes is zero. Therefore \(\mathbb E_\xi y_j=0\). For any original vectors \(a,b\), the matrix \(T=\int(Sa)(Sb)^{\mathsf T}dS\) satisfies \(QTQ^{\mathsf T}=T\) for every proper rotation \(Q\). The same three coordinate half-turns force every off-diagonal entry to vanish, and quarter-turns between coordinate axes force the diagonal entries to agree. Thus \(T=cI\), and its retained trace is \(3c=a\cdot b\). Consequently

\[ \begin{gathered} \int y_j\,d\mathcal M_\xi=0,\qquad \int y_jy_k^{\mathsf T}\,d\mathcal M_\xi =\frac{\eta_j\cdot\eta_k}{24\pi r_1r_2r_3}I,\\ \eta_j\cdot\eta_k= \begin{cases} r_j^2,&j=k,\\ (r_\ell^2-r_j^2-r_k^2)/2,&\{j,k,\ell\}=\{1,2,3\}. \end{cases} \end{gathered} \tag{EX3} \]

The original mass factor is present in every unscaled integral. For arbitrary real test vectors \(a,b\), this also proves

\[ \mathbb E_\xi[(a\cdot y_j)(b\cdot y_k)] =\frac{(a\cdot b)(\eta_j\cdot\eta_k)}3. \tag{EX4} \]

Summing EX3 over \(k\) gives zero because \(\sum_k\eta_k=0\), exactly as required by the pointwise original constraint \(\sum_k y_k=0\). These identities concern the constructed closing measure; no statistical law for a fluid trajectory is being assumed.

Exercise 2. Find the smallest possible full tensor recovery kernel

Among all square-integrable tensor kernels \(K(\theta)\) that recover every original \(X\in E\) by integrating \(K(\theta)(T_\eta X)(\theta)\) against the full Lebesgue angle measure, prove that SP22 has the least squared norm. Compute the exact minimum at the original source base point.

Solution. Work first at a noncollinear original triangle with \(r_1\ne r_2\). The exact basis and observation formula SP20 and SP25 give

\[ T_\eta U_\sigma=c_\sigma e^{i\sigma\cdot\theta}. \]

Hence the recovery constraint is equivalent to all eight exact conditions

\[ \int_{[0,2\pi]^3}K(\theta)e^{i\sigma\cdot\theta}\,d\theta =\frac{U_\sigma}{c_\sigma}. \tag{EX5} \]

Necessity follows by testing each \(U_\sigma\); sufficiency follows by its full basis expansion. The orthogonal projection in tensor-valued \(L^2\) onto these eight characters is therefore forced to be exactly \(F_\eta\) from SP22. If \(K=F_\eta+H\), all eight corresponding Fourier coefficients of \(H\) vanish, so orthogonality gives

\[ \begin{gathered} \int\|K(\theta)\|^2d\theta =\int\|F_\eta(\theta)\|^2d\theta+\int\|H(\theta)\|^2d\theta,\\ \min_K\int\|K(\theta)\|^2d\theta =\frac1{8(2\pi)^3}\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^3}\frac1{|c_\sigma|^2}. \end{gathered} \tag{EX6} \]

This proof uses only the finite orthogonal projection: no completeness assertion about an infinite Fourier series is needed. Equality holds precisely when \(H=0\) almost everywhere. Thus the minimum is unique in \(L^2\), and its smooth real representative is the explicit SP22 kernel.

At the original source triangle, the eight real values of \(8ic_\sigma\) consist of four numbers of absolute value one, two of absolute value \(\sqrt2-1\), and two of absolute value \(\sqrt2+1\). Retaining every contribution,

\[ \begin{gathered} \sum_\sigma|c_\sigma|^{-2} =64\left[4+\frac2{(\sqrt2-1)^2}+\frac2{(\sqrt2+1)^2}\right] =1024,\\ \int_{[0,2\pi]^3}\|F_{\eta^0}(\theta)\|^2d\theta =\frac{1024}{8(2\pi)^3}=\frac{16}{\pi^3}. \end{gathered} \tag{EX7} \]

The torus-volume and original tensor norms have both been retained. When \(r_1=r_2\), no kernel can recover the lost subspace, because \(T_\eta\) is zero on it. For recovery of \(P_{\rm seen}X\), the same proof gives the unique minimum \(F_\eta^+\) using only the four signs \(\sigma_1\ne\sigma_2\), and the exact EX6 sum restricted to those signs. This proves the optimal recoverable map of the precise obstruction space.

Exercise 3. Differentiate the exact closing retraction

Compute the derivative of the actual retraction AF7 at a point of the original closing fiber. Include perturbations of the original frequencies, not only perturbations of the rotations.

Solution. Fix \(R\in\Sigma_\xi\), put \(y_j=R_j\xi_j\), and retain

\[ \mathcal D(v_1,v_2,v_3)=\sum_jv_j\times y_j,\qquad K=\mathcal D\mathcal D^*=\sum_j(r_j^2I-y_jy_j^{\mathsf T}). \tag{EX8} \]

The \(v_j\) are spatial angular velocities of the initial rotations. Let \(h_j\) be arbitrary original frequency variations, and define the full varying defect

\[ g=\mathcal Dv+\sum_jR_jh_j. \tag{EX9} \]

At the base point the actual auxiliary flow AF5 is constant, because its initial defect is zero. Differentiating its vector field, all derivatives of \(y_j\) and \(K^{-1}\) multiply that zero defect. The only nonzero first variation of the angular velocity is therefore \(-y_j\times K^{-1}g\), independent of the auxiliary time. Integrating from zero to one gives the entire derivative

\[ D\Pi_\xi(R)[v,h]_j =v_j-y_j\times K^{-1} \left(\mathcal Dv+\sum_kR_kh_k\right). \tag{EX10} \]

The output frequencies remain the original \(\xi+h\); the associated derivative in that component is \(h\). Applying the derivative of the full closing constraint to this pair gives

\[ \mathcal D\bigl(D\Pi[v,h]\bigr)+\sum_jR_jh_j =g-\mathcal D\mathcal D^*K^{-1}g=0. \tag{EX11} \]

Thus it is an actual tangent map of the complete fiber family.

For fixed frequencies \(h=0\), the derivative is the orthogonal projection

\[ D\Pi_\xi(R)=I-\mathcal D^*K^{-1}\mathcal D \tag{EX12} \]

in the declared nine-dimensional product angular metric. Self-adjointness follows from the symmetric positive \(K^{-1}\), and squaring the complementary term gives \(\mathcal D^*K^{-1}(\mathcal D\mathcal D^*)K^{-1}\mathcal D =\mathcal D^*K^{-1}\mathcal D\). Its range is exactly \(\ker\mathcal D=T_R\Sigma_\xi\), of dimension six; its kernel is \(\operatorname{ran}\mathcal D^*\), of dimension three. The projection norm is one. Its exact block entries and full correction cost are

\[ \begin{gathered} (D\Pi_\xi)_{jk} =\delta_{jk}I+[y_j]_\times K^{-1}[y_k]_\times,\\ \|v-D\Pi_\xi v\|^2 =(\mathcal Dv)^{\mathsf T}K^{-1}(\mathcal Dv). \end{gathered} \tag{EX13} \]

The block sign follows from \(\mathcal D_k=-[y_k]_\times\), while the adjoint block is \([y_j]_\times\). The norm formula follows by inserting \(\mathcal D\mathcal D^*=K\). These are derivatives of the actual nonlinear AF5 flow. They do not replace that flow by its tangent projection away from the fiber.

Exercise 4. Determine the exact allowed weights of the original pump scales

For any actual averaged Euler representation with source symbols \(m_{j,\omega}\), rotations \(R_{j,\omega}\) and original compact positive dilations \(\lambda_{j,\omega}\), its transformed inputs at a common original scale \(\lambda>0\) satisfy

Exercise 5. Prove stability of the actual original-viscosity flows under scale weights

For the same original pump, quantify the dependence of its actual local flow on the bounded scale sequence and on its initial datum, preserving the full original viscosity.

Solution. Let \(K_*>0\) be the full \(H^{5/2}\times H^{5/2}\to H^{5/2}\) coefficient of the three-term original pump from lesson38 CC7. For bounded real sequences \(w,\widetilde w\), its full series estimate gives

\[ \begin{gathered} \|C_{\rm pump}^{w}(u,v)\|_{H^{5/2}} \leq K_*\|w\|_\infty\|u\|_{H^{5/2}}\|v\|_{H^{5/2}},\\ \|(C_{\rm pump}^{w}-C_{\rm pump}^{\widetilde w})(u,v)\|_{H^{5/2}} \leq K_*\|w-\widetilde w\|_\infty \|u\|_{H^{5/2}}\|v\|_{H^{5/2}}. \end{gathered} \tag{EX20} \]

No original field norm or annular constant in \(K_*\) has been replaced. Fix the original \(\nu\geq0\), data \(u_0,v_0\), and retain

\[ M=\max(\|u_0\|_{H^{5/2}},\|v_0\|_{H^{5/2}}),\qquad L=\max(\|w\|_\infty,\|\widetilde w\|_\infty). \]

If \(M,L>0\), the actual heat Picard construction CC11 with EX20 gives both unique flows on

\[ T=(8K_*LM)^{-1},\qquad \|u(t)\|_{H^{5/2}},\|v(t)\|_{H^{5/2}}\leq2M. \tag{EX21} \]

Both equations keep the same original heat semigroup \(e^{\nu t\Delta}\). Subtract their exact mild formulas, decompose the nonlinear difference into the two \(w\)-bilinear differences and the coefficient difference applied to \(v,v\), and use its contraction norm. With

\[ E(t)=\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{H^{5/2}},\quad \delta_0=\|u_0-v_0\|_{H^{5/2}},\quad \delta_w=\|w-\widetilde w\|_\infty, \]

the full inequality is

\[ E(t)\leq\delta_0+ 4K_*LM\int_0^t E(r)\,dr+4K_*M^2\delta_w\,t. \tag{EX22} \]

To retain the exact forcing contribution, put \(a=4K_*LM\) and \(b=4K_*M^2\delta_w\). The absolutely continuous majorant \(F(t)=\delta_0+a\int_0^tE(r)\,dr+bt\) satisfies \(E\leq F\), \(F(0)=\delta_0\), and \(F'\leq aF+b\). Multiplication by \(e^{-at}\) and direct integration prove

\[ E(t)\leq \delta_0e^{4K_*LMt} +\frac{M\delta_w}{L}\bigl(e^{4K_*LMt}-1\bigr), \qquad 0\leq t\leq T. \tag{EX23} \]

If \(M=0\), both actual flows are zero. If \(L=0\), both weights vanish and both flows are the original heat evolutions, so \(E(t)\leq\delta_0\) for every \(t\geq0\); no division by zero is used. For positive original viscosity, every individual bounded-weight pump also has the unchanged full energy identity by its exact cancellation and CC12. EX23 proves local stability of these actual trajectories; it asserts no new global regularity or finite-time cascade.
