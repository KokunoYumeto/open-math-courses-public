CA2. Complete finite-strip remainder

Put \(R=2^{28}\) and retain the original finite square \(D_R=[1/2,R]\times[0,R]\). CB17 proves, including its exact square identity,

\[ \partial_rK_1(r,z)=\frac{z(z^2-3r^2)}{(r^2+z^2)^3},\qquad |\partial_rK_1(r,z)|\leq(r^2+z^2)^{-3/2}. \tag{CA3} \]

For \(x\geq R\), \(z\geq0\), set \(\rho=\sqrt{x^2+z^2}\). Every \((r,z)\) with \(r\in[x-1/2,x+1/2]\) has norm at least \(\rho-1/2\). The original difference integrates this derivative on that full length-one interval. On its support, \(T>0\), hence

\[ |\Delta(x,z)|\leq \left(\frac R{R-1/2}\right)^3\rho^{-3},\qquad \sqrt{x-T(x,z)}\leq\sqrt\rho,\qquad \rho\geq x. \tag{CA4} \]

For each finite \(0<b\leq R\), the part of the original integral outside \([1/2,R]\times[0,b]\) therefore satisfies

\[ \begin{aligned} 0\leq E_R(b) &:=\frac4\pi\int_0^b\!\int_{\max(R,f(z))}^{\infty} [-\Delta(x,z)]\sqrt{x-T(x,z)}\,dx\,dz\\ &\leq\frac4\pi\left(\frac R{R-1/2}\right)^3 \int_0^b\!\int_R^\infty x^{-5/2}\,dx\,dz\\ &=\frac{8b}{3\pi R^{3/2}} \left(\frac R{R-1/2}\right)^3. \end{aligned} \tag{CA5} \]

The enlargement only adds nonnegative terms. The inner integral is \(2/(3R^{3/2})\), as follows by differentiating \(-2x^{-3/2}/3\) and taking its limit at infinity. Thus no horizontal tail or physical factor has been omitted. At \(b=0\), the integral and the bound are zero. All 3,895 finite source cutoffs are below \(R\).

At the terminal cutoff \(b=\infty\), the complement of \(D_R\) also includes large \(z\). Use the complete quadrant bound from CB22:

\[ E_R(\infty)\leq 4R^{-1/2}\left(\frac R{R-1/2}\right)^3 =\frac1{4096}\left(\frac{2^{29}}{2^{29}-1}\right)^3. \tag{CA6} \]

This is the integral of the same majorant in polar coordinates over the entire first-quadrant exterior \(\rho\geq R\), including the Jacobian \(\rho\), angular length \(\pi/2\), radial integral \(2R^{-1/2}\), and factor \(4/\pi\).

AN1. Retain the original weight and both axis values

Let \(D\subset\mathbb R^2\), let \(f:D\to\mathbb R\), and let \(q_1,q_2,q_3>0\). For every nonzero difference \(h=(h_1,h_2)\), retain the source weight in its complete form:

\[ \begin{aligned} g_0(h)&=\left(\sqrt{|h_1|+q_1|h_2|} +q_3\sqrt{|h_2|+q_2|h_1|}\right)^{-1},\\ g(h)&=g_0(h)g_0(1,0)^{-1},\\ g_0(1,0)^{-1}&=1+q_3\sqrt{q_2},\\ g(1,0)&=1,\qquad g(0,1)=\frac{1+q_3\sqrt{q_2}}{\sqrt{q_1}+q_3},\\ r&=\frac{g(1,0)}{g(0,1)} =\frac{\sqrt{q_1}+q_3}{1+q_3\sqrt{q_2}}. \end{aligned}\tag{AN1} \]

Each identity follows by substituting the indicated axis vector into the two square roots; both terms and the full factor \(g_0(1,0)^{-1}\) remain. For every nonzero real \(s\), direct substitution also gives

\[ g(s,0)=|s|^{-1/2},\qquad g(0,s)=r^{-1}|s|^{-1/2}. \tag{AN2} \]

Define the seminorms by the actual pairs in \(D\):

\[ \begin{aligned} E_g(f;D)&=\sup_{p,q\in D,\ p\ne q}g(p-q)|f(p)-f(q)|,\\ H_x(f;D)&=\sup_{p,q\in D,\ p_2=q_2,\ p_1\ne q_1} \frac{|f(p)-f(q)|}{|p_1-q_1|^{1/2}},\\ H_y(f;D)&=\sup_{p,q\in D,\ p_1=q_1,\ p_2\ne q_2} \frac{|f(p)-f(q)|}{|p_2-q_2|^{1/2}},\\ N_\tau(f;D)&=\max\{H_x(f;D),\tau^{-1}H_y(f;D)\},\qquad \tau>0. \end{aligned}\tag{AN3} \]

In pair coordinates, \(q\) denotes a point; the three indexed \(q_i\) are the weight parameters. Empty directional suprema are zero. These definitions allow infinite values. All finite bounds below concern functions with finite \(E_g\). No rectangle completion or extension of \(D\) is used.

Taking only axis pairs in the full supremum and applying (AN2) proves

\[ H_x(f;D)\le E_g(f;D),\qquad H_y(f;D)\le rE_g(f;D),\qquad N_\tau(f;D)\le\max\{1,r/\tau\}E_g(f;D). \tag{AN4} \]

Thus the identity on functions, from the space with seminorm \(E_g\) to the space with seminorm \(N_\tau\), is a linear map bounded by the displayed factor. It descends to the quotient by constant functions when \(D\) is nonempty. In particular the exact axis match is \(\tau=r\); every \(\tau\ge r\) gives the upper bound \(N_\tau\le E_g\).

AN2. The map has the exact stated norm

On \(D=\mathbb R\times[0,\infty)\), consider the two functions

\[ f_x(p)=\sqrt{\max(p_1,0)},\qquad f_y(p)=r\sqrt{p_2}. \tag{AN5} \]

For nonnegative \(a,b\), suppose first that \(a\ge b\). The identity \((\sqrt a-\sqrt b)^2=a+b-2\sqrt{ab}\le a-b\) follows from \(b\le\sqrt{ab}\). Interchanging \(a,b\) proves \(|\sqrt a-\sqrt b|\le\sqrt{|a-b|}\). The map \(t\mapsto\max(t,0)\) satisfies \(|\max(s,0)-\max(t,0)|\le|s-t|\): this follows directly when both arguments have the same sign, and when \(s\ge0\ge t\) it reads \(s\le s-t\). Consequently the two increments in (AN5) are bounded by \(\sqrt{|h_1|}\) and \(r\sqrt{|h_2|}\), respectively.

The original denominator has both lower bounds

\[ \begin{aligned} \sqrt{|h_1|+q_1|h_2|}+q_3\sqrt{|h_2|+q_2|h_1|} &\ge(1+q_3\sqrt{q_2})\sqrt{|h_1|},\\ \sqrt{|h_1|+q_1|h_2|}+q_3\sqrt{|h_2|+q_2|h_1|} &\ge(\sqrt{q_1}+q_3)\sqrt{|h_2|}. \end{aligned}\tag{AN6} \]

Multiplying the increment bounds by the full weight in (AN1) proves \(E_g(f_x;D)\le1\) and \(E_g(f_y;D)\le1\). For \(f_x\), the pair \((0,1),(1,1)\) attains one. For \(f_y\), the pair \((0,0),(0,1)\) attains one. The directional values are therefore

\[ \begin{aligned} E_g(f_x;D)&=1,& H_x(f_x;D)&=1,& H_y(f_x;D)&=0,\\ E_g(f_y;D)&=1,& H_x(f_y;D)&=0,& H_y(f_y;D)&=r. \end{aligned}\tag{AN7} \]

The operator norm in (AN4) is exactly \(\max\{1,r/\tau\}\). This proves sharpness for every positive parameter triple and every \(\tau>0\), without imposing a special value of \(r\). These sharp bounds also hold in the bounded-function class: replace the two functions in (AN5) by \(\min\{f_x,1\}\) and \(\min\{f_y,r\}\). Truncation above at a constant cannot increase an absolute difference, as follows by considering the cases when neither, one, or both values exceed the constant. The displayed attaining pairs are unchanged. On the open upper half-plane or the open first quadrant, the same suprema follow by translating horizontal pairs into the domain and taking their smaller horizontal coordinate to zero, and taking vertical pairs with lower height tending to zero. In particular a ratio smaller than \(r\) incurs the factor \(r/\tau\); this is not merely a possible loss in the proof.

PV1. A quadrant rectangle and its two strip corrections

\[ K_2(u,v)=\frac{u^2-v^2}{2(u^2+v^2)^2},\qquad Q=[0,b_1]\times[0,b_2],\qquad b_1,b_2>0. \tag{PV1} \]
