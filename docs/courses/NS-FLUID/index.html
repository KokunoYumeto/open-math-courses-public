Incompressible flow: foundations and forced equations

A course on incompressible fluid equations, from pressure, energy, vorticity and scaling to weak and strong solutions, residual stresses and forced-flow constructions. It supplies the foundations for studying singularity constructions and their exact force and regularity requirements.

Seventeen complete lesson drafts, self-checked by GPT-6 Astra at Ultra. No independent review of this version is claimed. The rest of the course is in preparation.

Eighty-five solved exercises accompany complete proofs. The first lesson uses multivariable calculus and integration by parts; the second also assumes basic Lebesgue integration and supplies its Fourier ingredients. The third constructs periodic weak solutions with the full force and mean, proves compactness and the energy inequality, recovers pressure and proves weak–strong uniqueness and stability under changes of force. The fourth gives the complete whole-space construction, spatial-tail bounds and low-frequency pressure, and extends both domains to sums of the two stated force classes. The fifth constructs H1 strong solutions, retains the full original viscosity, force and periods, and proves stability and maximal continuation. The sixth proves the nonendpoint velocity criterion for energy weak solutions, the small L3 endpoint, force and viscosity comparison, and exact boundary maps with retained wall work and periodic surface forces. The seventh constructs critical mild solutions with their full pressure and force, proves small-data global existence in homogeneous spaces, retains arbitrary periodic means, and tests the initial trace and completeness in an original source. The eighth proves vorticity, directional and middle-eigenvalue continuation, finite-sum criteria and a small endpoint tail. Exact strain reconstruction and visible source corrections retain the periodic constant defect, full pressure pairing and compact transition forces.

The ninth constructs Euler solutions for every real s greater than 5/2, proves continuous dependence in the full top norm, and proves vorticity and strain continuation with the original force and periodic mean. Complete logarithmic estimates, source corrections and a compact multiscale example are included.

The tenth proves actual velocity-tail continuation and two finite weighted heat estimates. Original viscosity, the full heat kernel, additive equation errors, cutoff supports and both time endpoints remain explicit. Exact concentration and Fourier examples test the source steps. The general large L3 endpoint remains in preparation.

The eleventh proves a finite moving-annulus energy estimate with all sphere, harmonic, stretching and force contributions. Complete weighted Sobolev and local curl arguments support a repaired radius average, exact physical zoom and two reproducible figures. Pointwise annular regularity and the general endpoint remain in preparation.

The twelfth proves global nonlinear energy and total speed with the full force and original viscosity. Complete kernel and frequency arguments supply an actual bound for annular boundary displacement. Every tensor interaction is retained.

The thirteenth selects an actual annulus with controlled initial data and both force contributions. Exact heat propagation closes its energy budget; complete local div-curl and Fourier proofs give a local L4-time velocity bound. The localized vorticity equation and force heat potential retain every boundary term.

The fourteenth completes the positive-time annular heat iteration, full Newton velocity and gradient maps, and first vorticity derivative bounds. The actual unforced or compact-force receiving annulus can be chosen with all four maxima arbitrarily small. Every boundary and force term remains.

The fifteenth proves original-viscosity frequency backpropagation with the same amplitude, complete low/high interactions and every exterior kernel term. Exact parameter selection and a convergent lowest-frequency estimate recover the original source hierarchy. Finite backward chains retain their full time and displacement costs.

The sixteenth uses complete total speed and disjoint persistence intervals to prove quantitative backward iteration with the original viscosity, time, frequency and amplitude. It recovers the exact source time separation, improves constants using full intervals, and retains a quantitative set of earlier frequencies. The moving-frame exercise proves the actual Galilean map and its whole-space critical-class restriction.

The seventeenth proves original local vorticity mass, its persistence, and a quantitative regularity interval with velocity, vorticity and first derivatives. The full heat and Newton maps retain every cutoff and boundary term. A definite smaller interval meets the finite Gaussian coefficient bounds and preserves the same mass. Five solved exercises include the exact amplitude-duration optimum, energy thresholds, earlier heat lengths, transported frequency grid and complete off-center Gaussian.

Later lessons develop the remaining regularity criteria, residual stresses, forced singularity constructions and compact viscous flows. The series connects the earlier human results, the Alpöge–Buckmaster programme, OpenAI's proposed construction and subsequent mathematical derivations.
