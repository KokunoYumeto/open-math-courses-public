SQ1. Original kernel, actual data and the full signed identity

Let \(0<a\le b<\infty\). Retain

\[ K_2(u,t)=\frac{u^2-t^2}{2(u^2+t^2)^2},\qquad Q=[0,a]^2,\qquad I_{a,b}=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0} \int_\epsilon^a\int_0^b K_2(u,t)f(u,t)\,dt\,du. \tag{SQ1} \]

It suffices that \(f\) is measurable on \((0,a)\times[0,b]\), that for almost every \(u\) its height fiber has a representative with

\[ |f(u,t)-f(u,v)|\le H_y\sqrt{|t-v|}\quad(0\le t,v\le b), \qquad |f|\le Q_0\quad\hbox{on }Q \tag{SQ2} \]

for finite \(H_y,Q_0\ge0\). Essential bounds suffice with these fiber representatives. No horizontal continuity is needed. The square bound and SQ2 give the global bound \(Q_0+H_y\sqrt b\) on the actual rectangle. Thus every integral with \(\epsilon>0\) in SQ1 is absolutely convergent.

For each fixed \(u>0\), the original partner is the decreasing bijection

\[ T_u:(u,b)\longrightarrow(u^2/b,u),\qquad T_u(t)=\frac{u^2}{t},\qquad T'_u(t)=-\frac{u^2}{t^2}. \tag{SQ3} \]

When \(u=b\) both intervals are empty. Direct differentiation and substitution give

\[ \partial_t\frac{t}{2(u^2+t^2)}=K_2(u,t),\qquad K_2(u,T_u(t))\,\frac{u^2}{t^2}=-K_2(u,t). \tag{SQ4} \]

The first kernel is positive below \(u\) and negative above \(u\). Changing variables in the lower matched interval, with its reversed endpoints, proves the full identity

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_0^b K_2(u,t)f(u,t)\,dt &=\int_u^b K_2(u,t) \bigl(f(u,t)-f(u,u^2/t)\bigr)\,dt\\ &\quad+\int_0^{u^2/b}K_2(u,t)f(u,t)\,dt . \end{aligned}\tag{SQ5} \]

The residual has not been subtracted from the data or discarded.

Define its complete nonnegative mass and the complete paired cost by

\[ \begin{aligned} R(a,b)&=\int_0^a\int_0^{u^2/b}K_2(u,t)\,dt\,du =\frac12\int_0^a\frac{b}{u^2+b^2}\,du =\frac12\arctan(a/b),\\ C(a,b)&=\int_0^a\int_u^b \frac{t^2-u^2}{2(u^2+t^2)^2} \sqrt{t-u^2/t}\,dt\,du . \end{aligned}\tag{SQ6} \]

Here \(0\le t\le u^2/b\le a\) in the residual, so the data bound is exactly \(Q_0\) on the original square. Sections SQ2–SQ3 below prove \(C(a,b)<\infty\). Consequently both right-hand terms of SQ5, integrated over \(0<u<a\), converge absolutely. Taking the cutoff limit proves existence of SQ1 and

\[ |I_{a,b}|\le Q_0\,\frac12\arctan(a/b)+H_y C(a,b). \tag{SQ7} \]

In particular, the absolute value on the left follows from the actual signed identity, not an assumption that the data or the integral is positive.

For \(0<b\le a\), define the source's other strip by cutting off \(t\):

\[ \begin{aligned} J_{a,b}&=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0} \int_\epsilon^b\int_0^a K_2(u,t)f(u,t)\,du\,dt,\\ |J_{a,b}|&\le Q_0\,\frac12\arctan(b/a)+H_x C(b,a), \qquad Q_0=\|f\|_{L^\infty([0,b]^2)} . \end{aligned}\tag{SQ8} \]

Indeed, \(K_2(t,u)=-K_2(u,t)\); applying SQ5 to \(g(t,u)=f(u,t)\) proves \(J_{a,b}=-I_{b,a}(g)\), including the minus sign. Fixed reflections \((u,t)\mapsto(\sigma_1u,\sigma_2t)\), \(\sigma_i\in\{-1,1\}\), preserve the kernel and the positive area measure and replace the data by its reflected fiber. SQ7–SQ8 therefore hold on each reflected rectangle. A union of rectangles requires the sum of their contributions. The square endpoint \(a=b\) is included.

SQ2. Correct the intermediate sign and recover the source bound

The second integrand in source line 2416 uses \(K_2\) on \(u<t<a\), where it is strictly negative. Its integral is \(-C(a,a)\), whereas source line 2420 evaluates it as the positive upper-square constant. The corrected split, obtained directly from SQ6, is

\[ \begin{aligned} C(a,b) &= C(a,a)+\int_0^a\int_a^b |K_2(u,t)|\sqrt{t-u^2/t}\,dt\,du\\ &\le \sqrt a\left(\frac\pi2-\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt2}\right) +\sqrt a\left(f_s(\sqrt{b/a})-f_s(1)\right),\\ f_s(v)&=\int_0^v\frac{w^2}{1+w^4}\,dw . \end{aligned}\tag{SQ9} \]

The square value will also be proved independently below. For the upper rectangle, \(t\ge a\ge u\), so

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_0^a|K_2(u,t)|\,du&=\frac{a}{2(a^2+t^2)},\\ \int_a^b\frac{a\sqrt t}{2(a^2+t^2)}\,dt &=\sqrt a\left(f_s(\sqrt{b/a})-f_s(1)\right). \end{aligned}\tag{SQ10} \]

The first formula follows from the full primitive \(\partial_u[u/(2(u^2+t^2))]=-K_2(u,t)\). The second uses \(t=aw^2\), \(dt=2aw\,dw\), with both original endpoints retained.

This proves exactly the final bounds at source lines 2434–2436 and 2446–2448, with \(C_{K_2,\mathrm{up}}=\pi/2-\pi/(2\sqrt2)\). The erroneous intermediate sign is repairable without increasing either final coefficient. For \(b>a\), the bound on the second line of SQ9 is strict: on the open upper rectangle \(0<u<a<t<b\), the positive kernel multiplies \(\sqrt{t-u^2/t}<\sqrt t\). Thus SQ7 supplies a strictly smaller coefficient of \(H_y\); it is not a claim of the optimal operator norm, or of strict data-bound improvement when \(H_y=0\).

SQ3. Exact finite-strip cost

Set \(r=a/b\in(0,1]\) and introduce the integration variable \(q=u/t\). This changes no physical cutoff. Its map is \(u=qt\), \(du\,dt=t\,dq\,dt\), and its exact domain is

\[ 0<q<1,\qquad 0<t<\min(b,a/q). \tag{SQ11} \]

Consequently Tonelli's theorem, first for the nonnegative integrand, gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \phi(q)&=\frac{(1-q^2)^{3/2}}{(1+q^2)^2},\\ |K_2(qt,t)|\sqrt{t-q^2t}\,t &=\frac{\phi(q)}{2\sqrt t},\\ C(a,b)&=\sqrt b\int_0^r\phi(q)\,dq +\sqrt a\int_r^1\frac{\phi(q)}{\sqrt q}\,dq . \end{aligned}\tag{SQ12} \]

Both integrals are finite: \(0\le\phi\le1\), and \(q^{-1/2}\) is integrable at zero. This also supplies the convergence required in SQ7.

The first integral has the elementary primitive

\[ \begin{aligned} A(r)&=\arcsin r-\frac1{\sqrt2} \arcsin\!\left(\frac{\sqrt2\,r}{\sqrt{1+r^2}}\right) +\frac{r\sqrt{1-r^2}}{1+r^2},\\ A(0)&=0,\qquad A'(r)=\phi(r)\ (0<r<1),\\ A(1)&=\frac\pi2-\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt2}. \end{aligned}\tag{SQ13} \]

Both inverse sines take their values in \([0,\pi/2]\). The derivative follows using \(1-2r^2/(1+r^2)=(1-r^2)/(1+r^2)\); all denominators are positive on \(0<r<1\). The continuous endpoint values follow from the displayed formula. In particular, SQ12 at \(a=b\) proves the square term in SQ9 with its factor \(\sqrt a\).

For an exact evaluation of the second integral, define, for \(p,q>0\) and \(0\le x\le1\),

\[ B_x(p,q)=\int_0^x v^{p-1}(1-v)^{q-1}\,dv,\qquad B(p,q)=B_1(p,q),\qquad x_r=\frac{4r^2}{(1+r^2)^2}. \tag{SQ14} \]

Section SQ4 proves the remaining conversion. Its result is the exact full formula

\[ C(a,b)=\sqrt b\,A(a/b) +\frac{\sqrt a}{2\sqrt2} \left[B(5/4,1/4)-B_{x_{a/b}}(1/4,5/4)\right]. \tag{SQ15} \]

The two original physical square roots and both beta contributions remain. At \(a=b\), symmetry of the beta integral makes the bracket zero. At \(b\to\infty\), \(\sqrt b A(a/b)\le a/\sqrt b\to0\), and \(x_{a/b}\to0\). Thus the limit of SQ15 is the infinite-strip cost proved next. The representation SQ12 directly proves monotonic increase in \(b\), because it equals the original positive integral with increasing upper endpoint.

SQ4. Evaluate the complete infinite-strip coefficient and its tail

For actual \(0\le a<b<\infty\), retain the source quantity

\[ \begin{aligned} I_{K_2,\infty}(a,b) &=\int_a^b\int_u^\infty \frac{t^2-u^2}{2(u^2+t^2)^2} \sqrt{t-u^2/t}\,dt\,du\\ &=(\sqrt b-\sqrt a)\,\kappa,\\ \kappa&=\int_1^\infty \frac{s^2-1}{(s^2+1)^2}\sqrt{s-1/s}\,ds . \end{aligned}\tag{SQ16} \]

The substitution \(t=us\), \(dt=u\,ds\) gives the inner factor \(1/(2\sqrt u)\); integrating it from \(a\) to \(b\) gives precisely \(\sqrt b-\sqrt a\). When \(a=0\), this means the convergent improper integral, not division by zero on the fiber \(u=0\).

Use the strictly increasing bijection \(z=s-1/s\) from \([1,\infty)\) to \([0,\infty)\). Its inverse is \(s=(z+\sqrt{z^2+4})/2\), and

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{s}{s^2+1}&=\frac1{\sqrt{z^2+4}},\\ \frac{s^2-1}{(s^2+1)^2}\,ds &=\frac{z}{(z^2+4)^{3/2}}\,dz,\\ \kappa&=\int_0^\infty\frac{z^{3/2}}{(z^2+4)^{3/2}}\,dz =\frac1{2\sqrt2}B(5/4,1/4). \end{aligned}\tag{SQ17} \]

For the last equality put \(w=z^2/(z^2+4)\). Then \(z=2\sqrt{w/(1-w)}\) and \(dz=w^{-1/2}(1-w)^{-3/2}\,dw\). The complete transformed density is \((2\sqrt2)^{-1}w^{1/4}(1-w)^{-3/4}\,dw\). It is integrable at both endpoints, proving every improper convergence in SQ16–SQ17.

This constant also has the exact special-function value

\[ \kappa=\frac{\Gamma(1/4)^2}{4\sqrt{2\pi}}, \qquad \Gamma(p)=\int_0^\infty v^{p-1}e^{-v}\,dv\quad(p>0). \tag{SQ18} \]

Here is a derivation of the conversion. Apply Tonelli to the positive double integral defining \(\Gamma(p)\Gamma(q)\), then set \(v=\rho x,\ w=\rho(1-x)\), with Jacobian \(\rho\), to obtain \(\Gamma(p)\Gamma(q)=\Gamma(p+q)B(p,q)\). Integration by parts, whose endpoint terms vanish for \(p>0\), gives \(\Gamma(p+1)=p\Gamma(p)\). Finally, \(\Gamma(1/2)=2\int_0^\infty e^{-t^2}\,dt=\sqrt\pi\): square the integral over the real line and use polar coordinates, whose integral is \(2\pi\int_0^\infty r e^{-r^2}\,dr=\pi\). Substitution of \(p=5/4,q=1/4\) in SQ17 proves SQ18 with every factor.

The entire tail beyond an actual \(R\ge1\) is

\[ \begin{aligned} E(R)&=\int_R^\infty \frac{s^2-1}{(s^2+1)^2}\sqrt{s-1/s}\,ds\\ &=\frac1{2\sqrt2} B_{\,4R^2/(R^2+1)^2}(1/4,5/4) \le \frac2{\sqrt R}. \end{aligned}\tag{SQ19} \]

To prove the equality, use SQ17 and then \(v=4/(z^2+4)\), whose decreasing endpoints are \(4R^2/(R^2+1)^2\) and zero. The full density is \(-(2\sqrt2)^{-1}v^{-3/4}(1-v)^{1/4}\,dv\). For the inequality, the original integrand is bounded by \(s^{-3/2}\): \((s^2-1)/(s^2+1)^2\le s^{-2}\) and \(\sqrt{s-1/s}\le\sqrt s\). Integrating retains the source tail \(2R^{-1/2}\).

Putting \(q=1/s\) gives \(\int_0^r\phi(q)/\sqrt q\,dq=E(1/r)\) and \(\int_0^1\phi(q)/\sqrt q\,dq=\kappa\). Subtracting these two finite integrals proves SQ15.

SQ5. Actual data on the infinite strip

Suppose \(f\) has the fiber property SQ2 on \(0<u<b,\ 0\le t<\infty\), with \(|f(u,0)|\le Q_0\). For each \(\epsilon>0\), the integral over \(\epsilon<u<b,\ 0<t<\infty\) is absolutely convergent. Indeed \(|f(u,t)|\le Q_0+H_y\sqrt t\), and the full kernel is bounded away from its origin and has an integrable height tail after either weight.

For each \(u>0\), SQ3 now maps \((u,\infty)\) bijectively onto \((0,u)\). The same complete mass identity yields

\[ \begin{aligned} I_{b,\infty}(f) &=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0} \int_\epsilon^b\int_0^\infty K_2(u,t)f(u,t)\,dt\,du\\ &=\int_0^b\int_u^\infty K_2(u,t) \bigl(f(u,t)-f(u,u^2/t)\bigr)\,dt\,du,\\ |I_{b,\infty}(f)-I_{a,\infty}(f)| &\le(\sqrt b-\sqrt a)\kappa H_y \qquad(0\le a\le b),\qquad I_{0,\infty}=0 . \end{aligned}\tag{SQ20} \]

The paired integral is absolutely convergent by SQ16. Thus taking the horizontal cutoff limit is justified without assuming an unlocalized operator, horizontal continuity, or a limit of the trace at the origin. The two strip integrals on the last line use the same data on their nested domains; subtracting their convergent paired representations leaves exactly \(a<u<b\).

There is also direct control of the infinite height limit, retaining the entire trace contribution. For \(L\ge b>0\), let \(I_{b,L}\) mean SQ1 with first width \(b\) and second width \(L\). The original negative tail gives

\[ \begin{aligned} I_{b,\infty}-I_{b,L} &=\int_0^b\int_L^\infty K_2(u,t) \bigl(f(u,0)+f(u,t)-f(u,0)\bigr)\,dt\,du,\\ |I_{b,\infty}-I_{b,L}| &\le \frac{Q_0}{2}\arctan(b/L) +H_y\sqrt b\left(f_s(\infty)-f_s(\sqrt{L/b})\right). \end{aligned}\tag{SQ21} \]

For the trace, \(\int_L^\infty K_2(u,t)\,dt=-L/(2(u^2+L^2))\); integrating its magnitude gives the first term. For the height difference, integration in \(u\) gives the first formula of SQ10 with \(a=b\), and then its second formula with upper endpoint infinity gives the second term. Both terms tend to zero because their defining positive tails are integrable. This proves the infinite-limit passage while retaining the source's finite residual.

For constant data \(f\equiv c\), SQ5 gives exactly \(I_{a,b}=(c/2)\arctan(a/b)\), while SQ20 gives \(I_{b,\infty}=0\). These values check the sign and residual; no local principal-value correction is silently changed. If radial rather than strip cutoffs are wanted for jointly Hölder data, the separate PV1–PV3 quadrant correction must still be applied.

SQ6. Carry the exact coefficient into the original exterior receiver

The source at lines 2027–2028 uses auxiliary half-separation one. In those original source coordinates, let \(m\ge1\), \(\epsilon>0\), and \(\epsilon_m=\max(m,1+\epsilon)\). Inserting SQ16 gives exactly

\[ \begin{aligned} C_{\mathrm{out}}(m,\epsilon) &=C_{\mathrm{mid},2}(\epsilon_m-1)\\ &\quad+4\kappa\left[ \sqrt{\epsilon_m+1}-\sqrt{m+1} +\sqrt{\epsilon_m-1}-\sqrt{m-1}\right],\\ C_{\mathrm{out}}(1,\epsilon) &=C_{\mathrm{mid},2}(\epsilon) +4\kappa\left[\sqrt{2+\epsilon}-\sqrt2+\sqrt\epsilon\right]. \end{aligned}\tag{SQ22} \]

Every endpoint, both strip contributions, the factor four and the remaining middle integral are retained. The actual equality of the last two strip endpoints for \(m\ge1+\epsilon\) makes those terms zero; they are not discarded for smaller \(m\).

The source's monotonicity in \(m\) now follows explicitly. For \(1<m<1+\epsilon\), \(\epsilon_m\) is constant, the middle integral is constant, and the bracket has derivative \(-1/(2\sqrt{m+1})-1/(2\sqrt{m-1})<0\). For \(m\ge1+\epsilon\), the bracket is zero and \(C_{\mathrm{mid},2}(m-1)\) is an integral of a fixed nonnegative density over a decreasing domain. At \(m=1+\epsilon\), both formulas agree, and at \(m=1\) the displayed square-root limit is continuous. Thus the original lower-cell-endpoint bound is valid.

For the actual physical half-separation \(a_0>0\), physical midpoint \(M\ge a_0\), and physical strip width \(\delta>0\), set \(E=\max(M,a_0+\delta)\). The original-coordinate exterior integral is

\[ \begin{aligned} C_{\mathrm{vert}}(a_0,E) &=4\int_E^\infty\int_{\sigma_{a_0}(s)}^\infty \Delta_{a_0}(s,t)\sqrt{t-V_{a_0,s}(t)}\,dt\,ds,\\ \mathcal C_{\mathrm{out}}(a_0,M,\delta) &=C_{\mathrm{vert}}(a_0,E)\\ &\quad+4\kappa\left[ \sqrt{E+a_0}-\sqrt{M+a_0} +\sqrt{E-a_0}-\sqrt{M-a_0}\right]. \end{aligned}\tag{SQ23} \]

Here \(\Delta_{a_0}=K_2(s+a_0,t)-K_2(s-a_0,t)\), and \(\sigma_{a_0},V_{a_0,s}\) are precisely the threshold and positive cubic partner of RV12–RV14. Those formulas prove that this is a finite nonnegative integral.

To verify the full comparison with SQ22, use \(s=a_0y_1,\ t=a_0y_2\): the kernel contributes \(a_0^{-2}\), area contributes \(a_0^2\), the partner-distance square root contributes \(\sqrt{a_0}\), and the lower endpoint becomes \(E/a_0\). The exact threshold and cubic scale under this same map, as proved in PV14–PV19. Consequently \(C_{\mathrm{vert}}(a_0,E)=\sqrt{a_0}C_{\mathrm{mid},2}(E/a_0-1)\), and multiplying each endpoint square root in SQ22 by \(\sqrt{a_0}\) gives SQ23. The contribution to the full velocity quotient remains \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{out}}(a_0,M,\delta)H_x/(\pi\sqrt{2a_0})\). All inner and middle contributions and any local correction in the receiving velocity formula remain separate terms. This calculation exactly evaluates the two strip pieces; it does not certify the remaining middle integral numerically.

SQ7. Rational certification of the newly evaluated constant

The special-function expression need not be trusted as a floating-point oracle. Split the beta integral at \(1/2\), reflecting the upper half. For \(p,q>0\),

\[ \begin{aligned} B_{1/2}(p,q) &=2^{-p}\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(1-q)_n}{n!(p+n)\,2^n},\\ B(5/4,1/4) &=B_{1/2}(1/4,5/4)+B_{1/2}(5/4,1/4),\\ \kappa&=\frac{2^{-1/4}}{2\sqrt2} \left[ \sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(-1/4)_n}{n!(n+1/4)\,2^n} +\frac12\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(3/4)_n}{n!(n+5/4)\,2^n} \right]. \end{aligned}\tag{SQ24} \]

The rising factorial is \((c)_0=1\) and \((c)_n=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(c+j)\). To prove the series, its coefficients satisfy the differential equation for \((1-x)^{q-1}\), with initial value one. The ratio test gives uniform absolute convergence on \([0,1/2]\), so multiplying by \(x^{p-1}\) and integrating term by term is valid. This yields the first formula; reflection and SQ17 give the other two.

The terms of the first sum are negative from \(n=1\), and those of the second sum are positive. For either sum, at every \(n\ge1\), the ratio of absolute consecutive terms is

\[ \frac{n+1-q}{2(n+1)}\,\frac{p+n}{p+n+1}<\frac12 . \tag{SQ25} \]

Thus after terms \(0,\ldots,N-1\), \(N\ge1\), the magnitude of the omitted tail is at most twice the magnitude of term \(N\). Its sign is known. Every coefficient and tail bound is rational. Integer-square-root and fourth-root inequalities enclose \(\sqrt2\) and \(2^{1/4}\); interval multiplication then certifies the original \(\kappa\). The accompanying exact checker records its outward rational enclosure and all derivation identities. Numerical quadrature is used only as a separate diagnostic, never as the proof of this constant.

\[ \frac{131102877714605990523}{10^{20}} <\kappa< \frac{131102877714605990524}{10^{20}} . \tag{SQ26} \]

VA1. What the primitive already contains

Keep the actual source width \(\epsilon=2^{-12}\), the source height mesh \(t_j=j\epsilon/256\), and any increasing horizontal mesh \(0=x_0<\cdots<x_N=1\). Exercise 1. Differentiate the finite strip without losing its residual

Exercise 2. Integrate every height in the middle residual

Fix \(a>0\) and \(M>a\). The second term in VU4 is the complete middle residual. Describe its support with the horizontal coordinate first, evaluate its entire height integral, and recover its singular right limit without discarding any mass.

\[ \begin{aligned} B_0(a,M,s)&=M^2+s^2+2a^2,\\ C_0(a,M,s)&=(s^2-a^2)(M^2-a^2),\\ b_{a,M}(s)^2 &=\frac{2C_0(a,M,s)} {B_0(a,M,s)+\sqrt{B_0(a,M,s)^2+12C_0(a,M,s)}} . \end{aligned}\tag{EX5} \]

The integer ceiling is exact. Cross multiplication of the stated integers gives \(454428899208747<2^{64}\overline A<454428899208748\), which proves both strict bounds. The numerator is below \(2^{49}\); an integer of at most 49 binary digits times \(2^{-64}\) is a normal binary64 number with at most 49 significant binary digits. It is therefore exactly representable. This specifies the stored value without rounding the decimal display to the nearest number.

The source code's original \(P_{\rm code}\) is positive and smaller than \(A\), by VA7. The repaired per-quadrant expression is \(A_{64}\), with its original receiving factors. Its exact positive increase and a sufficient printed-norm budget are

\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta_{Y,64}&=\frac{2\sqrt2}{\pi}(A_{64}-P_{\rm code})>0,\\ d_{64}&=\frac{291}{500}\frac{2\sqrt2}{\pi}A_{64},\\ \frac{291}{500}\Delta_{Y,64} &<d_{64} <d_A+\frac{291}{500}\frac{2\sqrt2}{\pi\,2^{64}} <\frac{12909}{10^9}. \end{aligned}\tag{EX17} \]

The first two inequalities use EX16 and \(P_{\rm code}>0\). For the last one, and the numerical comparisons below, use exact rational upper bounds \(\sqrt2<14142135623730951/10^{16}\) and \(\pi>3141592653589793/10^{15}\). Squaring proves the former; the latter follows from the alternating arctangent bounds and Machin identity proved in PV43. Substitution in the complete positive products and cross multiplication prove the stated strict inequalities. The accompanying exact checker retains those integers.

For every actual nonnegative cost pair \(X_j,Y_j\), and \(0<r\le\tau=291/500\), the original change of weight is

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{X_j+\tau Y_j}{\pi} &=\frac{\tau}{r}\frac{X_j+rY_j}{\pi} -\frac{(\tau/r-1)X_j}{\pi},\\ \max_j\frac{X_j+\tau Y_j}{\pi} &\le\frac{\tau}{r}\max_j\frac{X_j+rY_j}{\pi}. \end{aligned}\tag{EX18} \]

This holds for any nonempty finite family, and for a bounded supremum by the same pointwise argument. It keeps the nonnegative horizontal term instead of silently identifying the two weights. A uniform additive correction passes through a maximum exactly: \(\max_j(c_j+d_{64})=\max_jc_j+d_{64}\). Using the actual \(r_-,V_+,W_+\) of VA12 gives

\[ \frac{291/500}{r_-}V_++d_{64}<\frac{13}{5}, \qquad \frac{291/500}{r_-}W_++d_{64}<\frac{261}{100}. \tag{EX19} \]

These follow by the same rational comparisons as EX17 and include the full saved-to-printed parameter change. They are comparisons of explicit coefficients. The complete validity of the saved vertical scalars is proved separately in VN and VU.

At the original downstream receiver, take its actual nonnegative seminorm \(H_{\max}\), current upper value \(U\), and separate upper value \(L\). For \(d\ge0\) define \((L-U)_+=\max(L-U,0)\). The entire effect of the componentwise minimum is the exact identity

\[ \min(U+dH_{\max},L)-\min(U,L) =\min\{dH_{\max},(L-U)_+\}. \tag{EX20} \]

If \(U\ge L\), both sides are zero. If \(U<L\), subtracting \(U\) reduces both sides to \(\min(dH_{\max},L-U)\). This proves all cases, including equality and \(H_{\max}=0\). The coefficient already contains its single \(1/\pi\); this receiver supplies no second division. Formula EX20 proves precisely this minimum operation. It is not a claim about the later covering or stability operations.

Exercise 5. Enlarge the actual weighted cell and measure the counterexample

Keep the original mesh \(h>0\) and \(F_{\alpha,h}(x)=x^{1-\alpha}J_2(x/h)\) from WJ13. Prove that for the actual exponent \(\alpha=1/2\) the increasing-cell range extends to \(0\le x\le h/6\). Then quantify the reversed endpoint inequality at \(\alpha=9/10\) on the exact WJ18 cell.

Solution. Set \(B=x/h\), retaining \(x\) in the derivative and \(R(0)=1-(3/2)\log2\). WJ11–WJ13 gives, for \(0<B\le1/6\),

\[ \begin{aligned} \partial_x F_{1/2,h}(x) &=x^{-1/2}\left\{\frac12(R(B)-\log B)+BR'(B)-1\right\},\\ \frac12(R(B)-\log B)+BR'(B)-1 &>\frac12\{1-(3/2)\log2-\log B\}+\frac34 B-1 . \end{aligned}\tag{EX21} \]

The lower expression has derivative \(-1/(2B)+3/4<0\) on this entire interval. Its value at \(B=1/6\) is \(\frac14\log(9/2)-3/8\). This value is strictly positive without a numerical logarithm. In fact, integrate the positive geometric series for \(1/(1-y^2)\) from zero to \(7/11\):

\[ \log(9/2) =2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(7/11)^{2k+1}}{2k+1} >2\sum_{k=0}^{4}\frac{(7/11)^{2k+1}}{2k+1} >\frac32 . \tag{EX22} \]

The last inequality is exact rational cross multiplication. The series identity uses \((1+7/11)/(1-7/11)=9/2\), with convergence because \(7/11<1\). Thus EX21 is positive throughout \(0<x\le h/6\). The zero endpoint is the continuous extension already proved in WJ17. For every full physical cell this yields

\[ \begin{aligned} 0\le z^l\le x\le z^u\le h/6 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x^{1/2}J_2(x/h)&\le (z^u)^{1/2}J_2(z^u/h). \end{aligned}\tag{EX23} \]

The new range strictly contains \(0\le x\le he^{-2}\): the exponential series gives \(e>1+1+1/2=5/2\), hence \(e^2>25/4>6\). The original additional restriction \(z^u\le h/2\) still holds. This is a proved editorial strengthening of the actual-exponent selector, not an optimality claim.

Now retain the different original exponent \(\alpha=9/10\), the same arbitrary \(h>0\), and the exact endpoints \(z^u=he^{-10}\), \(z^l=(999/1000)he^{-10}\). The full rational sum in WJ19 is

\[ -\frac1{270}+\frac{11}{10240}+\frac1{9990} =-\frac{575}{227328}. \tag{EX24} \]

Consequently the complete original derivative obeys \(F'_{9/10,h}(x)<-(575/227328)x^{-9/10}\) everywhere on that closed positive cell. Integrating and keeping both physical endpoints gives

\[ \begin{aligned} F_{9/10,h}(z^l)-F_{9/10,h}(z^u) &>\frac{2875}{113664} \{(z^u)^{1/10}-(z^l)^{1/10}\}\\ &=\frac{2875}{113664}h^{1/10}e^{-1} \left[1-\left(\frac{999}{1000}\right)^{1/10}\right]>0 . \end{aligned}\tag{EX25} \]

Here \(\int x^{-9/10}\,dx=10x^{1/10}\), and \(10(575/227328)=2875/113664\). No mesh factor, endpoint, exponent or strict sign has been suppressed. This gives a quantitative failure for the source's all-exponent claim alongside the larger proved range for the actual exponent.
