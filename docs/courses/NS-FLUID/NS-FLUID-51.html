Original two-point transport and certified kernel bounds

This lesson connects the original weighted transport equation to the localized nonlocal operator and its numerical bounds. The complete arguments keep both observation points, their original separation, the physical boundary, both cutoff lengths and every kernel prefactor.

Part 1 evaluates the exact angular coefficient and carries both coupled components through the two-point estimate. Part 2 proves the full rectangular transport comparison, corrects the data region in the general source statement and checks the actual Part II mesh geometry. Part 3 identifies a concrete wrong cell formula and proves the complete repair budget. Part 4 independently encloses the original global horizontal constant below 2.549, including rigorous rounding and the whole infinite exterior.

The five exercises calculate the coupled weighted bounds, an actual reflected mesh region, an exact rectangle transport partner, the cell-to-global comparison, and four actual cutoff-array lookups with their receiving costs. A global numerical certificate does not certify each finer stored entry; the last exercise makes the difference exact.

The source statements and editorial corrections remain identifiable. The original source authors are Jiajie Chen and Thomas Y. Hou. These results recover a specific global estimate while leaving the complete numerical stability and singularity proof under continuing audit.

HP1. Full two-point transport identity

Keep the original coordinates \(x=(x_1,x_2)\), \(z=(z_1,z_2)\) in the upper half-plane \(\Omega=\mathbb R\times(0,\infty)\), with \(x\ne z\). For a smooth scalar field satisfying

\[ f_t+b(x)\cdot\nabla f=c(x)f+R(t,x), \qquad Z(t,x)=\varphi(x)f(t,x),\quad d(x)=c(x)+\frac{b(x)\cdot\nabla\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)}, \tag{HP1} \]

assume the spatial weight is positive and continuously differentiable on this domain. Let \(g\) be positive and continuously differentiable away from zero and retain the original difference

\[ F(t,x,z)=(Z(t,x)-Z(t,z))g(x-z). \tag{HP2} \]

The product rule first gives \(Z_t+b\cdot\nabla Z=dZ+\varphi R\). Subtract that equation at the two original points. The two transports differentiate \(g(x-z)\) with coefficient \(b(x)-b(z)\). Use the complete identity

\[ d(x)Z(x)-d(z)Z(z) =d(x)(Z(x)-Z(z))+(d(x)-d(z))Z(z). \]

It yields exactly

\[ \begin{aligned} &(\partial_t+b(x)\cdot\nabla_x+b(z)\cdot\nabla_z)F\\ &\quad=\left(d(x)+ \frac{(b(x)-b(z))\cdot\nabla g(x-z)}{g(x-z)}\right)F\\ &\qquad +(d(x)-d(z))g(x-z)Z(t,z)\\ &\qquad +\big(\varphi(x)R(t,x)-\varphi(z)R(t,z)\big)g(x-z). \end{aligned} \tag{HP3} \]

Every source and both spatial points remain. This derivation also specifies the exact map from the scalar equation to its two-point equation, rather than treating the latter as a separate assumed model.

HP2. The precise angular constant, with original distances

Take \(0<a_1\leq a_2\), real \(a_3\), \(\gamma_2\geq0\), and \(0<\alpha\leq1\). For the source model put

\[ \begin{gathered} b(x)=(a_1x_1,a_2x_2),\qquad \varphi(x)=|x|^{-\gamma_2},\qquad g(h)=|h|^{-\alpha},\\ q(x)=\frac{x_2^2}{x_1^2+x_2^2},\qquad d_\eta(x)=a_3-\gamma_2a_1-\gamma_2(a_2-a_1)q(x),\\ e(x,z)=-\alpha \frac{a_1(x_1-z_1)^2+a_2(x_2-z_2)^2}{|x-z|^2}. \end{gathered} \tag{HP4} \]

These follow by differentiating the original radial powers. The full angular and difference quotients have not been replaced by averages. The exact best constant in the source's angular comparison is

\[ \sup_{x,z\in\Omega,\ x\ne z} |d_\eta(x)-d_\eta(z)|\frac{|z|^\alpha}{|x-z|^\alpha} =\gamma_2(a_2-a_1)=:C_*. \tag{HP5} \]

Here is a proof including the sharpness. Write the actual points as \(x=r_x(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\) and \(z=r_z(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)\), where both original radii are positive and \(\theta,\phi\in(0,\pi)\). For \(\delta=\theta-\phi\), the complete distance identity is

\[ \begin{aligned} |x-z|^2 &=r_x^2+r_z^2-2r_xr_z\cos\delta\\ &=(r_x-r_z\cos\delta)^2+r_z^2\sin^2\delta,\\ q(x)-q(z)&=\sin(\theta+\phi)\sin\delta. \end{aligned} \tag{HP6} \]

If \(\cos\delta\geq0\) and \(\sin\delta\ne0\), these identities imply

\[ |q(x)-q(z)|\frac{r_z^\alpha}{|x-z|^\alpha} \leq |\sin(\theta+\phi)|\,|\sin\delta|^{1-\alpha} \leq1. \tag{HP7} \]

When \(\sin\delta=0\), the numerator is zero. If \(\cos\delta<0\), the first line of HP6 gives \(|x-z|\geq r_z\), and \(0\leq q\leq1\) again gives the bound one. This covers all pairs in the original half-plane, including pairs with opposite signs of the first coordinate.

For any original radius \(R_0>0\) and \(0<\varepsilon<\pi/4\), choose the actual pair

\[ \begin{gathered} z_\varepsilon=R_0(\cos\varepsilon,\sin\varepsilon),\qquad x_\varepsilon=R_0\sin(2\varepsilon) (\sin\varepsilon,\cos\varepsilon),\\ |x_\varepsilon-z_\varepsilon|=R_0\cos(2\varepsilon),\qquad q(x_\varepsilon)-q(z_\varepsilon)=\cos(2\varepsilon),\\ |q(x_\varepsilon)-q(z_\varepsilon)| \frac{|z_\varepsilon|^\alpha} {|x_\varepsilon-z_\varepsilon|^\alpha} =\cos(2\varepsilon)^{1-\alpha}. \end{gathered} \tag{HP8} \]

Both points are in the positive quadrant for every permitted \(\varepsilon\). The last expression tends to one as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\); for \(\alpha=1\) it equals one for every such pair. All factors of \(R_0\) are displayed before they cancel in the ratio. Multiplication by the actual coefficient in HP4 proves HP5. If \(\gamma_2=0\) or \(a_1=a_2\), both sides of HP5 are exactly zero.

HP3. The complete scalar receiving estimate

For the full frozen solution define

\[ \begin{gathered} \gamma=\gamma_2+\alpha,\qquad M_f(t)=\sup_{x\in\Omega}|x|^{-\gamma}|f(t,x)|,\\ H_f(t)=\sup_{x,z\in\Omega,\ x\ne z} \frac{|\,|x|^{-\gamma_2}f(t,x) -|z|^{-\gamma_2}f(t,z)|}{|x-z|^\alpha}. \end{gathered} \tag{HP9} \]

Start with smooth compactly supported data in \(\Omega\); their supports avoid the origin and the boundary. Thus both norms are finite. Oddness in the first coordinate may additionally be imposed and is preserved by the diagonal flow. No boundary condition on the full nonlocal PDE is inferred from this class of test data.

For \(\eta_t+b\cdot\nabla\eta=a_3\eta\), LT19–LT20 with the original exponent \(\gamma\) give

\[ M_\eta(t)\leq e^{c t}M_\eta(0),\qquad c=a_3-a_1\gamma. \tag{HP10} \]

In HP3, the main coefficient obeys \(d_\eta(x)+e(x,z)\leq c\). The remaining term has the exact receiving bound

\[ \begin{aligned} &|d_\eta(x)-d_\eta(z)|\,|x-z|^{-\alpha} |z|^{-\gamma_2}|\eta(t,z)|\\ &\qquad\leq C_*M_\eta(t), \qquad C_*=\gamma_2(a_2-a_1). \end{aligned} \tag{HP11} \]

Indeed, factor its left side into the HP5 ratio and \(|z|^{-\gamma}|\eta(t,z)|\). Along the pair of actual characteristics, the scalar integrating-factor inequality therefore gives

\[ H_\eta(t)\leq e^{ct}H_\eta(0) +C_*\int_0^t e^{c(t-s)}M_\eta(s)\,ds \leq e^{ct}\big(H_\eta(0)+C_*tM_\eta(0)\big). \tag{HP12} \]

To justify the supremum, the diagonal flow maps \(\Omega\) bijectively to itself and preserves distinctness of pairs. Integrate the inequality for each fixed initial pair first, then take its supremum. No differentiability of a maximizing pair or norm is used. The explicit smooth solution makes all finite-time integrals defined.

The constant in HP11 is itself sharp over this test class. At any pair from HP8 choose a nonnegative smooth bump supported in a small ball about \(z_\varepsilon\), equal to one there, and multiply it by \(|z|^\gamma\). Its \(M\) norm is one and it realizes the entire ratio at that point. Its odd reflected copy can be added in the other half-plane sector with disjoint support and the same norm. This proves sharpness even with the original odd symmetry. It is not a claim that HP12 is the optimal entire evolution bound.

HP4. Both original coupled components, with every transfer factor

Retain the first frozen equation \(\omega_t+b\cdot\nabla\omega=-\omega+\eta\), and set

\[ \begin{gathered} b_0=-1-a_1\gamma,\qquad c=a_3-a_1\gamma,\\ K(t)=\int_0^t e^{b_0(t-s)}e^{cs}\,ds =e^{-a_1\gamma t} \begin{cases} (e^{a_3t}-e^{-t})/(a_3+1),&a_3\ne-1,\\ te^{-t},&a_3=-1. \end{cases} \end{gathered} \tag{HP13} \]

The full radial estimate and the exact two-point equation give

\[ \begin{aligned} M_\omega(t)&\leq e^{b_0t}M_\omega(0)+K(t)M_\eta(0),\\ H_\omega(t)&\leq e^{b_0t}H_\omega(0) +\int_0^t e^{b_0(t-s)} \big(H_\eta(s)+C_*M_\omega(s)\big)\,ds. \end{aligned} \tag{HP14} \]

The difference of the original \(+\eta\) forcing is exactly the quantity bounded by \(H_\eta\); it has not been deleted. The angular part of \(d_\omega=-1-\gamma_2a_1 -\gamma_2(a_2-a_1)q\) has the same precise constant \(C_*\). Substitute HP12 and the first line of HP14 into the second. The two terms involving \(C_*M_\eta(0)\) combine by the full integral identity

\[ \begin{aligned} &\int_0^t e^{b_0(t-s)}s e^{cs}\,ds +\int_0^t e^{b_0(t-s)}K(s)\,ds\\ &\quad=\int_0^t e^{b_0(t-u)}e^{cu} \big(u+(t-u)\big)\,du=tK(t). \end{aligned} \tag{HP15} \]

For the second integral insert HP13, integrate on \(0\leq u\leq s\leq t\), and interchange these finite continuous integrals. The inner integrand then has no remaining dependence on \(s\), giving exactly \(t-u\). Consequently

\[ H_\omega(t)\leq e^{b_0t}\big(H_\omega(0)+C_*tM_\omega(0)\big) +K(t)\big(H_\eta(0)+C_*tM_\eta(0)\big). \tag{HP16} \]

For the actual Part I triple \((a_1,a_2,a_3)=(1/2,11/2,1/2)\), every constant and time factor becomes

\[ C_*=5\gamma_2,\qquad c=\frac{1-\gamma}{2},\qquad b_0=-1-\frac\gamma2, \qquad K(t)=\frac23 \left(e^{(1-\gamma)t/2}-e^{-(1+\gamma/2)t}\right). \tag{HP17} \]

Thus for \(\gamma=\gamma_2+\alpha>1\), both complete weighted supremum norms and both Hölder seminorms tend to zero. The linear factors in time are retained and still tend to zero after multiplication by their strictly decaying exponentials. For merely quadratic vanishing at the origin, the source-compatible local range is \(1<\gamma\leq2\); on our compact class every finite \(\gamma\) is permitted. The displayed estimates also apply directly to other smooth initial fields whenever HP9 is finite, by the same pointwise characteristic proof. No hidden boundary term, nonlocal forcing or full-PDE estimate has been supplied by that extension. The next step is to apply the source's actual nonlocal operator bounds with their complete coefficients.

NL1. The operator and the data it actually reads

Let the physical upper half-plane be \(\Omega=\mathbb R\times(0,\infty)\). Take \(0<\alpha<1\), a bounded measurable vorticity \(\omega\) on its closure, and define its odd vertical extension by

\[ W(y_1,y_2)= \begin{cases} \omega(y_1,y_2),&y_2\geq0,\\ -\omega(y_1,-y_2),&y_2<0. \end{cases} \tag{NL1} \]

Values on the boundary do not affect the integrals. No vertical continuity of this extension is assumed. The source also assumes oddness in the first coordinate; our estimate holds with or without that extra symmetry, and the counterexample below has it.

Fix the original points and lengths

\[ \begin{gathered} x=(x_1,y_0),\quad z=(z_1,y_0),\quad x_1>z_1,\quad y_0>0,\\ h=x_1-z_1>0,\quad \delta=h/2,\quad m=(x_1+z_1)/2,\\ a\geq\delta,\quad b_1>0,\quad b_2>0,\\ Q(p)=[p_1-a,p_1+a]\times[y_0-b_1,y_0+b_2],\\ K_1(v_1,v_2)=\frac{v_1v_2}{(v_1^2+v_2^2)^2},\\ U(p)=-\frac1\pi\operatorname{PV}\int_{Q(p)}K_1(p-y)W(y)\,dy. \end{gathered} \tag{NL2} \]

The principal value deletes a disk centered at \(p\). At every fixed height, both the horizontal integration interval and the deleted interval are symmetric about \(p_1\). The integral of the kernel times \(W(p_1,y_2)\) is therefore exactly zero. Subtracting that value leaves an integrand bounded near \(p\) by a constant times \(|p-y|^{\alpha-2}\), provided the horizontal Hölder seminorm of the sampled data is finite. Its two-dimensional integral near the origin is finite because \(\int_0^\varepsilon r^{\alpha-1}\,dr =\varepsilon^\alpha/\alpha\). Away from the singularity, boundedness of the data suffices on the finite rectangle. This proves existence of the stated principal value and justifies horizontal slice integration after this subtraction.

For any set \(D\) in the physical half-plane, including its boundary, write

\[ [\omega]_{C_x^\alpha(D)}= \sup_{\substack{(u,v),(u',v)\in D\\u\ne u'}} \frac{|\omega(u,v)-\omega(u',v)|}{|u-u'|^\alpha}. \tag{NL3} \]

The reflection map \(\mathcal R(y_1,y_2)=(y_1,|y_2|)\) preserves the distance between two points at the same height. Their signs in NL1 agree. Consequently every horizontal difference of \(W\) in a set \(A\) is bounded by NL3 on \(\mathcal R(A)\), with exactly the same coefficient. This proves the actual receiving map between the two seminorms, including the data below the boundary.

The two horizontal intervals overlap because \(a\geq\delta\). Hence their union is the interval \(I=[m-a-\delta,m+a+\delta]\), and the complete physical region sampled by NL2 is

\[ \begin{aligned} D_{\rm ref}&=\mathcal R(Q(x)\cup Q(z))\\ &=I\times \left[\max(0,y_0-b_1),\ \max(y_0+b_2,b_1-y_0)\right]. \end{aligned} \tag{NL4} \]

Indeed, if the vertical interval stays above zero, reflection does nothing. If it crosses zero, its positive portion is \([0,y_0+b_2]\) and its reflected negative portion is \([0,b_1-y_0]\). This proves NL4 in both cases. The unreflected positive part in the printed Part I lemma is

\[ \begin{gathered} D_0=(Q(x)\cup Q(z))\cap\overline\Omega =I\times[\max(0,y_0-b_1),y_0+b_2],\\ D_{\rm ref}=D_0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b_1\leq2y_0+b_2. \end{gathered} \tag{NL5} \]

The equivalence follows by comparing the two upper endpoints. In particular it holds for symmetric vertical cutoffs. The estimate proved below uses any \(D\supset D_{\rm ref}\) with finite NL3.

NL2. A smooth counterexample to the unrestricted domain statement

Keep the original kernel and take exactly

\[ x=(2,1),\quad z=(1,1),\quad a=1,\quad b_1=4, \quad b_2=\tfrac12,\quad D_0=[0,3]\times[0,\tfrac32]. \tag{NL6} \]

Define the smooth compactly supported function

\[ \begin{gathered} \rho(s)= \begin{cases}e^{-1/(1-s^2)},&|s|<1,\\0,&|s|\geq1, \end{cases}\\ B(u,v)=\rho(8(u-\tfrac52))\rho(8(v-\tfrac52)),\\ \omega(u,v)=B(u,v)-B(-u,v),\qquad v\geq0. \end{gathered} \tag{NL7} \]

All derivatives of \(\rho\) tend to zero at either endpoint: each derivative inside the interval is the exponential times a rational function whose denominator is a power of \(1-s^2\), and \(q^N e^{-q}\to0\) for every finite \(N\) as \(q\to\infty\). This proves the claimed smoothness across the endpoints. Thus NL7 is smooth, compactly supported, odd in its first coordinate, and zero on a neighborhood of \(D_0\). Every seminorm in NL3 on \(D_0\) is zero.

The positive bump has support in \([19/8,21/8]^2\). Its original positive-height copy is outside both rectangles in NL6. Its reflected copy lies inside \(Q(x)\), whose horizontal interval is \([1,3]\) and vertical interval is \([-3,3/2]\), and outside \(Q(z)\), whose horizontal interval is \([0,2]\). The negative first-coordinate bump and its reflection are outside both rectangles. No support meets a kernel singularity. Direct substitution, retaining the sign in NL1 and the factor in NL2, gives

\[ \begin{gathered} U(z)=0,\\ U(x)=-\frac1\pi\int_{19/8}^{21/8}\int_{19/8}^{21/8} \frac{(u-2)(1+v)}{((u-2)^2+(1+v)^2)^2}B(u,v)\,du\,dv<0. \end{gathered} \tag{NL8} \]

The integrand is strictly positive on the interior of the support. For a quantitative lower bound, on the whole support its rational factor is at least

\[ \frac{(3/8)(27/8)}{((5/8)^2+(29/8)^2)^2} =\frac{1296}{187489}. \tag{NL9} \]

On the inner square of side \(1/8\) centered at \((5/2,5/2)\), each factor \(\rho\) is at least \(e^{-4/3}\). Therefore

\[ \frac{|U(x)-U(z)|}{|x-z|^\alpha} \geq\frac{81e^{-8/3}}{749956\pi}>0, \qquad [\omega]_{C_x^\alpha(D_0)}=0. \tag{NL10} \]

This disproves the domain statement in Part I, lines 3284–3303, when the vertical cutoffs are unrestricted. It applies in particular to its printed exponent \(\alpha=1/2\). It does not disprove the estimate with the reflected region NL4.

NL3. Full primitive, sign threshold, and transport map

Use actual translated integration coordinates \(r=y_1-m\), \(t=y_0-y_2\); their area Jacobian has absolute value one. The vertical interval is exactly \([-b_2,b_1]\). For \(t>0\), define

\[ \begin{gathered} k_{h,t}(r)=K_1(r+\delta,t)-K_1(r-\delta,t),\\ P_{h,t}(r)=\frac t2 \left(\frac1{(r-\delta)^2+t^2} -\frac1{(r+\delta)^2+t^2}\right)\\ =\frac{htr}{((r-\delta)^2+t^2)((r+\delta)^2+t^2)}. \end{gathered} \tag{NL11} \]

Differentiation proves \(P'_{h,t}=k_{h,t}\). Both endpoint values \(P_{h,t}(0)\) and \(\lim_{r\to\infty}P_{h,t}(r)\) are zero; inside the positive half-line the primitive is positive. The full numerator of the kernel is

\[ k_{h,t}(r)= \frac{ht\left(t^4-2r^2t^2-3r^4+2\delta^2r^2 +2\delta^2t^2+\delta^4\right)} {((r-\delta)^2+t^2)^2((r+\delta)^2+t^2)^2}. \tag{NL12} \]

Its sign polynomial, considered as a quadratic in \(r^2\), has negative leading coefficient and positive constant term, so exactly one positive root. The positive sign threshold and its exact displacement are

\[ \begin{gathered} F_{h}(t)^2= \frac{\delta^2-t^2+2\sqrt{\delta^4+\delta^2t^2+t^4}}3,\\ F_{h}(t)^2-\delta^2= \frac{t^4}{2\sqrt{\delta^4+\delta^2t^2+t^4}+2\delta^2+t^2}>0. \end{gathered} \tag{NL13} \]

Thus \(k_{h,t}>0\) on \((0,F_h(t))\) and \(k_{h,t}<0\) on \((F_h(t),\infty)\). The primitive is strictly increasing and then strictly decreasing on these intervals. Each value between zero and its maximum has exactly one preimage on each branch. Define \(T_{h,t}(r)\) as the preimage on the other branch, and set \(T_{h,t}(F_h(t))=F_h(t)\). This constructs a continuous strictly decreasing involution of \((0,\infty)\), with \(T_{h,t}(0+)=\infty\) and \(T_{h,t}(\infty)=0\), satisfying

\[ \begin{gathered} P_{h,t}(T_{h,t}(r))=P_{h,t}(r),\\ T'_{h,t}(r)\,k_{h,t}(T_{h,t}(r))=k_{h,t}(r) \quad(r\ne F_h(t)),\\ rT\left(T^2+rT+r^2+2t^2-2\delta^2\right) -(t^2+\delta^2)^2=0,\qquad T=T_{h,t}(r). \end{gathered} \tag{NL14} \]

The last identity follows by cross-multiplication in the first and division by \(T-r\) away from the fixed point; continuity gives it at the fixed point. It is not a license to select the trivial solution \(T=r\) of the primitive equality at other points. The cubic has exactly one positive root: after division by \(r\), its coefficients are \(1,r,r^2+2t^2-2\delta^2,-(t^2+\delta^2)^2/r\), with one sign change whether or not the third coefficient is negative. To avoid requiring a root-counting theorem, its derivative on the positive axis is \(3T^2+2rT+r^2+2t^2-2\delta^2\), which is strictly increasing there. If initially nonnegative the cubic strictly increases from a negative value to infinity. If initially negative it first decreases from a negative value and then strictly increases to infinity. In either case it has exactly one positive zero.

There are no negative real zeros: a negative zero would, by reversing the cross-multiplication (all denominators are positive for \(t>0\)), give \(P_{h,t}(T)=P_{h,t}(r)>0\), whereas the primitive is negative for \(T<0\). Zero is not a zero of the cubic. Its positive zero is simple by the derivative argument. This also proves uniqueness of its real root without a floating-point discriminant computation.

For every horizontal function \(g\) with finite Hölder seminorm, change variables from one branch to the other using NL14 to obtain

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_0^\infty k_{h,t}(r)g(r)\,dr &=\int_0^{F_h(t)} k_{h,t}(r) \bigl(g(r)-g(T_{h,t}(r))\bigr)\,dr,\\ J_{h,\alpha}(t) &=\int_0^{F_h(t)} k_{h,t}(r)(T_{h,t}(r)-r)^\alpha\,dr\\ &=\int_{F_h(t)}^\infty[-k_{h,t}(r)] (r-T_{h,t}(r))^\alpha\,dr. \end{aligned} \tag{NL15} \]

For fixed \(t>0\), the kernel is regular on the entire real axis and decays as \(r^{-4}\); subtracting a fixed value of \(g\) allows its growth bound \(O(r^\alpha)\). Thus these changes of variables can first be made on compact subintervals and then passed to the endpoints by absolute convergence. The cost integrability in the remaining variable is proved in NL5 below.

NL4. Exact rectangular localization decreases the cost

Retain \(h,t,a\), and define the actual truncated horizontal kernel and its primitive by

\[ \begin{gathered} k_{a,h,t}(r)=K_1(r+\delta,t)\mathbf1_{|r+\delta|\leq a} -K_1(r-\delta,t)\mathbf1_{|r-\delta|\leq a},\\ P_{a,h,t}(r)=\int_0^r k_{a,h,t}(u)\,du\quad(r\geq0),\\ P_{a,h,t}(r)= \begin{cases} P_{h,t}(r),&0\leq r\leq a-\delta,\\ \displaystyle\frac t2\left(\frac1{(r-\delta)^2+t^2} -\frac1{a^2+t^2}\right),&a-\delta\leq r\leq a+\delta,\\ 0,&r\geq a+\delta. \end{cases} \end{gathered} \tag{NL16} \]

On the first interval both kernels remain. On the second only the negative second kernel remains. Integration and matching at \(a-\delta\) prove NL16, including the case \(a=\delta\). It also proves exact zero total mass, including the contribution at the rectangle edge. There is no discarded boundary term.

The primitive is strictly increasing and then decreasing on its support, with maximum at

\[ c_{a,h}(t)=\min\bigl(F_h(t),\max(\delta,a-\delta)\bigr). \tag{NL17} \]

For \(\delta\leq a\leq2\delta\), its maximum is at \(\delta\); for \(2\delta<a<F_h(t)+\delta\), at \(a-\delta\); and for \(a\geq F_h(t)+\delta\), at \(F_h(t)\). These follow directly from the signs in NL12 and the sign of \(-K_1(r-\delta,t)\) in the second interval of NL16. Construct \(T_{a,h,t}\) by matching the two primitive branches as above. Its domain is \((0,a+\delta)\), its fixed point is \(c_{a,h}(t)\), and its endpoints are interchanged. It is continuous and strictly decreasing, and is differentiable away from the finitely many joins and the fixed point. The branch change-of-variables formula uses its derivative only on those open pieces; continuity and zero measure of the joins give the full formula.

Here is a direct comparison preserving the edge contribution. On \([0,a+\delta]\), define \(E(r)=P_{h,t}(r)-P_{a,h,t}(r)\). Its full formula is

\[ E(r)= \begin{cases} 0,&0\leq r\leq a-\delta,\\ \displaystyle\frac t2\left(\frac1{a^2+t^2} -\frac1{(r+\delta)^2+t^2}\right), &a-\delta\leq r\leq a+\delta. \end{cases} \tag{NL18} \]

It is nonnegative and nondecreasing, since on the second interval its derivative is \(K_1(r+\delta,t)>0\). For \(0<r<c_{a,h}(t)\), put \(T=T_{h,t}(r)\). If \(T\geq a+\delta\), the local partner is automatically at most \(T\). Otherwise \(T\geq F_h(t)\geq c_{a,h}(t)\), and

\[ P_{a,h,t}(T)=P_{h,t}(r)-E(T) \leq P_{h,t}(r)-E(r)=P_{a,h,t}(r). \tag{NL19} \]

The local primitive strictly decreases on its right branch. Its point with value \(P_{a,h,t}(r)\) is therefore no farther right than \(T\). On the left branch, differentiating NL18 almost everywhere also gives \(0\leq k_{a,h,t}(r)\leq k_{h,t}(r)\). Consequently

\[ \begin{gathered} r\leq c_{a,h}(t)\leq T_{a,h,t}(r)\leq T_{h,t}(r) \quad(0<r\leq c_{a,h}(t)),\\ J_{a,h,\alpha}(t) =\int_0^{c_{a,h}(t)}k_{a,h,t}(r) (T_{a,h,t}(r)-r)^\alpha\,dr \leq J_{h,\alpha}(t). \end{gathered} \tag{NL20} \]

The inequality first bounds the integrand on the smaller interval and then extends to \([0,F_h(t)]\), where the added integrand is nonnegative. This proves the localization comparison for every \(\alpha>0\) for which the costs exist. NL18 records precisely the primitive contribution removed by the rectangular edge.

NL5. Convergence and a bound for the transport distance

For \(r\geq F_h(t)\), the partner lies to its left and satisfies

\[ 0\leq r-T_{h,t}(r)\leq\min\bigl(r,2(r-\delta)\bigr). \tag{NL21} \]

Positivity of \(T\) gives the first upper bound. If \(r\geq2\delta\), it also gives the second. Otherwise write \(r=\delta+w\) with \(0\leq w\leq\delta\). The value \(\delta-w\) lies on the increasing branch. Comparing its primitive with that at \(\delta+w\), using the common positive denominators in NL11, has numerator

\[ (\delta-w)((2\delta+w)^2+t^2) -(\delta+w)((2\delta-w)^2+t^2) =-2w(t^2+w^2)\leq0. \tag{NL22} \]

Thus \(P_{h,t}(\delta-w)\leq P_{h,t}(r)\), so strict monotonicity on that branch gives \(T_{h,t}(r)\geq\delta-w=2\delta-r\), which proves NL21.

The only possible finite singularity of the integral in the last line of NL15, for \(r\geq F_h(t)\) and \(t\geq0\), is \((r,t)=(\delta,0)\). Let \(\varrho=((r-\delta)^2+t^2)^{1/2}\). The singular kernel obeys \(|K_1(r-\delta,t)|\leq1/(2\varrho^2)\). By NL21 its cost multiplier is at most \(2^\alpha\varrho^\alpha\). The resulting singular majorant is \(2^{\alpha-1}\varrho^{\alpha-2}\), integrable in two dimensions for every \(\alpha>0\). The other kernel is regular there since \(h=2\delta>0\).

For the tail set \(\rho=(r^2+t^2)^{1/2}\geq R>\delta\), retain the full difference by writing

\[ \begin{gathered} k_{h,t}(r)=\int_{-\delta}^{\delta} \frac{t(t^2-3(r+s)^2)}{((r+s)^2+t^2)^3}\,ds,\\ \left|\frac{t(t^2-3v^2)}{(v^2+t^2)^3}\right| \leq(v^2+t^2)^{-3/2},\\ |k_{h,t}(r)|\leq\frac{h}{(\rho-\delta)^3} \leq h\left(\frac R{R-\delta}\right)^3\rho^{-3}. \end{gathered} \tag{NL23} \]

For the middle bound, write \(v=\ell\cos\theta\), \(t=\ell\sin\theta\); the numerator divided by \(\ell^3\) is \(-\sin3\theta\), of absolute value at most one. The reverse triangle inequality gives the other bounds. The first upper bound in NL21 now yields the explicit tail estimate

\[ \int_{\substack{t\geq0,\ r\geq F_h(t)\\r^2+t^2\geq R^2}} [-k_{h,t}(r)](r-T_{h,t}(r))^\alpha\,dr\,dt \leq\frac{\pi h}{2(1-\alpha)} \left(\frac R{R-\delta}\right)^3 R^{\alpha-1}. \tag{NL24} \]

This follows by integrating over the entire first quadrant in polar coordinates. The angular interval has length \(\pi/2\), and the radial integral is \(R^{\alpha-1}/(1-\alpha)\). Near the singularity, on compact subsets away from it, and at infinity we have now proved absolute convergence. No numerical quadrature is used in this proof.

NL6. The full estimate and exact comparison to the source integral

Returning to the original operator and its original fields, the sign of the first kernel coordinate and the two indicator functions give

\[ U(x)-U(z)=-\frac1\pi\operatorname{PV} \int_{-b_2}^{b_1}\int_{\mathbb R} k_{a,h,t}(r)W(m+r,y_0-t)\,dr\,dt. \tag{NL25} \]

\[ \begin{aligned} |U(x)-U(z)| &\leq\frac{2H}{\pi} \left(\int_0^{b_1}J_{a,h,\alpha}(t)\,dt +\int_0^{b_2}J_{a,h,\alpha}(t)\,dt\right)\\ &\leq\frac{2H}{\pi} \left(\int_0^{b_1}J_{h,\alpha}(t)\,dt +\int_0^{b_2}J_{h,\alpha}(t)\,dt\right). \end{aligned} \tag{NL26} \]

NC1. Original objects and the integral being estimated

Retain the author's coordinates \((x,z)=(s_1,s_2)\), the two shifts \(1/2\), and the full kernel

\[ K_1(r,z)=\frac{rz}{(r^2+z^2)^2},\qquad \Delta(x,z)=K_1(x+1/2,z)-K_1(x-1/2,z). \tag{NC1} \]

For \(x>0,z\geq0\), let \(T(x,z)>0\) solve

\[ xT\bigl(T^2+xT+x^2+2z^2-1/2\bigr) -(z^2+1/4)^2=0. \tag{NC2} \]

There is exactly one positive root. After division by \(x\), the polynomial has value \(-(z^2+1/4)^2/x<0\) at zero. Its derivative is \(3T^2+2xT+x^2+2z^2-1/2\), whose derivative in \(T\) is \(6T+2x>0\). If its derivative starts nonnegative, the polynomial increases to infinity. Otherwise it first decreases from a negative value and subsequently increases to infinity. Both cases give precisely one positive root, on the increasing portion.

Write \(A=z^2+1/4\), \(P=4xT\) and \(Q=4z^2+1\). Equation (NC2) and \(T^2+xT+x^2\geq3xT\) give

\[ Q^2\geq3P^2+2PQ-4P,\qquad P\leq Q,\qquad xT\leq A. \tag{NC3} \]

For the middle implication, if \(P>Q\geq1\), the expression \(3P^2+2PQ-4P-Q^2\) is strictly larger than its value \(4Q(Q-1)\geq0\) at \(P=Q\), since its derivative in \(P\) is positive there and beyond. This contradicts the first inequality. In particular the derivative of the divided polynomial at its root is

\[ D_T=3T^2+2xT+x^2+2z^2-1/2 =2T^2+xT+\frac{A^2}{xT}\geq\frac14. \tag{NC4} \]

Implicit differentiation now proves

\[ T_x=-\frac{T^2+2xT+A^2/x^2}{D_T}<0,\qquad T_z=\frac{4z(A-xT)}{xD_T}\geq0. \tag{NC5} \]

The positive fixed point is

\[ f(z)=\sqrt{\frac{1/2-2z^2+\sqrt{16z^4+4z^2+1}}6}\geq\frac12. \tag{NC6} \]

Indeed substitution \(T=x\) in (NC2) gives this unique positive solution. Its lower bound follows by squaring the nonnegative terms in \(\sqrt{16z^4+4z^2+1}\geq1+2z^2\). Because \(T_x<0\), \(x\geq f(z)\) implies \(T(x,z)\leq x\). Direct expansion of (NC1) gives

\[ \Delta(x,z)= \frac{z\{z^4-2x^2z^2-3x^4+x^2/2+z^2/2+1/16\}} {((x+1/2)^2+z^2)^2((x-1/2)^2+z^2)^2}. \tag{NC7} \]

The numerator polynomial in \(x^2\) has negative leading coefficient, positive constant for \(z>0\), and its unique positive root is \(f(z)^2\). Thus \(\Delta\leq0\) when \(x\geq f(z)\). The exact cell contribution used in Part II's numerical supplement is

\[ S_Q=\int_Q\mathbf1_{x\geq f(z)} \sqrt{x-T(x,z)}\,[-\Delta(x,z)]\,dx\,dz. \tag{NC8} \]

The indicator is retained. Bounds on \(x-T\) proved on its support must not be asserted at all points of a rectangle crossing that support. The physical sharp constant multiplies the sum of these contributions, including all other regions and tails, by \(4/\pi\).

NC2. The exact primitive and its boundary values

For \(s,t>0\), put

\[ \begin{aligned} F(s,t)=\frac{\sqrt t}{4\sqrt2}\Bigg[& 2\arctan\!\left(1+\sqrt{2s/t}\right) -2\arctan\!\left(1-\sqrt{2s/t}\right)\\ &+\log\frac{s+\sqrt{2st}+t}{s-\sqrt{2st}+t}\Bigg]. \end{aligned} \tag{NC9} \]

Every sign, factor and argument in the author's primitive is retained. The denominator in the logarithm is positive: it equals \((\sqrt s-\sqrt{t/2})^2+t/2\). An exact integral representation, with both limits unchanged, is

\[ \begin{aligned} F(s,t) &=\int_0^s\!\int_0^t \frac{r^{3/2}v}{(r^2+v^2)^2}\,dv\,dr\\ &=\int_0^{\sqrt s}\frac{t^2}{u^4+t^2}\,du =\sqrt t\int_0^{\sqrt{s/t}}\frac{dv}{1+v^4}. \end{aligned} \tag{NC10} \]

To prove it, integration in the inner variable in the first line gives \(t^2/(2\sqrt r(r^2+t^2))\); then set \(r=u^2\), retaining \(dr=2u\,du\). The final substitution is \(u=\sqrt t\,v\). For the closed formula, differentiate the bracket in (NC9), after putting \(v=\sqrt{s/t}\). Its derivative divided by \(4\sqrt2\) is \(1/(1+v^4)\), and its value at zero is zero. This proves all three equalities, including their additive constants.

Define \(F(0,t)=F(s,0)=0\). These are the continuous boundary values: the final integral in (NC10) is bounded by \(\int_0^1 1\,dv+\int_1^\infty v^{-4}\,dv=4/3\). The nonnegative integrand also proves rectangle additivity. In particular, for a cell \([x_l,x_u]\times[z_l,z_u]\) with \(x_l\geq1/2\),

\[ \begin{aligned} J_Q={}&F(x_u-1/2,z_u)-F(x_l-1/2,z_u)\\ &-F(x_u-1/2,z_l)+F(x_l-1/2,z_l)\\ ={}&\int_Q\sqrt{x-1/2}\,K_1(x-1/2,z)\,dx\,dz\geq0. \end{aligned} \tag{NC11} \]

NC4. The repaired formula and its total increase

For a cell \(Q_{ij}=[x_l,x_u]\times[z_l,z_u]\), retain the complete endpoint and derivative formulas

CB1. The complete original integral

Retain the exact variables of the source's defining integral:

\[ K_1(r,z)=\frac{rz}{(r^2+z^2)^2},\qquad \Delta(x,z)=K_1(x+1/2,z)-K_1(x-1/2,z). \tag{CB1} \]

\[ P_{x,z}(T)=xT(T^2+xT+x^2+2z^2-1/2)-(z^2+1/4)^2=0. \tag{CB2} \]

After division by \(x\), the polynomial is negative at zero. Its derivative is strictly increasing for \(T\geq0\), since its second derivative is \(6T+2x>0\). It either increases immediately, or decreases first and then increases to infinity from a negative minimum. Thus it has exactly one positive root, with positive derivative there.

This cubic retains the paper's transport equation exactly. For \(z>0\), put

\[ \begin{aligned} D_z(r)&=((r-1/2)^2+z^2)((r+1/2)^2+z^2)\\ &=r^4+(2z^2-1/2)r^2+(z^2+1/4)^2,\\ H_z(r)&=\frac z2\left( \frac1{(r-1/2)^2+z^2}-\frac1{(r+1/2)^2+z^2}\right) =\frac{zr}{D_z(r)},\qquad H_z'(r)=\Delta(r,z). \end{aligned} \tag{CB2a} \]

Factoring the full numerator, with both original denominators retained, gives

\[ \int_T^x\Delta(r,z)\,dr =H_z(x)-H_z(T) =\frac{z(T-x)P_{x,z}(T)}{D_z(T)D_z(x)}. \tag{CB2b} \]

The two denominators are positive. Away from the trivial solution \(T=x\), the paper's zero-integral equation is therefore equivalent to (CB2). The monotonicities proved below place its positive root in \(0<T<f(z)\) whenever \(x>f(z)\), exactly the branch required in the original definition. Its positive fixed point and the original constant are

\[ \begin{aligned} f(z)&=\sqrt{\frac{1/2-2z^2+\sqrt{16z^4+4z^2+1}}6},\\ C_1(b)&=\frac4\pi\int_0^b\!\int_{f(z)}^\infty [-\Delta(x,z)]\sqrt{x-T(x,z)}\,dx\,dz. \end{aligned} \tag{CB3} \]

Substitution of \(T=x\) into (CB2) gives the formula for \(f\). The inequality \(\sqrt{16z^4+4z^2+1}\geq1+2z^2\), proved by squaring, yields \(f(z)\geq1/2\). Direct expansion gives

\[ \Delta(x,z)= \frac{z\{z^4-2x^2z^2-3x^4+x^2/2+z^2/2+1/16\}} {((x+1/2)^2+z^2)^2((x-1/2)^2+z^2)^2}. \tag{CB4} \]

The numerator polynomial in \(x^2\), for \(z>0\), has negative leading coefficient and positive constant, hence one positive root. That root is \(f(z)^2\). CB2. Root bounds and the singular distance

Set \(A=z^2+1/4\), \(P=4xT\), \(Q=4z^2+1\). The inequality \(T^2+xT+x^2\geq3xT\) and (CB2) imply \(Q^2\geq3P^2+2PQ-4P\). If \(P>Q\geq1\), the right side minus \(Q^2\) is strictly larger than its nonnegative value \(4Q(Q-1)\) at \(P=Q\): its derivative in \(P\) is \(6P+2Q-4>0\). Therefore

\[ T_x=-\frac{T^2+2xT+A^2/x^2}{D_T}<0,\qquad T_z=\frac{4z(A-xT)}{xD_T}\geq0. \tag{CB6} \]

Thus \(x-T(x,z)\) is strictly increasing in \(x\), and vanishes precisely at \(f(z)\). This also proves that \(x\geq T(x,z)\) is exactly the support condition \(x\geq f(z)\).
