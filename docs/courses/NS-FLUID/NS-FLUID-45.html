The complete uniform viscous cascade and its finite-time growth

The preceding lessons constructed the original averaged equation, its actual viscous flow and the first complete transfer. This lesson proves that the same solution transfers through every later scale with uniform bounds, and that the physical transfer times accumulate at its finite maximal endpoint.

The proof retains the actual fields, viscosity, coefficients, packet supports and physical clock. Every receiver moment starts at physical time zero. The previous band receives an input of its own, which enters both the energy and the corrected product. The complete forward weighted tail provides continuation before any later activation is used. The corrected receiving-drive range is preserved at every checkpoint, and every coarse-energy forcing interval has its own proved bound.

UI1. Original parameters and the stronger energy ranges

Fix the original unit packet system, its original \(q=1+\epsilon_0\in(1,2)\), and \(\nu>0\). Retain

\[ \chi=4\pi^2(1+\epsilon_0/2)^2,\quad \kappa_n=q^{5n/2},\quad \alpha=1/100,\quad B=K^{10}. \tag{UI1} \]

For \(a>0\) and an integer \(H\geq0\), define the exact finite sum

\[ G_a(H)=\sum_{h=1}^Hq^{-ah} =\frac{q^{-a}(1-q^{-aH})}{1-q^{-a}}, \qquad G_a(0)=0 . \tag{UI2} \]

Set

\[ \begin{gathered} C_t=(1-q^{-(5/2-\alpha)})^{-1},\qquad C=200+\frac{32q^5}{1-q^{-(10-\alpha)}},\\ S=1+q^{100}+C+(1-q^{-\alpha})^{-1} +(1-q^{-2/25})^{-1}+C_t\\ \hspace{2em}+(1-q^{-5/2})^{-1} +(1-q^{-5})^{-1} +(1-q^{-(15/2-\alpha)})^{-1},\qquad M=10^{12}S^4 . \end{gathered} \tag{UI3} \]

These are explicit positive functions of the original \(q\), not changed scale parameters. Choose

\[ \begin{gathered} K\geq M^{1000},\qquad 0<\epsilon\leq K^{-1000}\exp(-M^{100}B),\\ n_0\in\mathbb Z,\qquad n_0\geq\max\left\{1,\frac2{\log q} [\log(\nu\chi)+1000\log K+M^{100}B]\right\}. \end{gathered} \tag{UI4} \]

Thus \(\delta_0=\nu\chi q^{-n_0/2}\leq K^{-1000}\exp(-M^{100}B)\). Every parameter restriction in FT and ST follows from UI3–UI4.

Here are parameter margins used throughout the proof. Each positive summand of \(S\) is less than \(M\), and \(M\geq10^{12}\), \(M\leq K^{1/1000}\), \(K\geq10^{12000}\). Thus

\[ M^aK^{-b}\leq K^{a/1000-b}\quad(a,b\geq0). \]

In particular \(C^2q^{10}\leq M^3<K^{18}\), \((q^{20}-1)C^2\leq M^3<K^{38}\), and \(1-q^{-\alpha}>M^{-1}\), \(1-q^{-2/25}>M^{-1}\). The fixed coefficients in UI20 divided by its initial margin are at most \(100M^3K^{-19}<1/2\); the positive power of \(q\) has been retained in UI20 before this upper bound is used. The amplitude errors below obey \(41MK^{-39}<1\) and \(3MK^{-20}<1\). For the exponentially small terms, \(M^{100}>12M+4\) and the bounds on \(\epsilon,\mu\) imply

\[ \epsilon^2\exp(6MB)\leq K^{-2000}\exp(-M^{100}B),\qquad \mu\exp((3M+1)B)\leq K^{-1000}. \]

These inequalities also hold with smaller positive exponential coefficients. They justify the small weak-gate and viscosity terms without evaluating enormous exponentials numerically.

We construct actual times \(t_n<T_*\) and amplitudes \(e_n=a_n(t_n)>0\), starting with \(t_{n_0}=0,e_{n_0}=1\). At every constructed transition they will obey

\[ q^{-\alpha}e_n\leq e_{n+1}\leq q^\alpha e_n,\qquad \frac1{2\kappa_ne_n}<t_{n+1}-t_n<\frac2{\kappa_ne_n}. \tag{UI5} \]

The full band energies remain \(E_m=\tfrac12\sum_i\|u_{i,m}\|_2^2\). Throughout each completed interval \(I_n=[t_n,t_{n+1}]\), retain

\[ \begin{gathered} E_{n+1-j}\leq K^{-40}q^{j/10}e_n^2\quad(j\geq2),\qquad E_n+E_{n+1}\leq e_n^2,\\ Y_n^2:=2\sum_{m\geq n+2}q^{20(m-n-2)}E_m \leq C^2e_n^2K^{-78},\\ E_{n+1+j}\leq K^{-60}q^{-10j}e_n^2\quad(j\geq1). \end{gathered} \tag{UI6} \]

The last line follows from the weighted bound: it uses \(C^2q^{10}/2\leq K^{18}\), which UI3–UI4 imply. This retains a stronger bound than the source's \(K^{-10}\) coarse and \(K^{-30}\) forward estimates. The extra room is used in the incoming-pump calculation below.

At each noninitial checkpoint \(t_n\), the full retained data are

\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{\epsilon e_nK^{-1/2}}{25600} \leq b_n(t_n)\leq10^{-5}\epsilon e_n,\qquad \epsilon^2e_n\mathrm e^{-B}<c_n(t_n) \leq10^{-5}\epsilon^2e_n\mathrm e^{-B/2},\\ |d_n(t_n)|\leq e_nK^{-10},\qquad E_{n-1}(t_n)\leq e_n^2K^{-50},\\ \frac{\epsilon e_n}{100}\leq b_{n-1}(t_n)\leq10\epsilon e_n,\qquad \epsilon^2e_n\mathrm e^{K^9}\leq c_{n-1}(t_n) \leq\epsilon^2e_n\mathrm e^B,\\ \frac{\kappa_n}{e_n}\int_0^{t_n}a_n(s)^2\,ds \leq MK^{-1/2}. \end{gathered} \tag{UI7} \]

The stronger printed receiving-drive upper bound with exponent \(-B\) is not among these invariants. Its failure at the constructed checkpoints is proved in FT64 and ST68; the corrected range is preserved here.

UI2. Complete finite histories with the original amplitude factors

\[ A=e_N,\qquad k=\kappa_N,\qquad \mu=\frac{\nu\chi q^{-N/2}}{A}. \tag{UI8} \]

These abbreviations retain the original physical coordinates and clock. By the previous amplitude comparisons and the complete energy identity,

\[ q^{-\alpha(N-n_0)}\leq A\leq1,\qquad 0<\mu\leq\delta_0q^{-(1/2-\alpha)(N-n_0)}\leq\delta_0 . \tag{UI9} \]

For \(j\leq N-1\), the actual elapsed time from the beginning of \(I_j\) satisfies

\[ t_N-t_j\leq\frac2{\kappa_je_j} \sum_{\ell=0}^{N-j-1}q^{-(5/2-\alpha)\ell} \leq\frac{2C_t}{\kappa_je_j}. \tag{UI10} \]

\[ \begin{aligned} J_{N+r}(t_N):=\int_0^{t_N}a_{N+r}^2 &\leq\frac{4A}{k}K^{-60}q^{-10(r-1)} G_{15/2-\alpha}(H),\\ H_{N+r}(t_N):=\int_0^{t_N}(t_N-s)a_{N+r}(s)^2\,ds &\leq\frac{8C_t}{k^2}K^{-60}q^{-10(r-1)}G_5(H),\\ L_{N+r}(t_N):=\int_0^{t_N}|a_{N+r}| &\leq\frac{2\sqrt2}{k}K^{-30}q^{-5(r-1)}G_{5/2}(H). \end{aligned} \tag{UI11} \]

Here is the full calculation of the factors. On \(I_j\), put \(h=N-j\). The energy bound is \(E_{N+r}\leq K^{-60}e_j^2q^{-10(r+h-1)}\), and \(|a_{N+r}|\leq\sqrt2K^{-30}e_jq^{-5(r+h-1)}\). Multiply respectively by the actual length bound \(2/(\kappa_je_j)\), and for the weighted moment also by \(2C_t/(\kappa_je_j)\). The first product contains \(4e_j/\kappa_j\), whose ratio to \(4A/k\) is at most \(q^{(5/2+\alpha)h}\). The weighted product contains \(8C_t/\kappa_j^2\), with ratio \(q^{5h}\) to \(8C_t/k^2\). The absolute-input product contains \(2\sqrt2/\kappa_j\), with ratio \(q^{5h/2}\) to \(2\sqrt2/k\). Summing proves UI11. Each canceled amplitude factor has thus been accounted for; none of the physical contributions was dropped.

For comparison with the source's common-scale display, the exact map is \(\tau=kA(t-t_N)\) and \(\widetilde E_\ell(\tau)=A^{-2}E_{N+\ell}(t)\). On \(I_j\), with source index \(s=j+1-N\), \(e_j^2/A^2\leq q^{2\alpha(N-j)}=q^{|s-1|/50}\). Thus the original absolute-index weight is present in the comparison. UI11 improves a separate worst-case multiplication by retaining the same actual \(e_j\) in both energy and physical interval length. It does not replace the original flow by the comparison variables.

We shall use an interval of length at most \(4/(kA)\) after \(t_N\), on which the proposed UI6 bounds are retained up to their first exit. For \(m=N-1,N\), the same finite history gives

\[ J_m(t)\leq MA/k,\qquad H_m(t)\leq M/k^2,\qquad \int_0^t|X_{i,m}(s)|\,ds\leq M/k. \tag{UI12} \]

To verify these bounds, the only earlier intervals where \(m\) is one of the two active scales are \(I_{m-1},I_m\), when present. There are at most two and their indices differ from \(N\) by at most two. Their square-input integrals are at most \(4e_j/\kappa_j\), and their absolute-input integrals at most \(2\sqrt2/\kappa_j\). For the weighted moment up to the current \(t\), use \(t-s\leq(2C_t+4)/(\kappa_je_j)\leq6C_t/(\kappa_je_j)\). Each active interval contributes at most \(24C_t/\kappa_j^2\). The current interval contributes at most \(8A/k,16/k^2,4\sqrt2/k\), respectively, since its involved energies are at most \(A^2\). Every coefficient is bounded by \(10^4S^4<M\), directly from UI3. This proves UI12 for each of the four original coordinates as needed.

\[ h_{i,m}'=\nu Q_{i,m}\Delta Q_{i,m}h_{i,m} +\nu X_{i,m}Q_{i,m}\Delta\psi_{i,m}. \]

UI5. Exact incoming-pump correctors and the last exit

Exercise 1. Retain the sharper amplitude ratios

For any fixed integer \(J\), times sufficiently near \(T_*\) exceed \(t_{n_0+J}\). Thus \(H\) tends to infinity as \(t\) tends to \(T_*\). It follows that the full norm tends to infinity for every \(\sigma>a_K\) for which the norm is finite before the endpoint. In particular this proves the assertion for \(a_K<\sigma\leq10\). The smooth local theory in VD supplies finiteness at every fixed higher order for these original smooth initial data as well.

There is a sharper physical-time statement than just inserting the left bound of EX10 into EX12. Define

\[ \gamma_-(\sigma)= \begin{cases} (\sigma-a_K)/(p-a_K),&a_K<\sigma\leq p,\\ \sigma/p,&\sigma\geq p . \end{cases} \tag{EX13} \]

For \(a_K<\sigma\leq p\), the first bound in EX10 gives \(e_n\geq l/(\kappa_0q^{pH}\tau)\). Since \(\sigma-p\leq0\), the right frequency bound in EX10 gives

\[ \begin{split} \|u(t)\|_{H^\sigma} &\geq c_K(4\pi^2)^{\sigma/2}q^{\sigma n_0} \frac l{\kappa_0\tau(t)}q^{(\sigma-p)H}\\ &\geq c_K(4\pi^2)^{\sigma/2}q^{\sigma n_0} l\,U^{(\sigma-p)/(p-a_K)} [\kappa_0\tau(t)]^{-(\sigma-a_K)/(p-a_K)}. \end{split} \tag{EX14} \]

The sign of \(\sigma-p\) is essential to the direction of the second inequality.

For \(\sigma\geq p\), retain the complete amplitude in the identity

\[ q^{\sigma H}e_n =(q^{pH}e_n)^{\sigma/p}e_n^{1-\sigma/p}. \]

Since \(0<e_n\leq1\), the last factor is at least one. EX10 therefore gives

\[ \|u(t)\|_{H^\sigma}\geq c_K(4\pi^2)^{\sigma/2}q^{\sigma n_0} \left(\frac l{\kappa_0\tau(t)}\right)^{\sigma/p}. \tag{EX15} \]

The two expressions agree at \(\sigma=p\). In particular, throughout \(t_{n_0+1}\leq t<T_*\),

\[ \|u(t)\|_{H^{5/2}}\geq \frac{c_K(4\pi^2)^{5/4}l}{T_*-t}. \tag{EX16} \]

The initial scale cancels here because its original factor is \(q^{pn_0}=\kappa_0\). This is a proved cancellation, not a choice of a new scale.

Exercise 4. Retain every band and prove the matching critical upper bound

Bound the full \(H^\sigma\) norm from above for \(0\leq\sigma\leq10\), including all coarse bands and the complete weighted forward tail. Identify the exact geometric-sum threshold and prove a matching upper bound at \(\sigma=5/2\).

Solution. With the same physical interval as Exercise 3, the Fourier support of each \(u_{i,m}\) lies in the original annulus \(|\xi|\leq(1+\epsilon_0/2)q^m\). Bands below \(n_0\) vanish by VD. Consequently orthogonality and the definition \(\chi=4\pi^2(1+\epsilon_0/2)^2\) give

\[ \|u(t)\|_{H^\sigma}^2 \leq 2\sum_{m=n_0}^{\infty}(1+\chi q^{2m})^\sigma E_m(t). \tag{EX17} \]

This includes the complete band fields, not only four scalar coordinates. For \(\sigma=0\) this is equality.

The coarse part uses \(m=n+1-j\), \(2\leq j\leq H+1\). For the forward part put \(h=m-n-2\geq0\). The exact inequalities

\[ \begin{split} (1+\chi q^{2(n+1-j)})^\sigma &\leq(\chi+q^{-2n_0})^\sigma q^{2\sigma(n+1-j)},\\ (1+\chi q^{2(n+2+h)})^\sigma &\leq(1+\chi q^{2(n+2)})^\sigma q^{2\sigma h} \leq(1+\chi q^{2(n+2)})^\sigma q^{20h} \end{split} \tag{EX18} \]

hold for \(0\leq\sigma\leq10\). The first follows from \(q^{-2m}\leq q^{-2n_0}\); the last uses \(h\geq0\). Insert UI6 separately into the coarse, active and forward parts:

\[ \begin{split} \|u(t)\|_{H^\sigma}^2\leq e_n^2\bigg[ &2K^{-40}(\chi+q^{-2n_0})^\sigma q^{2\sigma(n+1)} \sum_{j=2}^{H+1}q^{(1/10-2\sigma)j}\\ &+2(1+\chi q^{2(n+1)})^\sigma\\ &+C^2K^{-78}(1+\chi q^{2(n+2)})^\sigma\bigg]. \end{split} \tag{EX19} \]

The factor \(2\) from the definition of \(E_m\) is present in both first terms. In the last term it is already part of the exact definition of \(Y_n^2\), and therefore must not be inserted a second time. Finite partial sums prove the estimates for the tail, and monotone convergence gives the full infinite sum.

For any \(d\in\mathbb R\) and integer \(H\geq0\), the full finite sum is

\[ \sum_{j=2}^{H+1}q^{dj}= \begin{cases} q^{2d}(1-q^{dH})/(1-q^d),&d\ne0,\\ H,&d=0. \end{cases} \tag{EX20} \]

For \(H=0\) both versions give the empty sum zero. For \(d\ne0\), multiplication by \(1-q^d\) cancels consecutive terms. As \(d\) tends to zero, the original finite sum tends to \(H\), so there is no singularity in the finite expression at \(\sigma=1/20\). When \(\sigma>1/20\), its uniform bound is

\[ \sum_{j=2}^{H+1}q^{(1/10-2\sigma)j} \leq \frac{q^{-2(2\sigma-1/10)}}{1-q^{-(2\sigma-1/10)}}. \tag{EX21} \]

At \(\sigma=1/20\) it grows exactly as \(H\); below this order the infinite geometric majorant cannot be used. EX19 remains valid in every case, and the original energy identity still gives \(\|u(t)\|_2\leq1\).

For \(1/20<\sigma\leq10\), define the explicit positive constant

\[ \begin{split} D_\sigma^2=(\chi+q^{-2n_0})^\sigma\bigg[ &\frac{2K^{-40}q^{2\sigma}q^{-2(2\sigma-1/10)}} {1-q^{-(2\sigma-1/10)}}\\ &+2q^{2\sigma}+C^2K^{-78}q^{4\sigma}\bigg]. \end{split} \tag{EX22} \]

For the two remaining inhomogeneous terms in EX19 use \(1+\chi q^{2m}\leq(\chi+q^{-2n_0})q^{2m}\) at \(m=n+1,n+2\). Thus the full-band estimate is

\[ \|u(t)\|_{H^\sigma}\leq D_\sigma e_n q^{\sigma n}. \tag{EX23} \]

All original constants and support factors in this comparison have been retained in EX19 and EX22.

The upper half of EX10 now yields explicit physical-time upper bounds:

\[ \begin{split} \|u(t)\|_{H^\sigma} &\leq D_\sigma\left(\frac U{\tau(t)}\right)^{\sigma/p}, &&1/20<\sigma\leq p,\\ \|u(t)\|_{H^\sigma} &\leq D_\sigma q^{\sigma n_0} \left(\frac U{\kappa_0\tau(t)}\right)^{(\sigma-a_K)/(p-a_K)}, &&p\leq\sigma\leq10. \end{split} \tag{EX24} \]

For the first line write \(e_nq^{\sigma n}=(\kappa_ne_n)^{\sigma/p}e_n^{1-\sigma/p}\); its second factor is at most one. For the second write \(e_nq^{\sigma n}=(\kappa_ne_n)q^{(\sigma-p)n}\); the exponent is nonnegative, so apply the upper frequency bound in EX10. The two expressions agree at \(\sigma=p\). In particular EX16 and EX24 prove

\[ \frac{A_{\rm low}}{T_*-t}\leq\|u(t)\|_{H^{5/2}} \leq\frac{A_{\rm high}}{T_*-t}, \quad A_{\rm low}=c_K(4\pi^2)^{5/4}l,\quad A_{\rm high}=D_{5/2}U \tag{EX25} \]

throughout \(t_{n_0+1}\leq t<T_*\). Both constants are positive, finite and given explicitly above. This is a matching order of growth for the actual averaged flow, not a proposed trajectory or a numerical fit.

Exercise 5. Determine the time-integrability consequences

Use the complete physical-time bounds to determine the critical Sobolev norm's time-integrability threshold. State exactly what the other proved rates imply, including their remaining gap.

Solution. Put \(t_{\rm start}=t_{n_0+1}\) and \(\tau_{\rm start}=T_*-t_{\rm start}>0\). For \(0<\delta<\tau_{\rm start}\) and \(r>0\), EX25 gives

\[ \begin{split} A_{\rm low}^r I_r(\delta) &\leq\int_{t_{\rm start}}^{T_*-\delta} \|u(t)\|_{H^{5/2}}^r\,dt \leq A_{\rm high}^r I_r(\delta),\\ I_r(\delta) &=\int_\delta^{\tau_{\rm start}} s^{-r}ds =\begin{cases} \bigl(\tau_{\rm start}^{1-r}-\delta^{1-r}\bigr)/(1-r), &r\ne1,\\ \log(\tau_{\rm start}/\delta),&r=1 . \end{cases} \end{split} \tag{EX26} \]

The substitution is \(s=T_*-t\), \(dt=-ds\), with both original physical endpoints retained. The antiderivative can be checked by differentiation. Letting \(\delta\) decrease to zero proves

\[ \int_0^{T_*}\|u(t)\|_{H^{5/2}}^r\,dt \begin{cases} <\infty,&0<r<1,\\ =\infty,&r\geq1 . \end{cases} \tag{EX27} \]

The integral over \([0,t_{\rm start}]\) is finite because the actual strong solution is continuous in \(H^{10}\) on that compact interval. For \(r=1\) the divergence is logarithmic between the exact constants in EX26. For \(0<r<1\) this is integrability of the positive power, without asserting that the usual \(L^r\) expression is a norm.

There is an exact weak-\(L^1\) consequence as well. Let \(F(t)=\|u(t)\|_{H^{5/2}}\) and \(B_{\rm early}=\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{\rm start}}F(t)<\infty\). For \(\lambda>\max\{B_{\rm early},A_{\rm high}/\tau_{\rm start}, A_{\rm low}/\tau_{\rm start}\}\), EX25 gives the inclusions, up to an endpoint of measure zero,

\[ (T_*-A_{\rm low}/\lambda,T_*) \ \subseteq\ \{t\in[0,T_*):F(t)>\lambda\} \ \subseteq\ (T_*-A_{\rm high}/\lambda,T_*). \]

Consequently, with \(|\cdot|\) denoting physical time Lebesgue measure,

\[ \frac{A_{\rm low}}{\lambda} \leq |\{t:F(t)>\lambda\}| \leq \frac{A_{\rm high}}{\lambda}. \tag{EX28} \]

For smaller positive \(\lambda\) the measure is at most \(T_*\). Thus \(\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda|\{F>\lambda\}|<\infty\), which is precisely membership in weak \(L^1(0,T_*)\). EX27 proves failure of membership in \(L^1(0,T_*)\). This concerns the time function of the full Sobolev norm; no spatial weak-space conclusion is being substituted for it.

For \(1/20<\sigma\leq10\), define

\[ \gamma_+(\sigma)= \begin{cases} \sigma/p,&1/20<\sigma\leq p,\\ (\sigma-a_K)/(p-a_K),&p\leq\sigma\leq10 . \end{cases} \tag{EX29} \]

Here \(\sigma>a_K\) follows from \(a_K<1/100<1/20\). EX14–EX15 and EX24 give positive finite constants \(A_\sigma,B_\sigma\), with their exact values displayed there, such that \(A_\sigma\tau^{-\gamma_-(\sigma)} \leq\|u(t)\|_{H^\sigma} \leq B_\sigma\tau^{-\gamma_+(\sigma)}\). Differentiating the same elementary antiderivative as in EX26 proves

\[ \begin{split} r\gamma_+(\sigma)<1 &\ \Longrightarrow\ \int_0^{T_*}\|u(t)\|_{H^\sigma}^r\,dt<\infty,\\ r\gamma_-(\sigma)\geq1 &\ \Longrightarrow\ \int_0^{T_*}\|u(t)\|_{H^\sigma}^r\,dt=\infty. \end{split} \tag{EX30} \]

The exact gap between the two proved exponents is

\[ \gamma_+(\sigma)-\gamma_-(\sigma) =\frac{a_K|\sigma-p|}{p(p-a_K)}. \tag{EX31} \]

For \(\sigma\leq p\), subtract \((\sigma-a_K)/(p-a_K)\) from \(\sigma/p\). For \(\sigma\geq p\), subtract in the reverse order. Both computations give the displayed nonnegative expression. It vanishes at \(\sigma=p\), producing the complete classification EX27. For other orders the estimates do not decide the intervening range \(1/\gamma_+(\sigma)\leq r<1/\gamma_-(\sigma)\); the actual amplitudes have not been identified with either bound.
