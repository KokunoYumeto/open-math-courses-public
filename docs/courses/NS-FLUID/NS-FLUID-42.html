The original five-mode circuit creates its own delayed transfer. The original circuit and its visible repairs

The original five-mode flow and all its initial data are constructed in lesson 41, QG18–QG20. That proof retains all five gates and gives \(a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2+\widetilde a^2=1\), \(c(t)>0\) for \(t>0\), and \(0\leq\widetilde a(t)\leq1\). The following computations concern that actual flow.

CR1. Differentiate the complete original rotor product

Write \(Z=\widetilde a\), and display the two auxiliary coefficients with every original contribution:

\[ \omega=\epsilon^{-2}c,\qquad p=\epsilon b+\epsilon^2e^{-K^{10}}c. \tag{CR1} \]

They are names for the retained terms; the original equations remain

\[ \begin{aligned} a'&=-\epsilon^{-2}cd-\epsilon ab-\epsilon^2e^{-K^{10}}ac =-\omega d-pa,\\ d'&=\epsilon^{-2}ca-KdZ=\omega a-KdZ,\qquad Z'=Kd^2. \end{aligned} \tag{CR2} \]

Thus, for every \(t>0\), the actual product \(M=ad\,\epsilon^2/c=ad/\omega\) has derivative

\[ \begin{aligned} (ad)'&=\omega(a^2-d^2)-(p+KZ)ad,\\ M'&=a^2-d^2- \left(\epsilon b+\epsilon^2e^{-K^{10}}c+KZ+\frac{c'}c\right)M,\\ (MZ)'&=(a^2-d^2)Z- \left(\epsilon b+\epsilon^2e^{-K^{10}}c+KZ+\frac{c'}c\right)MZ +KMd^2. \end{aligned} \tag{CR3} \]

The first line is \(a'd+ad'\) using both CR2 equations. Division by \(\omega>0\) and the derivative of \(1/\omega\) give the second. Multiplication by \(Z\) and \(Z'=Kd^2\) give the third. Every omitted-looking contribution has therefore been written explicitly. The leading sign in the source's douse display is negative; the original equations give the positive sign in CR3. Its later unweighted product display has the same sign discrepancy.

CR2. Construct the correctly signed modified energy and exact remainder

Retain the actual energy outside the final output:

\[ E=\frac12(a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2)=\frac12(1-Z^2), \qquad E'=-Kd^2Z. \tag{CR4} \]

The source uses \(E+(K/2)MZ\) after its negative-sign product display. Using the actual positive CR3 instead, the modified energy that produces the stated symmetric drain is

\[ E_{\rm corr}=E-\frac K2MZ. \tag{CR5} \]

This is a visible change of sign; the original \(E\), \(M\) and every coordinate are retained. Subtract \(K/2\) times CR3 from CR4. The two contributions to \(d^2Z\) combine to \(-Kd^2Z/2\), giving the exact identities

\[ \begin{aligned} E_{\rm corr}'={}&-\frac K2(a^2+d^2)Z\\ &+\frac K2M \left[\left(\epsilon b+\epsilon^2e^{-K^{10}}c+KZ+\frac{c'}c\right)Z-Kd^2\right],\\ E_{\rm corr}'={}&-KZE_{\rm corr}+\mathcal R,\\ \mathcal R={}&\frac K2Z(b^2+c^2) +\frac K2M \left[\left(\epsilon b+\epsilon^2e^{-K^{10}}c+\frac{c'}c\right)Z-Kd^2\right]. \end{aligned} \tag{CR6} \]

For the last two lines, use \((a^2+d^2)/2=E_{\rm corr}+(K/2)MZ-(b^2+c^2)/2\); the resulting \(K^2MZ^2/2\) terms cancel exactly. This derives the full remainder, including the small-coordinate energies, original weak pump, varying rotor frequency and output pump.

For any fixed \(0<t_1\leq t\leq t_2<\infty\), all terms are continuous and \(c>0\). Multiplying CR6 by the actual integrating factor gives

\[ E_{\rm corr}(t)= e^{-K\int_{t_1}^t Z}E_{\rm corr}(t_1) +\int_{t_1}^t e^{-K\int_s^t Z}\mathcal R(s)\,ds. \tag{CR7} \]

This retains the true starting time. In the source, strong rotor activation is proved only on \(I=[t_c+K^{-9},2]\); an estimate using that activation cannot start its product integral at \(t_c\) without an additional bound for the intervening interval. CR7 is valid on its entire displayed interval independently of that later estimate.

Here are actual explicit bounds that also remove any possible existence assumption from CR7. From QG19 and \(2|uv|\leq u^2+v^2\leq1\),

\[ |a'|\leq L_a:=\frac{\epsilon^{-2}+\epsilon+\epsilon^2e^{-K^{10}}}{2}. \tag{CR8} \]

Let \(h_0=\min\{t_1,(2L_a)^{-1}\}\). On \(0\leq s\leq h_0\), \(a(s)\geq1-L_as\geq1/2\). Set \(\beta=\epsilon^{-1}K^{10}\). The exact QG20 integral and \(b\geq-1\) then give, throughout \([t_1,t_2]\),

\[ \begin{gathered} c(t)\geq c_{\min}:= \frac{\epsilon^2e^{-K^{10}}h_0}{4}e^{-\epsilon^{-1}K^{10}t_2}>0,\\ \omega(t)\geq\Omega:=\epsilon^{-2}c_{\min},\qquad \left|\frac{c'}c\right| \leq\Lambda:=\frac{\epsilon^2e^{-K^{10}}}{c_{\min}}+\epsilon^{-1}K^{10}. \end{gathered} \tag{CR9} \]

Indeed the exponent in QG20 is at least \(-\beta(t-s)\geq-\beta t_2\), and its forcing has \(a^2\geq1/4\) on the full interval of length \(h_0\). The last inequality is the original \(c'\) equation divided by this positive lower bound. QG20 and regularization of \(\sqrt{b^2+c^2}\) as in QG2 also give

\[ \sqrt{b(t)^2+c(t)^2} \leq \sqrt{\epsilon^2+\epsilon^4e^{-2K^{10}}} \int_0^t a(s)^2\,ds \leq t\sqrt{\epsilon^2+\epsilon^4e^{-2K^{10}}}. \tag{CR10} \]

Both input terms are retained through Cauchy–Schwarz applied to their two-component vector. Since \(|ad|\leq1/2\), CR9–CR10 imply

\[ \begin{gathered} |E_{\rm corr}-E|\leq\frac K{4\Omega},\\ |\mathcal R(t)|\leq R_0:=\frac K2(\epsilon^2+\epsilon^4e^{-2K^{10}})t_2^2 +\frac K{4\Omega} \left(\epsilon+\epsilon^2e^{-K^{10}}+\Lambda+K\right),\\ |E_{\rm corr}(t)| \leq e^{-K\int_{t_1}^tZ}|E_{\rm corr}(t_1)|+R_0(t-t_1). \end{gathered} \tag{CR11} \]

The last inequality uses \(Z\geq0\) in the complete integral CR7. These are explicit proved finite-interval bounds for every positive \(K,\epsilon\). They can be large. The complete delayed-transfer proof DA1–DA37 obtains the required stronger drive bound and small remainder on its actual interval, under an explicit sufficient parameter restriction.

DA1. The actual equations and a quantitative statement

Keep all five original coordinates and coefficients:

\[ \begin{aligned} a'&=-\epsilon^{-2}cd-\epsilon ab-\epsilon^2e^{-K^{10}}ac,\\ b'&=\epsilon a^2-\epsilon^{-1}K^{10}c^2,\\ c'&=\epsilon^2e^{-K^{10}}a^2+\epsilon^{-1}K^{10}bc,\\ d'&=\epsilon^{-2}ca-Kd\widetilde a,\\ \widetilde a'&=Kd^2,\qquad (a,b,c,d,\widetilde a)(0)=(1,0,0,0,0). \end{aligned} \tag{DA1} \]

The following explicit parameter range is sufficient; no optimal size is claimed:

\[ K\geq10^{10},\qquad 0<\epsilon\leq K^{-100}e^{-10K^{10}}. \tag{DA2} \]

For these parameters there is a unique global solution. There is a first time \(t_c\) with \(c(t_c)=\epsilon^2K^{-10}\), and

\[ 1<t_c<\sqrt2<\tfrac32,\qquad 0<\sqrt2-t_c\leq\frac{10\log K+2}{K^{10}} \leq K^{-1/2}. \tag{DA3} \]

Before this first threshold, the exact quantitative bounds are

\[ \begin{gathered} 0\leq1-a(t)\leq5K^{-20},\qquad \sqrt{b(t)^2+c(t)^2}\leq3\epsilon,\\ \sqrt{d(t)^2+\widetilde a(t)^2}\leq2K^{-10} \qquad(0\leq t\leq t_c). \end{gathered} \tag{DA4} \]

At the later time and for the entire future one has

\[ \begin{gathered} 0\leq1-\widetilde a(t)\leq4K^{-80},\\ \sqrt{a(t)^2+b(t)^2+c(t)^2+d(t)^2}\leq2K^{-40} \qquad(t\geq t_c+K^{-1/2}). \end{gathered} \tag{DA5} \]

In particular DA3–DA5 imply all conclusions of the source's Theorem daet, with the original initial data, coefficients, coordinates and time variable. They also give a smaller explicit error and a more precise location of the first threshold. The proof follows without assuming any transfer estimate.

For readable calculations below, \(A:=K^{10}\) and \(Z:=\widetilde a\) are only names for the displayed original power and output coordinate. No time, amplitude or equation is rescaled.

DA2. Construct the global flow and prove all initial energy bounds

The polynomial right side of DA1 is a bilinear expression \(G(U,U)\) on \(\mathbb R^5\), where each mixed monomial may be split equally between the two input orders. With the Euclidean norm, its coefficient sum is bounded by

\[ L=2\epsilon^{-2}+2\epsilon+2\epsilon^2e^{-K^{10}} +2\epsilon^{-1}K^{10}+2K. \tag{DA6} \]

Indeed there are two terms of each of the five displayed coupling strengths in the complete vector field. The absolute value of each bilinear coordinate product is at most the product of the two Euclidean input norms, and the Euclidean output norm is at most the sum of the absolute output coordinates. Consequently \(\|G(U,V)\|\leq L\|U\|\|V\|\) and

\[ \|G(U,U)-G(V,V)\|\leq L(\|U\|+\|V\|)\|U-V\|. \]

For any initial vector \(U_*\), put \(R=1+\|U_*\|\). On the closed space of continuous functions with \(\sup_{[0,h]}\|U-U_*\|\leq R\), the integral map \(U\mapsto U_*+\int_0^tG(U(s),U(s))\,ds\) has displacement at most \(4LR^2h\) and Lipschitz constant at most \(4LRh\). Taking \(h=(8LR)^{-1}\) maps the space into itself with contraction constant at most one half. Successive iterates converge uniformly: their differences form a geometric series. Their limit is the unique fixed point, is continuously differentiable, and satisfies DA1. The same estimate on overlapping intervals proves local uniqueness.

Differentiate half the complete squared norm, retaining every contribution:

\[ \begin{aligned} \tfrac12(a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2+Z^2)'={}& -\epsilon^{-2}acd-\epsilon a^2b-\epsilon^2e^{-A}a^2c\\ &+\epsilon a^2b-\epsilon^{-1}A bc^2 +\epsilon^2e^{-A}a^2c+\epsilon^{-1}A bc^2\\ &+\epsilon^{-2}acd-Kd^2Z+Kd^2Z=0. \end{aligned} \tag{DA7} \]

Hence \(a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2+Z^2=1\). On any hypothetical finite maximal interval, DA6 bounds the derivative by \(L\), so the solution has a limit at its finite right endpoint. The same contraction construction restarts it there, contradicting maximality. The solution is therefore global, and uniqueness holds throughout it.

The original output and drive have the exact integral representations

\[ \begin{aligned} Z(t)&=K\int_0^t d(s)^2\,ds,\\ c(t)&=\epsilon^2e^{-A} \int_0^t a(s)^2 \exp\left(\epsilon^{-1}A\int_s^t b(r)\,dr\right)ds. \end{aligned} \tag{DA8} \]

The second identity follows by multiplying the original linear equation for \(c\), with its actual coefficients along the constructed solution, by its integrating factor. Since \(a(0)=1\), its integrand is positive on a nonempty initial interval. Thus \(c(t)>0\) for every \(t>0\), and \(0\leq Z(t)\leq1\) is nondecreasing on the entire half-line.

The two exact partial energy identities are

\[ \begin{aligned} (b^2+c^2)'&=2a^2(\epsilon b+\epsilon^2e^{-A}c),\\ (d^2+Z^2)'&=2\epsilon^{-2}cad. \end{aligned} \tag{DA9} \]

To integrate the first, differentiate \(\sqrt{b^2+c^2+\eta}\), where \(\eta>0\). Cauchy–Schwarz bounds its derivative above by \(a^2\sqrt{\epsilon^2+\epsilon^4e^{-2A}}\). Integrate from zero and let \(\eta\downarrow0\). The same regularization for the second identity bounds its square-root derivative above by \(\epsilon^{-2}c|a|\). These calculations prove

\[ \begin{aligned} \sqrt{b(t)^2+c(t)^2} &\leq\sqrt{\epsilon^2+\epsilon^4e^{-2A}} \int_0^t a(s)^2ds\leq\sqrt2\epsilon t,\\ \sqrt{d(t)^2+Z(t)^2} &\leq\epsilon^{-2}\int_0^t c(s)|a(s)|ds \leq\epsilon^{-2}\int_0^t c(s)ds. \end{aligned} \tag{DA10} \]

In particular \(b^2+c^2\leq9\epsilon^2\) on \([0,2]\). Inserting \(b\leq3\epsilon\) into the actual drive equation gives \(c'\leq\epsilon^2e^{-A}+3Ac\). Its integrating factor, initial value zero and positivity yield

\[ 0\leq c(t)\leq \epsilon^2e^{-A}\frac{e^{3At}-1}{3A} \leq\epsilon^2e^{5A}\qquad(0\leq t\leq2). \tag{DA11} \]

Every small forcing term remains present in the first expression; the last expression is an upper bound for that same drive.

DA3. Prove that the original threshold is reached and locate it

Let \(t_c\) be the supremum of the times \(t\in[0,2]\) for which \(c(s)\leq\epsilon^2/A\) for all \(0\leq s\leq t\). It is positive by continuity and \(c(0)=0\). All estimates in this paragraph hold up to that supremum, whether or not it equals two.

DA10 gives \(\sqrt{d^2+Z^2}\leq2/A\) on \([0,t_c]\). From DA7 and DA10,

\[ a^2\geq1-9\epsilon^2-4A^{-2}\geq1-5A^{-2}>0. \tag{DA12} \]

For the second inequality use \(9\epsilon^2\leq9K^{-200}\leq K^{-20}=A^{-2}\), valid in DA2. By continuity \(a>0\); since \(a\leq1\), this also proves \(0\leq1-a\leq1-a^2\leq5A^{-2}\).

The original equation for \(b\), including its negative term, gives

\[ \epsilon(1-6A^{-2})\leq b' =\epsilon a^2-\epsilon^{-1}Ac^2\leq\epsilon, \qquad \epsilon t(1-6A^{-2})\leq b(t)\leq\epsilon t. \tag{DA13} \]

Here \(\epsilon^{-1}Ac^2\leq\epsilon^3/A\) and \(\epsilon^2/A\leq A^{-2}\) were used; the latter follows from \(\epsilon^2\leq K^{-200}\leq A^{-1}\).

Define the actual comparison integral and a fixed lower factor by

\[ I_A(t)=\int_0^t e^{-As^2/2}ds,\qquad \ell_A=(1-5A^{-2})e^{-12/A}\geq\tfrac12. \tag{DA14} \]

The last inequality follows from \(e^{-x}\geq1-x\): \(\ell_A\geq1-5A^{-2}-12/A\geq1-13/A\geq1/2\). Use DA12–DA13 in the full integral DA8. For \(0\leq s\leq t\leq2\), its exponent lies between \(A(t^2-s^2)/2-12/A\) and \(A(t^2-s^2)/2\). The retained forcing \(a(s)^2\) lies between \(1-5A^{-2}\) and one. Therefore

\[ \ell_A\epsilon^2 e^{A(t^2/2-1)}I_A(t) \leq c(t)\leq \epsilon^2 e^{A(t^2/2-1)}I_A(t) \qquad(0\leq t\leq t_c). \tag{DA15} \]

For \(t\leq1\), its upper bound is at most \(\epsilon^2e^{-A/2}<\epsilon^2/A\). Indeed \(e^{A/2}\geq A^2/8>A\). If \(t_c\leq1\), the strict bound at \(t_c<2\) and continuity would allow a longer interval in its definition, a contradiction. Thus \(t_c>1\).

For \(t\geq1\), retaining the complete prefactor in the substitution \(v=\sqrt A s\) gives

\[ \frac{e^{-1/2}}{\sqrt A}\leq I_A(t) <\frac2{\sqrt A}. \tag{DA16} \]

The lower bound integrates only \(0\leq s\leq A^{-1/2}\). For the upper bound,

\[ \int_0^\infty e^{-v^2/2}dv\leq1+\int_1^\infty v e^{-v^2/2}dv=1+e^{-1/2}<2. \]

If \(t_c\geq3/2\), DA15–DA16 at \(3/2\) would give \(c(3/2)\geq\epsilon^2 e^{A/8-1/2}/(2\sqrt A)>\epsilon^2/A\). For this last strict inequality, \(e^{A/8}\geq1\), \(e^{-1/2}>1/2\), and \(\sqrt A>4\) suffice. This contradicts the defining bound. Consequently \(1<t_c<3/2\) and \(c(t_c)=\epsilon^2/A\). Since DA13 makes \(b>0\) before this time, DA1 gives \(c'>0\) there, so this is its first threshold time.

Substitute its exact threshold value into DA15–DA16 and take logarithms:

\[ -\tfrac12\log A-\log2 \leq A(t_c^2/2-1) \leq-\tfrac12\log A+\log2+\tfrac12<0. \tag{DA17} \]

The right side is negative because \(A\geq10^{100}>4e\). This proves \(t_c<\sqrt2\). Since \(t_c>1\),

\[ 0<\sqrt2-t_c \leq\frac{\log A+2\log2}{A(1+\sqrt2)} <\frac{10\log K+2}{K^{10}}\leq K^{-1/2}. \tag{DA18} \]

For the final inequality, \(\log K\leq\sqrt K\) on \([1,\infty)\), as differentiation shows its ratio has maximum \(2/e<1\). Thus \(10\log K+2\leq12\sqrt K\leq K^{19/2}\) in DA2. DA12 and DA10 already give all of DA4.

DA4. Bound the actual activation gap and the full rotor coefficient

The crude drive bound DA11 now supplies the needed lower bound on the original \(b\), without assuming its sign after \(t_c\):

\[ \begin{aligned} b(t)&=b(t_c)+\epsilon\int_{t_c}^t a(s)^2ds -\epsilon^{-1}A\int_{t_c}^t c(s)^2ds\\ &\geq\epsilon(1-6A^{-2})-2\epsilon^3Ae^{10A} \geq\epsilon/2\qquad(t_c\leq t\leq2). \end{aligned} \tag{DA19} \]

Here \(6A^{-2}\leq1/4\) and \(2\epsilon^2Ae^{10A}\leq2K^{-190}e^{-10A}\leq1/4\). Both are consequences of DA2. It follows from the actual \(c\) equation that

\[ c(t)\geq\epsilon^2K^{-10}e^{K^{10}(t-t_c)/2} \qquad(t_c\leq t\leq2). \tag{DA20} \]

Set

\[ s_0=t_c+K^{-9},\qquad t_1=t_c+K^{-1},\qquad t_2=t_c+K^{-1/2}. \tag{DA21} \]

DA5. Prove the transfer starts, using the correct product and interval

For \(t>0\) define the actual inverse-frequency product \(M=ad/\omega=ad\epsilon^2/c\). Direct differentiation using all terms in DA1 gives

Thus its leading sign is positive. This corrects the negative sign in the source's douse display and later unweighted product calculation.

On \([s_0,2]\), DA7 and DA22 imply

\[ |M|\leq\tfrac12K^{-100},\qquad 0\leq p+KZ+r\leq1+K+4A\leq6A. \tag{DA24} \]

For every \(s_0\leq u\leq v\leq2\), integrate DA23 with its correct sign and bound the complete product term:

\[ \left|\int_u^v(a^2-d^2)dt\right| \leq K^{-100}+3K^{-90}(v-u). \tag{DA25} \]

\[ \left|\int_{t_c}^{s_0}(a^2-d^2)dt\right|\leq K^{-9}. \tag{DA26} \]

Thus no small-frequency product at \(t_c\) is assigned the later high-frequency bound. DA25 plus DA26 also gives a complete bound for any requested integral starting at \(t_c\).

We next prove \(Z(t_1)>1/10\). Suppose instead \(Z(t_1)\leq1/10\). Monotonicity then gives this bound throughout \([s_0,t_1]\). DA7 and DA10 imply there \(a^2+d^2\geq99/100-9\epsilon^2\geq49/50\). Put \(h=t_1-s_0=K^{-1}-K^{-9}\geq(2K)^{-1}\). By DA25,

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_{s_0}^{t_1}d^2dt &=\tfrac12\int_{s_0}^{t_1}(a^2+d^2)dt -\tfrac12\int_{s_0}^{t_1}(a^2-d^2)dt\\ &\geq\left(\tfrac{49}{100}-\tfrac32K^{-90}\right)h -\tfrac12K^{-100}\\ &\geq\frac{12}{25}h-\tfrac12K^{-100} \geq\frac6{25K}-\tfrac12K^{-100}>\frac1{5K}. \end{aligned} \tag{DA27} \]

The numeric inequalities follow respectively from \(\frac32K^{-90}\leq1/100\) and \(K^{99}>25/2\), both satisfied by DA2. Since \(Z'=Kd^2\) and \(Z(s_0)\geq0\), DA27 would give \(Z(t_1)>1/5\), contradicting the supposition. Therefore

DA6. Bound every term of the repaired energy and complete the transfer

Retain the actual energy outside the output, and define its corrected companion:

\[ E=\tfrac12(a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2)=\tfrac12(1-Z^2), \qquad E_{\rm corr}=E-\tfrac K2MZ. \tag{DA29} \]

This minus sign is the visible repair of the source's plus correction. First \(E'=-Kd^2Z\) by DA7 and \(Z'=Kd^2\). Multiplying DA23 by \(Z\), and retaining its derivative, gives

\[ (MZ)'=(a^2-d^2)Z-(p+KZ+r)MZ+KMd^2. \tag{DA30} \]

Subtract \(K/2\) times this identity from \(E'\). The exact result, before any estimate, is

\[ \begin{aligned} E_{\rm corr}' &=-\tfrac K2(a^2+d^2)Z +\tfrac K2M\big[(p+KZ+r)Z-Kd^2\big]\\ &=-KZE_{\rm corr}+\mathcal R,\\ \mathcal R &=\tfrac K2Z(b^2+c^2) +\tfrac K2M\left[ \left(\epsilon b+\epsilon^2e^{-K^{10}}c+\frac{c'}c\right)Z-Kd^2 \right]. \end{aligned} \tag{DA31} \]

For the second equality substitute

\[ (a^2+d^2)/2=E_{\rm corr}+(K/2)MZ-(b^2+c^2)/2. \]

The two terms with \(K^2MZ^2/2\) cancel. This accounts for every contribution to the remainder, including the weak pump, changing rotor frequency and the output pump.

\[ \begin{aligned} |E_{\rm corr}-E|&\leq\tfrac14K^{-99},\\ |\mathcal R| &\leq\tfrac92K\epsilon^2 +\tfrac14K^{-99}(1+4A+K) \leq2K^{-89}\qquad(s_0\leq t\leq2). \end{aligned} \tag{DA32} \]

\[ g(10^{10})=99999/10-800\log10>99999/10-2400>0. \]

This proves DA5 on the whole asserted half-line, rather than just through time two, and completes the finite-dimensional theorem.

Exercise 1. Classify every polynomial first integral of degree at most two

For the original five coordinates \(U=(a,b,c,d,Z)\), keep \(\varepsilon,K>0\) and the full vector field

\[ \begin{aligned} F_1&=-\varepsilon^{-2}cd-\varepsilon ab-\varepsilon^2e^{-K^{10}}ac,\\ F_2&=\varepsilon a^2-\varepsilon^{-1}K^{10}c^2,\\ F_3&=\varepsilon^2e^{-K^{10}}a^2+\varepsilon^{-1}K^{10}bc,\\ F_4&=\varepsilon^{-2}ca-KdZ,\qquad F_5=Kd^2. \end{aligned} \tag{EX1} \]

Determine all real polynomials of degree at most two that are constant along every actual trajectory of \(U'=F(U)\).

Solution. The full energy cancellation in QG19 constructs a global real flow for every initial state, in both time directions. Thus a polynomial \(P\) is a first integral exactly when the polynomial \(\nabla P\cdot F\) vanishes identically: necessity follows by differentiating at time zero for every initial point, and sufficiency follows by the chain rule on every constructed trajectory.

Write all coefficients without assuming a diagonal form:

\[ P(U)=p_0+\sum_{i=1}^5\ell_iU_i +\sum_{i=1}^5 A_iU_i^2 +\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq5}B_{ij}U_iU_j. \tag{EX2} \]

The linear part of \(P\) contributes a homogeneous polynomial of degree two to its derivative; the quadratic part contributes degree three. They must vanish separately. In the degree-two part, the coefficients of \(ab,c^2,a^2,ac,d^2\), in that order, give

\[ \begin{gathered} -\varepsilon\ell_1=0,\qquad -\varepsilon^{-1}K^{10}\ell_2=0,\qquad \varepsilon\ell_2+\varepsilon^2e^{-K^{10}}\ell_3=0,\\ -\varepsilon^2e^{-K^{10}}\ell_1+\varepsilon^{-2}\ell_4=0, \qquad K\ell_5=0. \end{gathered} \tag{EX3} \]

Every original coefficient divided by here is strictly positive; hence all five \(\ell_i\) vanish.

For the cubic part, the following individual monomials force eight mixed coefficients to vanish:

\[ \begin{array}{c|c} \text{monomial}&\text{coefficient in }\nabla P\cdot F\\ \hline ab^2&-\varepsilon B_{12}\\ abd&-\varepsilon B_{14}\\ abZ&-\varepsilon B_{15}\\ b^2c&\varepsilon^{-1}K^{10}B_{23}\\ bcZ&\varepsilon^{-1}K^{10}B_{35}\\ bd^2&KB_{25}\\ bdZ&-KB_{24}\\ d^3&KB_{45} \end{array} \tag{EX4} \]

The coefficient of \(a^3\) is \(\varepsilon B_{12}+\varepsilon^2e^{-K^{10}}B_{13}\), so \(B_{13}=0\). The coefficient of \(ac^2\) is \(-\varepsilon^{-1}K^{10}B_{12}-\varepsilon^2e^{-K^{10}}B_{13}+\varepsilon^{-2}B_{34}\), so \(B_{34}=0\). These are all ten mixed coefficients. With them zero, the coefficients of \(a^2b,bc^2,acd,d^2Z\) are respectively

\[ \begin{gathered} 2\varepsilon(A_2-A_1),\quad 2\varepsilon^{-1}K^{10}(A_3-A_2),\\ 2\varepsilon^{-2}(A_4-A_1),\quad 2K(A_5-A_4). \end{gathered} \tag{EX5} \]

They force \(A_1=A_2=A_3=A_4=A_5\). Conversely, the full cancellation QG19 proves that every such polynomial is constant. The complete answer, including constants and all possible cross terms, is therefore

\[ P(U)=p_0+\beta(a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2+Z^2), \qquad p_0,\beta\in\mathbb R. \tag{EX6} \]

This classifies degree at most two only; it makes no claim about higher-degree or nonpolynomial integrals. For a continuous original force \(f=(f_1,\ldots,f_5)\), the same polynomial has the exact derivative \(P'=2\beta\sum_iU_if_i\), with no coordinate discarded.

The same classification holds if the final pump coefficient alone is replaced by any \(\Gamma>0\). In EX3–EX5 only the coefficients arising from that pair change from \(K\) to \(\Gamma\); each remains strictly positive, and every elimination and the converse cancellation still holds. The amplifier coefficient remains \(\varepsilon^{-1}K^{10}\) throughout.

Indeed differentiation of the first expression gives \(\kappa A^2F(U)=\kappa F(AU)\). Its initial value is the prescribed one. The reverse derivative proves the converse, and uniqueness identifies the image with the actual global physical flow. The full squared radius is \(A^2\). Neither \(\varepsilon\) nor \(K\) has changed.

Retain the explicit DA range

Thus the QG Picard construction and full radius identity give an actual global flow for every state. The last coefficient estimate follows from \(0\leq Z\leq1\), \(\Gamma\leq6K\), and \(1+6K+4K^{10}\leq6K^{10}\). In particular direct integration gives the same precise averaging bound

No averaging assertion has been extended into the shorter interval before \(s_0\).

The numerical inequality follows for every \(K\geq10^{10}\), by inserting the lower bound on \(h\). Integrating \(Z'=\Gamma d^2\), with \(\Gamma\geq K\), now gives \(Z(t_1)>0.2\), a contradiction. Hence \(Z(t_1)>1/10\).

Exercise 4. Prove a quantitative neighborhood of finite delayed transfer

Keep EX8 and \(K\leq\Gamma\leq6K\). Construct forced trajectories near the physical flow of Exercises 2–3 and give an explicit tolerance guaranteeing its delayed output. For an unforced perturbed initial point, extend the resulting output bound to all later time.

Solution. Define the complete positive coefficient bound

\[ C_F=2\left(\varepsilon^{-2}+\varepsilon +\varepsilon^2e^{-K^{10}}+\varepsilon^{-1}K^{10}+\Gamma\right). \tag{EX21} \]

Polarize each monomial of \(F_\Gamma\) symmetrically to obtain \(G_\Gamma(U,V)\). For example the term \(-\varepsilon^{-2}cd\) becomes \(-\varepsilon^{-2}(U_cV_d+V_cU_d)/2\); a square term keeps its coefficient. Summing the absolute values of all ten original vector contributions gives

\[ |G_\Gamma(U,V)|\leq C_F|U||V|,\qquad |F_\Gamma(U)-F_\Gamma(V)| \leq C_F(|U|+|V|)|U-V|. \tag{EX22} \]

The first inequality uses the Euclidean norm bounded by the sum of component absolute values. Every product is at most \(|U||V|\). The second follows from the exact bilinear difference \(G_\Gamma(U-V,U)+G_\Gamma(V,U-V)\). Sharpness of \(C_F\) is not claimed.

Let \(f:[t_0,t_0+2/(\kappa A)]\to\mathbb R^5\) be continuous and solve

\[ V'=\kappa F_\Gamma(V)+f(t),\qquad V(t_0)=V_0. \tag{EX23} \]

The QG1 Picard construction applies with the bound \(\kappa C_F\). Its exact forced energy derivative is \((|V|^2)'=2V\cdot f\); regularization by \(\sqrt{|V|^2+\zeta}\) and integration give \(|V(t)|\leq|V_0|+\int_{t_0}^t|f(s)|ds\). This bound and bounded derivatives on compact intervals extend the actual solution through the full forcing interval. No unconstructed perturbation is assumed.

Set

\[ \begin{gathered} \Delta=|V_0-(A,0,0,0,0)| +\int_{t_0}^{t_0+2/(\kappa A)}|f(t)|dt,\\ \Delta\leq A K^{-80}e^{-6C_F}. \end{gathered} \tag{EX24} \]

This is an explicit positive neighborhood, including nonzero continuous forces. For example any constant force whose Euclidean norm is at most \(\kappa A^2K^{-80}e^{-6C_F}/4\), combined with an initial displacement of at most \(AK^{-80}e^{-6C_F}/2\), satisfies it. \[ |V(t)-Y(t)|\leq\Delta e^{3\kappa A C_F(t-t_0)} \leq\Delta e^{6C_F}\leq AK^{-80} \quad\left(t_0\leq t\leq t_0+\frac2{\kappa A}\right). \tag{EX25} \]

For completeness, if \(w(t)=|V(t)-Y(t)|\), its integral inequality is \(w(t)\leq\Delta+3\kappa AC_F\int_{t_0}^t w(s)ds\). The function on the right has derivative at most \(3\kappa AC_F\) times itself and initial value \(\Delta\); its integrating factor proves EX25, including \(\Delta=0\).

\[ V_Z(T_2)\geq A(1-5K^{-80}),\qquad \sqrt{V_a(T_2)^2+V_b(T_2)^2+V_c(T_2)^2+V_d(T_2)^2} \leq3AK^{-40}. \tag{EX26} \]

These are bounds at that actual specified time, with no monotonicity assertion under arbitrary forcing.

If \(f=0\), the perturbed flow exists globally, its squared radius is the constant \(|V_0|^2\), and \(V_Z'=\kappa\Gamma V_d^2\geq0\). Write \(x=K^{-80}\). Since \(|V_0|\leq A(1+x)\), EX26 implies for all \(t\geq T_2\)

\[ \begin{gathered} A(1-5x)\leq V_Z(t)\leq A(1+x),\qquad |V_Z(t)-A|\leq5Ax,\\ V_a(t)^2+V_b(t)^2+V_c(t)^2+V_d(t)^2\\ \leq A^2\bigl[(1+x)^2-(1-5x)^2\bigr] =A^2(12x-24x^2)\leq12A^2x. \end{gathered} \tag{EX27} \]

Here \(1-5x>0\) follows from EX8, so squaring the output lower bound is legitimate. This proves a persistent delayed output on an explicitly constructed open neighborhood of the original initial state. It is a finite-circuit stability result, with its full, possibly very small tolerance retained.

Exercise 5. Locate the original threshold in a much shorter explicit interval

For the actual unit-amplitude circuit of Exercise 3, retain the original threshold \(c(t_c)=\varepsilon^2/K^{10}\). Derive a sharper bound on \(t_c\) from the entire Gaussian integrating factor, without removing its endpoint or coefficient.

Solution. Put \(B=K^{10}\) only as an abbreviation. The unchanged pre-threshold proof DA8–DA14 gives

\[ \begin{gathered} I_B(t)=\int_0^t e^{-Bs^2/2}ds,\qquad \ell_B=(1-5/B^2)e^{-12/B},\\ \ell_B\varepsilon^2e^{B(t^2/2-1)}I_B(t) \leq c(t)\leq \varepsilon^2e^{B(t^2/2-1)}I_B(t) \quad(0<t\leq t_c),\qquad 1<t_c<3/2. \end{gathered} \tag{EX28} \]

The original integral and coefficients remain in the comparison. At the actual threshold define \(\theta=c(t_c)/[\varepsilon^2e^{B(t_c^2/2-1)}I_B(t_c)]\). It is well-defined and lies in \([\ell_B,1]\). The exact substitution \(u=\sqrt B\,s\), including both endpoints, gives

\[ J_B:=\sqrt B I_B(t_c)=\int_0^{\sqrt B t_c}e^{-u^2/2}du, \qquad B(t_c^2/2-1)=-\tfrac12\log B-\log J_B-\log\theta. \tag{EX29} \]

Let \(g=\sqrt{\pi/2}\). To verify its role without an unevaluated integral, square \(\int_0^\infty e^{-u^2/2}du\), apply nonnegative two-dimensional integration, and use polar coordinates on the first quadrant. The squared integral is \(\int_0^{\pi/2}\int_0^\infty e^{-r^2/2}r\,dr\,d\phi=\pi/2\). The integral itself is positive, so it equals \(g\). For any \(R>0\), its full omitted tail satisfies

\[ 0<\int_R^\infty e^{-u^2/2}du \leq\frac1R\int_R^\infty ue^{-u^2/2}du =\frac{e^{-R^2/2}}R. \tag{EX30} \]

Since \(t_c>1\), this proves

\[ g(1-\eta_B)\leq J_B<g,\qquad \eta_B=\frac{e^{-B/2}}{g\sqrt B},\qquad D_B=-\log\ell_B-\log(1-\eta_B). \tag{EX31} \]

All quantities are positive or well-defined in EX8. Specifically \(B\geq10\) implies \(\eta_B\leq B^{-2}\): the logarithm of the equivalent inequality is \(B/2-(3/2)\log B+\log g\geq0\), which is positive at \(B=10\) and has positive derivative for \(B\geq10\). Moreover, \(-\log(1-z)\leq2z\) for \(0\leq z\leq1/2\), by integrating \((1-z)^{-1}\leq2\). Consequently, for our \(B\geq10^{100}\),

\[ 0<D_B\leq\frac{12}{B}+\frac{10}{B^2}+\frac2{B^2} \leq\frac{13}{B}. \tag{EX32} \]

Combining EX29 with \(\ell_Bg(1-\eta_B)\leq\theta J_B\leq g\) gives the complete two-sided inequality

\[ \begin{gathered} S_B=\tfrac12\log B+\log g,\qquad -S_B\leq B(t_c^2/2-1)\leq-S_B+D_B,\\ L_B=\sqrt{2-2S_B/B},\qquad L_B\leq t_c\leq\sqrt{2-2S_B/B+2D_B/B}. \end{gathered} \tag{EX33} \]

The square roots are real: \(2S_B=\log B+\log(\pi/2)\leq B\) for \(B\geq10\), since the difference is positive at ten and increasing afterwards. Thus \(L_B\geq1\). Rationalizing the difference of the two square roots in EX33 bounds its length by \(D_B/B\leq13/B^2\), since their sum is at least two. Returning every original power gives

\[ \begin{gathered} \sqrt{\displaystyle2-\frac{10\log K+\log(\pi/2)}{K^{10}}} \leq t_c\\ \leq \sqrt{\displaystyle2-\frac{10\log K+\log(\pi/2)}{K^{10}}} +13K^{-20}. \end{gathered} \tag{EX34} \]

This is an explicit interval for the actual threshold, uniform over the original allowed \(\varepsilon\) and over \(K\leq\Gamma\leq6K\). The sharper upper square root in EX33 retains the complete Gaussian tail. For initial amplitude \(A\) and physical coupling \(\kappa\), EX7–EX9 multiply the interval length by exactly \((\kappa A)^{-1}\) and add \(t_0\) to its endpoints. In particular the original choice \(\kappa=q^{5n/2}\) retains the full length bound \(13q^{-5n/2}A^{-1}K^{-20}\). No claim about the remaining infinite cascade is needed for any of these finite-flow conclusions.
