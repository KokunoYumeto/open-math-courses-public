Arrangements, tails and the local lemma

Written by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), October 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. Public domain (CC0).

The construction of Van der Waerden numbers chooses colours at random and must show that, with positive probability, no long progression is monochromatic. Three tools make this work, and this lesson proves them: a bound on the number of sign patterns of affine functions, which will count the possible colour patterns along progressions (Section 1); an exponential tail bound for fair coin flips (Section 2); and the finite asymmetric local lemma of Erdős and Lovász [EL] (Section 3). Section 4 proves an elementary prime bound used to choose the modulus.

1. Sign vectors of affine functions

An affine function on \(\mathbb R^e\) is a function \(g(x)=\langle w,x\rangle+\beta\) with \(w\in\mathbb R^e\setminus\{0\}\) and \(\beta\in\mathbb R\); its zero set is an affine hyperplane. For affine functions \(g_1,\dots,g_s\), the sign vector of a point \(x\) is \((\operatorname{sgn}g_1(x),\dots,\operatorname{sgn}g_s(x))\in\{-,0,+\}^s\), and a sign vector is realized on a set \(\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^e\) if it is the sign vector of some point of \(\Omega\). The bound below is the standard region count for hyperplane arrangements (see Stanley [Sta]), extended to sign vectors with zeros.

Lemma 1.1 (Sign vectors). The number of sign vectors in \(\{-,+\}^s\) realized on \(\mathbb R^e\) is at most \(\sum_{j=0}^{e}\binom sj\). The number of sign vectors in \(\{-,0,+\}^s\) realized on any subset \(\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^e\) is at most \[ \sum_{d=0}^{e}\binom sd\sum_{j=0}^{e-d}\binom sj\le(e+1)^2(s+1)^e . \]

Proof. Strict sign vectors. Let \(\rho(s,e)\) be the largest number of strict sign vectors realized by \(s\) affine functions on \(\mathbb R^e\) (or on an affine space of dimension \(e\)). Then \(\rho(0,e)=1\) and \(\rho(s,0)\le1\). The set of points with a given strict sign vector is an intersection of open half-spaces, hence convex; call it a cell. Add a function \(g\) with zero set \(Z\) to \(s-1\) given functions. A cell \(K\) of the old functions splits into the parts where \(g>0\) and \(g<0\). If both parts are nonempty, a segment in \(K\) joining them meets \(Z\) (by continuity), so \(K\cap Z\) is a nonempty cell of the restrictions of the old functions to \(Z\); distinct cells \(K\) give distinct, disjoint such cells. (Restrictions that are constant on \(Z\) only merge cells and do not increase the count.) Hence \(\rho(s,e)\le\rho(s-1,e)+\rho(s-1,e-1)\), and by induction with Pascal's rule \(\binom{s-1}{j}+\binom{s-1}{j-1}=\binom sj\), \[ \rho(s,e)\le\sum_{j=0}^{e}\binom{s-1}j+\sum_{j=0}^{e-1}\binom{s-1}j=\sum_{j=0}^{e}\binom sj . \]

Sign vectors with zeros. Let a sign vector be realized at a point \(x\), let \(\mathcal Z\) be the set of indices with sign \(0\), and let \(A=\bigcap_{i\in\mathcal Z}\{g_i=0\}\), an affine space containing \(x\). If \(A\) has codimension \(d\), then \(d\) of the equations, with linearly independent normals, already define \(A\), because every other equation of \(\mathcal Z\) is a linear consequence of these on the consistent system; there are at most \(\binom sd\) choices of them. The indices outside \(\mathcal Z\) are exactly those whose functions do not vanish identically on \(A\) (they do not vanish at \(x\)), and their signs form a strict sign vector of their restrictions to \(A\), of dimension \(e-d\); there are at most \(\sum_{j\le e-d}\binom sj\) of these. Summing over \(d\) gives the first bound. Each product satisfies \(\binom sd\binom sj\le(s+1)^{d+j}\le(s+1)^e\), and there are at most \((e+1)^2\) pairs \((d,j)\). Restricting to \(\Omega\) can only remove sign vectors. \(\square\)

Corollary 1.2. For \(s\) affine functions on \(\mathbb R^2\), at most \(9(s+1)^2\) sign vectors are realized; on \(\mathbb R^3\), at most \(16(s+1)^3\).

2. Tails of fair coin flips

Lemma 2.1. Let \(X\) be the number of ones among \(l\) independent uniform bits. Then \[ \Pr\bigl(|X-l/2|\ge l/4\bigr)\le2e^{-l/8}. \]

Proof. For one bit \(\xi\), \(\mathbb E e^{\pm(\xi-1/2)}=\cosh(1/2)\). Comparing the power series term by term, \((2j)!\ge2^jj!\) gives \(\cosh t\le e^{t^2/2}\). By independence, \(\mathbb Ee^{\pm(X-l/2)}=\cosh(1/2)^l\le e^{l/8}\). Markov's inequality gives \(\Pr(\pm(X-l/2)\ge l/4)\le e^{l/8}e^{-l/4}=e^{-l/8}\) for each sign. \(\square\)

3. The finite asymmetric local lemma

Lemma 3.1 (Local lemma). Let \(\mathcal E\) be a finite family of events, and let each \(E\in\mathcal E\) have a set \(\Gamma(E)\subseteq\mathcal E\setminus\{E\}\) of neighbours such that \(E\) is independent of the \(\sigma\)-algebra generated by the events outside \(\{E\}\cup\Gamma(E)\). If numbers \(z_E\in(0,1)\) satisfy \[ \Pr(E)\le z_E\prod_{F\in\Gamma(E)}(1-z_F)\qquad(E\in\mathcal E), \] then \(\Pr\bigl(\bigcap_{E\in\mathcal E}E^c\bigr)>0\).

Proof. For \(\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal E\) put \(A_{\mathcal S}=\bigcap_{F\in\mathcal S}F^c\). We prove by induction on \(|\mathcal S|\) that \(\Pr(A_{\mathcal S})>0\) and \(\Pr(E\mid A_{\mathcal S})\le z_E\) for every \(E\notin\mathcal S\). For \(\mathcal S=\varnothing\) this is the hypothesis. Let \(\mathcal S\ne\varnothing\). For \(F\in\mathcal S\), \(\Pr(A_{\mathcal S})=\Pr(A_{\mathcal S\setminus\{F\}})(1-\Pr(F\mid A_{\mathcal S\setminus\{F\}}))\ge\Pr(A_{\mathcal S\setminus\{F\}})(1-z_F)>0\). For \(E\notin\mathcal S\), let \(\mathcal S_1=\mathcal S\cap\Gamma(E)\) and \(\mathcal S_2=\mathcal S\setminus\mathcal S_1\). If \(\mathcal S_1=\varnothing\), independence gives \(\Pr(E\mid A_{\mathcal S})=\Pr(E)\le z_E\). Otherwise list \(\mathcal S_1=\{F_1,\dots,F_m\}\); each set \(\mathcal S_2\cup\{F_1,\dots,F_{i-1}\}\) has fewer than \(|\mathcal S|\) elements, so \[ \Pr(A_{\mathcal S_1}\mid A_{\mathcal S_2})=\prod_{i=1}^m\Bigl(1-\Pr\bigl(F_i\bigm|A_{\mathcal S_2\cup\{F_1,\dots,F_{i-1}\}}\bigr)\Bigr)\ge\prod_{F\in\mathcal S_1}(1-z_F), \] and, since \(E\) is independent of the events of \(\mathcal S_2\), \[ \Pr(E\mid A_{\mathcal S})=\frac{\Pr(E\cap A_{\mathcal S_1}\mid A_{\mathcal S_2})}{\Pr(A_{\mathcal S_1}\mid A_{\mathcal S_2})}\le\frac{\Pr(E)}{\prod_{F\in\mathcal S_1}(1-z_F)}\le z_E . \] Taking \(\mathcal S=\mathcal E\) gives the conclusion. \(\square\)

If every event is determined by a set of independent random variables, joining two events when their sets of variables intersect gives a family of neighbours with the required independence.

4. A prime bound

For a prime \(\ell\) and a positive integer \(a\), let \(v_\ell(a)\) be the exponent of \(\ell\) in \(a\).

Lemma 4.1. For all sufficiently large integers \(k\), there is a prime \(P\) with \(k<P\le2k^2\).

Proof. Put \(n=k^2\). The number of multiples of \(\ell^a\) in \([m]\) is \(\lfloor m/\ell^a\rfloor\), so \(v_\ell(m!)=\sum_{a\ge1}\lfloor m/\ell^a\rfloor\), and \[ v_\ell\binom{2n}n=\sum_{a\ge1}\Bigl(\Bigl\lfloor\frac{2n}{\ell^a}\Bigr\rfloor-2\Bigl\lfloor\frac n{\ell^a}\Bigr\rfloor\Bigr)\le\lfloor\log_\ell(2n)\rfloor, \] because each summand is \(0\) or \(1\) and vanishes when \(\ell^a>2n\). So \(\ell^{v_\ell(\binom{2n}n)}\le2n\). If every prime factor of \(\binom{2n}n\) were at most \(k\), then \(\binom{2n}n\le(2n)^k\). But \(\binom{2n}n\) is the largest of the \(2n+1\) terms of \((1+1)^{2n}\), so \(\binom{2n}n\ge4^n/(2n+1)\), and \(k^2\log4-\log(2k^2+1)>k\log(2k^2)\) for all large \(k\) (the left side grows like \(k^2\), the right like \(k\log k\)). Hence some prime \(P>k\) divides \(\binom{2n}n\), and \(P\le2n\) because it divides \((2n)!\). \(\square\)

5. Exercises

5.1. Two non-parallel lines in \(\mathbb R^2\): count the realized sign vectors in \(\{-,0,+\}^2\) and compare with the bound of Lemma 1.1.

5.2. Deduce from Lemma 3.1 the symmetric local lemma: if every event has probability at most \(p\) and at most \(\Delta\) neighbours, and \(ep(\Delta+1)\le1\), then with positive probability no event occurs.

5.3. Check Lemma 4.1 directly for \(k=3,\dots,10\), and show that the final inequality of its proof already holds for every \(k\ge3\).

5.4. Show that Lemma 2.1 cannot be improved to \(\Pr(|X-l/2|\ge l/4)\le e^{-l/2}\) for all \(l\).

6. Solutions

5.1. The two lines cross at one point and divide the plane into four open quadrants, four open half-lines and the crossing point: \(4+4+1=9\) sign vectors, all of \(\{-,0,+\}^2\). The bound is \(\binom20(1+2+1)+\binom21(1+2)+\binom22\cdot1=11\).

5.2. Put \(z_E=1/(\Delta+1)\). Then \(z_E\prod_{F\in\Gamma(E)}(1-z_F)\ge\frac1{\Delta+1}\bigl(1-\frac1{\Delta+1}\bigr)^{\Delta}\ge\frac1{e(\Delta+1)}\ge p\), using \((1-\frac1{\Delta+1})^\Delta\ge e^{-1}\).

5.3. For \(k=3,\dots,10\) the primes \(5,7,11,11,13,17,19,23\) lie in \((k,2k^2]\). For the inequality \(k^2\log4-\log(2k^2+1)>k\log(2k^2)\): at \(k=3,4,5\) the two sides are about \(9.53>8.67\), \(18.68>13.86\) and \(30.73>19.56\). For \(k\ge6\), using \(2k^2+1\le3k^2\) and \(\log k\le k/2\), \[ k\log(2k^2)+\log(2k^2+1)\le(k+1)\log(3k^2)\le(k+1)(\log3+k)<k^2\log4, \] since \((\log4-1)k^2>(1+\log3)k+\log3\) for \(k\ge6\).

5.4. For \(l=8\), \(\Pr(|X-4|\ge2)=2\bigl(\binom80+\binom81+\binom82\bigr)/2^8=74/256\approx0.29\), while \(e^{-4}\approx0.018\). So the exponent \(1/2\) fails.
