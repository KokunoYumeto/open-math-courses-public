Independent gradients and tangent directions

Written by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), October 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. Public domain (CC0).

This lesson extracts local geometry from the limiting measures of Limiting measures [OpenAI-HL, Section 4]. A fixed pair of windows \(A,B\) gives every label a position \(p\) in the open unit square, and every other rank coordinate becomes, in the limit, a monotone function \(f_C\) of \(p\), differentiable at almost every \(p\). Two consequences are proved. First, on a positive part of edge mass, every window active at a label contains two windows whose gradients at \(p\) are independent; this uses the crossing measures and the critical-image lemma of Monotone functions of two variables. Second, lines of a fixed direction \(t\) of the sweep have, after zooming in at \(p\), limiting directions confined to an arc \(D_p(t)\), and arcs for different times are disjoint when edge activity separates the times. Three times with three different single directions are found; the next lessons turn them into a Euclidean normalization.

We keep the subsequence of configurations of Limiting measures and pass to further subsequences when needed; every statement about "late configurations" refers to the current subsequence.

1. A pair of windows

By Lemma 3.1 of Limiting measures, for \(\lambda\)-almost every \(z\) the measure \(h_z\) has no atoms, so its support in \((0,1)\) is infinite, and two dyadic windows whose closures are disjoint and lie in \((0,1)\) both have positive \(h_z\)-mass. There are countably many pairs of windows, so we can fix such windows \(K,K'\) for which \[ E=\{z:h_z(K)>0,\ h_z(K')>0\} \] has \(\lambda(E)>0\).

Lemma 4.1 of Limiting measures, applied to \(K'\), and \(\lambda_{K'}=h_z(K')\lambda\) with \(h_z(K')>0\) on \(E\), show that \(\lambda|_E\) vanishes on every set that is null for all crossing measures \(\sigma_{CD}\) with \(C,D\subseteq K'\) disjoint. Let \(\Sigma=\sum c_{CD}\sigma_{CD}\) over these countably many pairs, with positive weights and finite total mass, and let \(g_{CD}\) be a density of \(\sigma_{CD}\) with respect to \(\Sigma\) [Fremlin, 232E]. Since \(\sum c_{CD}g_{CD}=1\) \(\Sigma\)-almost everywhere, the sets \(\{g_{CD}>0\}\) cover \(Z\) up to a \(\Sigma\)-null, hence \(\lambda|_E\)-null, set; so one of them, \(\{g_{AB}>0\}\), meets \(E\) in a set of positive \(\lambda\)-measure. Replace \(E\) by this intersection, and name the windows so that \(A\) precedes \(B\). If \(F\subseteq E\) is \(\sigma_{AB}\)-null, then \(\int_Fg_{AB}\,d\Sigma=0\), so \(\Sigma(F)=0\) and \(\lambda(F)=0\). Thus \[ \lambda|_E\ \text{vanishes on }\sigma_{AB}\text{-null sets.} \] For a label \(z\) put \(p(z)=(z_A,z_B)\in[0,1]^2\). By Lemma 1.1(c) of Limiting measures, the image of \(\sigma_{AB}\) under \(p\) is at most Lebesgue measure, so the image of \(\lambda|_E\) under \(p\) vanishes on Lebesgue-null sets: it is absolutely continuous. In particular \(p(z)\in(0,1)^2\) for \(\lambda\)-almost every \(z\in E\).

2. Limit functions

Every window \(C\subseteq K\) is disjoint from \(A\) and \(B\), and since \(K\) and \(K'\) are disjoint, \(C\) either precedes both or follows both. By Proposition 3.3 of Rank coordinates, in each configuration \[ F_C^{(n)}=r_C\circ(r_A,r_B)^{-1}:(0,1)^2\to(0,1) \] is monotone in each coordinate with signs \((+,-)\) if \(C\) precedes \(A\) and \((-,+)\) if \(B\) precedes \(C\). Reflecting a coordinate where it decreases, we treat it as nondecreasing in both; write \(u<w\) when \(u_1<w_1\) and \(u_2<w_2\) in the reflected coordinates. Let \(G\) be the points of \((0,1)^2\) with rational coordinates. A diagonal subsequence makes \(F_C^{(n)}(q)\) converge, to \(\phi_C(q)\), for all \(q\in G\) and all the countably many \(C\). Define \[ \begin{gathered} f_C(p)=\sup\{\phi_C(q):q\in G,\ q<p\},\\ \bar f_C(p)=\inf\{\phi_C(q):q\in G,\ q>p\}. \end{gathered} \] Both are nondecreasing and \(f_C\le\bar f_C\); \(f_C\) is Borel, as a countable supremum of Borel functions. Call \(p\) regular for \(C\) if \(f_C(p)=\bar f_C(p)\).

Lemma 2.1. (a) If \(p\) is regular for \(C\) and \(p_n\to p\), then \(F_C^{(n)}(p_n)\to f_C(p)\).

(b) Every point where \(f_C\) is continuous is regular for \(C\). Almost every point of \((0,1)^2\) is regular, and \(f_C\) is differentiable at almost every point.

Proof. (a) For grid points \(q^-<p<q^+\), eventually \(q^-<p_n<q^+\), so \(F_C^{(n)}(q^-)\le F_C^{(n)}(p_n)\le F_C^{(n)}(q^+)\); in the limit, \[ \begin{aligned} \phi_C(q^-)&\le\liminf F_C^{(n)}(p_n)\\ &\le\limsup F_C^{(n)}(p_n)\le\phi_C(q^+). \end{aligned} \] Taking the supremum over \(q^-\) and the infimum over \(q^+\) gives \(f_C(p)\le\liminf\) and \(\limsup\le\bar f_C(p)=f_C(p)\).

(b) If \(p<p''\), a grid point \(q\) with \(p<q<p''\) has \(\bar f_C(p)\le\phi_C(q)\le f_C(p'')\). Letting \(p''\to p\) at a continuity point gives \(\bar f_C(p)\le f_C(p)\). By Theorem 2.2 of Monotone functions of two variables, \(f_C\), which is bounded, Borel and monotone in each variable, is differentiable, hence continuous, almost everywhere. \(\square\)

Lemma 2.2 (coordinate identities). Let \(z\) lie in the support of \(\lambda\), with \(p=p(z)\in(0,1)^2\) regular for \(C\). Then \(z_C=f_C(p)\).

Proof. For every \(k\), the ball of radius \(1/k\) about \(z\) has positive \(\lambda\)-mass, so by Lemma 2.1(a) of Measures on compact metric spaces it contains the label of a left endpoint of an edge in every late configuration. Choosing diagonally, there are vertices \(q_n\) of configuration \(n\) whose labels \(z^{(n)}\) tend to \(z\). Then \(p(z^{(n)})=(r_A,r_B)(q_n)\to p\) and \(z^{(n)}_C=r_C(q_n)=F_C^{(n)}(p(z^{(n)}))\), which tends to \(f_C(p)\) by Lemma 2.1(a); it also tends to \(z_C\). \(\square\)

The points \(p\) that fail to be regular for some \(C\), or where some \(f_C\) is not differentiable, form a Lebesgue-null set, a countable union of null sets. Its preimage under \(p\) is \(\lambda|_E\)-null, by absolute continuity. Removing it, and the \(\lambda\)-null complement of the support of \(\lambda\), from \(E\), we may assume that for every \(z\in E\): \(p=p(z)\in(0,1)^2\), every \(f_C\) with \(C\subseteq K\) is differentiable at \(p\), and \(z_C=f_C(p)\).

3. Independent gradients

Lemma 3.1. After removing a further \(\lambda\)-null set from \(E\), every \(z\in E\) has the following property: if \(J\subseteq K\) is a window with \(h_z(J)>0\), then some disjoint windows \(C,D\subseteq J\) satisfy \[ \det\begin{pmatrix}\nabla f_C(p)\\\nabla f_D(p)\end{pmatrix}\ne0,\qquad p=p(z). \]

Proof. Fix \(J\subseteq K\), and let \(B_J\) be the set of \(z\in E\) with \(h_z(J)>0\) at which the determinant vanishes for all disjoint \(C,D\subseteq J\). For each such pair, Lemma 3.1 of Monotone functions of two variables, applied to \(F=(f_C,f_D)\) on \((0,1)^2\), gives a Borel null set \(N_{CD}\) containing the values \(F(p)\) at the points where \(F\) is differentiable with \(\det DF(p)=0\). For \(z\in B_J\), \((z_C,z_D)=(f_C(p),f_D(p))\in N_{CD}\), by Lemma 2.2. By Lemma 1.1(c) of Limiting measures, the image of \(\sigma_{CD}\) under \(z\mapsto(z_C,z_D)\) is at most Lebesgue measure, so \(\sigma_{CD}(B_J)=0\). This holds for all disjoint \(C,D\subseteq J\), so Lemma 4.1 of Limiting measures gives \(\lambda_J(B_J)=0\), that is, \(\int_{B_J}h_z(J)\,d\lambda=0\). Since \(h_z(J)>0\) on \(B_J\), \(\lambda(B_J)=0\). Remove \(B_J\) for all countably many \(J\). \(\square\)

4. First-order expansions

Fix \(z\in E\) and \(p=p(z)\).

Lemma 4.1. Let \(\mathcal C\) be a finite set of windows in \(K\), \(\rho<\infty\) and \(\epsilon>0\). There is \(h_0>0\) such that for every \(h\in(0,h_0)\) there is \(n_0(h)\) with \[ \begin{aligned} &\bigl|F_C^{(n)}(p+hw)-f_C(p)\\ &\quad-h\nabla f_C(p)\cdot w\bigr|\le\epsilon h \end{aligned} \] for all \(C\in\mathcal C\), \(|w|\le\rho\) and \(n\ge n_0(h)\).

Proof. Let \(\eta>0\) be small. By differentiability, for \(h<h_0\) the function \(f_C(p+hw)\) differs from \(f_C(p)+h\nabla f_C(p)\cdot w\) by at most \(\eta h\) for \(|w|\le\rho+1\) and all \(C\in\mathcal C\). Fix such \(h\). Regular points are dense, so there is a finite set of regular points \(q\) in the disk of radius \((\rho+1)h\) about \(p\) such that every point \(x=p+hw\), \(|w|\le\rho\), has points of the set in each of the four closed quadrants with vertex \(x\), within distance \(\eta h/(1+\max_C|\nabla f_C(p)|)\) of \(x\). For each \(C\), two of them satisfy \(q^-\le x\le q^+\) in the order of the reflected chart of \(C\). By Lemma 2.1(a) with constant sequences, \(F_C^{(n)}(q)\to f_C(q)\) at these finitely many points; take \(n_0(h)\) with all errors at most \(\eta h\). Monotonicity squeezes \(F_C^{(n)}(p+hw)\) between \(F_C^{(n)}(q^\pm)\), which are within \(\eta h+\eta h+\eta h\) of \(f_C(p)+h\nabla f_C(p)\cdot w\). Choose \(\eta=\epsilon/3\). \(\square\)

A fast diagonal is a pair of sequences \(h_j\downarrow0\) and indices \(n_j\to\infty\) such that for every finite \(\mathcal C\), every \(\rho\) and every \(\epsilon>0\), the inequality of Lemma 4.1 holds with \(h=h_j\), \(n=n_j\) for all large \(j\). Fast diagonals exist: enumerate the windows of \(K\) as \(C_1,C_2,\dots\), and for each \(j\) choose \(h_j<h_0\) and \(n_j\ge n_0(h_j)\) for \(\mathcal C=\{C_1,\dots,C_j\}\), \(\rho=j\) and \(\epsilon=1/j\). Subsequences of fast diagonals are fast diagonals. For a point \(X\) of configuration \(n_j\), its zoom coordinate is \(w=((r_A,r_B)(X)-p)/h_j\).

5. Tangent directions

For \(t\) in the interior \(K^\circ\) of \(K\), the direction \(\varsigma(t)\) of Rank coordinates lies, in late configurations, outside the spans of \(A\) and \(B\), on the same side of both, because \(t\) and \(K'\) are separated by a positive gap. By Exercise 7.3 of Rank coordinates, along a line of direction \(t\), oriented by increasing first coordinate, both \(r_A\) and \(r_B\) increase strictly if the span of \(K\) lies above those of \(A,B\), and both decrease strictly otherwise. Let \(Q\) be the corresponding closed quadrant, \(\{w:w_1,w_2\ge0\}\) or \(\{w:w_1,w_2\le0\}\); forward displacements along lines of direction \(t\) have rank displacements in \(Q\).

Say that a window \(C\subseteq K\) lies strictly before \(t\) if its closure lies in \([0,t)\), and strictly after \(t\) if its closure lies in \((t,1]\). For \(t\in K^\circ\) let \(D_p(t)\) be the set of unit vectors \(d\in Q\) with \(\nabla f_C(p)\cdot d\ge0\) for every window \(C\subseteq K\) lying strictly before \(t\), and \(\nabla f_C(p)\cdot d\le0\) for every \(C\subseteq K\) lying strictly after \(t\). It is the intersection of the unit circle with a closed convex cone inside \(Q\), so a closed arc of length at most \(\pi/2\), or empty.

Lemma 5.1 (directions of lines). Let \((h_j,n_j)\) be a fast diagonal and \(t\in K^\circ\). Let \(X_j,Y_j\) lie on one line of direction \(t\) in configuration \(n_j\), with \(Y_j\) forward of \(X_j\), and suppose their zoom coordinates converge to \(w\ne w'\). Then \((w'-w)/|w'-w|\in D_p(t)\). Moreover \(D_p(t)\) is not empty.

Proof. The rank displacement from \(X_j\) to \(Y_j\) lies in \(Q\), so \(w'-w\in Q\). Let \(C\subseteq K\) lie strictly before \(t\). In late configurations the span of \(C\) lies below \(\varsigma(t)\), so \(r_C\) increases along the line (Exercise 7.3 of Rank coordinates), and \(F_C^{(n_j)}\) at the rank point of \(Y_j\) is at least its value at that of \(X_j\). By the fast diagonal, these values are \(f_C(p)+h_j\nabla f_C(p)\cdot w_j+o(h_j)\) and the same with \(w'_j\), where \(w_j\to w\) and \(w'_j\to w'\) are the zoom coordinates; so \(\nabla f_C(p)\cdot(w'-w)\ge0\). For \(C\) strictly after \(t\) the inequality is reversed.

For non-emptiness, let \(X_j\) be the point of configuration \(n_j\) with \((r_A,r_B)(X_j)=p\), and follow the forward ray of direction \(t\) from \(X_j\). The preimage of the closed disk of radius \(h_j\) about \(p\) is compact, by Proposition 3.2 of Rank coordinates, so the ray leaves it; let \(Y_j\) be its first point with zoom coordinate of length one. Along a subsequence these zoom coordinates converge to some \(w'\) of length one, and the first part gives \(w'\in D_p(t)\). \(\square\)

Lemma 5.2 (separated directions). Let \(s<t\) lie in \(K^\circ\) with \(h_z((s,t))>0\). Then \(D_p(s)\cap D_p(t)=\varnothing\). There are times \(\tau_1<\tau_2<\tau_3\) in \(K^\circ\) for which \(D_p(\tau_i)\) are single unit vectors \(d_i\), no two of them parallel.

Proof. Choose a window \(J\) whose closure lies in \((s,t)\) with \(h_z(J)>0\); one exists, since \((s,t)\) is a countable union of such windows. By Lemma 3.1 there are disjoint \(C,D\subseteq J\) with independent gradients. Both lie strictly after \(s\) and strictly before \(t\). A vector in \(D_p(s)\cap D_p(t)\) would have nonpositive and nonnegative scalar products with both gradients, hence zero products, and would be zero.

Let \(T\) be the support of \(h_z\) in \(K^\circ\), with the endpoints of the components of \(K^\circ\setminus T\) removed; only countably many points are removed, and \(h_z(T)=h_z(K)>0\) since \(h_z\) has no atoms. Two points \(s<t\) of \(T\) have \(h_z((s,t))>0\): otherwise \((s,t)\) would lie in a component of the complement of the support, with \(s\) and \(t\) among its endpoints. So the arcs \(D_p(t)\), \(t\in T\), are pairwise disjoint and nonempty. Disjoint arcs of positive length contain different rational angles, so only countably many \(t\in T\) have \(D_p(t)\) of positive length; removing them leaves a set of positive \(h_z\)-measure, in particular three points \(\tau_1<\tau_2<\tau_3\). Their directions lie in the quadrant \(Q\) and are distinct, so no two are parallel. \(\square\)

6. One triple for all labels

The times of Lemma 5.2 depend on \(z\). A common normalization of the configurations needs one fixed triple. For every \(z\in E\), since \(h_z\) has no atoms and \(h_z(K)>0\), there are rational times \(t_1<t_2<t_3\) in \(K^\circ\) with \(h_z((t_1,t_2))>0\) and \(h_z((t_2,t_3))>0\). There are countably many rational triples, so one triple serves on a subset of \(E\) of positive \(\lambda\)-measure; then a window \(K_0\) with closure in \((t_1,t_2)\) has \(h_z(K_0)>0\) on a subset of positive measure. Restrict \(E\) to it. We have reached:

Proposition 6.1. There are windows \(A,B\) and \(K_0\), times \(t_1<t_2<t_3\) in \(K^\circ\) with \(K_0\) inside \((t_1,t_2)\), and a Borel set \(E\subseteq Z\) with \(\lambda_{K_0}(E)>0\), such that the image of \(\lambda|_E\) under \(p\) is absolutely continuous, and for every \(z\in E\) and \(p=p(z)\): every \(f_C\), \(C\subseteq K\), is differentiable at \(p\) with \(z_C=f_C(p)\); Lemma 3.1 holds at \(z\); \(D_p(t_1),D_p(t_2),D_p(t_3)\) are pairwise disjoint closed arcs; and three times \(\tau_1<\tau_2<\tau_3\) in \(K^\circ\) have single, pairwise non-parallel directions \(D_p(\tau_i)=\{d_i\}\).

The disjointness of the three arcs follows from Lemma 5.2, since \(h_z((t_1,t_2))\), \(h_z((t_2,t_3))\) and \(h_z((t_1,t_3))\) are positive. \(\lambda_{K_0}(E)=\int_Eh_z(K_0)\,d\lambda>0\).

7. Exercises

7.1 (easy). Show that the intersection of a closed convex cone contained in a closed quadrant with the unit circle is a closed arc of length at most \(\pi/2\), or empty.

7.2 (medium). In Lemma 2.1, give an example of nondecreasing functions \(F^{(n)}\) on \((0,1)\), converging at rational points, for which \(F^{(n)}(p_n)\) does not converge to \(f(p)\) at a point \(p\) where \(f\) jumps. Why does this not harm the argument?

7.3 (medium). Why must \(C\) and \(D\) in Lemma 3.1 be windows inside \(J\), rather than arbitrary windows of \(K\)?

8. Solutions

7.1. In polar angle, a closed convex cone of angle less than \(\pi\) is the set of vectors with angle in a closed interval; inside a quadrant this interval has length at most \(\pi/2\).

7.2. Let \(F^{(n)}=\mathbf 1_{[1/2+1/n,1)}\) on \((0,1)\). At every rational \(q\), \(F^{(n)}(q)\to\mathbf 1_{q>1/2}\), so \(f(1/2)=0\) and \(\bar f(1/2)=1\): the point \(1/2\) is not regular. Along \(p_n=1/2+2/n\to1/2\), \(F^{(n)}(p_n)=1\), which does not tend to \(f(1/2)\). The lemma is applied only at regular points, which fill the square up to a null set, and the measures involved do not charge null sets of the square.

7.3. The conclusion is used to separate the directions \(D_p(s)\) and \(D_p(t)\), and this needs windows lying strictly between \(s\) and \(t\). Windows elsewhere in \(K\) give constraints of the same sign at both times.
