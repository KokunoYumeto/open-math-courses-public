Geometric waiting times and controlled conditioning

Written by GPT-6 Astra, Ultra reasoning, in Codex, October 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. Original exposition, proofs and exercises: CC0 1.0.

An independent random model is useful only if we know when it describes the objects being studied and what happens when we restrict it to an event. Both questions arise when a long calculation is divided into a prefix and a tail. A restriction that looks innocuous can couple those two pieces and invalidate a product formula.

We begin with geometric waiting times, whose sums and conditional distributions can be counted exactly. We then prove concentration estimates, use residue classes to compare this model with finite Collatz valuation words, and explain how to stop a random word while keeping its unused tail independent. The resulting estimates quantify the errors in the prefix–tail decomposition of Quotient distributions and Fourier mixing.

We assume elementary differentiation and integration, geometric series, binomial coefficients, and countable probability. Pushforward measures and logarithmic sampling, Proposition 1, supplies regrouping of nonnegative sums and contraction under a map. The arithmetic application uses Symbolic itineraries and affine composition, Theorem 4, which proves the correspondence between finite parity words and residue classes. Basic references are Scott Sheffield’s lectures on moment generating functions, Kyle Siegrist’s Random Services treatment of negative binomial waiting times, and Tao’s Almost all orbits of the Collatz map attain almost bounded values, Sections 2, 4 and 6. The required concentration and conditioning arguments are proved here.

Counting waiting-time words

Fix 0<p<10<p<1. A positive geometric variable AA has law

ℙ(A=b)=p(1−p)b−1,b=1,2,…. \mathbb P(A=b)=p(1-p)^{b-1},\qquad b=1,2,\ldots.

One interpretation is the number of independent trials needed to obtain a first success, each trial having success probability pp: the first b−1b-1 trials fail and the next succeeds. The displayed probabilities sum to one. We will use finite independent lists A1,…,AnA_1,\ldots,A_n, and put

S0=0,Sj=A1+⋯+Aj. S_0=0,\qquad S_j=A_1+\cdots+A_j.

The law of the whole word is the product of its coordinate laws. In particular, a specified word (a1,…,ak)(a_1,\ldots,a_k) of total length ss has probability pk(1−p)s−kp^k(1-p)^{s-k}.

Proposition 1 (Sums and conditioning on a sum). For integers k≥1k\geq1 and s≥ks\geq k,

ℙ(Sk=s)=(s−1k−1)pk(1−p)s−k. \mathbb P(S_k=s)=\binom{s-1}{k-1}p^k(1-p)^{s-k}. \tag{1} (1)

Given Sk=sS_k=s, the word is uniform on all positive kk-tuples with sum ss. This conditional law does not depend on pp.

Proof. Send a positive tuple to its first k−1k-1 partial sums:

(a1,…,ak)↦{a1,a1+a2,…,a1+⋯+ak−1}. (a_1,\ldots,a_k)\longmapsto \{a_1,a_1+a_2,\ldots,a_1+\cdots+a_{k-1}\}.

This is a bijection onto the (k−1)(k-1)-element subsets of {1,…,s−1}\{1,\ldots,s-1\}. In increasing order, take successive differences, including the differences from zero and to ss, to recover the tuple. There are therefore (s−1k−1)\binom{s-1}{k-1} tuples. Each has the same probability pk(1−p)s−kp^k(1-p)^{s-k}. Summing proves (1), and dividing each word probability by this positive sum proves the conditional statement. For k=1k=1, the subset is empty and the only tuple is (s)(s). ∎

The coordinates are generally no longer independent after the total is fixed. For example, given A1+A2=3A_1+A_2=3, the only words are (1,2)(1,2) and (2,1)(2,1), with equal probabilities. Each coordinate has probability 1/21/2 of being one, but they cannot both be one.

There is a different statement for separate totals of disjoint blocks. Partition the coordinate indices into finitely many disjoint blocks. For a block of length kjk_j, prescribe total sj≥kjs_j\geq k_j. Under this conditioning, the blocks remain independent, and each block is uniform on its positive compositions of sjs_j. Indeed, the probability of a collection of specified block words is the product of their original probabilities. The probability of the conditioning event is the product of the block-total probabilities. Dividing cancels separately in each factor. Coordinates inside a block need not be independent.

In particular, for p=1/2p=1/2, let Bj=A2j−1+A2jB_j=A_{2j-1}+A_{2j}. Then

ℙ(Bj=b)=(b−1)2−b,b≥2, \mathbb P(B_j=b)=(b-1)2^{-b},\qquad b\geq2,

and, given the separate values of all the BjB_j, the pairs are independent. Within a pair of total bb, the first coordinate is uniform on {1,…,b−1}\{1,\ldots,b-1\}, and the second is its complement to bb. This distinction is useful in Tao’s Fourier argument: averaging over a finite set of possible splits gives cancellation, while separate pair totals still permit multiplication across pairs.

An exponential estimate with explicit constants

For the rest of the concentration calculation, take p=1/2p=1/2, so ℙ(A=b)=2−b\mathbb P(A=b)=2^{-b}. Its mean is two. One way to see this without differentiating an infinite series is to write

𝔼A=∑j≥1ℙ(A≥j)=∑j≥121−j=2. \mathbb E A=\sum_{j\geq1}\mathbb P(A\geq j) =\sum_{j\geq1}2^{1-j}=2.

The first equality follows by counting bb as the sum of bb ones and regrouping nonnegative terms.

𝔼eλA=∑b≥1(eλ/2)b=eλ2−eλ,λ<log⁡2. \mathbb E e^{\lambda A} =\sum_{b\geq1}(e^\lambda/2)^b =\frac{e^\lambda}{2-e^\lambda},\qquad \lambda<\log2.

Set α=log⁡(4/3)>0\alpha=\log(4/3)>0. Direct differentiation of this finite expression gives

ψ(0)=ψ′(0)=0,ψ″(λ)=2eλ(2−eλ)2≤6(|λ|≤α). \psi(0)=\psi'(0)=0,\qquad \psi''(\lambda)=\frac{2e^\lambda}{(2-e^\lambda)^2}\leq6 \quad (|\lambda|\leq\alpha).

Theorem 2 (Concentration for geometric sums). For k≥1k\geq1 and t≥0t\geq0, put

Φk(t)=min⁡{t212k,αt2}. \Phi_k(t)=\min\left\{\frac{t^2}{12k},\frac{\alpha t}{2}\right\}.

ℙ(Sk−2k≥t)≤e−Φk(t),ℙ(Sk−2k≤−t)≤e−Φk(t),ℙ(|Sk−2k|≥t)≤2e−Φk(t). \begin{aligned} \mathbb P(S_k-2k\geq t)&\leq e^{-\Phi_k(t)},\\ \mathbb P(S_k-2k\leq-t)&\leq e^{-\Phi_k(t)},\\ \mathbb P(|S_k-2k|\geq t)&\leq2e^{-\Phi_k(t)}. \end{aligned} \tag{3} (3)

Proof. For λ>0\lambda>0, on the event Sk−2k≥tS_k-2k\geq t the nonnegative variable eλ(Sk−2k)e^{\lambda(S_k-2k)} is at least eλte^{\lambda t}. Thus

Independence factors the expectation into kk identical factors. This follows directly by summing the nonnegative product over all coordinate tuples. For 0<λ≤α0<\lambda\leq\alpha, (2) bounds the right side by exp⁡(−λt+3kλ2)\exp(-\lambda t+3k\lambda^2).

If 0<t≤6kα0<t\leq6k\alpha, choose λ=t/(6k)\lambda=t/(6k). The exponent is −t2/(12k)-t^2/(12k). The estimate has two regimes. Deviations up to size proportional to kk have a quadratic exponent t2/(12k)t^2/(12k). Larger deviations have a linear exponent αt/2\alpha t/2. Keeping both is important because one unusually large waiting time can produce a very large deviation.

Controlling every interval of a word

For 0≤i<j≤n0\leq i<j\leq n, write Sj−Si=Ai+1+⋯+AjS_j-S_i=A_{i+1}+\cdots+A_j. This block has exactly j−ij-i summands and mean 2(j−i)2(j-i). For u>0u>0, define

bm(u)=12mu+2uα,m≥1. b_m(u)=\sqrt{12mu}+\frac{2u}{\alpha},\qquad m\geq1.

Both terms in the minimum defining Φm(bm(u))\Phi_m(b_m(u)) are at least uu. Theorem 2 therefore bounds the failure probability for one block by 2e−u2e^{-u}.

Corollary 3 (Simultaneous block control). Let Gn(u)G_n(u) be the event that

|Sj−Si−2(j−i)|≤bj−i(u)for every 0≤i<j≤n. |S_j-S_i-2(j-i)|\leq b_{j-i}(u) \quad\text{for every }0\leq i<j\leq n.

Then

ℙ(Gn(u)c)≤n(n+1)e−u. \mathbb P(G_n(u)^c)\leq n(n+1)e^{-u}. \tag{4} (4)

Proof. There are n(n+1)/2n(n+1)/2 intervals. For any finite list of events, the indicator of their union is at most the sum of their indicators. Taking expectations proves that the probability of their union is at most the sum of their probabilities. Apply this to the block failures. They do not have to be independent. ∎

For a desired error 0<ε<10<\varepsilon<1, the choice

u=log⁡n(n+1)ε u=\log\frac{n(n+1)}{\varepsilon}

makes the right side ε\varepsilon. This is the source of the frequently useful combination mlog⁡n+log⁡n\sqrt{m\log n}+\log n when the desired error is a negative power of nn. The formula (4) specifies the constants and the number of intervals instead of hiding either in an asymptotic phrase.

How much information a residue class provides

We now connect the model to arithmetic. Let mm be a positive odd integer, and let

S(m)=3m+12ν2(3m+1) S(m)=\frac{3m+1}{2^{\nu_2(3m+1)}}

be the odd-return Collatz map. The notation S(m)S(m) denotes a map here, while the subscripted SjS_j above denotes a random partial sum. For k≥1k\geq1, the valuation word of mm is

Vk(m)=(a1,…,ak),ai=ν2(3Si−1(m)+1). V_k(m)=(a_1,\ldots,a_k),\qquad a_i=\nu_2(3S^{i-1}(m)+1).

Fix a positive word 𝒂=(a1,…,ak)\boldsymbol a=(a_1,\ldots,a_k), let si=a1+⋯+ais_i=a_1+\cdots+a_i, s0=0s_0=0, and s=sks=s_k. Define the integer

C𝒂=∑i=1k3k−i2si−1. C_{\boldsymbol a}=\sum_{i=1}^k3^{k-i}2^{s_{i-1}}.

Proposition 4 (The exact valuation cylinder). The set of positive odd starts having valuation word 𝒂\boldsymbol a is exactly one residue class modulo 2s+12^{s+1}, namely

m≡3−k(2s−C𝒂)(mod⁡2s+1). m\equiv3^{-k}(2^s-C_{\boldsymbol a})\pmod {2^{s+1}}. \tag{5} (5)

Here 3−k3^{-k} is the multiplicative inverse of 3k3^k in that residue ring. The represented class is odd.

Proof. Use the shortcut map T(m)=(3m+1)/2T(m)=(3m+1)/2 for odd mm and T(m)=m/2T(m)=m/2 for even mm. An odd-return step with valuation aia_i follows the parity block consisting of one 1 and then ai−1a_i-1 zeros. After kk such blocks there have been ss shortcut steps, and the next state must again be odd. Thus the valuation cylinder is exactly the cylinder of the length-(s+1)(s+1) parity word

10a1−110a2−1⋯10ak−11. 1\,0^{a_1-1}\,1\,0^{a_2-1}\,\cdots\,1\,0^{a_k-1}\,1.

The final 1 is a parity test at time ss, not an additional completed odd-return step. The word/residue theorem in Symbolic itineraries and affine composition gives exactly one class modulo 2s+12^{s+1}, and every positive member of it has the required valuation word.

On this cylinder, iterating the affine branch equations gives

Sk(m)=3km+C𝒂2s. S^k(m)=\frac{3^k m+C_{\boldsymbol a}}{2^s}.

The formula is also equation (6) of Quotient distributions and Fourier mixing after multiplying through by 2s2^s. The output is odd, so its numerator is congruent to 2s2^s modulo 2s+12^{s+1}. There is exactly one solution for mm because 3k3^k is invertible modulo that power of two. It must therefore be the already established cylinder class. Finally, C𝒂C_{\boldsymbol a} is odd: its first term is odd and every later term is even. Since s≥1s\geq1, (5) is odd. ∎

The extra bit in the modulus matters. Divisibility by 2s2^s alone would not ensure that the endpoint is odd and that the last removed exponent is exactly the prescribed one.

∥ℒ(Vk(M0))−γk∥1≤η+2ℙ(Sk≥q). \|\mathcal L(V_k(M_0))-\gamma_k\|_1 \leq\eta+2\,\mathbb P(S_k\geq q). \tag{6} (6)

∥ℒ(Vk(M0))−γk∥1≤η+2e−Φk(q−2k). \|\mathcal L(V_k(M_0))-\gamma_k\|_1 \leq\eta+2e^{-\Phi_k(q-2k)}. \tag{7} (7)

This partitions the finite set of odd classes into short-word cells and one residual cell; if there are no short words, the partition has only the residual cell.

Changing an event without losing its mass

Let ZZ be a random variable in a countable set, and let E,FE,F be events. Define subprobability mass functions

μE(z)=ℙ(Z=z,E),μF(z)=ℙ(Z=z,F). \mu_E(z)=\mathbb P(Z=z,E),\qquad \mu_F(z)=\mathbb P(Z=z,F).

Lemma 6 (Event replacement).

∥μE−μF∥1≤ℙ(E△F), \|\mu_E-\mu_F\|_1\leq\mathbb P(E\mathbin\triangle F), \tag{9} (9)

where E△FE\mathbin\triangle F consists of outcomes in exactly one of the events. If ZZ takes values in GNG_N and PP is the fibre-averaging operator of the preceding lesson, then

|∥μE−PμE∥1−∥μF−PμF∥1|≤2ℙ(E△F). \left|\|\mu_E-P\mu_E\|_1-\|\mu_F-P\mu_F\|_1\right| \leq2\mathbb P(E\mathbin\triangle F). \tag{10} (10)

Proof. At each zz, cancel the shared contribution from E∩FE\cap F. The absolute remaining difference is at most the sum of the masses at zz coming from E\FE\setminus F and F\EF\setminus E. Summing proves (9). For (10), the reverse triangle inequality bounds the left side by

∥(I−P)(μE−μF)∥1≤∥μE−μF∥1+∥P(μE−μF)∥1≤2∥μE−μF∥1. \|(I-P)(\mu_E-\mu_F)\|_1 \leq\|\mu_E-\mu_F\|_1+\|P(\mu_E-\mu_F)\|_1 \leq2\|\mu_E-\mu_F\|_1.

The last step uses the contraction proved in Proposition 1 of the preceding lesson. ∎

These are estimates for masses before dividing by an event probability. If a=ℙ(E)>0a=\mathbb P(E)>0 and b=ℙ(F)>0b=\mathbb P(F)>0, the conditional laws satisfy, for example,

∥μEa−μFb∥1≤2ℙ(E△F)a. \left\|\frac{\mu_E}{a}-\frac{\mu_F}{b}\right\|_1 \leq\frac{2\mathbb P(E\mathbin\triangle F)}{a}.

Indeed, insert μF/a\mu_F/a. The two differences have total norm at most ∥μE−μF∥1/a+|a−b|/a\|\mu_E-\mu_F\|_1/a+|a-b|/a, and |a−b|≤ℙ(E△F)|a-b|\leq\mathbb P(E\mathbin\triangle F). Interchanging the events gives the corresponding bound with denominator bb. A small absolute error can become substantial after conditioning on a rare event. Retaining subprobability masses, as in Lemma 6 and the convolution estimate, avoids paying that division prematurely.

Where to stop so that the tail remains independent

Consider a word A1,…,AnA_1,\ldots,A_n with independent positive geometric entries of parameter 1/21/2, and fix h≥0h\geq0. Define the first strict crossing

τ=min⁡{j∈{1,…,n}:Sj>h}, \tau=\min\{j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}:S_j>h\},

with τ=∞\tau=\infty if there is no crossing. Since all entries are positive,

{τ=j}={Sj−1≤h<Sj}. \{\tau=j\}=\{S_{j-1}\leq h<S_j\}.

This event is determined by the first jj entries. It does not inspect Aj+1,…,AnA_{j+1},\ldots,A_n. The entries after jj therefore retain their product law after conditioning on any positive-probability event involving just that prefix. For specified tail values, factor their probability from the prefix-event probability and then divide by the latter. This proves the assertion directly; no stopping-time theorem is needed.

By contrast, fixing the number of entries before the crossing to be kk inspects Ak+1A_{k+1}, because its defining inequality is Sk≤h<Sk+1S_k\leq h<S_{k+1}. In that description, the independent unused tail starts at k+2k+2, not at k+1k+1. The distinction is one coordinate, but it determines which Fourier factorization is valid.

Let Gn(u)G_n(u) be the simultaneous-control event above. It examines the entire word. For j≤nj\leq n, let Gj(u)G_j(u) impose the same inequalities only on intervals within the first jj entries. Then Gn(u)⊆Gj(u)G_n(u)\subseteq G_j(u), and Gj(u)G_j(u) is a prefix event. Define disjoint cells

Ej,l={τ=j,Sj=l}∩Gj(u),1≤j≤n,l∈ℕ+. E_{j,l}=\{\tau=j,\ S_j=l\}\cap G_j(u), \qquad 1\leq j\leq n,\quad l\in\mathbb N_+.

They are disjoint because the first crossing and its total are uniquely specified. They are also finitely many nonempty cells: on Gj(u)G_j(u), Sj≤2j+bj(u)≤2n+bn(u)S_j\leq2j+b_j(u)\leq2n+b_n(u). Put D=⋃j,lEj,lD=\bigcup_{j,l}E_{j,l}.

Proposition 7 (A prefix-measurable decomposition).

Gn(u)∩{τ≤n}⊆D,ℙ(Dc)≤n(n+1)e−u+ℙ(Sn≤h). G_n(u)\cap\{\tau\leq n\}\subseteq D, \qquad \mathbb P(D^c)\leq n(n+1)e^{-u}+\mathbb P(S_n\leq h). \tag{11} (11)

For any countable-valued random variable ZZ, its law has the exact decomposition

ℒ(Z)=μDc+∑j,lμEj,l. \mathcal L(Z)=\mu_{D^c}+\sum_{j,l}\mu_{E_{j,l}}.

Each event Ej,lE_{j,l} depends only on the first jj entries and fixes their sum. Thus for the affine random sum of the preceding lesson, each cell with j<nj<n has the convolution representation of its Proposition 6, with an independent tail beginning at j+1j+1. For j=nj=n, the tail is empty and contributes unit mass at zero.

Proof. If all interval bounds hold and a crossing occurs, the unique pair (τ,Sτ)(\tau,S_\tau) places the word in one of the cells. Therefore DcD^c is contained in Gn(u)c∪{τ=∞}G_n(u)^c\cup\{\tau=\infty\}. Positivity of the entries gives {τ=∞}={Sn≤h}\{\tau=\infty\}=\{S_n\leq h\}. Apply Corollary 3 and the union bound to obtain (11). The cells and their complement partition the probability space, proving the law decomposition. Prefix dependence and the fixed sum allow the independent-tail calculation already proved in the preceding lesson. ∎

If h<2nh<2n, Theorem 2 gives the further explicit bound

ℙ(Dc)≤n(n+1)e−u+e−Φn(2n−h). \mathbb P(D^c)\leq n(n+1)e^{-u}+e^{-\Phi_n(2n-h)}.

There is no need to multiply the first error by the number of cells. All cells are disjoint, and every lost outcome lies in the same event Gn(u)cG_n(u)^c or fails to cross. Likewise, replacing Gn(u)G_n(u) by Gj(u)G_j(u) on the cell with crossing jj adds a set contained in Gn(u)cG_n(u)^c; the sum of those added masses is at most ℙ(Gn(u)c)\mathbb P(G_n(u)^c).

This provides the probabilistic part of a mixing argument: a finite sum of genuine convolutions and an explicitly bounded residual mass. The arithmetic separation and tail Fourier bounds still enter through the estimates proved in Quotient distributions and Fourier mixing. A concentration estimate controls which words are used; it does not itself estimate their oscillatory phases.

3. The last parity test

Find all positive starts with valuation word (2,1)(2,1). Check the first two odd-return steps for the least positive representative. Explain why retaining only the class modulo eight cannot distinguish this word from every other word beginning with valuation two.

Solution. Here s=3s=3, C=3+4=7C=3+4=7, and the inverse of nine modulo sixteen is nine. Formula (5) gives m≡9(8−7)=9(mod⁡16)m\equiv9(8-7)=9\pmod{16}. At m=9m=9, the steps are

4. Rare conditioning and the unused tail

First take a probability space with three points x,y,zx,y,z, of masses d,d,1−2dd,d,1-2d, where 0<d<1/20<d<1/2. Let Z(x)=0Z(x)=0, Z(y)=1Z(y)=1, and choose any value for Z(z)Z(z). Compare the subprobability laws on E={x}E=\{x\}, F={y}F=\{y\} with their conditional laws. Then, for independent geometric A1,…,A4A_1,\ldots,A_4, stop on first crossing of h=4h=4. On the event {τ=3,S3=6}\{\tau=3,S_3=6\}, which unused coordinates remain independent of the prefix? What changes if one also prescribes S4=7S_4=7?

Solution. The two subprobability laws are dδ0d\delta_0 and dδ1d\delta_1, whose full mass difference is 2d=ℙ(E△F)2d=\mathbb P(E\mathbin\triangle F). Their conditional laws are δ0\delta_0 and δ1\delta_1, whose difference is two regardless of how small dd is. Thus a small discarded mass alone does not imply a small conditional-law error.

The crossing event is {S2≤4<S3=6}\{S_2\leq4<S_3=6\}, determined by the first three entries. It has positive probability; the prefix (1,3,2)(1,3,2) is one possible word. Consequently A4A_4 retains its geometric law and is independent of the prefix under this conditioning. Adding S4=7S_4=7 forces A4=1A_4=1, so its geometric law is no longer retained. This additional event inspected the proposed unused coordinate. It cannot be ignored when forming the tail factor.

What carries forward

Three distinct facts now work together. Residue cylinders turn finite arithmetic sampling into a quantitative comparison with geometric words. Exponential moments control unlikely word sums, including all subintervals at once. Prefix-measurable restrictions leave a genuinely independent unused tail, with the full event masses retained.

The next analytic steps need both probabilities of events and probabilities of individual lattice points. The concentration bounds here supply the first kind. They do not include the local k−d/2k^{-d/2} factors for dd-dimensional sums in Tao’s Section 2, or the quantitative Fourier cancellation for the two-dimensional renewal process in Section 7. Those require their own proofs. The estimates already established here isolate the exact inputs those further arguments will use rather than replacing them by an assumption of randomness along an infinite integer orbit.
