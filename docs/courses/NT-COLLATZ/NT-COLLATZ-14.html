Compatible entrance laws and weighted orbit minima

Written by GPT-6 Astra, Ultra reasoning, in Codex, October 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. Original exposition, proofs and exercises: CC0 1.0.

A small error at one scale does not automatically remain small through arbitrarily many scales. The useful distinction is between an error that is merely small and a family of errors whose sum is finite. When the errors are summable, approximate agreement can produce an exactly compatible limiting family of probability laws.

We develop that principle for first entrance into nested targets. The same sequence of starting distributions will supply a limit at every threshold. Observing several thresholds together costs no more than observing the largest one, because the smaller entrance locations are determined by the largest. A second application controls orbit minima under logarithmic sampling. It yields Tao’s fixed-threshold estimate and his theorem for every prescribed bound tending to infinity, including bounds that increase arbitrarily slowly or are not monotone.

The general mathematical subjects are probability transport, compatible distributions, tightness, and weighted counting. Collatz supplies the concrete system for which the preceding lessons have proved the analytic estimates. The prerequisites are pushforward contraction and harmonic weights, nested first passage and summable transport, and logarithmic first-passage stabilization. We also use the exact equality of orbit minima under changes of clock. The main research reference is Tao’s Almost all orbits of the Collatz map attain almost bounded values, Remark 1.4 and Section 3. The compatible-family and joint-entrance statements are derived below from the first-passage estimate. All further proofs are supplied here.

Tightness under weighted counting

Let D⊆ℕ+D\subseteq\mathbb N_+ be infinite. Assign each n∈Dn\in D a positive real weight w(n)w(n), and suppose

W(X)=∑n∈Dn≤Xw(n)→∞. W(X)=\sum_{\substack{n\in D\\n\leq X}}w(n)\longrightarrow\infty.

For a nonempty prefix, let ρX(n)=w(n)/W(X)\rho_X(n)=w(n)/W(X) on D∩[1,X]D\cap[1,X], and zero elsewhere. Take a finite-valued function g:D→[0,∞)g:D\to[0,\infty). It need not be bounded as a function of nn. Its distributions under the different ρX\rho_X are uniformly tight if

limK→∞supXρX(g>K)=0. \lim_{K\to\infty}\sup_X\rho_X(g>K)=0. \tag{1} (1)

Thus one finite interval contains almost all the probability, uniformly over all the input cutoffs. The supremum is over nonempty prefixes.

Theorem 1 (A weighted tightness criterion). The following three statements are equivalent:

  1. For every real-valued function f:D→ℝf:D\to\mathbb R with f(n)→∞f(n)\to\infty, one has ρX(g≥f)→0\rho_X(g\geq f)\to0 as X→∞X\to\infty.
  2. lim⁡K→∞limsup⁡X→∞ρX(g>K)=0\lim_{K\to\infty}\limsup_{X\to\infty}\rho_X(g>K)=0.
  3. The family is uniformly tight, as in (1).

The limit of ff is taken along DD. Its speed of growth and its monotonicity play no role.

Proof. First assume statement 2. Fix a finite KK. Since f(n)→∞f(n)\to\infty, choose YKY_K such that f(n)>Kf(n)>K for n>YKn>Y_K. Then

{g≥f}⊆(D∩[1,YK])∪{g>K}. \{g\geq f\}\subseteq(D\cap[1,Y_K])\cup\{g>K\}.

The first set has probability at most W(YK)/W(X)W(Y_K)/W(X), tending to zero. Taking the limsup as X→∞X\to\infty at this fixed KK, and then letting K→∞K\to\infty, proves statement 1.

For the converse, suppose statement 2 fails. Because its inner limsup decreases as KK increases, there is an ε>0\varepsilon>0 such that, for every integer j≥1j\geq1, arbitrarily large integer cutoffs satisfy ρX(g>j)≥ε\rho_X(g>j)\geq\varepsilon. Start with an integer X0≥min⁡DX_0\geq\min D. Choose recursively Xj>Xj−1X_j>X_{j-1} with that property and with

W(Xj−1)W(Xj)≤ε2. \frac{W(X_{j-1})}{W(X_j)}\leq\frac\varepsilon2. \tag{2} (2)

Such a choice is possible because the suitable cutoffs are unbounded and W(X)→∞W(X)\to\infty. Define f(n)=jf(n)=j for Xj−1<n≤XjX_{j-1}<n\leq X_j, and choose any finite value on the initial prefix. The strictly increasing integer sequence XjX_j tends to infinity, so this defines ff on all of DD and gives f(n)→∞f(n)\to\infty. At cutoff XjX_j, remove the earlier prefix from the event {g>j}\{g>j\}. By (2), probability at least ε/2\varepsilon/2 remains. On it, g>j=fg>j=f, contradicting statement 1.

Next assume statement 2 and fix ε>0\varepsilon>0. Choose K0K_0 such that the limsup in that statement is less than ε/2\varepsilon/2. There is X0X_0 after which all the probabilities ρX(g>K0)\rho_X(g>K_0) are at most ε\varepsilon. Increase K0K_0 to exceed the finitely many values of gg on D∩[1,X0]D\cap[1,X_0]. Every earlier tail is now empty, and increasing the threshold cannot enlarge a later tail. This proves statement 3. Finally, statement 3 implies statement 2 because a limsup is bounded by the corresponding supremum. ∎

The finite-valued hypothesis is used when the initial prefix is removed. The divergence of WW ensures that a fixed prefix becomes negligible. The theorem is independent of any dynamical interpretation of gg.

First-passage operators at nested thresholds

Return to the odd-return map SS on 𝕆={1,3,5,…}\mathbb O=\{1,3,5,\ldots\}. Let pxp_x record its first entrance at most xx, with failure recorded by †\dagger, as in the preceding lesson. Extend px(†)=†p_x(\dagger)=\dagger, and write

Observing several entrance levels together

Fix 1≤x1≤⋯≤xr1\leq x_1\leq\cdots\leq x_r. A tuple of possible entrance values is compatible when

(z1,…,zr)∈𝒞,𝒞={(zi)∈∏iExi:pxi(zi+1)=zi(i<r)}. \begin{gathered} (z_1,\ldots,z_r)\in\mathcal C,\\ \mathcal C=\left\{(z_i)\in\prod_iE_{x_i}: p_{x_i}(z_{i+1})=z_i\ (i<r)\right\}. \end{gathered} \tag{12} (12)

Proposition 3 (Joint laws without an error for each coordinate). If NjN_j has law μtj+1\mu_{t_{j+1}}, then the joint law of (px1(Nj),…,pxr(Nj))(p_{x_1}(N_j),\ldots,p_{x_r}(N_j)) converges to a probability law Λx1,…,xr(b)\Lambda_{x_1,\ldots,x_r}^{(b)} on 𝒞\mathcal C. For xr≤tjx_r\leq t_j, its ℓ1\ell^1 convergence error is at most Dγ(log⁡tj)−γD_\gamma(\log t_j)^{-\gamma}, with no factor depending on rr. Its marginal at xix_i is λxi∞,(b)\lambda_{x_i}^{\infty,(b)}.

Proof. Define

J:Exr→𝒞,z↦(px1(z),…,pxr(z)). \begin{aligned} J:E_{x_r}&\longrightarrow\mathcal C,\\ z&\longmapsto(p_{x_1}(z),\ldots,p_{x_r}(z)). \end{aligned} \tag{13} (13)

Equation (4) proves that the image lies in 𝒞\mathcal C. Its last coordinate is zz, so JJ is injective. Conversely, repeated use of (12) and nested passage gives zi=pxi(zr)z_i=p_{x_i}(z_r). Thus projection to the last coordinate is its inverse. Every fibre is a singleton, and the tuple retains exactly the information in its last coordinate.

For a signed mass η\eta on ExrE_{x_r}, injectivity gives ∥J*η∥1=∥η∥1\|J_*\eta\|_1=\|\eta\|_1: no two atoms are combined. The finite joint law is exactly J*λxr,j(b)J_*\lambda_{x_r,j}^{(b)}. Define its limit as J*λxr∞,(b)J_*\lambda_{x_r}^{\infty,(b)}, and apply this isometry to (8). The marginal identity follows by projecting the tuple and applying (9). ∎

For example, the largest entrance value can contain information not recoverable from a smaller one, because several values may have the same subsequent entrance. Recording the entire compatible tuple adds no information beyond the largest value. This is why summing a separate error for every coordinate would be unnecessary.

A uniform estimate for a fixed orbit bound

For an odd start, write Smin(m)=min⁡j≥0Sj(m)S_{\min}(m)=\min_{j\geq0}S^j(m). This minimum exists by well-ordering, even if the orbit is unbounded. For the ordinary Collatz map

C(n)={n/2,n even,3n+1,n odd, C(n)=\begin{cases}n/2,&n\text{ even},\\3n+1,&n\text{ odd},\end{cases}

Every prescribed diverging bound

Let H(X)=∑n≤X1/nH(X)=\sum_{n\leq X}1/n. The harmonic-sum estimates prove H(X)=log⁡X+O(1)H(X)=\log X+O(1). A set B⊆ℕ+B\subseteq\mathbb N_+ has logarithmic density zero when H(X)−1∑n≤X,n∈B1/n→0H(X)^{-1}\sum_{n\leq X,\,n\in B}1/n\to0.

Equivalently, Cmin(n)<f(n)C_{\min}(n)<f(n) on a set of logarithmic density one. The odd version holds with SminS_{\min} and Ho(X)=∑m≤X,m odd1/mH_o(X)=\sum_{m\leq X,\,m\text{ odd}}1/m.

The order of limits is important. A bound for a tail of ff cannot be substituted as a lower bound for all its earlier values. Fixing KK first and removing the finite prefix avoids that problem. It also shows that the qualitative conclusion is exactly the weighted tightness property of Theorem 1, with w(n)=1/nw(n)=1/n and g=Cming=C_{\min}.

The theorem allows a different exceptional set for each prescribed ff. It does not supply a single finite bound reached by every orbit, and it is not a bound on the number of ordinary iterations. The clock identities explain why an orbit minimum and an elapsed-time estimate require different information.

The mathematical route through the case study

The endpoint is an arithmetic density theorem, but the proof brings together several subjects. Return maps identify the same minimum in different orbit records. Symbolic coding gives exact arithmetic domains for affine branches. Geometric concentration controls sampled finite words. Finite Fourier analysis and renewal estimates give mixing of affine offsets. Exact inverse branches and harmonic weights turn this into a comparison of entrance laws. Summable transport then yields compatible limits and tight orbit minima.

These ingredients have different roles. The maps between clocks preserve individual orbit information. Pushforwards can discard information within their fibres. Fourier estimates control distributions, not each orbit separately. The compatible joint observation in Proposition 3, by contrast, is an exact bijection and loses no information. Keeping those distinctions visible makes the methods reusable in other discrete systems, where the maps and the analytic hypotheses can be checked on their own terms.

Solution. For each fixed K≥2K\geq2, choose YKY_K with f(n)>Kf(n)>K beyond it. The mass up to XX is at most H(YK)+Alog⁡X/(log⁡K)cH(Y_K)+A\log X/(\log K)^c. Divide by H(X)H(X), and first let X→∞X\to\infty with KK fixed. The prefix term vanishes and the limsup is at most A/(log⁡K)cA/(\log K)^c. Only then let K→∞K\to\infty. A threshold defined using only values of ff after XX would not control the values being summed before XX.
