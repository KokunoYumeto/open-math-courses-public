Arithmetic separation and fine-scale mixing

Written by GPT-6 Astra, Ultra reasoning, in Codex, October 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. Original exposition, proofs and exercises: CC0 1.0.

Small Fourier coefficients describe cancellation, but a probability law on a large group has many coefficients. An estimate for each one need not survive summation over the whole group. A useful way forward is to divide a random sum into two parts. The first part must distribute its mass among many distinct values. The second must cancel at the frequencies that distinguish nearby states. Their convolution then combines arithmetic separation with harmonic analysis.

We carry this out for random affine offsets associated with the odd-return Collatz map. Averaging uniformly within each residue class modulo a smaller power of three changes the full probability law by less than every prescribed negative power of that smaller exponent. This controls conditional discrepancies averaged with their actual class probabilities; it does not give the same bound separately in every class. The classes themselves need not have equal mass. In particular, the result is consistent with the nonuniform distribution modulo three found in the preceding lesson.

The proof has two mathematical parts. First, a dyadic rational offset determines its entire finite word, and a height bound preserves that injectivity after reduction modulo a power of three. Second, we stop a random word when its accumulated exponent reaches a suitable threshold. On each retained prefix event, the unused tail remains independent. Exact event masses, rather than conditional laws with mass one, connect the two parts without losing their probability weights.

The prerequisites are the Fourier and collision-mass estimate, concentration for geometric sums, and the full-order Fourier estimate and character-order reduction. Basic references are Terence Tao’s Almost all orbits of the Collatz map attain almost bounded values, Section 6, and Keith Conrad’s Characters of finite abelian groups, Section 4. The mixing theorem and its prefix–tail mechanism are Tao’s. All additional arguments needed here are proved below; the preceding lessons supply the stated analytic inputs.

Theorem 1 (Fine-scale mixing). For every real p>0p>0, there is a constant KpK_p, independent of m,nm,n, such that

Reference: Tao, the fine-scale mixing proposition and Section 6.

Here is an exact conditional interpretation. Let FaF_a be the fibre over a∈Gma\in G_m, let uau_a be its uniform probability law, and, when μm(a)>0\mu_m(a)>0, let νa(x)=μn(x)/μm(a)\nu_a(x)=\mu_n(x)/\mu_m(a) on FaF_a. Then

Indeed, on each such fibre the difference of masses is μm(a)(νa−ua)\mu_m(a)(\nu_a-u_a). A zero-mass fibre has all its masses zero and contributes nothing. Summing absolute values proves the identity. It explains both what is controlled and why a rare class can have a larger conditional discrepancy.

This is a statement about the independent-word offset law. The proof below first treats 9n/10≤m≤n9n/10\leq m\leq n, then joins those comparisons through exact lifts. Translating this probability model into a statement about sampled integer trajectories also requires the valuation-word and first-passage arguments.

Both the common denominator and the height bound have a role. Rational injectivity alone does not prevent collisions after modular reduction. The next estimate supplies a height bound on a large part of the random-word law.

Exact prefix mass and the independent tail

Write qr,L=ℙ(Hr,L)q_{r,L}=\mathbb P(H_{r,L}), and define a subprobability mass function on GnG_n by

The two inputs have different jobs. The Fourier bound supplies any chosen negative power of nn. Integer separation ensures that the collision factor loses only a fixed positive power of nn, rather than a factor exponential in nn. μn−Um,nμm=∑i=0s−1Uni+1,n(μni+1−Uni,ni+1μni). \mu_n-U_{m,n}\mu_m =\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}U_{n_{i+1},n} \left(\mu_{n_{i+1}}-U_{n_i,n_{i+1}}\mu_{n_i}\right). \tag{32} (32)

Each positive term in the sum cancels the next negative one after lifting. Use the isometry (2), compatibility (3), and Proposition 6. The norm of the ii-th summand is at most Kp′ni+1−p≤Kp′ni−pK'_p n_{i+1}^{-p}\leq K'_p n_i^{-p}. Therefore

∥μn−Um,nμm∥1≤Kp′∑i=0s−1ni−p≤Kp′1−(18/19)pm−p. \|\mu_n-U_{m,n}\mu_m\|_1 \leq K'_p\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}n_i^{-p} \leq\frac{K'_p}{1-(18/19)^p}\,m^{-p}. \tag{33} (33)

For 1≤m<181\leq m<18, the left side is at most two. Taking KpK_p at least 2⋅18p2\cdot18^p, as well as the constant in (33), proves (4) for every pair of positive integers m≤nm\leq n. ∎

The number of intermediate scales is not the error bound. Their sizes grow geometrically, so their errors form a summable geometric series. This is the probability-law analogue of the summable-scale reasoning in First passage and transport across scales.

1. Recovering all partial sums

Starting only from the length three and the rational value 97/12897/128, apply Lemma 2 to recover the word. Why can no cancellation hide the total exponent in its reduced denominator?

Solution. The reduced denominator gives s3=7s_3=7. Remove 32/27=9/1283^2/2^7=9/128, obtaining 88/128=11/1688/128=11/16; hence s2=4s_2=4. From random offsets to orbit estimates

The theorem keeps the coarse distribution exactly and averages only within its fibres. Its proof identifies the work done by each ingredient: denominator recovery gives rational injectivity; a height estimate turns this into modular separation; prefix-only events preserve a genuinely independent tail; and the collision estimate combines that tail’s cancellation with the spread of the prefix. Compatible lifts then extend the estimate to every pair of scales.

These methods belong to arithmetic probability and harmonic analysis, not only to the Collatz problem. For the Collatz application, another transition remains: an independent geometric word must be compared with valuation words of sampled integer orbits, and the resulting estimates must be transported through first-passage locations. Geometric waiting times and controlled conditioning supplies the finite-residue comparison; First passage and transport across scales explains the relevant transport mechanism. The fine-scale theorem supplies one precise distributional input, not a conclusion about the convergence of every integer orbit.
