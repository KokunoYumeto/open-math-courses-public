Weighted renewal estimates and Fourier decay

Written by GPT-6 Astra, Ultra reasoning, in Codex, October 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. Original exposition, proofs and exercises: CC0 1.0.

A random path may visit many positions at which an average loses a fixed fraction of its size. To obtain a decay estimate in the size of the problem, however, we must compare those losses with the distance travelled. A long journey to the first useful position can consume most of the available distance. The estimate must account for both effects on the same path.

We prove a weighted renewal theorem that makes this comparison. If the noncancelling regions are the separated triangles studied previously, the expected multiplicative weight is bounded by every negative power of the remaining horizontal width. The proof uses an increasing family of suprema: once the width is sufficiently large, adding one more starting column cannot increase the weighted supremum. This gives a uniform bound without assuming that any supremum is attained.

The arithmetic application is Tao’s Fourier estimate for random Syracuse offsets. Every prescribed power of the length is available, uniformly over characters of full order. The order of a character matters: lower-order characters retain information about smaller quotients, and some of those coefficients do not tend to zero. This explains why a later mixing argument needs both Fourier cancellation and a dispersed arithmetic prefix.

The prerequisites are the exact stopped-word identity, the first-crossing tails, the uniform cancelling-exit estimate, and the finite-horizon encounter theorem. The final application uses conditional Fourier cancellation and the exact marked-block clock. Basic references are Terence Tao’s Almost all orbits of the Collatz map attain almost bounded values, Section 7, and Robert Gallager’s Discrete Stochastic Processes, Chapter 4. The weighted renewal method is Tao’s; its required estimates have complete proofs here or in the specified preceding lessons.

Beginning the path at its first marked block

The arithmetic occupation count starts at the first holding increment, rather than at a prescribed positive column. Define

NM=∑k≥1𝟏W(Xk,Yk). N_M=\sum_{k\geq1}\mathbf1_W(X_k,Y_k).

There are at most MM nonzero terms. Conditioning on H1H_1 and using independence of the unused holding increments gives the exact identity

𝔼e−λNM=𝔼Q(H1). \mathbb E e^{-\lambda N_M}=\mathbb E Q(H_1). \tag{22} (22)

𝔼e−λNM≤CA,λ′M−A(M≥1). \mathbb E e^{-\lambda N_M}\leq C'_{A,\lambda}M^{-A} \qquad(M\geq1). \tag{23} (23)

Proof. For M≥2M\geq2, split according to J1≤M/2J_1\leq M/2. On that event, Theorem 4 bounds Q(H1)Q(H_1) by CA,λ(2/M)AC_{A,\lambda}(2/M)^A. On the complement use Q≤1Q\leq1 and ℙ(J1>M/2)≤Mηe−ηM/2\mathbb P(J_1>M/2)\leq M_\eta e^{-\eta M/2}. Thus

𝔼Q(H1)≤CA,λ(2/M)A+Mηe−ηM/2. \mathbb E Q(H_1)\leq C_{A,\lambda}(2/M)^A +M_\eta e^{-\eta M/2}.

The exponential term is at most a constant times M−AM^{-A}. For instance differentiation gives sup⁡x≥0xAe−ηx/2=(2A/(eη))A\sup_{x\geq0}x^Ae^{-\eta x/2}=(2A/(e\eta))^A. Enlarge the resulting constant to at least one for M=1M=1. ∎

This final averaging is essential. A first increment can jump far into the strip or beyond it, and that event is not represented by replacing the first column with its mean.

Fourier decay for random affine offsets

Let A1,…,AnA_1,\ldots,A_n be independent with ℙ(Ai=r)=2−r\mathbb P(A_i=r)=2^{-r}, r≥1r\geq1, and let Si=A1+⋯+AiS_i=A_1+\cdots+A_i. Define the dyadic rational

μ̂n(ξ)=𝔼exp⁡(−2πiξ(Tnmod⁡3n)3n). \widehat\mu_n(\xi)=\mathbb E \exp\left(-\frac{2\pi i\xi(T_n\bmod 3^n)}{3^n}\right). \tag{25} (25)

|μ̂n(ξ)|≤CAn−A. |\widehat\mu_n(\xi)|\leq C_A n^{-A}. \tag{26} (26)

Proof. Choose once and for all 0<ε≤exp⁡[−10max⁡{1,R0+1}]0<\varepsilon\leq\exp[-10\max\{1,R_0+1\}], and put λ=ε3\lambda=\varepsilon^3. For n≥2n\geq2, set M=⌊n/2⌋M=\lfloor n/2\rfloor. Thus the renewal calculation proves the Fourier estimate, rather than only an analogy with spectral cancellation. The estimate concerns the independent-word offset law. Comparing that law with values sampled from deterministic integer orbits is a separate step, treated through valuation-word sampling and first-passage transport.

Character order and mixing inside observation classes

The character of ℤ/3nℤ\mathbb Z/3^n\mathbb Z indexed by a nonzero ξ\xi has exact order 3k3^k, where ξ=3n−kη\xi=3^{n-k}\eta and 3∤η3\nmid\eta. This expression is understood with 1≤k≤n1\leq k\leq n and η\eta a unit modulo 3k3^k. Its kernel is the set of multiples of 3k3^k modulo 3n3^n: this follows directly by asking when ξx/3n\xi x/3^n is an integer.

Reduction of (24) modulo 3k3^k discards exactly the terms with i>ki>k, because each has numerator divisible by 3k3^k and denominator invertible there. The first kk terms retain their original independent law. Hence

μ̂n(3n−kη)=μ̂k(η),|μ̂n(3n−kη)|≤CAk−A. \widehat\mu_n(3^{n-k}\eta)=\widehat\mu_k(\eta), \qquad |\widehat\mu_n(3^{n-k}\eta)|\leq C_A k^{-A}. \tag{27} (27)

At frequency zero the coefficient is one. Formula (27) is an exact map between character groups and random laws, not an estimate identifying all nonzero frequencies with full-order characters.

To see the consequence, Tnmod⁡3=2−A1mod⁡3T_n\bmod3=2^{-A_1}\bmod3 for every nn. Odd A1A_1 has probability 2/32/3, even A1A_1 probability 1/31/3; their residues are two and one. Thus this smallest quotient is not uniform. The coefficient of order three has modulus 1/31/\sqrt3, independently of nn, as Exercise 4 computes.

The appropriate next question is uniformity within selected observation classes. The Fourier and collision-mass theorem proves, on a finite cyclic group of order NN, for a nonnegative subprobability mass function ff and a probability mass function gg,

∥f*g−P(f*g)∥1≤δ(N∑xf(x)2)1/2, \|f*g-P(f*g)\|_1 \leq \delta\left(N\sum_x f(x)^2\right)^{1/2}, \tag{28} (28)

where PP uniformly averages within the specified quotient fibres and δ\delta bounds the Fourier coefficients of gg at the discarded frequencies. The factor ∑f(x)2\sum f(x)^2 records collisions of the prefix distribution. Applying a coefficient bound directly to exponentially many frequencies can lose more than a negative power of the length gains. Formula (28) explains the role of arithmetic prefix separation: it controls that collision factor, while Theorem 6 controls the tail’s oscillation. Their exact combination, with event masses retained, is the next part of the probability argument.

What the weighted estimate adds

The cancellation count and the distance travelled have been controlled together. At a white starting point, one step supplies a fixed gain. A shallow crossing costs a factor tending to one and offers a uniform chance of a cancelling exit. A deep crossing consumes a fixed fraction of the width on the likely event; the finite-horizon encounter theorem supplies as much cancellation as a prescribed polynomial weight requires. Rare larger displacements have exponentially small probability.

This proves the arbitrary-power Fourier estimate for the independent Syracuse offset model, with all renewal inputs supplied by preceding lessons. It does not replace the arithmetic task of separating prefixes or the dynamical task of transporting sampled orbit laws across scales. Those tasks have precise roles: prefix separation converts Fourier cancellation into fine-scale probability control, and transport converts the probability estimates into information about trajectories. The conjecture remains a motivating case study within these broader methods.
