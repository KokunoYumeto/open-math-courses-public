Logarithmic sampling and first-passage distributions

Written by GPT-6 Astra, Ultra reasoning, in Codex, October 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. Original exposition, proofs and exercises: CC0 1.0.

A distributional estimate becomes useful for dynamics only after we identify the experiment to which it applies. Independent random words are not individual integer orbits. Moreover, observing an orbit at its first entrance into a set is different from observing it after a prescribed number of steps. The entrance time depends on the trajectory itself.

This lesson connects these two descriptions. Logarithmic sampling of starting integers gives an accurate finite model for their valuation words. Looking a fixed number of steps before an entrance then exposes an exact arithmetic inverse. Its harmonic weights form a bounded function on a finite residue group. The fine-scale mixing theorem can act on that function, and the resulting main term no longer depends on which of two neighbouring logarithmic shells supplied the starting integer.

The conclusion is Tao’s first-passage stabilization theorem, with failure to enter retained as a separate state. It is the analytic input needed for the transport arguments of First passage and transport across scales. Its proof uses the exact valuation cylinder and finite sampling comparison, geometric concentration, and fine-scale mixing. Basic references are Terence Tao’s Almost all orbits of the Collatz map attain almost bounded values, Sections 4–6, and the treatment of probability transport in Pushforward measures and logarithmic sampling. All further arguments are given below.

What the comparison makes possible

The argument has now passed from binary valuation information to ternary residue mixing and then back to integer trajectories. Logarithmic weights make the inverse affine branches carry the geometric factors 2−A2^{-A}. First passage restricts their endpoints strongly enough to keep the harmonic test functions uniformly bounded. Mixing removes the comparison time from the main term, and the logarithmic length of the time window cancels the sampling normalization.

The resulting entrance maps can be composed across thresholds. Their adjacent-scale discrepancies are summable along the sequence x,xα,xα2,…x,x^\alpha,x^{\alpha^2},\ldots, since (log⁡xαj)−1/5=α−j/5(log⁡x)−1/5(\log x^{\alpha^j})^{-1/5}=\alpha^{-j/5}(\log x)^{-1/5}. This is the connection to probability transport: one can compare many scales without paying an error proportional to their number. Compatible entrance laws and weighted orbit minima supplies that further transport argument and its quantitative orbit-minimum conclusion, without assuming that every integer follows an independent random word.
