Results on optimal maps, Laguerre cells, cell moments and gradient interpolation

The course proves the statements below; each link leads to the statement with its assumptions and its proof. The lessons are self-checked by the writing AI; independent mathematical review is not complete.

  • Null boundaries and sections of convex bodies — Lemma 1.1. A compact convex set with nonempty interior has a null boundary; half-open segments from interior points stay interior; an affine section through the interior has relative interior equal to the section of the interior and a null relative boundary.
  • Optimality of finite affine maxima — Lemma 2.1. The gradient of a finite maximum of affine functions is the unique quadratic optimal map from the uniform measure on a convex body to its image measure, and every optimal plan lives on its graph.
  • One-third stability of Brenier maps — Theorem 3.1. For the uniform measure on a convex body and targets in a fixed compact set, optimal maps exist, are unique, and satisfy ||T_mu - T_nu||_{L^2} <= C W_2(mu,nu)^(1/3) with one constant; the exponent cannot be increased.
  • Sharpness on the cube — Proposition 4.1. On the cube with three-atom targets, tilting the central affine function keeps its mass but moves a set of mass b/2 between slopes at distance one, so the map distance is of order a^(1/2) while W_2 is of order a^(3/2).
  • Derivatives of Laguerre cells — Proposition 1.1. Masses and first moments of Laguerre cells with positive masses are continuously differentiable in the intercepts and sites, with face-integral formulas; the face graph is connected and the mass Laplacian has kernel the constants.
  • Intercepts for prescribed masses — Proposition 2.1. For positive weights and distinct sites there are unique normalized intercepts whose cells have the prescribed masses, and they depend continuously differentiably on the sites.
  • A midpoint coupling — Lemma 1.1. Two uniform laws on compact convex sets U and V can be coupled so that the midpoint has density at most 1/sqrt(|U||V|), by Knothe's triangular rearrangement.
  • The moment estimate — Proposition 2.1. With fixed sites, the weighted squared change of the cell moments is at most 162 R^2 times the weighted squared change of the cell masses, uniformly in the number and sizes of the cells.
  • Potentials of finite targets — Proposition 1.1. Two finite targets in B(0,L) have affine potentials whose gradients are their optimal maps and whose L^2 distance is at most (1 + sqrt 162) R W_2.
  • Gradients of convex functions — Lemma 1.1. A convex Lipschitz function has partial derivatives almost everywhere, is differentiable wherever all of them exist, and has a unique supporting slope there.
  • Gradient interpolation — Lemma 1.2. For convex L-Lipschitz functions on a convex body, the squared L^2 distance of the gradients is at most 12d h^-2 times that of the functions plus 28 L^2 P_K h/|K|.
  • Stability for finite targets — Corollary 1.3. For finite targets, ||T_mu - T_nu||_{L^2} <= C_* W_2^(1/3) with an explicit constant depending only on the body and the radius of the targets.
  • Existence and uniqueness of optimal maps — Proposition 2.1. Every target in a compact set has a unique quadratic optimal map from the uniform measure on a convex body, obtained as the limit of the maps of finite discretizations.
  • The theorem for all targets — Theorem 2.2. The one-third stability estimate holds for all Borel probability measures on the compact set, with the same constant.
