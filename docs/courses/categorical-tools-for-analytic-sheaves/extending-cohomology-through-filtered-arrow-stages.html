Extending cohomology through filtered arrow stages

Original text public domain (CC0).

A map into a target object can come from many objects of a triangulated category. Their outgoing comma category keeps every such map and every comparison between them. A left Kan extension assembles the values of a functor over this category. To preserve cohomology exactness, we must also assemble commuting squares to a target arrow. The resulting arrow index lets us compare the two target values over one filtered category and identify their kernel.

The argument works in an arbitrary abelian target with exact small filtered colimits. It does not need a chosen injective or projective object, nor a functorial choice of cones.

1. Hypotheses and the actual outgoing stages

Use the universe, local smallness and choice convention of Universes and small categories. Let

\[ \phi:\mathcal T\longrightarrow\mathcal D \]

be a triangulated functor, with its specified natural comparison

\[ \xi_U:\phi(U[1])\xrightarrow{\sim}(\phi U)[1]. \tag{1.1} \]

Let \(\mathcal A\) be abelian. Assume that all small filtered colimits exist in \(\mathcal A\), and that the colimit functor for each small filtered index preserves short exact sequences. Finally assume that, for each \(X\in\mathcal D\), the outgoing comma

\[ K_X=(\phi\downarrow X) \tag{1.2} \]

is cofinally small. Thus its objects are \((U,u:\phi U\to X)\). A morphism to \((V,v)\) is an actual arrow \(s:U\to V\) in \(\mathcal T\) with \(v\phi(s)=u\).

A covariant cohomological functor \(F:\mathcal T\to\mathcal A\) here means an additive functor such that each distinguished triangle \(P\to U\to V\to P[1]\) gives a sequence \(FP\to FU\to FV\) exact at its middle object. This convention is also called homological. Rotations give its full sequence of shifted exactness assertions.

The complete Hom-factorization and comma argument in Triangles, finite universal maps and the limits of cohomology, Lemma 1.1 and Corollary 2.3 proves that \(\phi\) is categorically exact: every outgoing comma is filtered, and every incoming comma is cofiltered. This statement concerns the triangulated functor \(\phi\), whose target is triangulated. It is not an assertion that the abelian-valued functor \(F\) is categorically exact.

The receiving-witness theorem in Incoming comma tests, Section 5, together with the small full replacement in Change an index, Section 5, gives a small full filtered cofinal subcategory of each \(K_X\). Hence

\[ E_F(X)=\operatorname{colim}_{(U,u)\in K_X}F(U) \tag{1.3} \]

exists: take the small filtered colimit and extend its cocone to the whole comma. The complete cocone extension and canonical inverse comparison are proved in Change an index, Section 2.

Retain the whole-comma legs

\[ \lambda^X_{U,u}:FU\longrightarrow E_F(X). \tag{1.4} \]

Their equations are

\[ \lambda^X_{V,v}F(s)=\lambda^X_{U,u} \quad\text{if }v\phi(s)=u. \tag{1.5} \]

These legs jointly determine a map out of \(E_F(X)\). The choice of a small witness computes this universal object; it does not discard the other labels or their cocone equations.

2. The Kan functor, unit and universal factors

For \(a:X\to X'\), define \(E_F(a)\) by

\[ E_F(a)\lambda^X_{U,u}=\lambda^{X'}_{U,au}. \tag{2.1} \]

The right side is a cocone by (1.5). Uniqueness on all legs proves the identity and composition laws. For a natural transformation \(z:F\to F'\), set

\[ (E_z)_X\lambda^X_{U,u} ={\lambda'}^X_{U,u}z_U. \tag{2.2} \]

The same uniqueness proves naturality in \(X\) and both transformation laws.

The unit has components

\[ \eta^F_U=\lambda^{\phi U}_{U,1_{\phi U}}: FU\longrightarrow E_F(\phi U). \tag{2.3} \]

If \(s:U\to V\), the comma arrow \((U,\phi s)\to(V,1_{\phi V})\), together with (2.1), gives \(E_F(\phi s)\eta^F_U=\eta^F_VF(s)\).

For any functor \(G:\mathcal D\to\mathcal A\), the inverse universal factors are

\[ \begin{aligned} \alpha:E_F\to G &\longmapsto (\alpha_{\phi U}\eta^F_U)_U,\\ \tau:F\to G\phi &\longmapsto \alpha, &\alpha_X\lambda^X_{U,u}&=G(u)\tau_U. \end{aligned} \tag{2.4} \]

The second line is a cocone because \(G(v)\tau_VF(s)=G(v)G(\phi s)\tau_U=G(u)\tau_U\). Its naturality in \(X\) follows by testing (2.1). Inserting \(u=1\) recovers \(\tau\). Conversely, naturality of \(\alpha\) at \(u\), and \(E_F(u)\eta^F_U=\lambda^X_{U,u}\), recover \(\alpha\) on every leg. The factors are inverse and natural in both \(F,G\), as in the complete pointwise Kan construction.

Thus \(E_F=\operatorname{Lan}_\phi F\) and

\[ \operatorname{Nat}(E_F,G) \cong\operatorname{Nat}(F,G\phi). \tag{2.5} \]

The construction applies to every functor \(F:\mathcal T\to\mathcal A\), since the filtered comma hypotheses concern \(\phi\). If the ambient transformation collections require a larger universe, use the ambient functor-category convention in Exactness and small Kan values, Section 7. The colimits in (1.3) still use small witnesses in the original working universe.

3. Zero, biproducts and addition on arrows

We first prove that \(E_F\) is additive when \(F\) is additive.

The object \((0,0:\phi0\to0)\) is terminal in \(K_0\): the unique map \(U\to0\) obeys its label equation. Since \(F0=0\), (1.3) gives \(E_F(0)=0\).

For \(X_1,X_2\), let

\[ \zeta:K_{X_1}\times K_{X_2}\longrightarrow K_{X_1\oplus X_2} \tag{3.1} \]

send \(((U_1,u_1),(U_2,u_2))\) to \((U_1\oplus U_2,u_1\oplus u_2)\), using the canonical biproduct comparison for the additive functor \(\phi\). Its left adjoint is

\[ \epsilon(U,u)=((U,p_1u),(U,p_2u)). \tag{3.2} \]

Indeed, an arrow from (3.2) to a pair \(((V_1,v_1),(V_2,v_2))\) is precisely a pair \(s_i:U\to V_i\) satisfying \(v_i\phi(s_i)=p_i u\). The column with entries \(s_1,s_2\) is precisely the arrow \((U,u)\to\zeta((V_1,v_1),(V_2,v_2))\). The inverse takes its two projections. These assignments preserve composition in both variables, so give the claimed adjunction.

A right adjoint is cofinal here for a concrete reason. The adjunction unit gives an initial object of each incoming comma \(((U,u)\downarrow\zeta)\): its arrows correspond to arrows from \(\epsilon(U,u)\). These commas are therefore nonempty and connected. Thus \(\zeta\) changes no colimit.

Both projections \(K_{X_1}\times K_{X_2}\to K_{X_i}\) are cofinal. For the first, its incoming comma at \(a\) is \((a\downarrow1_{K_{X_1}})\times K_{X_2}\). The first factor has initial object \((a,1)\); the second is nonempty and connected because it is filtered. This product is nonempty and connected. The other projection has the same proof with the factors exchanged.

Finite coproducts commute with these existing colimits by their universal property. A cocone on the diagram \(F(U_1)\oplus F(U_2)\) is exactly a pair of cocones on the two projected diagrams: take its two injection composites, or assemble those two composites by a biproduct. Naturality in each index and the two cofinal projections identify the latter cocones with those on \(K_{X_1},K_{X_2}\). Consequently

\[ \begin{aligned} E_F(X_1\oplus X_2) &\cong\operatorname{colim}_{K_{X_1}\times K_{X_2}} F(U_1\oplus U_2)\\ &\cong E_F(X_1)\oplus E_F(X_2). \end{aligned} \tag{3.3} \]

Every comparison is the canonical one respecting the original legs. On a pair of stages its composites with the two target projections are \(\lambda^{X_i}_{U_i,u_i}F(p_i)\). These are also the composites of \(E_F(p_i)\) by (2.1) and the comma projection arrows. Thus (3.3) is exactly the map with components \(E_F(p_1),E_F(p_2)\).

We spell out the implication for addition. A zero arrow factors through the zero object, so \(E_F\) sends it to zero. The biproduct identities and (3.3) identify the images of the canonical injections and projections with the corresponding canonical maps. The image of a diagonal is the diagonal, since its two projection composites are identities. The image of a codiagonal is the codiagonal, since its two injection composites are identities. For \(a,b:X\to Y\),

\[ a+b=\nabla_Y(a\oplus b)\Delta_X. \tag{3.4} \]

The four injection/projection composites of \(E_F(a\oplus b)\) are those of \(E_F(a)\oplus E_F(b)\), including the two zero off-diagonal entries. Applying \(E_F\) to (3.4) gives \(E_F(a+b)=E_F(a)+E_F(b)\). Together with the zero equation, this proves additivity on every Hom group.

4. Put both endpoints over one filtered index

Fix \(g:Y\to Z\) in \(\mathcal D\). Let

\[ J_g=(\operatorname{Mor}(\phi)\downarrow g). \tag{4.1} \]

An object \(j\) consists of an arrow \(t:U\to V\) and labels \[ a:\phi U\to Y,\qquad b:\phi V\to Z,\qquad b\phi(t)=ga. \tag{4.2} \]

An arrow to \(j'=(t':U'\to V',a',b')\) is a pair \(s:U\to U'\), \(r:V\to V'\) satisfying

\[ t's=rt,\qquad a'\phi(s)=a,\qquad b'\phi(r)=b. \tag{4.3} \]

The full arrow-comma theorem applies because \(\phi\) is categorically right exact and right small. It proves that \(J_g\) is filtered and cofinally small, and that the actual endpoint projections

\[ q_Y:J_g\to K_Y,\quad j\mapsto(U,a), \qquad q_Z:J_g\to K_Z,\quad j\mapsto(V,b) \tag{4.4} \]

are cofinal. Its proof identifies the whole arrow comma with \((g_*:K_Y\to K_Z\downarrow1_{K_Z})\); hence it includes the structured labels and all equations (4.3), rather than only the underlying source endpoints.

Choose one small full filtered cofinal \(S_g\subseteq J_g\). Composing its inclusion with the two cofinal projections gives

\[ E_F(Y)=\operatorname{colim}_{j\in S_g}F(U_j), \qquad E_F(Z)=\operatorname{colim}_{j\in S_g}F(V_j), \tag{4.5} \]

with the canonical comparison isomorphisms understood. Equation (4.2) gives \[ E_F(g)\lambda^Y_{U_j,a_j} =\lambda^Z_{V_j,b_j}F(t_j). \tag{4.6} \] Thus this same index represents the actual arrow \(E_F(g)\).

Put \(K_j=\ker F(t_j)\), with inclusion \(k_j:K_j\to FU_j\). For an arrow (4.3), \(F(r)F(t)=F(t')F(s)\) implies that \(F(s)k_j\) is killed by \(F(t')\). Its unique kernel factor defines \(K_j\to K_{j'}\). Kernel uniqueness proves the identity and composition laws and makes the \(k_j\) natural.

Lemma 4.1. The canonical map \[ \operatorname{colim}_{j\in S_g}K_j \longrightarrow\ker E_F(g) \tag{4.7} \] is an isomorphism.

Proof. Let \(I_j=\operatorname{im}F(t_j)\) and \(C_j=\operatorname{coker}F(t_j)\). The specified image factorizations and their universal maps make both into diagrams. At every stage there are natural short exact sequences \[ 0\to K_j\to FU_j\to I_j\to0, \qquad 0\to I_j\to FV_j\to C_j\to0. \tag{4.8} \] Pointwise kernels, images and cokernels form the corresponding short exact sequences of diagrams; the complete abelian diagram construction is Pointwise abelian structure, Sections 1–2.

Exact filtered colimits send the first sequence to an epimorphism \(\operatorname{colim}FU_j\to\operatorname{colim}I_j\) with kernel \(\operatorname{colim}K_j\). They send the second inclusion to a monomorphism \(\operatorname{colim}I_j\to\operatorname{colim}FV_j\). The composite is \(E_F(g)\) by (4.6). A monomorphism does not change the kernel of the map preceding it. The kernel inclusion so obtained has composite \(\lambda^Y_{U_j,a_j}k_j\) on every stage, which characterizes (4.7). This proves its exact canonical, rather than merely abstract, identification. \(\square\)

This proof uses no elements of objects of \(\mathcal A\), no projective test object, and no claim that a map into a colimit lifts to one stage.

5. Complete each square to a triangle

Theorem 5.1. Under the hypotheses of Section 1, if \(F:\mathcal T\to\mathcal A\) is covariantly cohomological, then \(\operatorname{Lan}_\phi F\) is additive and covariantly cohomological. It has the unit and full universal property (2.3)–(2.5).

Proof. Additivity and universality are already established. Take a distinguished triangle \[ X\xrightarrow fY\xrightarrow gZ\xrightarrow hX[1] \tag{5.1} \] in \(\mathcal D\). Consecutive triangle arrows have zero composite, so additivity gives \(E_F(g)E_F(f)=0\).

For a stage \(j=(t:U\to V,a,b)\) in (4.2), complete \(t\) to a distinguished triangle \[ P\xrightarrow pU\xrightarrow tV\xrightarrow rP[1]. \tag{5.2} \] The triangulated functor \(\phi\) makes \[ \phi P\xrightarrow{\phi p}\phi U \xrightarrow{\phi t}\phi V \xrightarrow{\xi_P\phi r}(\phi P)[1] \tag{5.3} \] distinguished. Rotate (5.3) and (5.1) once. The first two vertical maps \(a,b\) obey the square (4.2), so the triangle-completion axiom gives a third map \[ e:(\phi P)[1]\to X[1]. \] Full faithfulness of translation gives a unique \(d:\phi P\to X\) with \(d[1]=e\). The last square of the rotated triangle map, with both rotation signs retained, says \[ (-f[1])e=a[1](-(\phi p)[1]). \] Cancelling the two minus signs and using faithfulness of translation gives \[ fd=a\phi p. \tag{5.4} \] The other completed square is \(hb=d[1]\xi_P\phi r\). Thus we have the required morphism of the entire triangles, including its specified translation comparison.

Write \[ q:E_F(Y)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{coker}E_F(f). \tag{5.5} \] Cohomology exactness of \(F\) at \(FU\) in (5.2) identifies the image of \(Fp\) with \(K_j=\ker Ft\). Let its specified factor be \[ Fp=k_jv_j,\qquad v_j:FP\twoheadrightarrow K_j. \tag{5.6} \] The label \(d\) gives a whole-comma leg \(\lambda^X_{P,d}\). Equation (5.4), formula (2.1) and the comma arrow \(p:(P,fd)\to(U,a)\) give \[ E_F(f)\lambda^X_{P,d} =\lambda^Y_{U,a}Fp. \tag{5.7} \] Consequently \[ q\lambda^Y_{U,a}k_jv_j =qE_F(f)\lambda^X_{P,d}=0. \tag{5.8} \] Since \(v_j\) is epic, \(q\lambda^Y_{U,a}k_j=0\).

The maps \(\lambda^Y_{U_j,a_j}k_j\) form a cocone on the kernel diagram. Lemma 4.1 and the jointly determining colimit legs therefore give \[ qk=0,\qquad k:\ker E_F(g)\hookrightarrow E_F(Y). \tag{5.9} \] Only the existence of a triangle completion at each stage was used. No compatible family of the \(P,d\), or functorial cone construction, was required: every individual completion proves the same zero equation for that stage.

For completeness, let \(i:I\hookrightarrow E_F(Y)\) be the image inclusion of \(E_F(f)\). Since \(E_F(g)E_F(f)=0\), its epimorphic image factor implies \(E_F(g)i=0\), so \(i\) factors through \(k\). In an abelian category \(i\) is a kernel of \(q\). Equation (5.9) therefore makes \(k\) factor through \(i\). Writing \(i=ku\), \(k=iv\), the monicity of \(i,k\) gives \(vu=1_I\) and \(uv=1_{\ker E_F(g)}\). Hence the two subobjects coincide. This is exactly middle exactness of \[ E_F(X)\xrightarrow{E_F(f)}E_F(Y) \xrightarrow{E_F(g)}E_F(Z). \tag{5.10} \] Apply the same result to every rotation and shift of (5.1). These are distinguished triangles, with the original sign convention. Their middle exactness assertions give the entire cohomology sequence. \(\square\)

The theorem also makes (2.5) a universal property within cohomological functors whenever \(G\) is cohomological. Both sides are the same ordinary natural transformations; no extra compatibility condition is added.

6. Two explicit computations

Representable input. Take \(\mathcal A=\mathsf{Ab}\), fix \(P\in\mathcal T\), and put \(F(U)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal T}(P,U)\). Exact triangle Hom sequences prove that \(F\) is cohomological. Under the comma smallness assumption there is a canonical isomorphism \[ E_F(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D}(\phi P,X), \qquad \lambda^X_{U,u}(a)\longmapsto u\phi(a). \tag{6.1} \]

The displayed maps are homomorphisms and obey (1.5), so define the comparison. It is surjective: \(v:\phi P\to X\) is the image of \(1_P\) at the stage \((P,v)\). Every element of a filtered colimit of abelian groups is represented at a stage, including stages in a chosen small full witness; this is the full filtered module representative theorem.

If \(\lambda^X_{U,u}(a)\) maps to \(v\), then the comma arrow \(a:(P,v)\to(U,u)\) identifies this element with \(\lambda^X_{P,v}(1_P)\). Thus any two representatives with the same \(v\) are the same colimit element, proving injectivity. This includes zero representatives: at \((P,0)\), the arrows \(1_P\) and \(0\) from this object to itself give \(\lambda^X_{P,0}(1_P)=\lambda^X_{P,0}(0)=0\).

Naturality in \(X\) follows by postcomposing \(u\), and under (6.1) the unit sends \(a:P\to U\) to \(\phi(a)\). No full-faithfulness assumption on \(\phi\) was used.

Finite free complexes recover ordinary cohomology. Let \(R\) be a unital ring. Let \(\mathcal T\) be the full homotopy category of bounded complexes of finite-rank free left \(R\)-modules, and let \(\mathcal D=K(\operatorname{Mod}R)\). The cone, shift and finite-sum constructions preserve bounded finite-rank freeness, so \(\mathcal T\) is triangulated and its inclusion \(\phi\) is triangulated. The complete complexes and cone proof is Chain homotopies, mapping cones and triangles.

The category \(\mathcal T\) is essentially small: use standard modules \(R^n\), finite intervals of degrees, matrices for differentials satisfying \(d^2=0\), and then their homotopy classes. These are small sets. For fixed \(X\), the labels \(\phi U\to X\) form small Hom sets. Their union over those representatives, with all Hom sets retained, gives an essentially small comma \(K_X\), hence a small cofinal witness. There is no boundedness condition on \(X\).

For \(P=R[0]\), a chain map \(P\to U\) is a degree-zero cycle, determined by the image of \(1\). Two maps are homotopic exactly when their cycles differ by a degree-zero boundary. Therefore \[ \operatorname{Hom}_{K(\operatorname{Mod}R)}(R[0],U) \cong H^0(U) \tag{6.2} \] as abelian groups, naturally. Formula (6.1) gives \[ \operatorname{Lan}_{\phi}(H^0|_{\mathcal T})(X) \cong H^0(X). \tag{6.3} \] It identifies each stage map with the induced cohomology map. This computation uses arbitrary complexes and the finite free test \(R[0]\); it does not require a resolution of \(X\).

7. Four graded exercises with complete solutions

Exercise 1 (introductory: two terminal stages). Compute the extension for \(\phi=1_{\mathcal T}\). Then compute it for the zero triangulated functor \(\phi:\mathcal T\to\mathcal D\). Identify the two units and justify the smallness hypotheses.

Solution. For the identity functor, \((X,1_X)\) is terminal in \((1_{\mathcal T}\downarrow X)\): its incoming arrow from \((U,u)\) is exactly \(u\). Its one-object inclusion is cofinal because every incoming comma has this unique arrow. Thus \(E_F(X)=FX\), the stage map is \(F(u)\), and (2.1) identifies the functor with \(F\). The unit at \(U\) is the identity. The terminal singleton is small even when \(\mathcal T\) is ambient large.

For the zero functor, each label \(0\to X\) is unique, so \(K_X\) is the entire category \(\mathcal T\). Its zero object is terminal, making the singleton at zero cofinal. The zero triangulated functor sends every triangle to the all-zero distinguished triangle. Since \(F0=0\), every \(E_F(X)\) is zero, every arrow is its unique map, and the unit is \(FU\to0\). All these commas are filtered as well: they have objects, common receiver zero, and postcomposition to zero equalizes every parallel pair.

Exercise 2 (intermediate: keep the cone's degrees). In the second example take \(R=\mathbb Z\) and \(n\geq2\). Let \(C_n\) be the cone of multiplication by \(n\) on \(\mathbb Z[0]\). Compute the extension on \(\mathbb Z[0]\), \(C_n\) and \(\mathbb Z[1]\), and the maps in the associated triangle. Repeat the cone computation for multiplication by zero, including the preceding cohomology group.

Solution. With the positive cone-projection convention, \(C_n^{-1}=\mathbb Z\), \(C_n^0=\mathbb Z\), and its only nonzero differential is \(n:C_n^{-1}\to C_n^0\). Formula (6.3) identifies the extension with \(H^0\), including its arrow maps. Its three specified values are therefore \(\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z,0\). In the triangle \[ \mathbb Z[0]\xrightarrow n\mathbb Z[0] \xrightarrow jC_n\xrightarrow p\mathbb Z[1], \] the degree-zero map \(j\) is identity into the cone's degree-zero term, so its cohomology map is the quotient \(\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). The \(p\)-map has zero target in degree-zero cohomology. The sequence is \[ \mathbb Z\xrightarrow n\mathbb Z \twoheadrightarrow\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\to0. \] Its middle and next exactness are the elementary kernel and quotient equations. The preceding cone group is \(H^{-1}(C_n)=\ker(n:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z)=0\).

For \(n=0\), the cone differential is zero. Hence \(H^{-1}(C_0)=\mathbb Z\) and \(H^0(C_0)=\mathbb Z\). The latter receives the identity from the second \(\mathbb Z[0]\); the preceding connecting map \(H^{-1}(C_0)\to H^0(\mathbb Z[0])\) is identity because the cone projection has positive identity on its shifted summand. The full relevant part is \[ 0\to\mathbb Z\xrightarrow1\mathbb Z \xrightarrow0\mathbb Z\xrightarrow1\mathbb Z\to0. \] This also verifies the signs and the two different cone degrees. The identity-stage unit is the identity cohomology map in both cases.

Exercise 3 (advanced: compose the two universal extensions). Let \(\phi:\mathcal T\to\mathcal D\) and \(\psi:\mathcal D\to\mathcal E\) be triangulated functors, both with cofinally small outgoing commas. Retain the exact filtered-colimit assumption on \(\mathcal A\). Construct the canonical isomorphism \[ \operatorname{Lan}_\psi(\operatorname{Lan}_\phi F) \xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Lan}_{\psi\phi}F \tag{7.1} \] for cohomological \(F\). Specify it on all inner and outer legs, prove both inverse identities, and prove cohomology exactness.

Solution. The composite is triangulated, and right smallness composes by the complete small-composite theorem. Thus all three extensions exist by Section 1. Put \(L=\operatorname{Lan}_\phi F\), \(H=\operatorname{Lan}_\psi L\), \(N=\operatorname{Lan}_{\psi\phi}F\). Write their legs as \(\lambda^Y_{U,a}\), \(\mu^Z_{Y,b}\), \(\nu^Z_{U,c}\).

The composite's unit \(F\to N\psi\phi\), followed by the first adjunction, gives a transformation \(L\to N\psi\) whose component on \(\lambda^Y_{U,a}\) is \(\nu^{\psi Y}_{U,\psi a}\). The second adjunction gives \(\kappa:H\to N\) with \[ \kappa_Z\mu^Z_{Y,b}\lambda^Y_{U,a} =\nu^Z_{U,b\psi(a)}. \tag{7.2} \] These are natural factors, and both sets of legs jointly determine the result.

Conversely the units \(F\to L\phi\to H\psi\phi\) give \(\ell:N\to H\) with \[ \ell_Z\nu^Z_{U,c} =\mu^Z_{\phi U,c}\eta^\phi_U. \tag{7.3} \] Using \(a=1_{\phi U}\) in (7.2) shows \(\kappa\ell=1_N\) on every \(\nu\)-leg. For the other composite, (7.2)–(7.3) give \[ \ell_Z\kappa_Z\mu^Z_{Y,b}\lambda^Y_{U,a} =\mu^Z_{\phi U,b\psi(a)}\eta^\phi_U. \] The arrow \(a:(\phi U,b\psi(a))\to(Y,b)\) in the outer comma gives the right side as \(\mu^Z_{Y,b}L(a)\eta^\phi_U\). Equation (2.1) gives \(L(a)\eta^\phi_U=\lambda^Y_{U,a}\). Thus \(\ell\kappa=1_H\) on each inner leg and then each outer leg. This proves (7.1) with its actual canonical maps. Theorem 5.1 applies first to \(\phi\), then to \(\psi\), and also to their composite. All three functors are additive and cohomological.

Exercise 4 (expert: reverse the limit hypothesis and both commas). Suppose instead that \(\mathcal A\) admits exact small cofiltered limits and every incoming comma \((X\downarrow\phi)\) is co-initially small. Prove that the right Kan extension of a covariantly cohomological \(F:\mathcal T\to\mathcal A\) exists and is additive and covariantly cohomological. Specify its counit and universal property.

Solution. Use the actual opposite triangulated categories, with inverse translation and the specified rotation signs proved in Connecting signs and opposite triangle structures, Sections 3–6. Its Exercise 4 constructs the comparison that makes \(\phi^{\mathrm{op}}:\mathcal T^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal D^{\mathrm{op}}\) triangulated, retaining the inverse units and counits. The opposite target \(\mathcal A^{\mathrm{op}}\) is abelian: kernels and cokernels exchange, and the original coimage-to-image isomorphism, viewed oppositely, gives the required comparison.

By the opposite definition, a distinguished triangle with first three objects \(U,V,W\) in \(\mathcal T^{\mathrm{op}}\) reverses to a distinguished triangle with first three objects \(W,V,U\) in \(\mathcal T\). Applying \(F\) gives middle exactness of \(FW\to FV\to FU\) in \(\mathcal A\). Reversing that sequence gives middle exactness of \(F^{\mathrm{op}}U\to F^{\mathrm{op}}V\to F^{\mathrm{op}}W\) in \(\mathcal A^{\mathrm{op}}\). Thus \(F^{\mathrm{op}}\) is covariantly cohomological. Exact small cofiltered limits in \(\mathcal A\) are exact small filtered colimits in \(\mathcal A^{\mathrm{op}}\), with short exact sequences reversed. Finally \[ (\phi^{\mathrm{op}}\downarrow X) \cong(X\downarrow\phi)^{\mathrm{op}} \] as whole categories: the labels \(X\to\phi U\) are the same, and both source arrows and their triangles reverse. The assumed co-initial smallness is therefore exactly the outgoing cofinal smallness needed for Theorem 5.1 on the opposites.

Apply that theorem and reverse its result. It gives the functor \[ R_F(X)=\lim_{(U,u:X\to\phi U)\in(X\downarrow\phi)}FU. \tag{7.4} \] Its projections \(\pi^X_{U,u}:R_F(X)\to FU\) satisfy \[ \pi^{X'}_{U,u}R_F(a)=\pi^X_{U,ua} \quad(a:X\to X'), \] and its counit is \[ \varepsilon_U=\pi^{\phi U}_{U,1_{\phi U}}: R_F(\phi U)\to FU. \] Reversing both inverse factors (2.4) gives \[ \operatorname{Nat}(G,R_F) \cong\operatorname{Nat}(G\phi,F). \tag{7.5} \] For \(\tau:G\phi\to F\), the corresponding \(\alpha:G\to R_F\) is characterized by \(\pi^X_{U,u}\alpha_X=\tau_UG(u)\); the inverse is \((\varepsilon_U\alpha_{\phi U})_U\). The limit equations prove both naturalities and both inverse identities, or equivalently these are the exact reversals of the already-proved factors. Opposite additivity is the same Hom-group additivity. An original distinguished triangle with first three objects \(X,Y,Z\) determines an opposite distinguished triangle with first three objects \(Z,Y,X\): fullness of translation supplies its last arrow through the specified inverse counit. The proved opposite cohomology exactness, reversed in \(\mathcal A\), is exactly middle exactness of \(R_F(X)\to R_F(Y)\to R_F(Z)\). Hence \(R_F\) has every asserted property.
