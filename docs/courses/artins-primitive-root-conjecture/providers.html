Results on Artin's primitive root conjecture and their assumptions

The course proves the statements below; each link leads to the statement with its assumptions and its proof. The lessons are self-checked by the writing AI; independent mathematical review is not complete.

  • Primitive roots for every admissible base — Theorem 2.1. For every integer a other than -1 that is not a square there are c_a > 0 and x_a such that for x >= x_a at least c_a x/(log x)^2 primes p in (x, 2x) have p not dividing a and ord_p(a) = p - 1. Proved from Propositions 5.1 and 5.2 of lesson 1.
  • The splitting test — Lemma 3.1. For a != 0 and primes p, q with p not dividing aq, p is unramified in K_q = Q(mu_q, a^(1/q)), and it splits completely there if and only if p = 1 mod q and a^((p-1)/q) = 1 mod p.
  • A progression of quadratic nonresidues — Lemma 4.1. For admissible a and M = 8 times the product of the odd primes dividing a there are c in {2, 4} and u with (u, M) = 1, c | u - 1 and ((u - 1)/c, M/c) = 1 such that every prime p = u mod M satisfies p not dividing a and (a/p) = -1.
  • Uniform complete splitting — Proposition 5.2. For |a| > 1, large x and large primes q <= exp((log x)^0.3), the primes p in (x, 2x] that split completely in K_q number O_a(x/(q(q - 1) log x) + x^(1 - 10^-6)), uniformly in q. Proved in lesson 15 from the zero-free strip.
  • Simultaneous primitive roots — Theorem 6.1. For distinct positive primes q_1, ..., q_k, at least c x/(log x)^2 primes p in (x, 2x) have every q_i as a primitive root.
  • Sextic reciprocity without supplementary factors — Proposition 2.1. For distinct exterior primes with allowed generators p, p' of a cyclotomic field containing mu_12, chi_p(p') = chi_p'(p); units enter only through the Hilbert pairing H_S.
  • Kummer characters — Lemma 3.1. For an S-integer u, the ideal function a -> chi_a(u) agrees with a finite-order Hecke character kappa_u whose conductor has norm O(N rad(uR)); kappa_u = kappa_v exactly when u/v is a sixth power.
  • Weil's index — Named result N2. The local Weil index depends only on the square class, satisfies w(a)w(b) = (a, b)_2 w(ab), is 1 at almost all places with product 1 over all places, and is a normalized quadratic Gauss sum at odd places. Used without proof (Rao; Liu and Zhu).
  • The duplication identity — Proposition 5.1. At every exterior prime gamma_1 gamma_2 = -Lambda G, where Lambda comes from the Eisenstein normalization of cubic Jacobi sums and G from the Weil index; the normalized Gauss sums gamma_j are multiplicative up to the reciprocity twist.
  • The reflected second moment — Proposition 4.1. The sum of |B_m(Z)|^2 over the row box is O(Z^(2M_0 - 1 + eps)), with the dyadic refinement Z^eps P^(1/2)(D + D^2 P/Z). Proved in lessons 6 to 10.
  • Mellin continuation — Proposition 6.1. If H is holomorphic, bounded and nowhere zero on Re s >= 1 - delta_1 and the inverse Mellin integral of Z^(s - c) e^((s - s_0)^2) H(s)/L^S(s, eta) is O(Z^(1 - c - delta)), then L_F(s, eta) has no zero in Re s > 1 - delta.
  • The cubic theta representation (Kazhdan and Patterson) — Named result N1. The cubic cover of PGL_2 over a number field containing mu_3 carries an exceptional theta representation with a nonzero global Whittaker functional, local uniqueness at the exterior primes and the stated local exceptional representations. Used without proof.
  • Local Whittaker values — Proposition 4.1. W(a(p^e u)) = chi(u)^(2e) times Q^-k, Q^(-k-1/2) g_2 or 0 for e = 3k, 3k + 1, 3k + 2 with k >= 0, and 0 for e < 0, by an explicit shell computation.
  • The exterior theta coefficient — Corollary 5.1. The product of the local Whittaker values at A = c n^3 with c squarefree is gamma_2(c)/(N c^(1/2) N n), and 0 when A has no such form.
  • Exact Gaussian smoothing — Lemma 6.1. The Mellin transform of the archimedean profile V is C_0 A_0^s [Gamma(s + 5/6) Gamma(s + 7/6)]^r, and a rapidly decaying kernel with transform e^(s^2)/V^(s) turns V into the Gaussian weight P_G.
  • Local reflection — Lemma 1.1. The local reflected sums equal s chi(t)^b with b = 8 - j at e = 0 and b = 4 + 2e for e > 0; for j = 1 only e = 0 survives, with a factor xi_p of modulus 1.
  • The reflected expansion — Lemma 5.1. B_m(Z) is an absolutely convergent sum of frozen, row and column factors with the quadratic interaction chi_d(c')^3.
  • A quadratic large sieve over number fields (Goldmakher and Louvel) — Named result N3. For a quadratic Hecke family, the sum over squarefree a with N a <= M of |sum over squarefree b with N b <= N of lambda_b chi_b(a)|^2 is O_eps((MN)^eps (M + N) sum |lambda_b|^2). Used without proof.
  • The large sieve for the exterior symbols — Corollary 2.2. For complex z_c on squarefree exterior ideals of norm at most C, the sum over squarefree d of norm at most D of |sum z_c chi_d(c)^3|^2 is O_eps((C + D)(CD)^eps sum |z_c|^2).
  • The probe bound (P1) — Proposition 4.1. I(Z^a, Z^b, Z) = O_eps(Z^(933/2000 + eps)).
  • The Euler product of the probe — Proposition 5.1. F_u(xi, w, z) = zeta_F^S(6z) L^S(w, chi(u)) H_u(xi, w, z)/L^S(xi, eta conj chi(u)), with H_u holomorphic and O(q_u^eps), and H_1 bounded and nowhere zero.
  • A large sieve for primitive characters — Lemma 2.1. For distinct primitive finite-order Hecke characters with conductor norms at most C_0 U, the mean square of sum b_n omega(n) over N n <= N is O((N + U^2)(NU)^eps sum |b_n|^2).
  • Exceptional frequencies — Proposition 4.1. Outside a set of O(U^(1/2)) frequencies u with U <= q_u < 2U, 1/L^S(xi, eta conj chi(u)) = O(U^eps) for Re xi >= 0.998 and |Im xi| <= 2U^rho.
  • The probe asymptotic (P2) — Theorem 4.1. I(Z^a, Z^b, Z) = c_0 f_eta(Z) + O(Z^(7/15 - 0.0004)).
  • The zero-free strip — Corollary 4.2. For every cyclotomic field F containing mu_12 and every finite-order Hecke character eta of F, L_F(s, eta) has no zero in Re s > 1 - 1/20000. This is Theorem 5.3 of lesson 1.
  • A uniform zero count (Hasanalizade, Shen and Wong) — Named result N4. |N_K(T) - (T/pi) log(d_K (T/(2 pi e))^(n_K))| <= 0.228 (log d_K + n_K log T) + 23.108 n_K + 4.520 for every number field K and T >= 1. Used without proof.
  • Smooth prime ideal sums — Lemma 3.1. If zeta_K has no zero in Re s > 1 - delta, the smoothed prime ideal sum Theta_(K, phi)(x) is O(x + x^(1 - delta)(log D + n)), with a constant independent of K.
  • A block sieve (Ford and Halberstam) — Lemma 2.1. If g(p) <= 1 - eta_0 and the sum of g(p) over v < p <= v^2 is at most C_0, the sifted weight is X prod (1 - g(p))(1 + O(e^-H)) + O(sum over d <= z^(4H + 2) of |E(d)|); with H = 2, an upper bound with moduli up to z^10.
  • Buchstab's identity and an upper bound — Lemma 3.1. The integral from b to 1/2 of D_t(1 - t) dt/t equals D_b(1) - 1, and D_b(1) <= (e^-gamma/b)(1 + C_d e^(-1/(20b))) for small b.
  • A one-sided marked Type II estimate — Named result N5. Bilinear sums of the prime indicator minus its W-rough average against W-rough coefficients, weighted by a group-invariant F and the marked weight W(mn - 1), are O(X (log x)^(-D_*)) once the number K of prime groups is large. Used without proof (OpenAI, The Poisson-Dirichlet law for prime predecessors, Theorem 3.1).
  • The coefficient criterion — Named result N6. The estimate N5 holds for every coefficient sequence that cancels to (log x)^(-A) against characters of modulus at most (log x)^(A_0) twisted by m^(it), |t| <= 2X (log x)^B. Used without proof (extracted from the proof of N5).
  • Long prime polynomials and logarithmic phases — Named result N7. Sums of chi(p) p^(-1 + iu) over primes in long intervals, and sums of n^(iu) over progressions, cancel at large heights u, uniformly in characters of modulus at most a power of log x. Used without proof.
  • A rough-factor Type II estimate — Proposition 5.1. The marked Type II estimate holds for the indicator of x^gamma-rough integers minus D_gamma(log m/L)/(L V(W)) times the indicator of W-rough integers.
  • Distribution of the weighted family — Lemma 4.1. The weighted family d = crQ + 1 is well distributed in progressions to moduli up to x^(1/2 - kappa/2), by Bombieri-Vinogradov, and its total weight X_0 is asymptotic to x J_0 A_Psi/(c phi(M/c) log x).
  • Primes with controlled predecessors — Theorem 6.1. For M a positive multiple of 8, c in {2, 4} and u as in lesson 1, at least c_(M,c,u) x/(log x)^2 primes p in (x, 2x) satisfy p = u mod M and p - 1 = crQ with Q > x^0.9 prime and every prime factor of r in (exp((log x)^0.1), exp((log x)^0.3)). This is Proposition 5.1 of lesson 1.

Reading order

Nineteen lessons, to be read in order. The first states the theorem and reduces it to two propositions: many primes p = crQ + 1 in a progression of quadratic nonresidues, and a uniform bound for the primes that split completely in the Kummer fields. Lessons 2 to 14 prove a zero-free strip Re s > 1 - 10^-6 for the Hecke L-functions of cyclotomic fields containing the twelfth roots of unity: lessons 2 to 4 set up S-integers, sextic reciprocity, Kummer characters and the correction characters of cubic Gauss sums; lesson 5 defines the coefficient family and plans the argument; lessons 6 to 10 prove a reflected second moment with the cubic theta function, a Weyl reflection and a quadratic large sieve; lessons 11 to 14 evaluate a Poisson probe and its Euler product and detect the zeros. Lesson 15 deduces the uniform splitting bound, and lessons 16 to 19 construct the primes p = crQ + 1 with a block sieve, Bombieri-Vinogradov and marked bilinear estimates. The theory of Kazhdan and Patterson, Weil's index, the quadratic large sieve of Goldmakher and Louvel, a uniform zero count of Hasanalizade, Shen and Wong, and the marked Type II estimates of OpenAI's companion paper are used without proof, as the named results N1 to N7.
