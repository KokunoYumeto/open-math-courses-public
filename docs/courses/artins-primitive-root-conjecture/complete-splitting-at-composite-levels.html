Complete splitting at composite levels

Written by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic) on 23 October 2026. The question was posed by Claude while writing this course and solved by GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI) in ChatGPT's Pro mode; the solution was refereed by Claude Opus 5.5 in a separate session, and this lesson was written from it and refereed and revised by Claude Opus 5.5 in two further sessions. Public domain (CC0).

This lesson extends Proposition 5.2 of Artin's conjecture and the index of a prime from prime levels \(q\) to every integer level \(n\), with no lower threshold on \(n\). The Kummer field at level \(n\) is \(K_n=\mathbb Q(\mu_n,a^{1/n})\).

  • Section 1 bounds the degree of \(K_n\) from both sides, and Section 2 bounds its discriminant.
  • Section 3 shows that the zero-free strip descends to \(\zeta_{K_n}\) for every \(n\), and Section 4 proves the uniform count of completely split primes.
  • Section 5 proves a deletion theorem. It turns a supply of primes with prescribed index at finitely many small primes into primes with \(i_p(a)=m\). It also shows that the required Frobenius condition is nonempty whenever one prime with index \(m\) exists.

Prerequisites.

  • Artin's conjecture and the index of a prime, Lemmas 1.1, 3.1 and 4.1, Theorem 2.1 and Proposition 5.1.
  • Uniform complete splitting in Kummer fields, Lemmas 1.1 and 3.1 and Section 4.
  • The zero-free strip, Corollary 4.2.
  • Primes with controlled predecessors, Theorem 6.1.
  • Hilbert's ramification theory in Galois extensions, Corollary 6.5.
  • The different and the discriminant, (1)–(4), Proposition 14.2 and Theorem 14.3.
  • Discriminants and integral bases, Proposition 2.2.
  • Artin L-functions, conductors and discriminants, Proposition 21.1 and Theorem 21.2.

1. Degrees

Fix \(a\in\mathbb Z\) with \(A=|a|>1\). Put \(E_n=\mathbb Q(\mu_n)\) and \(K_n=E_n(\alpha)\), where \(\alpha^n=a\). The field \(K_n\) is the splitting field of \(T^n-a\), so it is Galois over \(\mathbb Q\). Write \(d_n=[K_n:\mathbb Q]\) and \(D_n\) for its absolute discriminant.

Write \(A=b^h\), where \(h\) is the greatest common divisor of the exponents in the prime factorization of \(A\). Then \(b>1\), and the exponents of \(b\) have greatest common divisor \(1\).

Lemma 1.1. Let \(v,w\ge1\) be coprime integers. If the positive real number \(b^{w/v}\) lies in an abelian extension of \(\mathbb Q\), then \(v\le2\).

Proof. We first show that \(X^v-b^w\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\). Let \(P\) be a monic factor of degree \(j\) with \(1\le j\le v\). By Gauss's lemma, \(P\) has integer coefficients. All roots of \(X^v-b^w\) have absolute value \(b^{w/v}\), so the constant term of \(P\) is an integer \(B\) with \(|B|=b^{wj/v}\). Then \(|B|^v=b^{wj}\), so \(v\mid wj\,v_\ell(b)\) for every prime \(\ell\mid b\). Since the \(v_\ell(b)\) have greatest common divisor \(1\) and \((v,w)=1\), we get \(v\mid j\), hence \(j=v\).

Every subgroup of an abelian group is normal, so the field \(\mathbb Q(b^{w/v})\) is Galois over \(\mathbb Q\). It is a real field. By irreducibility, it contains all the conjugates \(b^{w/v}\zeta_v^j\). These are all real only if \(v\le2\). \(\square\)

Proposition 1.2. Put \(g=(n,h)\). Then \[ \frac{n\varphi(n)}{2g}\le d_n\le n\varphi(n). \] For every prime \(q>\max(h,2)\) one has \(d_q=q(q-1)\).

Proof. Put \(m=n/g\) and \(u=h/g\), so that \((u,m)=1\). Let \(\beta=b^{u/m}>0\); then \(\beta^n=A\). Let \(F\) be a cyclotomic field containing \(\mu_m\), and put \(e=[F(\beta):F]\).

Since \(\beta^m\in\mathbb Q\), the map \(\sigma\mapsto\sigma(\beta)/\beta\) is an injective homomorphism from \(\operatorname{Gal}(F(\beta)/F)\) into \(\mu_m\). Its image is \(\mu_e\). So \(\beta^e=b^{ue/m}\) is fixed by the whole Galois group and lies in \(F\). In lowest terms, \(ue/m\) has denominator \(m/e\), and Lemma 1.1 gives \(m/e\le2\).

If \(a>0\), take \(\alpha=\beta\) and \(F=E_n\). If \(a<0\), take \(\alpha=\zeta_{2n}\beta\), so that \(\alpha^n=a\), and \(F=E_{2n}\). Since \(\zeta_{2n}\in F\), we have \(K_nF=F(\beta)\), and \[ \begin{aligned} [K_n:E_n]&\ge[K_nF:F]\\ &=[F(\beta):F]\ge m/2 . \end{aligned} \] In both cases \(d_n\ge\varphi(n)n/(2g)\). The upper bound holds because \(\alpha\) is a root of \(T^n-a\) over \(E_n\).

For a prime \(q>\max(h,2)\) we have \(g=1\), so \(e_q=[K_q:E_q]\ge q/2\). Moreover \(e_q\mid q\), so \(e_q=q\). \(\square\)

Example 1.3. Take \(a=-4\). If \(c^j=-4\) with \(c\in\mathbb Z\) and \(j\ge2\), then \(j\) is odd and divides \(v_2(4)=2\), which is impossible. So \(-4\) is not a perfect power of an integer.

Let \(n=4k\), and choose \(\alpha\) with \(\alpha^k=1+i\). Then \(\alpha^n=(1+i)^4=-4\). Since \(1+i\in E_n\), this gives \([K_n:E_n]\le k\). Here \(h=2\) and \(g=2\), so Proposition 1.2 gives the reverse inequality, and \[ d_n=\frac{n\varphi(n)}4\qquad(4\mid n). \] For instance, \(T^4+4=(T^2-2T+2)(T^2+2T+2)\) has the roots \(\pm1\pm i\), so \(K_4=\mathbb Q(i)\). Consequently, no formula \(d_n=n\varphi(n)/\epsilon\) with \(\epsilon\in\{1,2\}\) holds for all integers that are not perfect powers.

2. The discriminant

Proposition 2.1. For all \(n\ge1\), \[ \begin{aligned} \log D_n&\le d_n(2\log n+\log A),\\ \log D_n+d_n&\le C_A\,d_n\log(2n), \end{aligned} \] where \(C_A=3+\log A/\log2\).

Proof. The cyclotomic field. Write \(T^n-1=\Phi_n(T)G(T)\) with \(G\in\mathbb Z[T]\). Differentiating at \(\zeta_n\) gives \[ n\zeta_n^{n-1}=\Phi_n'(\zeta_n)G(\zeta_n). \] By Proposition 2.2 of Discriminants and integral bases, \(\pm N(\Phi_n'(\zeta_n))\) is the discriminant of \(\mathbb Z[\zeta_n]\), and the field discriminant divides it. The integer \(N(G(\zeta_n))\) is nonzero, so this norm divides \(N(n\zeta_n^{n-1})=\pm n^{\varphi(n)}\). Hence \(D_{E_n}\mid n^{\varphi(n)}\).

The relative step. Let \(e=[K_n:E_n]\). The map \(\sigma\mapsto\sigma(\alpha)/\alpha\) embeds \(\operatorname{Gal}(K_n/E_n)\) in \(\mu_n\), with image \(\mu_e\). The conjugates of \(\alpha\) are therefore \(\alpha\mu_e\), and its minimal polynomial is \(f=T^e-c\) with \(c=\alpha^e\). The element \(c\) is integral, and \(|c|=A^{e/n}\) at every embedding.

The argument of Lemma 1.1 of Uniform complete splitting in Kummer fields applies to \(f\). It uses Euler's lemma and (1), (4) and (15) of The different and the discriminant. It shows that \(f'(\alpha)\in\mathfrak D_{K_n/E_n}\), so the relative discriminant divides \(N_{K_n/E_n}(e\alpha^{e-1})=\pm e^ec^{e-1}\). Its absolute norm is at most \[ e^{e\varphi(n)}A^{(e-1)e\varphi(n)/n}. \]

The tower. The tower formula (16) there gives \[ \begin{aligned} \log D_n&\le e\varphi(n)\log n\\ &\quad+e\varphi(n)(\log e+\log A), \end{aligned} \] using \((e-1)/n\le1\). Since \(e\le n\) and \(d_n=e\varphi(n)\), this is the first bound.

For the second bound, \(1-2\log2<0\) and \(\log(2n)\ge\log 2\) give \[ \begin{aligned} &2\log n+\log A+1\\ &\le2\log(2n)+\tfrac{\log A}{\log2}\log(2n)\\ &\le C_A\log(2n). \qquad\square \end{aligned} \]

3. Splitting and the descent of the strip

Lemma 3.1. Let \(p\nmid an\). The following are equivalent:

  1. \(n\mid i_p(a)\);
  2. \(p\) splits completely in \(K_n\);
  3. \(p\equiv1\pmod n\) and \(a^{(p-1)/n}\equiv1\pmod p\).

Proof. Conditions 1 and 3 are equivalent by the proof of Lemma 1.1 of Artin's conjecture and the index of a prime with \(q\) replaced by \(n\).

For the equivalence of 2 and 3, follow the proof of Lemma 3.1 of Artin's conjecture and the index of a prime with \(q\) replaced by \(n\). The discriminant \(\pm n^na^{n-1}\) of \(T^n-a\) is prime to \(p\), so the roots of \(T^n-a\) have distinct reductions and \(p\) is unramified. The identity \(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(1-\zeta_n^i)=n\) makes reduction injective on \(\mu_n\). The Frobenius \(\sigma\) therefore fixes \(\zeta_n\) exactly when \(p\equiv1\pmod n\). Under that condition, \(\sigma(\alpha)=\alpha\) exactly when \(a^{(p-1)/n}\equiv1\pmod p\).

By Corollary 6.5 of Hilbert's ramification theory in Galois extensions, an unramified prime splits completely exactly when its Frobenius is trivial. \(\square\)

Proposition 3.2. For every \(n\ge1\), \(\zeta_{K_n}(s)\ne0\) in \(\operatorname{Re}s>1-10^{-6}\).

Proof. Let \(F_n=\mathbb Q(\mu_{12n})\) and \(T_n=K_nF_n=F_n(\alpha)\).

  • The extension \(T_n/F_n\) is Galois, and \(\sigma\mapsto\sigma(\alpha)/\alpha\) embeds its group in \(\mu_n\). So the group is cyclic.
  • The extension \(T_n/K_n\) is Galois, and restriction to \(F_n\) embeds its group in the abelian group \(\operatorname{Gal}(F_n/\mathbb Q)\). So it is abelian.

By (4) of Proposition 21.1 and Theorem 21.2 of Artin L-functions, conductors and discriminants, \[ \begin{gathered} \zeta_{T_n}(s)=\prod_\eta L_{F_n}(s,\eta),\\ \frac{\zeta_{T_n}(s)}{\zeta_{K_n}(s)}=\prod_{\eta'\ne1}L_{K_n}(s,\eta'). \end{gathered} \] Here \(\eta\) runs over the characters of \(\operatorname{Gal}(T_n/F_n)\), and \(\eta'\) over the nontrivial characters of \(\operatorname{Gal}(T_n/K_n)\).

Since \(F_n\supseteq\mu_{12}\), Corollary 4.2 of The zero-free strip makes every \(L_{F_n}(s,\eta)\), and hence \(\zeta_{T_n}\), zero-free in the half-plane. Each \(L_{K_n}(s,\eta')\) is entire. A zero of \(\zeta_{K_n}\) in the half-plane would therefore be a zero of \(\zeta_{T_n}\), which is impossible. \(\square\)

4. The bound at every level

Theorem 4.1. Put \(L=\log x\) and \(\delta_0=10^{-6}\). There is an absolute constant \(C_*\) such that, for all \(n\ge1\) and \(x\ge\max(A,n,2)+1\), \[ \begin{aligned} &\#\{x<p\le2x:\ p\ \text{splits}\\ &\qquad\text{completely in}\ K_n\}\\ &\le\frac{C_*x}{d_nL}\\ &\quad+C_*x^{1-\delta_0}\,\frac{2\log n+\log A+1}{L}. \end{aligned} \] For \(n\le\exp(L^{0.3})\) and \(L\ge1\), the count is at most \[ C_*\Bigl(\frac{x}{d_nL}+(4+\log A)x^{1-\delta_0}\Bigr). \]

Proof. Since \(x>\max(A,n)\), no prime in \((x,2x]\) divides \(an\). Fix \(\phi\) as in Section 4 of Uniform complete splitting in Kummer fields. Proposition 3.2 allows us to apply Lemma 3.1 there to \(K_n\) with \(\delta=\delta_0\). Each split prime contributes at least \(d_nL\) to the nonnegative sum \(\Theta_{K_n,\phi}(x)\). Inserting Proposition 2.1 gives the first bound.

For \(n\le\exp(L^{0.3})\) we have \(2\log n/L\le2\), and \((\log A+1)/L\le\log A+1\). This gives the second bound. \(\square\)

5. Deletion and seeds

Put \(y=\exp(L^{0.1})\) and \(z=\exp(L^{0.3})\).

Theorem 5.1. Fix an even integer \(C\), a divisor \(m\mid C\) and \(\kappa>0\). Suppose that, for large \(x\), there is a set \(\mathcal T(x)\) of at least \(\kappa x/L^2\) primes \(p\in(x,2x)\) with the following two properties:

  • \(p-1=CrQ\), where \(Q>x^{0.9}\) is prime and every prime factor of \(r\) lies in \((y,z)\);
  • \(v_\ell(i_p(a))=v_\ell(m)\) for every prime \(\ell\mid C\).

Then, for large \(x\), at least \(\frac\kappa2x/L^2\) primes of \(\mathcal T(x)\) satisfy \(i_p(a)=m\).

Proof. Take \(x\) so large that \(x>A\), \(y>\max(h,2)\) and \(z<x\). The second property and \(m\mid C\) give \(m\mid i_p(a)\).

Suppose \(i_p(a)>m\), and let \(q\) be a prime factor of \(i_p(a)/m\). By the second property, \(q\nmid C\). Since \(q\mid p-1=CrQ\), either \(q=Q\) or \(q\mid r\).

The case \(q=Q\). Then \(\operatorname{ord}_p(a)\le Cr<2x^{0.1}\), so \(p\) divides \(B_a(x)\) as in the proof of Theorem 2.1 of Artin's conjecture and the index of a prime. There are \(O_a(x^{0.2}/L)\) such primes.

The case \(q\mid r\). Then \(y<q<z\). Since \(q>h\), Proposition 1.2 gives \(d_q=q(q-1)\). By Lemma 3.1, \(p\) splits completely in \(K_q\), and \(p\nmid aq\). Summing Theorem 4.1 over \(q\), using \(\sum_{j>y}1/(j(j-1))\le2/y\), bounds these primes by \[ C_a\Bigl(\frac{2x}{Ly}+zx^{1-\delta_0}\Bigr). \] Both exceptional counts are \(o(x/L^2)\), so for large \(x\) together they are at most \(\frac\kappa2x/L^2\). \(\square\)

Lemma 5.2. Suppose \(i_{p_1}(a)=m\) for some prime \(p_1\nmid a\). Put \[ C=\prod_{\ell\mid2m}\ell^{\,v_\ell(p_1-1)}. \] Then \(C\) is even, \(m\mid C\) and \(p_1\nmid aC\). Let \(\Sigma\) be the set of \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K_C/\mathbb Q)\) whose restriction to \(K_m\) is trivial and whose restriction to \(K_{m\ell}\) is nontrivial for every prime \(\ell\mid C\) with \(m\ell\mid C\). Then \(\Sigma\) is stable under conjugation and contains the Frobenius class of \(p_1\).

Proof. The integer \(p_1-1\) is even and divisible by \(m\), so \(C\) is even and \(m\mid C\). Also \(C\mid p_1-1\), hence \(p_1\nmid C\).

For \(t\mid C\), the field \(K_t\) is a Galois subfield of \(K_C\), via \(\zeta_t=\zeta_C^{C/t}\) and \(\alpha_t=\alpha^{C/t}\). Restriction to \(K_t\) is a homomorphism, so \((\tau\sigma\tau^{-1})|_{K_t}\) is the conjugate of \(\sigma|_{K_t}\) by \(\tau|_{K_t}\). A conjugate of an element is trivial exactly when the element is, so \(\Sigma\) is stable under conjugation.

Finally, \(m\mid i_{p_1}(a)\) and \(m\ell\nmid i_{p_1}(a)\). Lemma 3.1 converts these into \(\operatorname{Frob}_{p_1}\in\Sigma\). \(\square\)

Proposition 5.1 of Artin's conjecture and the index of a prime, proved as Theorem 6.1 of Primes with controlled predecessors, holds for every triple \((M,c,u)\) satisfying (4.1) there, with no reference to \(a\).

An index-2 instance. Take \((M,c,u)=(8,2,7)\) and \(a=2\); (4.1) holds since \((7,8)=1\), \(2\mid6\) and \((3,4)=1\). Then \(\bigl(\tfrac2p\bigr)=1\) and \(v_2(p-1)=1\), so \(v_2(i_p(2))=1\). Theorem 5.1 with \(C=m=2\) gives at least a constant times \(x/L^2\) primes with \(i_p(2)=2\).

What that proposition cannot reach. Its primes satisfy \(3\nmid p-1\) once \(y>3\), so none of them satisfies \(i_p(2)=3\). A lower bound for the sieve with the Frobenius condition \(\Sigma\) inserted is not provided by that proposition.

6. Exercises

6.1. Show that \(d_6=4\) for \(a=-8\).

6.2. Deduce Theorem 2.1 of Artin's conjecture and the index of a prime from Theorem 5.1.

6.3. Show that \(K_n\) is unramified outside the primes dividing \(an\).

7. Solutions

6.1. We have \((\sqrt{-2})^6=(-2)^3=-8\), so \(K_6=E_6(\sqrt{-2})=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3},\sqrt{-2})\). The two quadratic fields are distinct, so the degree is \(4\).

6.2. Let \(a\) be admissible and take the triple \((M,c,u)\) of Lemma 4.1 of Artin's conjecture and the index of a prime. Proposition 5.1 there supplies the first property with \(C=c\). Put \(m=1\). Since \(\bigl(\tfrac ap\bigr)=-1\), the index \(i_p(a)\) is odd, so \(v_2(i_p(a))=0\), which is the second property. Theorem 5.1 gives the conclusion.

6.3. The proof of Proposition 2.1 shows two things:

  • the relative discriminant of \(K_n/E_n\) divides \(e^ec^{e-1}\), with \(e\mid n\), so it is supported on the primes dividing \(an\);
  • \(D_{E_n}\) divides \(n^{\varphi(n)}\).

By the tower formula, \(D_n\) is divisible only by primes dividing \(an\). A prime ramifies exactly when it divides the discriminant (Proposition 14.2 of The different and the discriminant).
