Preparation and proof dependencies

The course assumes functional analysis, elementary topology, Banach and C*-algebras. Each lesson specifies the exact results used from other courses.

A missing public reading route and a missing mathematical proof are different. The entries below distinguish exact programme proof providers from other prerequisite readings and optional comparisons.

Bott–Dirac foundations for the crossed-product comparison

Bott periodicity in KK, Lemma 4.0 and Theorem 4.1 prove the weighted oscillator domain, compact resolvent, even Gaussian kernel and first inverse product. Theorem 5.2 proves the reverse product by a rotation of the two position and Clifford spaces. Propositions 7.1–7.2 and Theorem 7.3 identify the cone and coisometry cycles, their positive Cayley winding and both extension products.

These are the scalar inputs in Descent and the K-theory of crossed products, Lemma 5.1. With its positive Fourier transform and right-Clifford order, the inverse Thom class is minus the dual Thom class followed by the Takai Morita class. Theorem 6.1 and Proposition 6.2 give both inverse products and the ordinary boundary comparison used in Lesson 21, Proposition 6.3. The comparison has a separable coefficient algebra, possibly nonunital, and countably generated modules. It does not assert the same KK result for arbitrary nonseparable sources.

Thom equivalence and the K-amenable quotient

For the mapping-torus comparison, Descent and the K-theory of crossed products, Sections 4–6, Theorem 6.1 and Proposition 6.2 supply the real Thom pair, both inverse products, and the positive Fourier/right-Clifford boundary normalization. Its scalar foundation is the Bott–Dirac calculation linked above.

Small equivalence modules and cancellation

Lesson 20, Lemma 5.5 uses Hilbert C*-modules Lesson 15, Theorem 3.2, the trace identity (4.4) and Theorem 6.1. These construct full finite projective right modules over an irrational rotation algebra with arbitrarily small positive trace dimension and with a rotation algebra as their entire adjointable endomorphism algebra. They are an essential input to the full cancellation proof, not just a classification comparison.

For positive irrational t, the constructed left A1/t, right At module induces a K-group map from A1/t to At with trace multiplier t; the inverse has multiplier 1/t. Tensor products multiply these factors in the stated direction by Lesson 14, Proposition 7.5. The compact-identity criterion is Finite projective modules, Theorem 2.2.

The coefficient positivity and frame are proved in Connections and curvature, Lemma 7.1 and Theorem 7.2. Inverse modules and tensor transitivity are Imprimitivity bimodules, Theorem 3.2 and Corollary 4.2. The torus provider uses only Lesson 20, Theorems 2.1, 3.1 and 5.1, not its cancellation theorem; this dependency is acyclic.

Used in Lesson 20.

Strong Morita classification of irrational rotation algebras

Hilbert C*-modules Lesson 15, Theorem 6.1 proves the full GL2(ℤ) fractional-linear orbit criterion for irrational real parameters. The proof includes determinant −1 signs, nonzero denominators, the Schwartz equivalence and tensor transitivity. It is separate from the unital isomorphism classification in Lesson 20.

Degree-zero trace pairing: comparison

Cyclic forms that survive norm completion, Theorem 4.3, degree-zero paragraph proves extension of a bounded trace, its unnormalized matrix pairing and the scalar-zero convention for relative K0. This agrees with Lesson 13, Theorem 1.1, whose proof is given directly in that lesson. It is a comparison, not an additional missing proof.

The neighbouring Theorem 3.1 and Theorem 4.1 explain the inverse-closure and approximation mechanism; the general higher-degree pairing is separate from this degree-zero comparison.

Used in Lesson 13.

Dense dual-valued derivations and the odd pairing

Cyclic forms that survive norm completion, Theorem 5.3 proves closability for a densely defined antisymmetric derivation from a Banach algebra to its dual. Lemma 5.1 gives matrix holomorphic functional calculus in the closed graph domain. The inverse and polygonal approximation arguments of Theorem 4.1 supply representatives and homotopies for every ambient K1 class; an arbitrary initial dense domain is not assumed to do so.

Lesson 14, Section 5 constructs d(a)(b) = τ(bδa). Boundedness of τ makes this a bounded functional in b; τδ = 0 and cyclicity give antisymmetry. The matrix pairing uses the unnormalized trace, with the factor 1/(2πi) for winding number. Extension by zero on the new scalar unit handles relative classes in a nonunital algebra.

The smooth-action case has its own proof in Connections and curvature, Theorem 5.2, using Lemma 1.1 for density and matrix functional calculus. These arguments do not require the separate weak-amenability result.

Used in Lesson 14.

Trace-class approximation and rectangular cyclicity

Cyclic forms that survive norm completion, Lemma 7.3a proves the Schatten Banach-ideal theorem, finite-rank density and bounded multiplier estimates. Its p = 1 case supplies the trace-norm completeness and continuity used in Lesson 13, Theorem 4.1.

A compact operator from H1 to H0 embeds in an off-diagonal corner of H0 ⊕ H1, preserving its nonzero singular values. Finite-rank approximation and the multiplier estimate therefore give the rectangular trace identity proved in Lesson 13. In the Calderón–Fedosov formula only the stated powers of the two parametrix errors are assumed trace class; the errors themselves need not be.

Used in Lesson 13.

Finite domains of an arbitrary C*-weight

Finite domains and the GNS space of a C*-weight, CS.1–CS.3 proves the square-finite left ideal, its finite-product algebra and the unique positive linear extension on the finite cone. No faithfulness, continuity or density hypothesis is used for these constructions; the algebra may be nonunital or zero.

Lesson 13, Lemma 5.0 proves the additional two-sided ideal and cyclicity properties from the trace assumption. These do not hold for an arbitrary weight. Norm density is a separate hypothesis in Theorem 5.1 and Lemma 6.1.

Used in Lesson 13.

The weight GNS quotient and representation

Finite domains and the GNS space of a C*-weight, CS.5–CS.6 proves Cauchy–Schwarz, the null-space quotient, Hilbert completion, bounded left representation and uniqueness. Inner products are linear in their first variable. The representation need not be faithful, and no single cyclic vector is asserted.

Lesson 13, Lemma 6.1 uses this quotient for a possibly nonfaithful trace, then constructs a faithful trace on the generated von Neumann algebra. Equality with the original weight is proved for finite values and infinity; faithfulness is not silently added to the original trace.

Used in Lesson 13.

The four Hilbert-algebra axioms

Building the two multiplication actions of a Hilbert algebra, starting data states bounded left multiplication, the adjoint identity, closability of the involution and Hilbert-norm density of products. This is the definition, not an omitted theorem.

Lesson 13, Sections 6.1–6.2 checks all four properties for its coefficient and convolution quotients. Neither a unit nor separability is assumed. Density of products in the Hilbert norm is not confused with being a core for the graph norm.

Used in Lesson 13.

Integration on actual measurable operators

Trace densities and noncommutative integration, TI.12–TI.18 constructs the complete L1 space of closed measurable operators, with bounded multiplier estimates, adjoint isometry and the continuous trace. TI.27–TI.30 proves the complete finite-exponent norms, and TI.31–TI.38 proves product integrability, Hölder estimates and cyclicity, including L2L2 ⊆ L1.

The hypothesis is a faithful normal semifinite trace on a von Neumann algebra, on an arbitrary Hilbert space. Finite projections form a net, not an assumed countable exhaustion. Lesson 13 applies these results to its constructed faithful trace and uses uniqueness of measure limits to prove the graph-norm domains complete. The original C*-trace may be nonfaithful, and the identity may have infinite trace.

Used in Lesson 13.

Constructing the weight from a Hilbert algebra

Weights and the Hilbert spaces of multiplication, WH.6–WH.9 constructs the bounded multiplication domains and their full completion. WH.11–WH.18 defines the weight by square-root vectors and proves its exact finite ideals and GNS norm. WH.19–WH.20 proves faithfulness, arbitrary-net normality and semifiniteness. WH.21–WH.23 recovers the representation and the full algebra.

The original algebra need not already be full; Hilbert spaces and index sets are arbitrary. Lesson 13 proves that its closed involution is an everywhere-defined antiunitary, and uses WH.8 and WH.16 to make the constructed weight a trace. This extra tracial conclusion is checked in the consumer, not assumed for every Hilbert algebra. The provider retains its stated Hilbert-algebra, graph, polar and weak-compactness foundations.

Used in Lesson 13.

Stable isomorphism and the Brown–Green–Rieffel theorem

Hilbert C*-modules Lesson 13, Theorem 2.1 proves that strong Morita equivalence is equivalent to isomorphism after stabilization when both C*-algebras are σ-unital. Lesson 23, Section 2 proves its AF corner is full; the corner and crossed product are separable, hence both σ-unital. The conclusion retains a tensor factor of the compact operators on each side.

The absorption argument uses Kasparov stabilization, Theorem 3.1, the compact-algebra countable-generation criterion, inverse-module evaluation, interior tensor associativity and the exterior compact-operator identification. It uses two explicitly constructed absorption isomorphisms, not cancellation of infinite direct sums.

Used in Lesson 23.

The projection-pair index identity

The local index formula, Section 10, Lemma 10.1 proves Index(QP : PH → QH) = Tr((P − Q)2j+1) for orthogonal projections with compact difference and trace-class odd power. The parametrix gives Fredholmness; anticommuting operators pair the interior positive and negative eigenvalues, leaving the two finite defect spaces.

This supplies the asserted comparison in Lesson 13, Section 4 with its stated sign and hypotheses. The Calderón–Fedosov proof in Theorem 4.1 remains independent of this comparison; no claim about the other local-index theorems follows.

Used in Lesson 13.

Finite-factor traces and matrix amplification

Projection comparison and normal center-valued traces, CT constructs the faithful normal center-valued trace on every finite von Neumann algebra. In a factor its scalar values give the normalized faithful normal tracial state. The argument uses normal central states and monic projection decompositions, not an assumed pre-existing scalar trace.

C02 proves arbitrary projection suprema and strong convergence; C05 proves general comparison. Traces on von Neumann algebras, Corollary 5.4 gives equivalence exactly when the faithful trace values agree. Proposition 6.5, using Theorem 6.2, proves faithful normal matrix amplification by the sum of diagonal traces, without division by the matrix size.

No separability or countable-decomposition hypothesis is imposed. Exercise 7.5 itself proves the full interval of projection traces, then the real-valued K-theory isomorphism, using faithfulness in the amplified factor. Its proof is unchanged.

Used in Lesson 3.

Further development

The mapped source comparisons and pertinent proof additions include AF determinant criteria, automorphism paths, bundle classification and graded products, general trace pairings, coefficient Toeplitz sequences, interval trace constructions, ordered Cantor invariants and hereditary pure infiniteness. Broader subjects such as general Künneth theorems, general AT classification and converse Cantor realization remain separate developments. The linked Bott–Dirac and descent results are used with the hypotheses and signs specified above; they do not supply the separate developments just listed.
