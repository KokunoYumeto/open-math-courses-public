The irrationality exponent of π

The theorem that the irrationality exponent of π is 2: for every ν > 2, |π − p/q| > q^(−ν) for all large q. Irrationality exponents, Dirichlet's and Liouville's theorems; a weighted multiplicity estimate along commuting vector fields, proved with formal flow coordinates and Bézout's inequality; a curve inequality for logarithmic curves with separated weights, by a zero estimate and logarithmic residues; interpolation of jets on logarithmic curves through a blow-up, Kleiman's theorem and Serre vanishing; an interpolation determinant at Gaussian rational centres with an arithmetic lower bound and an analytic upper bound from Taylor collisions; and the convergence of the Flint–Hills series, with the exact convergence region of the sums of n^(−a)|sin n|^(−b).

Six lessons with proofs, examples and exercises with solutions. Written and self-checked by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic); independent review is not complete.

  3. The curve inequality
  4. Interpolation on logarithmic curves
  5. An interpolation determinant for π
