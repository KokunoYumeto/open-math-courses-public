Results on finite-energy functions on Cayley complexes, with their assumptions

The course proves the statements below; each link leads to the statement with its assumptions and its proof. The lessons are self-checked by the writing AI; independent mathematical review is not complete.

  • Cocycles are gradients — Lemma 1.1. On the Cayley complex of a presentation, every 1-cochain with values in an abelian group whose integral around every cell vanishes is the gradient of a function, unique up to a constant.
  • The space of finite-energy gradients — Lemma 2.1. The gradients of finite-energy functions form a closed subspace of the square-summable functions on the edges.
  • Constant at infinity — Lemma 2.3. A finite-energy function that tends to a constant at infinity has its gradient in the closure of the gradients of finitely supported functions.
  • Interaction of two cuts — Lemma 3.1. For two finite-energy functions, the number of pairs of equal or cell-sharing edges in their cuts at levels s and t has integral at most D times the product of the gradient norms; so it is finite for almost every pair of levels.
  • A function with connected level sides — Theorem 4.1. If the finite-energy gradients are not all limits of gradients of finitely supported functions, there is a finite-energy function with values in [0,1], not tending to a constant at infinity, whose level sides at regular levels are connected and whose level cuts are dually connected.
  • Good boxes exist — Lemma 2.1. Every neighbourhood of a point of the base cube contains a box around the point whose endpoint levels are regular and whose endpoint cuts interact in finitely many pairs.
  • Finitely many components — Proposition 2.2. The vertex set of a good box has finitely many connected components.
  • Localized functions — Lemma 3.1. The margin of a box times the indicator of one of its components has finite energy, and its cuts lie in shifted cuts of the functions defining the box.
  • The compact space — Proposition 4.1. The closure of the profiles is a compact metrizable space in which the group is a dense set of isolated points and on which it acts by homeomorphisms.
  • Square summable edge lengths — Proposition 4.2. In the weighted metric the lengths of the edges of the Cayley graph are square summable.
  • The boundary is a continuum — Theorem 5.1. For a one-ended Cayley graph with finite-energy gradients beyond the closure of the finitely supported ones, the boundary is compact, connected and has more than one point.
  • Paths near a boundary point — Theorem 6.2. Every neighbourhood of a boundary point contains a smaller one any two of whose vertices are joined by a graph path inside the larger.
  • Virtually cyclic groups are not one-ended — Lemma 1.2. A finitely generated group with an infinite cyclic subgroup of finite index has a Cayley graph with at least two infinite components after removing a finite set.
  • Translated cut lengths — Lemma 2.2. The integral over all levels of the total translated length near a level cut of a finite-energy function is finite and tends to zero as the translating element leaves finite sets.
  • Collapse of translated cuts — Proposition 2.3. Along a subsequence of group elements converging to a boundary point, the translated ends of almost every level cut of finitely many coordinates converge uniformly to that point.
  • Convergence — Theorem 3.1. If group elements converge to a boundary point a and their inverses to b, they map every closed set avoiding b into any neighbourhood of a, for almost all terms.
  • Convergence group — Corollary 3.2. The group acts on the boundary as a convergence group, and the boundary is infinite.
  • Parabolic comparison — Theorem 4.2. For a parabolic fixed point p, the distance between g and gp tends to zero as g leaves finite sets.
  • Dense orbits — Theorem 5.2. If the group is torsion-free, every orbit in the boundary is dense.
  • Thin walls — Lemma 1.1. For almost every level, the set of boundary points at which a level cut accumulates is covered by finitely many sets of arbitrarily small total diameter.
  • Dimension of the boundary — Theorem 2.3. The finite-energy boundary of a one-ended group has covering dimension at most one.
  • First Čech cohomology of the boundary — Theorem 3.2. If every 1-cochain on the Cayley complex with finitely supported coboundary can be corrected by a finitely supported cochain, the first Čech cohomology of the boundary vanishes.
  • No circles; cut points — Corollary 3.3. Under these assumptions the boundary contains no simple closed curve, and it is locally connected only if it is a dendrite with cut points.
