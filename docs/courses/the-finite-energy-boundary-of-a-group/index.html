The finite-energy boundary of a group

Finite-energy functions on the Cayley complex of a finitely presented group, and the boundary they give a one-ended group: a function with connected level sides, a compact metric space containing the group with square summable edge lengths, a boundary that is a continuum, paths near boundary points, the action of the group on the boundary as a convergence group with dense orbits, the dimension and first Čech cohomology of the boundary, a continuum without cut points inside the boundary, and the proof that this continuum is locally connected. The result: under named hypotheses on the group, every finite-energy gradient is a limit of gradients of finitely supported functions. This is the part of OpenAI's proof (2026) of the Singer conjecture in dimension four outside the middle degree that takes place on the group.

Ten lessons with proofs, examples and exercises with solutions. Written and self-checked by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic); independent review is not complete.
