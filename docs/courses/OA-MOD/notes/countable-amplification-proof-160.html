Countable amplification and all three operator closures

This supplemental proof supplies the full mathematical contract imported in BA-01. This is a complete local proof for the cumulative course.

The exact inputs are BK-01 for Hilbert projection, bounded operators and adjoints; BK-02 for the finite-vector bicommutant proof; and CP-03–06 for the concrete predual and its vector-series description. No tensor-product theorem, separability assumption or uniform bound on a strongly convergent net is required.

The countable Hilbert sum

For any complex Hilbert space \(H\), let \(L\) consist of sequences \(\xi=(\xi_n)\) with \(\sum_n\|\xi_n\|^2<\infty\). Its inner product, linear in the first variable, is \[ \langle\xi,\eta\rangle_L =\sum_n\langle\xi_n,\eta_n\rangle_H. \] The series is absolutely convergent by finite Cauchy–Schwarz and passage to the increasing real partial sums. Truncating a sequence proves density of the finite-support sequences.

For completeness, a Cauchy sequence \(\xi^{(j)}\) in \(L\) is Cauchy in every coordinate, giving limits \(\xi_n\in H\). The original sequence has a common norm bound \(C\). Taking coordinate limits in each finite sum gives \(\sum_{n\leq N}\|\xi_n\|^2\leq C^2\); hence \(\xi\in L\). Given \(\varepsilon>0\), choose \(j_0\) with \(\|\xi^{(j)}-\xi^{(k)}\|_L\leq\varepsilon\) for \(j,k\geq j_0\). Fix \(j\geq j_0\), take \(k\to\infty\) in every finite partial sum of squared differences, and then take the supremum over the number of coordinates. This gives \(\|\xi^{(j)}-\xi\|_L\leq\varepsilon\). Thus \(L\) is a Hilbert space.

Let \(J_n:H\to L\) insert a vector in coordinate \(n\), and let \(P_n:L\to H\) extract that coordinate. They have norm at most one and satisfy \(J_n^*=P_n\). If \(H\ne0\), both norms are one. For \(a\in B(H)\), the diagonal operator \(D(a)\) on \(L\) satisfies \[ \|D(a)\|=\|a\|,\qquad D(a)^*=D(a^*). \] The upper norm bound follows by summing the coordinate estimates; a vector in a single coordinate gives the lower bound. The adjoint identity follows first on finite-support vectors, then on all vectors by continuity. These facts also give multiplicativity and linearity of \(D\).

Approximation of a square-summable family

Let \(A\subset B(H)\) be a unital complex *-subalgebra, and let \(T\in A''\). For any \(\xi\in L\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), we prove that there is \(a\in A\) with \[ \|D(T-a)\xi\|_L<\varepsilon. \tag{CA.1} \] On the zero Hilbert space the statement is immediate. Otherwise let \(Q\) be the orthogonal projection onto \[ V=\overline{\{D(a)\xi:a\in A\}}\subset L. \] The set being closed is a linear space because \(A\) is linear. Multiplicativity and the *-property show that \(V\) is invariant under both \(D(a)\) and \(D(a)^*\). Its orthogonal complement is therefore invariant too, and \(Q\) commutes with every \(D(a)\).

Each bounded matrix entry \(Q_{mn}=P_m QJ_n\) lies in \(A'\): multiply \(QD(a)=D(a)Q\) on the left by \(P_m\) and on the right by \(J_n\). Consequently \(TQ_{mn}=Q_{mn}T\). For a finite-support vector \(\eta\), the \(m\)-th coordinates of \(D(T)Q\eta\) and \(QD(T)\eta\) are respectively \[ T\sum_n Q_{mn}\eta_n,\qquad \sum_n Q_{mn}T\eta_n. \] The sums are finite, so they agree. Agreement of all coordinates gives agreement of the two vectors. Since the two operators are bounded and finite-support vectors are dense, \(D(T)Q=QD(T)\) on \(L\).

The unit of \(A\) puts \(\xi\) in \(V\). Thus \(D(T)\xi=D(T)Q\xi=QD(T)\xi\) also lies in \(V\). The defining norm closure of \(V\) supplies (CA.1). No bound on the norm of the chosen \(a\) has been asserted.

Concatenating finitely many square-summable families into one sequence proves simultaneous approximation for every finite family of sigma-strong seminorms. Choose the single tolerance smaller than all the prescribed positive tolerances. Every separate squared norm is a partial sum of the concatenated squared norm and is therefore no larger.

Strong, weak and ultraweak closures

The preceding approximation includes the finite-vector strong tests, so \(A''\) lies in the strong closure of \(A\). Conversely \(A''\) is weak-operator closed: for each \(b\in A'\), the equations \(xb=bx\) are closed under all matrix coefficients, as seen by testing the fixed vectors \(b\xi\) and \(b^*\eta\). It follows that \[ \begin{gathered} \overline A^{\,\mathrm{strong}}\\ =\overline A^{\,\mathrm{weak}}=A''. \end{gathered} \tag{CA.2} \] This also follows from BK-02, but the direct argument records the full imported conclusion.

For the ultraweak statement, take a finite collection of normal functionals \(f_k\) on \(B(H)\). The exact CP vector-series representation gives \[ \begin{gathered} f_k(x)=\sum_n\langle x u_{k,n},v_{k,n}\rangle,\\ \sum_n\|u_{k,n}\|\,\|v_{k,n}\|<\infty. \end{gathered} \] Omit zero terms. Rescale each pair by reciprocal positive numbers so that both norms equal \(\sqrt{\|u_{k,n}\|\|v_{k,n}\|}\). This preserves every coefficient and makes both sequences square summable.

Put \(U_k=(u_{k,n})_n\) and \(V_k=(v_{k,n})_n\) in the countable Hilbert sum. For each \(k\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives \[ \begin{gathered} |f_k(T-a)|\\ \leq E_k\|V_k\|_L,\\ E_k\\ =\|D(T-a)U_k\|_L. \end{gathered} \tag{CA.3} \] Concatenate all the \(u\)-families and apply (CA.1), with tolerance small enough for the finitely many desired bounds in (CA.3). Thus every ultraweak neighborhood of \(T\) meets \(A\). The reverse inclusion follows because every vector coefficient is normal, so the weakly closed set \(A''\) is ultraweakly closed as well. We have proved \[ \overline A^{\,\mathrm{ultraweak}}=A''. \tag{CA.4} \] In a representation with identity projection \(p\), apply these statements on \(pH\). Compression and zero extension preserve the vector-series coefficients after replacing both vectors by their projections under \(p\). This identifies the inherited ultraweak topology with the corner topology. It covers precisely the supported application in BA-01.
