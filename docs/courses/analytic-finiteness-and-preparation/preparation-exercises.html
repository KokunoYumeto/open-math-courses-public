15. A bounded function that needs a negative preparation exponent

Level: advanced. Let 0<ϵ<10<\epsilon<1,

16. Even substitutions and parameter-dependent poles

Level: intermediate. Why does t=τ2t=\tau^2 not make t\sqrt t analytic near τ=0\tau=0, while t=τ4t=\tau^4 does? Then consider the globally subanalytic function

f(x,t)=xt−1/2+t1/3,x∈ℝ,0<t<1. f(x,t)=x\,t^{-1/2}+t^{1/3},\qquad x\in\mathbb R,\quad 0<t<1.

Find a substitution t=τkt=\tau^k under which ff becomes a Laurent polynomial in τ\tau for τ>0\tau>0, and one under which this expression agrees with ff for both signs of τ\tau. On which pieces of the parameter line does f(x,⋅)f(x,\cdot) extend continuously, and analytically, in the original variable tt at zero? Answer the analytic-extension question separately for the composed function in the new variable τ\tau.

Solution. We have τ2=|τ|\sqrt{\tau^2}=|\tau|, which is not analytic at 00, whereas τ4=τ2\sqrt{\tau^4}=\tau^2 is. For a two-sided substitution it is not enough that the substitution exponent be even: every exponent obtained after clearing denominators must be even.

The exponents −1/2-1/2 and 1/31/3 have common denominator 66. With t=τ6t=\tau^6 and τ>0\tau>0,

f(x,τ6)=xτ−3+τ2. f(x,\tau^6)=x\tau^{-3}+\tau^2 .

Doubling the exponent, t=τ12t=\tau^{12} gives t−1/2=|τ|−6=τ−6t^{-1/2}=|\tau|^{-6}=\tau^{-6} and t1/3=τ4t^{1/3}=\tau^4 for every τ≠0\tau\ne0, so

f(x,τ12)=xτ−6+τ4 f(x,\tau^{12})=x\tau^{-6}+\tau^4

agrees with ff for both signs of τ\tau.

If x≠0x\ne0, then f(x,t)→±∞f(x,t)\to\pm\infty as t↓0t\downarrow0, with the sign of xx, so there is no continuous extension before or after substitution. If x=0x=0, then f(0,t)=t1/3f(0,t)=t^{1/3} extends continuously by zero but has no real analytic extension in tt, since its difference quotient at zero is t−2/3t^{-2/3}, which diverges. After ramification, f(0,τ12)=τ4f(0,\tau^{12})=\tau^4 is analytic across τ=0\tau=0. Thus only the parameter piece {0}\{0\} permits continuous extension in tt, no parameter permits analytic extension in tt, and exactly {0}\{0\} permits analytic extension of the ramified function. The partition (−∞,0)(-\infty,0), {0}\{0\}, (0,∞)(0,\infty) separates these behaviours. The extension is not jointly continuous at (0,0)(0,0): along x=t1/4x=t^{1/4}, f=t−1/4+t1/3→∞f=t^{-1/4}+t^{1/3}\to\infty. Continuity in tt on the single slice x=0x=0 is weaker than the joint continuity that a parameter theorem requires as its hypothesis.
