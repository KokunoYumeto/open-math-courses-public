A smooth model from a positive polynomial approximation

A Taylor polynomial of a nonnegative function can become negative away from its expansion point. A spatial phase can also create terms outside a prescribed weighted degree. Both facts matter when passing from a smooth differential operator to its polynomial model. The approximation must preserve the quantities actually used in the estimate, rather than properties that a Taylor truncation or phase need not preserve.

This lesson proves the complete smooth model estimate. A small positive correction to the time polynomial preserves the residual intercept and the strict weighted degree of a corresponding ungauged pair. Quantitative quotient comparisons then admit the corrected pair to A weighted equation controls the polynomial model, Theorem 1.1. Its stronger estimate has no mixed-derivative remainder. For the smooth high frequencies we use the full nonnegative transport estimate in Two frequency signs for a nonnegative transport coefficient, Sections 2–5, verifying its global coefficient interface below. The polynomial phase and jet bounds come from A parameter gain from polynomial commutators, Section 2, and its algebraic prerequisite. The new Taylor, quotient and frequency arguments are proved here. Hörmander [H, Lemma 27.5.5] supplies the exact source comparison.

1. The smooth hypotheses

Let \(Q=(-1,1)^2\), \(D_t=-i\partial_t,D_z=-i\partial_z\), with \(\{\tau,t\}=\{\eta,z\}=1\). Fix positive integers \(n\leq M\), and let \(F,G\in C^\infty(Q;\mathbb R)\), \(G\geq0\). The functions \(G\) belong to one fixed bounded subset of \(C^\infty(Q)\). Thus every derivative is uniformly bounded on every fixed compact subrectangle used below. Write \(\operatorname{wt}(\alpha)=\alpha_t+n\alpha_z\). Suppose, for large \(\lambda,p\),

\[ \begin{gathered} p>\lambda^8,\qquad \max_{0\leq j<n}|\partial_t^jG(0,0)|=1,\\ |\partial^\alpha F|\leq\lambda^{-6} \quad(|\alpha|\leq M,\ \operatorname{wt}(\alpha)\geq M),\\ |\partial^\alpha G|\leq p^{-1} \quad(|\alpha|\leq M,\ \alpha_t\geq n\text{ or }\alpha_z>0),\\ |\partial^\alpha F|\leq p\lambda^{-6} \quad(\operatorname{wt}(\alpha)<M). \end{gathered} \tag{1.1} \]

The derivative bounds hold throughout \(Q\). Define the left-nested words of \(L_1=\tau,L_2=F+G\eta\) as in the polynomial lessons, with length counting leaves, and assume

\[ \min_{\eta\in\mathbb R} \max_{1\leq|I|\leq M}|L_I(0,0,0,\eta)|=\lambda. \tag{1.2} \]

All non-leaf words are independent of \(\tau\); thus including \(\tau\) in the minimization gives the same value, attained at \(\tau=0\). The finite pure time jet in (1.1) will also prove attainment of the \(\eta\) minimum. The smooth words need not vanish above length \(M\); (1.2) uses precisely the stated finite family.

Assume at every \(|z|<1\) the two one-sided conditions

\[ \begin{aligned} &\partial_t(F/G)(t,z)\geq-\tfrac12 &&\text{when }G(t,z)>\tfrac12\lambda^{-2},\\ &(F/G)(t,z)-(F/G)(y,z)\geq-\tfrac12 &&\text{when }-1<y<t<1,\\ &&&\hspace{-3em}\min(G(t,z),G(y,z))>\tfrac12\lambda^{-2}. \end{aligned} \tag{1.3} \]

These weak inequalities include the strict inequalities of the source. Put \(\varepsilon=2^{1-M}\) and \(r_0=8/9\).

Theorem 1.1 (smooth normalized model). For every compact \(K\subset r_0Q\), there are \(C_K,\lambda_0\), depending on \(M,n,K\) and the fixed coefficient bounds, such that for \(\lambda\geq\lambda_0\),

\[ \begin{aligned} \|D_tu\|^2+\lambda^{2\varepsilon}\|u\|^2 &\leq C_K\int_Q \frac{|(D_t+i(F+GD_z))u|^2}{\max(G,\lambda^{-2})},\\ &\hspace{5em}u\in C_c^\infty(K). \end{aligned} \tag{1.4} \]

Furthermore, uniformly for every \(x\in Q\),

\[ c\lambda\leq \min_{\eta}\max_{1\leq|I|\leq M}|L_I(x,0,\eta)| \leq C\lambda. \tag{1.5} \]

Norms are on \(L^2(\mathbb R^2)\), with compactly supported functions extended by zero. Neither constant grows with \(p\), which may be arbitrarily large compared with \(\lambda^8\), or with the removable spatial phase.

2. Taylor errors and the first frequency shift

Every word other than the single leaf \(\tau\) has the form \(A_I+B_I\eta\). Its coefficients use derivatives through order at most \(|I|-1\); \(B_I\) is polynomial in jets of \(G\), and \(A_I\) is linear in jets of \(F\), with coefficients polynomial in those of \(G\). The exact recurrences are

\[ \begin{aligned} \{\tau,A+B\eta\}&=A_t+B_t\eta,\\ \{F+G\eta,A+B\eta\} &=GA_z-BF_z+(GB_z-BG_z)\eta. \end{aligned} \tag{2.1} \]

Together with \(\{F+G\eta,\tau\}=-F_t-G_t\eta\), these prove all claims by induction. In particular, no square of the large intercept occurs.

Repeated brackets with \(\tau\) give \(\partial_t^jF+\partial_t^jG\eta\) at length \(j+1\leq n\leq M\). Select \(s<n\) with \(|\partial_t^sG(0,0)|=1\). This affine word makes the center maximum tend to infinity with \(|\eta|\), so its continuous minimum is attained. At a minimizing \(\eta_0\), it also gives

\[ |\eta_0|\leq\lambda+p\lambda^{-6} \leq C p\lambda^{-6}. \tag{2.2} \]

Replace \(F\) by \(F+\eta_0G\). This is the exact modulation \(u=e^{i\eta_0z}u_1\). It preserves the final norms, the weighted equation norm and both quotient conditions, and places the center minimizing frequency at zero. For a high-weight derivative, the added term is bounded by \(C p\lambda^{-6}p^{-1}=C\lambda^{-6}\): a derivative of \(G\) not listed as bad has \(\alpha_z=0,\alpha_t<n\leq M\), hence weight below \(M\). For a low-weight derivative, bounded lower pure time jets of \(G\) give the same weak bound \(Cp\lambda^{-6}\). Those pure time bounds follow directly by time Taylor expansion from the normalized center jet, the small \(n\)-th derivative and spatial variation. Hence all subsequent errors retain fixed constants. We use \(F\) for this shifted coefficient for the rest of the proof.

Define the finite Taylor polynomials

\[ \begin{aligned} F_0(t,z)&=\sum_{a+nb<M} \frac{\partial_t^a\partial_z^bF(0,0)}{a!b!}t^az^b,\\ G_0(t)&=\sum_{j<n}\frac{\partial_t^jG(0,0)}{j!}t^j. \end{aligned} \tag{2.3} \]

For the maximum of all derivatives through order \(M\) on \(Q\), we have

\[ \|F-F_0\|_{C^M(Q)}\leq C\lambda^{-6}, \qquad \|G-G_0\|_{C^M(Q)}\leq Cp^{-1}. \tag{2.4} \]

Here is a full remainder verification. If \(\operatorname{wt}(\gamma)\geq M\), the derivative of \(F_0\) of order \(\gamma\) vanishes: no retained monomial can dominate that multi-index. The high-weight bound then applies directly to \(\partial^\gamma F\). If its weight is below \(M\), expand \(\partial^\gamma F\) in the ordinary total Taylor polynomial of degree \(M-|\gamma|-1\). The radial integral remainder uses derivatives of total order \(M\), which have weight at least \(M\) since \(n\geq1\), and are bounded by \(C\lambda^{-6}\). Among the finite Taylor coefficients, subtracting \(\partial^\gamma F_0\) leaves only original multi-indices of weight at least \(M\), also bounded by that quantity at zero. All powers of \(t,z\) are bounded on \(Q\). This proves the first claim for every \(|\gamma|\leq M\), including total order \(M\).

For \(G\), every derivative with a spatial component or pure time order at least \(n\) is already bounded by \(p^{-1}\), and the corresponding derivative of \(G_0\) vanishes. For a pure time derivative \(j<n\), first expand along the time axis to order \(n-j-1\); its remainder is bounded by the \(n\)-th time derivative. At the same time point, its change from \(z=0\) to \(z\) is the integral of \(\partial_z\partial_t^jG\), of total order \(j+1\leq n\leq M\), also a prescribed bad derivative. This proves the second claim. All segments used lie in the convex rectangle \(Q\).

The lower jets of \(G\) are consequently bounded on all of \(Q\), through order \(M\). The recurrences (2.1) and (2.4) compare smooth and polynomial words:

\[ |L_I-L_I^0|\leq C\lambda^{-6}+Cp^{-1}|\eta| \quad(|I|\leq M). \tag{2.5} \]

The coefficient error in a term linear in \(F\) is bounded by \(Cp^{-1}\cdot p\lambda^{-6}\), and the direct intercept-jet error by \(C\lambda^{-6}\). Every other finite product uses bounded jets of \(G\). This proves (2.5), rather than presuming that the large weak bound is itself small. At the center and frequency zero, the polynomial maximum is therefore at most \(\lambda+C\lambda^{-6}\), and its minimum is at most \(2\lambda\).

3. Remove the phase and repair positivity

The exact polynomial reduction of the parameter-gain lesson gives

\[ \begin{gathered} H_0(z)=\partial_t^sF_0(0,z)/G_0^{(s)}(0),\\ R=F_0-G_0H_0,\qquad S=F-GH_0,\qquad \Phi'=H_0,\\ \text{coefficients of }R=O(\lambda), \quad\text{coefficients of }H_0=O(p\lambda^{-6}),\\ \|S-R\|_{C^M(Q)}\leq C\lambda^{-6}. \end{gathered} \tag{3.1} \]

The last claim follows from \(S-R=(F-F_0)-(G-G_0)H_0\), (2.4), and the finite product rule. Every fixed derivative of the polynomial \(H_0\) on \(Q\) has that same coefficient bound. The time and spatial degrees of \(R\) are at most \(M-1\). Its weighted degree can increase; no strict weighted-degree statement will be applied to it. The change \(u_1=e^{-i\Phi}v\) is exact: it preserves \(D_t\), the ordinary norm and the weighted equation, and replaces the intercept by \(S\). It also preserves the time derivative and two-time difference of \(F/G\), since it subtracts \(H_0(z)\) from the quotient.

Choose a fixed \(\kappa\) larger than the constant in the \(G\) error of (2.4), and define

\[ \begin{gathered} G_*=G_0+\kappa/p,\qquad F_*=F_0+(\kappa/p)H_0,\\ F_*-G_*H_0=R. \end{gathered} \tag{3.2} \]

Since \(G(t,0)\geq0\), (2.4) gives \(G_*\geq0\) for \(|t|<1\). Its center jet maximum is between one and two for large \(p\): derivatives of positive order are unchanged, and its nonnegative zeroth value only increases by \(\kappa/p\). The chosen \(s\) still has \(|G_*^{(s)}(0)|\geq1\). Every monomial of \(H_0\) alone has weight below \(M\), because it comes from a monomial of \(F_0\) after a pure time derivative. Thus \(F_*\) retains strict weight below \(M\), and \(\deg G_*<n\). Moreover

\[ F_*^{(s)}(0,z)/G_*^{(s)}(0)=H_0(z), \tag{3.3} \]

including \(s=0\); both numerator and denominator acquire the same constant correction times \(H_0\). Hence \(H_0\) remains the exact normalization phase of the corrected pair.

Let \(\sigma\) be its central bracket minimum through length \(M\). Comparing its words to the smooth, pre-phase shifted pair as in (2.5) gives errors \(C\lambda^{-6}+Cp^{-1}|\eta|\), since its intercept perturbation \((\kappa/p)H_0\) has all relevant jets \(O(\lambda^{-6})\). At zero frequency this proves \(\sigma\leq\lambda+C\lambda^{-6}\). At a minimizing polynomial frequency \(\eta_*\), the affine word with coefficient \(G_*^{(s)}(0)\), of absolute value at least one, gives

\[ |\eta_*|\leq\sigma+Cp\lambda^{-6} \leq Cp\lambda^{-6}. \tag{3.4} \]

Comparison at that frequency with the smooth minimum \(\lambda\) yields the opposite inequality. Therefore

\[ |\sigma-\lambda|\leq C\lambda^{-6}. \tag{3.5} \]

This quantitative comparison retains the one-half threshold in the smooth hypothesis; mere comparability would not suffice.

We next verify both polynomial quotient conditions. If \(G_*(t)\geq\sigma^{-2}\), (2.4) and (3.5) give \(G(t,z)\geq\tfrac45\lambda^{-2}>\tfrac12\lambda^{-2}\) for large \(\lambda\). Put \(a=G_*,b=G,e=S-R\). On these points,

\[ J=R/a-S/b=-e/b+R(b-a)/(ab). \tag{3.6} \]

We have \(|R|+|R_t|\leq C\lambda\), \(|e|+|e_t|\leq C\lambda^{-6}\), \(|b-a|+|b_t-a_t|\leq Cp^{-1}\), denominators at least \(c\lambda^{-2}\), and uniformly bounded first derivatives of both denominators. The displayed identity and its full product rule consequently give

\[ \begin{aligned} |J|&\leq C(\lambda^{-4}+\lambda^5/p),\\ |J_t|&\leq C(\lambda^{-2}+\lambda^7/p). \end{aligned} \tag{3.7} \]

For clarity, differentiating \(-e/b\) produces bounds \(C\lambda^{-4}\) and \(C\lambda^{-2}\). Differentiating the numerator of the other fraction gives \(C\lambda^5/p\); differentiating either reciprocal denominator adds at most two more powers of \(\lambda\), giving \(C\lambda^7/p\). These include every term. Under \(p>\lambda^8\), both lines tend to zero. The derivative condition for \(S/b\) therefore gives \((R/a)_t\geq-1\). For the two-time condition, apply the first line independently at the two endpoints; its two errors plus the original tolerance \(1/2\) are at most one. This proves both conditions for \(R/G_*\), with tolerance one and exact threshold \(\sigma^{-2}\), throughout \(Q\).

The weighted polynomial theorem now applies to the strict-weight pair \(F_*,G_*\), and returns, through its exact phase (3.3),

\[ \begin{aligned} \|D_tw\|^2+\sigma^{2\varepsilon}\|w\|^2 &\leq C\int\frac{|P_*w|^2}{g_*},\\ P_*&=D_t+i(R+G_*D_z),\\ g_*(t)&=\max(G_*(t),\sigma^{-2}), \end{aligned} \tag{3.8} \]

for smooth \(w\) supported in \(|t|\leq r_0,|z|\leq1\). To invoke its stated theorem, take the test function \(e^{-i\Phi}w\) with intercept \(F_*\); it has the same support and time norm, and its equation is exactly \(e^{-i\Phi}P_*w\). We have not treated \(R\) as a new strict-weight polynomial.

Lemma 2.2 of that same weighted lesson also gives

\[ |R|+|R_z|\leq C\lambda g_*(t) \quad(|t|\leq r_0,\ |z|\leq1). \tag{3.9} \]

Its level between \(\sigma^{-2}\) and \(2\sigma^{-2}\) produces a comparable weight, and (3.5) replaces \(\sigma\) by \(\lambda\). Finally, throughout \(Q\),

\[ g_*(t)\asymp\max(G(t,z),\lambda^{-2}). \tag{3.10} \]

Indeed \(|G-G_*|\leq Cp^{-1}\ll\lambda^{-2}\) and the two thresholds differ by \(o(\lambda^{-2})\). The time-only weight on the left will make exact Fourier contractions possible.

4. A smooth saturation and its global analytic interface

Choose \(r_K<r_A<r_B<r_0\) enclosing the spatial projection of \(K\), and a fixed real \(0\leq\psi\leq1\), equal to one near that projection, supported in \((-r_A,r_A)\). Let \(\alpha\) be even, smooth, between zero and one, equal to one on \([-r_A,r_A]\), and supported in \((-r_B,r_B)\). Define

\[ c(z)=\int_0^z\alpha(y)\,dy, \quad \widetilde G(t,z)=G(t,c(z)), \quad \widetilde S(t,z)=S(t,c(z)). \tag{4.1} \]

This smooth map agrees with \(z\) on the support of \(\psi\), has compact range inside \((-r_B,r_B)\), and is constant on both outside half-lines. It has bounded derivatives depending only on the chosen radii. For inner times and all real spatial points,

\[ \begin{gathered} \widetilde G\geq0,\quad \|\widetilde G_z(t,\cdot)\|_\infty\leq C_Kp^{-1},\\ |\widetilde S(t,z)|\leq C_K\lambda g_*(t),\quad \operatorname{Lip}_z\widetilde S(t,\cdot) \leq C_K\lambda g_*(t). \end{gathered} \tag{4.2} \]

The last two bounds use (3.1), (3.9), the chain rule and \(g_*\geq c\lambda^{-2}\). Thus the small \(C^1\) residual error is bounded by \(C\lambda g_*\). All these coefficients agree with the original ones near the test support and on the support of \(\psi\).

To use the earlier nonnegative transport proof on functions spread over the spatial line, choose one time cutoff \(0\leq\theta\leq1\), equal to one near \([-r_0,r_0]\), supported in \((-1,1)\), and extend

\[ \widehat G=\theta\widetilde G+1-\theta. \tag{4.3} \]

It is globally smooth, nonnegative and bounded in every required derivative uniformly. The spatial saturation uses only a fixed compact subrectangle of the bounded coefficient class. Taylor translation of the normalized \(G_0\) jet gives a positive floor for its time jet through order \(n-1\) on the time-cutoff neighborhood. By (2.4), \(\widetilde G\) retains that floor for large \(p\), at every spatial point.

The extension retains a uniform positive jet floor as well. At a zero of \(\widehat G\), \(\theta=1\) and \(\widetilde G=0\). Its first nonzero time Taylor coefficient through order \(n-1\) is even and positive. The nonnegative function \(1-\theta\) either contributes an earlier positive coefficient, a positive coefficient at that order, or vanishes through that order; multiplication by \(\theta=1\) leaves the first coefficient of \(\widetilde G\) unchanged. There is no cancellation. For uniformity, apply the finite-derivative compactness proof in Section 2 of the nonnegative-coefficient lesson to the original bounded functions \(G(t,y)\) on the compact effective rectangle with \(y\) in the range of \(c\). A sequence of vanishing extended jet floors would have a convergent point and a \(C^{n-1}\) coefficient limit retaining the original floor and nonnegativity, contradicting the same first-coefficient argument. Outside the fixed time interval the zeroth value is one. For \(n=1\), positivity of the zeroth jet gives the claim directly.

The global positive-symbol interface in Sections 3–5 of that lesson thus applies to \(\widehat G(t,z)|\eta|\), with \(k=n-1\). Its time reflection, two frequency signs, low branch and full potential norm give

\[ \begin{aligned} \|D_tw\|+\|\widetilde GD_zw\|+\|J^{1/n}w\| &\leq C_K\big(\|(D_t+i\widetilde GD_z)w\|+\|w\|\big),\\ J&=(1+D_z^2)^{1/2}, \end{aligned} \tag{4.4} \]

for all \(w\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^2)\) supported in the inner time strip. On that strip \(\widehat G=\widetilde G\). The analytic proof is global on Schwartz functions; its constants use the verified global coefficient bounds and time jet floor. No dependence on an expanding compact spatial support is presumed. This application retains the exact declared positive-symbol and operator-calculus prerequisites of that earlier lesson.

Extend \(\widetilde S\) in time with a fixed cutoff equal to one on the same inner strip, and put \(\widetilde P=D_t+i(\widetilde S+\widehat GD_z)\). All subsequent functions retain the original time support. On it \(\widehat G=\widetilde G\), and on \(v\)'s support the equation is exactly the phase transform of the original equation.

5. Two direct cutoff commutators

Take a real smooth \(0\leq\chi\leq1\), equal to one on \([-1,1]\) and supported in \([-2,2]\). Set

\[ \begin{gathered} N=\lambda^{1+1/(2n)},\qquad T=\chi(D_z/N),\\ w_\ell=Tv,\qquad w_h=(1-T)v,\\ \mathcal E=\|\widetilde Pv/\sqrt{g_*}\|. \end{gathered} \tag{5.1} \]

Both pieces are Schwartz and keep their time support. The exact Lipschitz kernel proof of the weighted lesson gives

\[ \|[\widetilde S,T]v/\sqrt{g_*}\| \leq C_K\lambda N^{-1}\|v\|. \tag{5.2} \]

The transport commutator has a different order, so we compute it separately. If \(K_N(r)=NK(Nr)\) is the Schwartz convolution kernel of \(T\), then integration by parts in \(y\) gives

\[ \begin{aligned} [a,T]D_zv(z)=-i\int\big(&a_y(y)K_N(z-y)\\ &+(a(z)-a(y))K_N'(z-y)\big)v(y)\,dy. \end{aligned} \tag{5.3} \]

Time is a fixed parameter here. If \(a\) is bounded and Lipschitz with constant \(L\), its norm is at most

\[ L\left(\|K\|_{L^1}+\int|sK'(s)|\,ds\right)\|v\|, \tag{5.4} \]

because \(\|K_N\|_1=\|K\|_1\) and \(\int|rK_N'(r)|\,dr=\int|sK'(s)|\,ds\). The weak derivative suffices; our coefficient is smooth. With \(a=\widetilde G\), (4.2) and \(g_*^{-1/2}\leq C\lambda\) imply

\[ \|[\widetilde G,T]D_zv/\sqrt{g_*}\| \leq C_K\lambda p^{-1}\|v\|. \tag{5.5} \]

The signs in the exact equation are

\[ \widetilde Pw_\ell =T\widetilde Pv+i[\widetilde S,T]v +i[\widetilde G,T]D_zv; \tag{5.6} \]

the high piece has the opposite commutators. The time derivative commutes with \(T\). At each time Plancherel makes both \(T,1-T\) contractions; their weighted norms also contract because \(g_*\) depends only on time. Since \(\lambda/N\) and \(\lambda/p\) are bounded, we obtain

\[ \|\widetilde Pw_\ell/\sqrt{g_*}\| +\|\widetilde Pw_h/\sqrt{g_*}\| \leq2\mathcal E+C_K\|v\|. \tag{5.7} \]

These are exact convolution estimates; no high derivatives of the large original intercept are needed for either commutator.

6. Low localization, high inversion and reconstruction

For the low piece take \(v_\ell=\psi w_\ell\). It has compact support within the polynomial rectangle. Since saturation is the identity there,

\[ \begin{aligned} P_*v_\ell={}&\psi\widetilde Pw_\ell +i(R-\widetilde S)\psi w_\ell\\ &+i(G_*-\widetilde G)\psi D_zw_\ell +G_*\psi'w_\ell. \end{aligned} \tag{6.1} \]

Dividing by \(\sqrt{g_*}\), the last three terms have respective norm bounds

\[ C_K\lambda^{-5}\|v\|,\qquad C_K\lambda Np^{-1}\|v\|,\qquad C_K\|v\|. \tag{6.2} \]

The first uses (3.1), the second uses (2.4) and the exact low-frequency bound \(\|D_zw_\ell\|\leq2N\|v\|\). The last uses \(G_*/\sqrt{g_*}\leq\sqrt{G_*}\), which is bounded on the time strip. Thus the spatial cutoff error is uniform even where \(G_*\) is very small. Under \(p>\lambda^8\), \(\lambda N/p\leq\lambda^{-6+1/(2n)}\). Insert (5.7) and (6.2) into (3.8), and use \(\sigma\asymp\lambda\):

\[ \|D_tv_\ell\|+\lambda^\varepsilon\|v_\ell\| \leq C_K(\mathcal E+\|v\|). \tag{6.3} \]

For the high piece, the coefficient \(\widetilde G\) depends on space and cannot be moved through frequency inversion without a commutator. Choose a smooth real \(h\), zero near zero and one for \(|\eta|\geq1\), and let \(b(\eta)=h(\eta)/\eta\), smoothly zero near zero. Define

\[ A_N=N^{-1}b(D_z/N),\qquad H_N=h(D_z/N). \tag{6.4} \]

Plancherel gives \(\|A_N\|\leq C/N\), and \(A_ND_z=H_N\). The spectral gap of \(w_h\) gives \(w_h=A_ND_zw_h\). For a real Lipschitz multiplication coefficient \(a\), the exact relation \(aD_z=D_za+ia_z\) yields

\[ [a,A_N]D_z=[a,H_N]-iA_Na_z. \tag{6.5} \]

The first commutator equals that with \(h-1\), a compactly supported smooth frequency function. Its kernel first moment, as in (5.2), bounds its norm by \(C\operatorname{Lip}(a)/N\). The second term has that same bound by the multiplier norm. It follows that

\[ \|\widetilde Gw_h\| \leq C N^{-1}\|\widetilde GD_zw_h\| +C_K(pN)^{-1}\|w_h\|. \tag{6.6} \]

This proves the required inversion estimate with the correct reciprocal frequency \(1/\eta\).

The approximation and (3.5) give \(g_*\leq C(\widetilde G+\lambda^{-2})\) at all spatial points and inner times. Thus (4.2) and (6.6) imply

\[ \|\widetilde Sw_h\| \leq C_K\lambda N^{-1}\|\widetilde GD_zw_h\| +C_K\big(\lambda/(pN)+\lambda^{-1}\big)\|w_h\|. \tag{6.7} \]

Insert \((D_t+i\widetilde GD_z)w_h=\widetilde Pw_h-i\widetilde Sw_h\) in (4.4). Since \(\lambda/N=\lambda^{-1/(2n)}\to0\), the first term in (6.7) is absorbed into its transport potential norm. The remaining coefficient is bounded. Also \(g_*\) is uniformly bounded above, so its weighted equation norm bounds its ordinary equation norm. Equation (5.7) gives

\[ \|D_tw_h\|+\|\widetilde GD_zw_h\|+\|J^{1/n}w_h\| \leq C_K(\mathcal E+\|v\|). \tag{6.8} \]

For every \(n\geq1\), \(n\leq2^{n-1}\), by induction: from \(n\leq2^{n-1}\), \(n+1\leq2n\leq2^n\). Therefore \(\varepsilon=2^{1-M}\leq1/n\) since \(M\geq n\). The exact spectral gap gives

\[ \lambda^\varepsilon\|w_h\| \leq N^{1/n}\|w_h\| \leq\|J^{1/n}w_h\|. \tag{6.9} \]

Finally \(\psi v=v\) and \(v=w_\ell+w_h\), hence \(v=v_\ell+\psi w_h\). Multiplication by \(\psi\) decreases the ordinary norm and commutes with \(D_t\). Equations (6.3), (6.8) and (6.9) therefore prove

\[ \|D_tv\|+\lambda^\varepsilon\|v\| \leq C_K(\mathcal E+\|v\|). \tag{6.10} \]

For large \(\lambda\) its last norm is absorbed into the gain. The total transformation from the original function is \(v=e^{i\Phi-i\eta_0z}u\), so its time derivative and ordinary norm are unchanged, and its equation has exactly the same pointwise absolute value. On its support \(\mathcal E\) is comparable to the original weighted equation by (3.10). Squaring the final inequality proves (1.4). ∎

7. The bracket minimum throughout the rectangle

The exact polynomial comparison of the parameter-gain lesson, applied to \(F_*,G_*\) and its verified phase \(H_0\), gives for the shifted pair \(\tau,R+G_*\eta\)

\[ \mathcal B_*(x,\eta) \asymp |\eta|+\sigma \asymp |\eta|+\lambda\quad(x\in Q). \tag{7.1} \]

The full polynomial jet translates uniformly throughout this fixed rectangle. Its frequency shift in these residual coordinates is zero, because \(R^{(s)}(0,z)=0\) and (3.3) identifies the phase exactly. Vanishing of longer words is inherited from the ungauged strict-weight pair, without a new degree assertion for \(R\).

Compare now the smooth shifted pair \(\tau,S+G\eta\) with this polynomial pair. All needed jets of \(S-R\) are bounded by \(C\lambda^{-6}\), those of \(G-G_*\) by \(Cp^{-1}\), and the jets of \(S,R\) themselves by \(C\lambda\). The finite recurrences (2.1), linear in intercept jets, give

\[ |\mathcal B_{\mathrm{smooth}}(x,\eta) -\mathcal B_*(x,\eta)| \leq C\lambda^{-6}+Cp^{-1}|\eta|. \tag{7.2} \]

Indeed the additional intercept error from changing a coefficient product is \(C\lambda/p\leq C\lambda^{-7}\), already included in \(C\lambda^{-6}\). The difference of finite maxima is bounded by the maximum of their differences. Absorb (7.2) into the positive lower bound in (7.1) for large \(\lambda,p\). This yields

\[ \mathcal B_{\mathrm{smooth}}(x,\eta) \geq c(|\eta|+\lambda),\qquad \mathcal B_{\mathrm{smooth}}(x,0)\leq C\lambda. \tag{7.3} \]

Taking the minimum proves (1.5) in shifted coordinates, including attainment. Both preceding frequency shifts are canonical bijections on each fixed base frequency line, leaving \(\tau\) unchanged. Thus the original finite family has the same minimum. This completes both conclusions of Theorem 1.1. ∎

8. Graded exercises with complete solutions

Exercise 1 — basic. Explain why a constant frequency shift preserves the quotient assumptions, and why its size in (2.2) is compatible with the high-weight Taylor errors even when \(p\) is very large.

Solution. Adding \(\eta_0G\) to \(F\) adds the same real constant \(\eta_0\) to \(F/G\). Its time derivative and every two-time difference are unchanged. Every high-weight derivative of \(G\) is one of the bad derivatives in (1.1), since a pure time order below \(n\) has weight below \(M\). Consequently its contribution to that derivative of the shifted intercept is at most \(Cp\lambda^{-6}\cdot p^{-1}=C\lambda^{-6}\). The weak low-weight bound need not be uniformly small; it is used together with the small coefficient error, not as a perturbation norm by itself.

Exercise 2 — intermediate. Give a normalized nonnegative time coefficient whose quadratic Taylor polynomial is negative somewhere. Take \(p\geq1\), \(a=1/2\), \(e=1/(10p)\), \(c=1/(2(1-e))\), and \(G(t)=c(t-a)^2(1+et)\). Use \(n=3\).

Solution. The coefficient is nonnegative on \((-1,1)\), since \(1+et>0\). Its expansion is

\[ G_0(t)=c\big((1-e)t^2+(-1+e/4)t+1/4\big), \qquad G-G_0=ce t^3. \tag{8.1} \]

The second center derivative is exactly one. The zeroth is \(c/4<1\), and the absolute first is \(c(1-e/4)<1\) for \(0<e\leq1/10\), so the normalized jet maximum through order two is one. Also \(G'''=6ce=3/(10p-1)<p^{-1}\). At the smooth zero \(t=a\), \(G_0(a)=-ce/8<0\). Finally \(|G-G_0|\leq ce\) on the unit interval, so \(G_0+e\geq(1-c)e>0\), since \(c\leq5/9<1\). The example proves that the positivity correction is a substantive step. It concerns the coefficient alone; no full intercept or central two-operator minimum is specified by this exercise.

Exercise 3 — intermediate. Derive (6.5) and explain why the term containing \(a_z\) cannot be discarded when the smooth transport coefficient depends on space.

Solution. The differential identity is \(aD_z=D_za+ia_z\), where \(D_za\) denotes operator composition and \(a_z\) multiplication. Thus \(A_NaD_z=H_Na+iA_Na_z\), whereas \(aA_ND_z=aH_N\). Subtracting gives \([a,A_N]D_z=[a,H_N]-iA_Na_z\). The last term vanishes for a time-only coefficient but generally survives. Its norm is bounded by \((C/N)\|a_z\|_\infty\). Together with the kernel first-moment bound for \([a,H_N]\), this supplies the exact \(C\operatorname{Lip}(a)/N\) error used in (6.6).

Exercise 4 — advanced. Explain why \(\sigma\asymp\lambda\) alone does not verify the transfer in Section 3. Identify the separate error needed for the differential quotient condition.

Solution. If the unspecified comparison allowed \(\sigma=10\lambda\), the polynomial threshold \(\sigma^{-2}\) would be \(\lambda^{-2}/100\), below the smooth threshold \(\lambda^{-2}/2\); the smooth hypothesis would not apply there. The stronger \(\sigma=\lambda+O(\lambda^{-6})\) makes the threshold ratio tend to one, while the denominator error \(O(p^{-1})\) is smaller than \(\lambda^{-2}\). The differential condition also needs \(J_t\), not just the endpoint error \(J\). Differentiating the reciprocal denominators yields the larger bound \(C\lambda^7/p\), together with \(C\lambda^{-2}\). Both tend to zero under \(p>\lambda^8\). Endpoint errors \(C(\lambda^{-4}+\lambda^5/p)\) alone verify only the two-time comparison.

Exercise 5 — advanced. Verify the residual-preserving correction (3.2), including the phase identity at \(s=0\). Why is adding a positive constant to \(G_0\) alone insufficient for that exact interface?

Solution. Direct subtraction gives \(F_0+(\kappa/p)H_0-(G_0+\kappa/p)H_0=R\). For \(s>0\), the constant correction has zero time derivative, so the quotient defining \(H_0\) is unchanged. For \(s=0\), \(F_0(0,z)=G_0(0)H_0(z)\); both corrected terms add the same \((\kappa/p)H_0\), giving (3.3). If only \(G_0\) were changed, its residual under the old phase would become \(R-(\kappa/p)H_0\). Although that term is small under these hypotheses, it would require a separate residual and normalization comparison. The paired correction preserves the exact existing residual and keeps every added monomial within the original strict-weight class.

Exercise 6 — advanced. Generalize the split to \(N=\lambda^{1+\kappa_1}\), \(\kappa_1>0\), using the stronger polynomial estimate (3.8). Give sufficient inequalities for the argument, and compare them with a proof retaining a mixed-derivative remainder.

Solution. The high potential and intercept commutator require \(\lambda/N=\lambda^{-\kappa_1}\to0\), so \(\kappa_1>0\). The low coefficient comparison requires \(\lambda N/p\leq\lambda^{-6+\kappa_1}\) to remain bounded; \(0<\kappa_1<6\) is a sufficient interval making it small. The high gain condition is \(\lambda^\varepsilon\leq N^{1/n}\), which follows for every positive \(\kappa_1\) from \(\varepsilon\leq1/n\). Our choice \(\kappa_1=1/(2n)\) satisfies all three. A polynomial proof retaining the earlier mixed-derivative remainder would additionally require \(\kappa_1<1/n\) to absorb its low-frequency time-derivative error. The present stronger estimate makes that extra restriction unnecessary; the conservative fixed choice still works throughout the entire integer class.
