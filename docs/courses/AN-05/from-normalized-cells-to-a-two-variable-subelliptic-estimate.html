From normalized cells to a two-variable subelliptic estimate

A local model estimate has to survive changes of scale and localization before it becomes an estimate on an arbitrary compact support. Two scales are needed here. A rooted bracket maximum fixes the time scale; a normalized time jet fixes the spatial scale. Neither scale has to be differentiable. Their comparability on intersecting cells is enough to integrate the local inequalities.

We use the complete normalized result in A smooth model from a positive polynomial approximation, Theorem 1.1. Its hypotheses, both quotient thresholds and the exact compact support domain are checked below. The finite affine-word recurrence in Section 2 of that lesson controls the coefficient algebra. The new rooted normalization, positive time cutoffs and variable-center integration are proved here. The exact historical comparison is Hörmander [H, Proposition 27.5.1]. Its printed complex leaf requires the explicit correction in Section 7; the theorem below uses the real leaf consistent with the normalized proof and the differential operator being estimated.

1. The real-leaf estimate

Write \(Q=(-1,1)^2\), with coordinates \((t,z)\), and use \(D_t=-i\partial_t,D_z=-i\partial_z\) and \(\{\tau,t\}=\{\eta,z\}=1\). Fix a positive integer \(k\). Let \(F,G\in C^\infty(Q;\mathbb R)\), \(G\geq0\), and suppose, uniformly for large \(\rho\),

\[ \begin{gathered} |\partial^\alpha F|\leq C_\alpha\rho,\qquad |\partial^\alpha G|\leq C_\alpha/\rho \quad(\alpha_t>k\text{ or }\alpha_z>0),\\ \max_{0\leq j\leq k}|\partial_t^jG(0,0)|=1. \end{gathered} \tag{1.1} \]

For the real leaves \(L_1=\tau,L_2=F+G\eta\), form the left-nested Poisson words, with length counting leaves. Assume throughout \(Q\) and for every real frequency that

\[ \max_{1\leq|I|\leq k+1} \left(|L_I(x,\tau,\eta)|/\rho\right)^{1/|I|} \geq1. \tag{1.2} \]

Equivalently at least one of these finitely many absolute word values is at least \(\rho\). The roots in (1.2) do not change that unit-level condition. Suppose also

\[ \begin{aligned} \partial_t(F/G)(t,z)&\geq-1 &&\text{if }G(t,z)>\rho^{-k-1},\\ (F/G)(t,z)-(F/G)(s,z)&\geq-1 &&\text{if }-1<s<t<1,\\ &&&\min(G(t,z),G(s,z))>\rho^{-k-1}. \end{aligned} \tag{1.3} \]

Theorem 1.1. For every compact \(K\subset Q\), there are \(C_K,\rho_K\), determined by \(k,K\) and the coefficient bounds, such that

\[ \rho^{1/(k+1)}\|u\|+\|D_tu\| \leq C_K\|(D_t+i(F+GD_z))u\|, \quad u\in C_c^\infty(K),\quad \rho\geq\rho_K. \tag{1.4} \]

The strict source quotient inequalities are included in the weak ones used here. The constants are uniform over the stated coefficient class. A finite set of coefficient derivative bounds suffices; Section 6 explains that qualification without claiming an explicit optimal derivative count.

2. Two attained scales

Fix for the moment an auxiliary number \(T\), with \(1\leq T\leq\rho\). It will ultimately be one sufficiently large constant. Put

\[ \begin{gathered} m=k^2+k+1,\qquad n=k+1,\qquad \varepsilon=2^{1-m},\\ M(x)=\min_{\tau,\eta} \max_{1\leq|I|\leq m} \left(|L_I(x,\tau,\eta)|/T\right)^{1/|I|},\\ B(x)^{-1}=\max_{0\leq j\leq k} \frac{|\partial_t^jG(x)|}{M(x)^{j+1}}. \end{gathered} \tag{2.1} \]

These definitions use the longer finite family through \(m\). Every non-leaf word is independent of \(\tau\), so the minimum may be taken at \(\tau=0\).

First, the pure time jet of \(G\) has upper and lower bounds on the whole rectangle:

\[ 1/3\leq\max_{j\leq k}|\partial_t^jG(x)|\leq3 \quad(x\in Q), \tag{2.2} \]

for sufficiently large \(\rho\). To prove the upper bound, Taylor-expand each derivative on the time axis from zero through the remaining degree \(k-j\). Its remainder uses the small \((k+1)\)-st derivative. Variation from \(z=0\) uses the small spatial derivative of that same time derivative. The sum of the reciprocal factorials is below \(e<3\), and the finite remainder is \(O(\rho^{-1})\). For the lower bound, reverse the time Taylor expansion from the point to zero, and reverse the spatial variation. The normalized center maximum is at most \(e\) times the maximum at the point, plus \(O(\rho^{-1})\). Since \(1/e>1/3\), this proves the other inequality uniformly on \(Q\).

Repeated brackets with \(\tau\) give

\[ \partial_t^jF(x)+\partial_t^jG(x)\eta \quad(j\leq k) \tag{2.3} \]

at length \(j+1\). By (2.2), their maximum grows at least as \(|\eta|/3-C\rho\). The rooted maximum is consequently proper in \(\eta\), so its continuous minimum is attained. At \(\eta=0\), every word through length \(m\) is \(O(\rho)\). Indeed the affine-word recurrence is linear in jets of \(F\), and polynomial in the uniformly bounded finite jets of \(G\); it has no quadratic intercept term. For \(\rho/T\geq1\), (1.2) and this upper test give

\[ (\rho/T)^{1/(k+1)}\leq M(x)\leq C\rho/T. \tag{2.4} \]

In the lower bound the witnessing length is at most \(k+1\); a shorter root of a number at least one is larger. In particular \(M\geq1\). Equation (2.2) then gives

\[ M(x)/3\leq B(x)\leq3M(x)^{k+1}. \tag{2.5} \]

The upper estimate uses the largest denominator \(M^{k+1}\); the lower uses \(M\).

The scales are continuous. On any compact base rectangle, (2.3) makes the rooted maximum tend to infinity uniformly in \(\eta\), whereas the test at zero has a uniform upper bound. All minimizing frequencies therefore lie in one compact interval. The minimum of this continuous finite family over that fixed compact interval is continuous: uniform continuity bounds the difference of the two maxima, and hence of their minima. The positive finite maximum defining \(B^{-1}\) is continuous as well. This supplies measurability for the integration below; no derivatives of \(M\) or \(B\) will be used.

3. Exact normalization on a cell

Choose a fixed open rectangle \(R\), with \(K\subset R\) and \(\overline R\subset Q\). At a center \(y\in R\), abbreviate \(M=M(y),B=B(y)\), and set

\[ \begin{gathered} x=y+(X/M,Z/B),\qquad (\tau_{\mathrm{old}},\eta_{\mathrm{old}})=(M\tau,B\eta),\\ f_y(X,Z)=F(x)/M,\qquad g_y(X,Z)=BG(x)/M,\\ P_y=D_X+i(f_y+g_yD_Z). \end{gathered} \tag{3.1} \]

For fixed \(T\) and large \(\rho\), the entire normalized rectangle \((X,Z)\in Q\) is inside the original domain, uniformly for \(y\in R\), by (2.4)–(2.5). The coordinate and frequency map is symplectic: it preserves \(\tau\,dt+\eta\,dz\). Dividing each pulled-back leaf by \(M\) divides a word with \(l\) leaves by \(M^l\), because the Poisson bracket is bilinear and the dilation preserves its form.

There are two exact center normalizations:

\[ \max_{j<n}|\partial_X^jg_y(0)|=1, \qquad \min_{\tau,\eta}\max_{|I|\leq m}|L_I^y(0,\tau,\eta)|=T. \tag{3.2} \]

The first is the definition of \(B\). For the second, at a frequency minimizing (2.1), all original words satisfy \(|L_I|\leq TM^{|I|}\), with equality for at least one. Thus their normalized maximum is \(T\). At every other frequency the rooted maximum is at least \(M\), so at least one normalized word value is at least \(T\). The symplectic frequency dilation is a bijection. This proves equality of the unrooted normalized minimum with \(T\), including attainment, rather than confusing the two maxima.

For every multi-index \(\alpha=(a,b)\), direct differentiation gives

\[ \begin{aligned} |\partial_X^a\partial_Z^bf_y| &\leq C_\alpha\rho M^{-a-1}B^{-b},\\ |\partial_X^a\partial_Z^bg_y| &\leq C_\alpha\rho^{-1}M^{-a-1}B^{1-b} \quad(a\geq n\text{ or }b>0). \end{aligned} \tag{3.3} \]

For a bad coefficient derivative with \(b>0\), (2.5) and \(M\geq1\) make its second factor uniformly bounded. For \(b=0,a\geq n=k+1\), use \(B\leq3M^{k+1}\), obtaining a factor at most \(3/M\). All these derivatives are \(O(\rho^{-1})\). The lower pure time derivatives are uniformly bounded by Taylor from their normalized center jets. This puts \(g_y\) in one fixed bounded smooth coefficient class, independent of \(T\) and of the centers, once the required inequalities have been arranged. It is nonnegative.

Take a sufficiently small fixed \(c_*>0\), so that for all bad coefficient derivatives through order \(m\) their bound is at most \((c_*\rho)^{-1}\), and set \(p_*=c_*\rho\). For each fixed large \(T\), eventually \(p_*>T^8\). Give \(X,Z\) the weights \(1,n\). If \(a+nb\geq m\), then \(a+b>k\), since

\[ a+nb\leq(k+1)(a+b)\leq k^2+k<m \quad\text{when }a+b\leq k. \tag{3.4} \]

Thus the first line of (3.3) and \(B\geq M/3\) give, for the finite high-weight family,

\[ |\partial^\alpha f_y| \leq C\rho M^{-k-2} \leq C T^{(k+2)/(k+1)}\rho^{-1/(k+1)} \leq T^{-6}. \tag{3.5} \]

The last inequality is arranged by making \(\rho\) large after fixing \(T\). For all low-weight indices the same elementary scale factors give

\[ |\partial^\alpha f_y|\leq C\rho/M \leq p_*/T^6 \quad(a+nb<m), \tag{3.6} \]

because \(M\to\infty\) uniformly for fixed \(T\). Only finitely many indices are involved, so one threshold for \(\rho\) suffices. Equations (3.2), (3.5), (3.6) and the bad \(g_y\) bounds are all the normalized smooth hypotheses.

Their quotient hypotheses also hold with the exact thresholds. If \(g_y>T^{-2}/2\), then

\[ G>\frac{M}{2T^2B}\geq\frac1{6T^2M^k} \geq c_T\rho^{-k}>\rho^{-k-1} \tag{3.7} \]

for large \(\rho\), using the upper bound in (2.4). The same reasoning applies independently at each of two endpoints. Moreover

\[ f_y/g_y=(F/G)/B, \qquad \partial_X(f_y/g_y)=\partial_t(F/G)/(BM). \tag{3.8} \]

Both \(B\) and \(BM\) tend uniformly to infinity. Thus (1.3) yields the normalized derivative lower bound \(-1/2\), and the normalized oriented two-time difference lower bound \(-1/2\), for the source threshold \(T^{-2}/2\). The physical time ordering is preserved by \(M>0\). Both endpoint segments lie inside the original rectangle. We may now apply the complete smooth theorem with \(\lambda=T,n=k+1,m=k^2+k+1\).

4. Uniform cutoffs with positive time anchors

We need an estimate on a fixed smaller cell for an arbitrary smooth \(v\) on the normalized rectangle, rather than only for a compactly supported test there. Its time localization error contains the reciprocal coefficient weight. A cutoff chosen without regard to zeros of \(g_y\) would lose the needed uniform constant.

There are uniform positive anchors on each side of the smaller time interval. Let \(g_{0,y}(X)\) be the time Taylor polynomial through degree \(k\) at zero. Its center jet maximum is one, and

\[ |g_y(X,Z)-g_{0,y}(X)| \leq C/\rho\quad((X,Z)\in Q). \tag{4.1} \]

This follows from the small \((k+1)\)-st time and first spatial derivatives, exactly as in the earlier Taylor proof.

Choose \(k+1\) fixed distinct nodes strictly inside \((2/3,3/4)\). The finite Vandermonde matrix is invertible. Its inverse, with the factorial conversion between coefficients and center derivatives, gives a fixed \(A\) such that the center jet maximum of any degree-\(k\) polynomial is at most \(A\) times the maximum of its absolute values at those nodes. Consequently at some node \(|g_{0,y}|\geq1/A\). Its value must be positive for large \(\rho\), since nonnegativity of \(g_y\) and (4.1) give \(g_{0,y}\geq-C/\rho\). At that node \(g_y(X,Z)\geq1/(2A)\), uniformly for \(|Z|\leq3/4\), after increasing the threshold. Its uniformly bounded first time derivative supplies a whole time interval of fixed radius \(\delta>0\) where \(g_y\geq1/(4A)\). Choose \(\delta\) smaller than the fixed node distances to the endpoints. The same argument at nodes inside \((-3/4,-2/3)\) supplies a left interval. All constants are independent of the chosen nodes that witness positivity, the center, \(T\), and large \(\rho\).

Construct a smooth real \(0\leq\theta_y\leq1\), increasing from zero to one in the left positive interval and decreasing back to zero in the right one. Use fixed smooth transition profiles scaled by \(\delta\). It equals one on \([-2/3,2/3]\), has support inside \((-3/4,3/4)\), and

\[ |\theta_y'|\leq C, \qquad g_y\geq c_0>0 \text{ on }\operatorname{supp}\theta_y'\times[-3/4,3/4]. \tag{4.2} \]

No differentiability of this cutoff as a function of the center is needed: only a local inequality with uniform constants will be integrated.

Choose one fixed smooth \(0\leq\psi\leq1\), equal to one on \([-1/2,1/2]\), supported in \((-3/4,3/4)\). Put \(w=\theta_y\psi v\). Its support lies in one fixed compact subset of \((8/9)Q\). The exact equation is

\[ P_yw=\theta_y\psi P_yv -i\theta_y'\psi v +g_y\theta_y\psi'v. \tag{4.3} \]

On the support of the second term, (4.2) bounds its squared norm divided by \(\max(g_y,T^{-2})\) by \(C\|v\|^2\). For the third term,

\[ g_y^2/\max(g_y,T^{-2})\leq g_y\leq C, \qquad \max(g_y,T^{-2})^{-1}\leq T^2. \tag{4.4} \]

The fixed spatial cutoff error is therefore uniform as well. On the smaller square the two cutoffs equal one and their time derivatives vanish. The smooth model theorem now gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_{Q_{1/2}}\big(|D_Xv|^2+T^{2\varepsilon}|v|^2\big) &\leq C\int_{Q_{3/4}} \big(T^2|P_yv|^2+|v|^2\big),\\ Q_a&=(-a,a)^2. \end{aligned} \tag{4.5} \]

The constant \(C\) is independent of \(T\geq T_0\), once \(\rho\geq\rho_0(T)\). The normalized smooth theorem is uniform in its large parameter, the coefficient class is fixed, and the cutoff derivative bounds and positive anchors above are uniform. This order of choices is essential when absorbing the final norm.

5. Comparable cells and their exact mass

For a center \(y\) and radius \(a\leq1\), define the physical cell

\[ E_a(y)=\{x:|t-y_t|M(y)<a,\ |z-y_z|B(y)<a\}. \tag{5.1} \]

Lemma 5.1. Uniformly when \(y\in R\) and \(x\in E_1(y)\),

\[ M(x)\asymp M(y),\qquad B(x)\asymp B(y). \tag{5.2} \]

Proof. The second conclusion of the normalized smooth theorem gives at every normalized base point

\[ cT\leq\min_{\tau,\eta}\max_{|I|\leq m}|L_I^y|\leq CT. \tag{5.3} \]

Choose \(c\leq1\leq C\). A maximum at least \(cT\) implies that at least one root of its value divided by \(T\) is at least \(c\). Conversely a frequency with maximum at most \(CT\) makes every root at most \(C\). Thus the normalized minimum of the rooted maximum is between \(c,C\). Exact dilation of each word, and its bijective frequency map, identify that rooted minimum with \(M(x)/M(y)\). This proves the first claim without interchanging a maximum and a root of varying order.

The normalized pure time jet of \(g_y\) through order \(k\) is between two positive constants throughout \(Q\). Taylor from its unit center jet, the small \((k+1)\)-st derivative and the small spatial derivative prove this as in (2.2). With \(r=M(x)/M(y)\), exact differentiation gives

\[ B(x)^{-1}=B(y)^{-1} \max_{j\leq k}r^{-j-1}|\partial_X^jg_y(X,Z)|. \tag{5.4} \]

Both \(r\) and its reciprocal are bounded, and the finite normalized jet maximum has positive upper and lower bounds. This proves \(B(x)\asymp B(y)\). ∎

Lemma 5.2. For \(a=1/2\) and \(a=3/4\), and every \(x\in K\),

\[ \begin{gathered} c_a\leq\int_R\mathbf1_{E_a(y)}(x)M(y)B(y)\,dy\leq C_a,\\ c_aM(x)^2\leq \int_R\mathbf1_{E_a(y)}(x)M(y)^3B(y)\,dy \leq C_aM(x)^2. \end{gathered} \tag{5.5} \]

Proof. If \(x\in E_a(y)\), Lemma 5.1 makes the center scales comparable to the scales at \(x\). All such centers lie in a rectangle of side lengths at most \(C/M(x),C/B(x)\), and their weight \(M(y)B(y)\) is at most \(CM(x)B(x)\). Multiplying area by that weight gives the upper bound.

For the lower bound choose a fixed small \(\delta>0\). Every \(y\in E_\delta(x)\) lies in \(R\) for large \(\rho\), because \(x\in K\), the distance from \(K\) to the boundary of \(R\) is positive, and both inverse scales tend uniformly to zero. Apply Lemma 5.1 with center \(x\). It gives \(M(y)\leq CM(x),B(y)\leq CB(x)\), so \(x\in E_a(y)\) if \(C\delta<a\). It also gives \(M(y)B(y)\geq cM(x)B(x)\). The rectangle \(E_\delta(x)\) has exact area \(4\delta^2/[M(x)B(x)]\); integration over it proves the positive lower mass. Finally throughout the center set, \(M(y)^2\asymp M(x)^2\). Multiply the established bounds by these comparisons to prove the second line. ∎

All integrands are measurable by continuity of the scales. No covering theorem, differentiation of a scale, or smooth partition in the center variable has been assumed.

6. Integrate first, then fix the auxiliary parameter

Take \(v_y(X,Z)=u(y+(X/M(y),Z/B(y)))\) in (4.5). Direct differentiation and the exact Jacobian give

\[ D_Xv_y=M(y)^{-1}(D_tu)(x),\qquad P_yv_y=M(y)^{-1}(Pu)(x),\qquad dX\,dZ=M(y)B(y)\,dx, \tag{6.1} \]

where \(P=D_t+i(F+GD_z)\). Return to \(x\), cancel the common Jacobian in the local inequality, and multiply by \(M(y)^3B(y)\). The result is

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_{E_{1/2}(y)}M(y)B(y) \big(|D_tu|^2+T^{2\varepsilon}M(y)^2|u|^2\big) \leq C\int_{E_{3/4}(y)}M(y)B(y) \big(T^2|Pu|^2+M(y)^2|u|^2\big). \end{aligned} \tag{6.2} \]

This explicitly accounts for the factor \(M^3B\): two powers compensate the derivative normalization, and \(MB\) supplies the inverse cell area.

Integrate in the centers \(y\in R\). Positive-integrand interchange is valid, and every nonzero function in (6.2) has support in \(K\). Lemma 5.2 gives

\[ \|D_tu\|^2+T^{2\varepsilon}\|Mu\|^2 \leq C_1\big(T^2\|Pu\|^2+\|Mu\|^2\big). \tag{6.3} \]

All mass and local constants are independent of large \(T\); increase their common value into \(C_1\). Now choose one fixed \(T\geq T_0\) such that \(T^{2\varepsilon}\geq2C_1\). Only after this choice enlarge \(\rho\) to meet every earlier threshold. The last norm is absorbed, so (6.3) controls \(D_tu\) and \(Mu\) by \(Pu\), with constants depending on this fixed \(T\). Finally (2.4) gives

\[ \|Mu\|\geq (\rho/T)^{1/(k+1)}\|u\|. \tag{6.4} \]

Taking square roots and combining the two controlled norms proves (1.4). ∎

The proof also explains the finite-derivative variant mentioned in [H]. Every word and Taylor comparison uses a finite family through order \(m\), together with the finite jet and anchor bounds. The normalized smooth proof uses finite Taylor and kernel estimates. Its only operator-calculus input is the nonnegative transport proof, whose positive-symbol provider, Theorem 1.1 of From scalar time estimates to an operator bound, has a constant depending on finitely many symbol seminorms. Its classical composition and operator bounds likewise use fixed finite derivative families. Saturation and fixed cutoff products convert those finitely many seminorms into finitely many coefficient derivatives. Taking the largest of this finite collection and the explicit Taylor/word orders produces an integer \(N(k)\) for which (1.1) through order \(N(k)\) suffices. Smooth coefficients still justify all intermediate operator identities individually; higher seminorms do not enter the estimate constant. This proves existence of a finite bound, without assigning a numerical value that has not been tracked through the declared calculus.

There is also a direct sign-condition consequence. Suppose for every real \(\eta\) the real function \(F(t,z)+G(t,z)\eta\) never passes from positive to negative as \(t\) increases. If two positive-denominator endpoints had \((F/G)(t,z)<(F/G)(s,z)\), choose \(\eta\) strictly between their negative quotients. The first value would be positive and the later one negative, a contradiction. Thus the quotient is nondecreasing between any such endpoints, even if the coefficient vanishes between them. Local difference quotients then give \(\partial_t(F/G)\geq0\) wherever \(G>0\). Both inequalities (1.3) follow. In the oriented symbol condition \((\overline\Psi)\), defined in Full sign conditions for mixed weighted polynomials, Section 1, taking the unrotated real part \(\tau\) gives exactly these increasing-time Hamilton curves at \(\tau=0\). The strict endpoint signs persist under small changes of \(\eta\); it can therefore be chosen nonzero if a punctured cotangent domain is used. The argument verifies the claimed quotient implication without assuming it across a zero.

7. The literal complex leaf fails

The printed initial statement in [H, p. 202] places an \(i\) in its bracket leaf \(F+iG\eta\), while its conclusion estimates \(D_t+i(F+GD_z)\). That literal combination of hypothesis and conclusion is false. This correction concerns the exact leaf in the hypothesis, not a change of the target differential operator.

Take \(G=1,F=-\rho\), any fixed positive integer \(k\), and the printed leaves

\[ \widetilde L_1=\tau,\qquad \widetilde L_2=-\rho+i\eta. \tag{7.1} \]

Every word of length at least two vanishes, since both leaves have constant coefficients in the base variables. For every real frequency,

\[ |\widetilde L_2|=(\rho^2+\eta^2)^{1/2}\geq\rho. \tag{7.2} \]

Thus the literal printed bracket condition holds everywhere. All bounds (1.1) hold, with zeroth intercept constant one, all higher intercept derivatives zero, every bad \(G\) derivative zero, and normalized center jet exactly one. The quotient \(F/G=-\rho\) has derivative and every two-time difference zero, so both original strict conditions hold as well, at every positive denominator.

Choose a fixed nonzero \(\phi\in C_c^\infty(K)\), normalized by \(\|\phi\|=1\), and set \(u_\rho=e^{i\rho z}\phi\). Its support is exactly the same fixed compact support. The original equation and time derivative satisfy the exact identities

\[ \begin{aligned} \|u_\rho\|&=1,\qquad \|D_tu_\rho\|=\|D_t\phi\|,\\ (D_t+i(-\rho+D_z))u_\rho &=e^{i\rho z}(D_t+iD_z)\phi. \end{aligned} \tag{7.3} \]

The equation norm is independent of \(\rho\), whereas the claimed gain is \(\rho^{1/(k+1)}\). No uniform estimate of the printed form can hold. With the corrected real leaf \(-\rho+\eta\), taking \(\eta=\rho,\tau=0\) instead makes every word zero, so the same model fails (1.2) and is correctly excluded. Theorem 1.1 proves the full repaired hypothesis class, including all real frequencies, both quotient roles and arbitrary compact supports.

8. Graded exercises with complete solutions

Exercise 1 — basic. Why does \(\min\max|L_I|\asymp T\) in normalized coordinates control the minimum of the differently rooted family divided by \(T\)? State the inequalities without claiming that roots commute with maxima.

Solution. Take \(c\leq1\leq C\) in the unrooted comparison. At every frequency some value is at least \(cT\), so its root of order \(l\leq m\) is at least \(c^{1/l}\geq c\). At a frequency where all values are at most \(CT\), every root is at most \(C^{1/l}\leq C\). The rooted minimum is therefore between \(c,C\). These pointwise implications are exactly what is needed in Lemma 5.1; no algebraic interchange was used.

Exercise 2 — intermediate. Explain why \(m=k^2+k+1\) makes the high-weight intercept derivatives small after normalization. Why must \(T\) be fixed before choosing the lower bound on \(\rho\)?

Solution. If \(a+nb\geq m\) and \(n=k+1\), then \(a+b\geq k+1\), by (3.4). The derivative gains at least \(k+2\) powers of \(M^{-1}\), including the intercept division by \(M\), while \(B\geq M/3\). Its bound is \(C\rho M^{-k-2}\leq CT^{(k+2)/(k+1)}\rho^{-1/(k+1)}\). For fixed \(T\) it tends to zero and can be made at most \(T^{-6}\). If \(T\) were allowed to increase with \(\rho\) without restriction, that conclusion would not follow from this bound. The proof first obtains constants uniform in \(T\), chooses \(T\) to absorb the final norm, and then increases \(\rho\).

Exercise 3 — intermediate. A time cutoff derivative lands where \(g_y=0\). What estimate results from the trivial weight bound, and why do the positive anchors change the absorption argument?

Solution. The weighted square of the time cutoff term is \(|\theta_y'v|^2/\max(g_y,T^{-2})\). The trivial reciprocal bound gives \(CT^2\|v\|^2\). After center integration this would give \(CT^2\|Mu\|^2\), which cannot be absorbed into \(T^{2\varepsilon}\|Mu\|^2\) for our \(\varepsilon\leq1\) merely by increasing \(T\). The positive anchors place the derivative where \(g_y\geq c_0\), giving instead \(C\|v\|^2\) with a \(T\)-independent constant. That is the term absorbed in (6.3).

Exercise 4 — advanced. Derive the two cell-mass estimates (5.5) if the outer normalized cell has any fixed radius \(a\in(0,1)\). Identify which statements actually require continuity of \(M,B\).

Solution. For the upper mass, intersecting centers have comparable scales by (5.2), lie in a rectangle of area at most \(C/[M(x)B(x)]\), and have weight at most \(CM(x)B(x)\). For the lower mass, choose \(\delta<a/C\); then every center in \(E_\delta(x)\) sees \(x\) in \(E_a(y)\), remains in \(R\) for large \(\rho\), and has weight at least \(cM(x)B(x)\). Its exact area gives a positive constant. Scale comparability supplies the extra factor \(M(x)^2\) for the other mass. Continuity guarantees measurable cells and weights; no derivative or center-dependent smooth cutoff is required.

Exercise 5 — advanced. Verify every hypothesis in the literal printed complex-leaf counterexample. Explain why the real-leaf repair excludes it before any estimate is invoked.

Solution. All base derivatives of \(F=-\rho\) and \(G=1\) except their zeroth values vanish. The intercept bound is \(\rho\); all bad coefficient derivatives are zero; the center pure jet maximum is one. The quotient is constant, so its derivative and every oriented difference are zero, strictly above \(-1\). The complex frequency leaf has modulus \(\sqrt{\rho^2+\eta^2}\), at least \(\rho\), and every higher word vanishes. Thus even its leaf alone satisfies the printed bracket condition at every real frequency. Equation (7.3) gives a constant equation norm on the modulated fixed test and unit ordinary norm. For the real leaf, \(\eta=\rho,\tau=0\) annihilates both leaves and all words, violating (1.2). The estimate with corrected hypotheses makes no claim about that test class.

Exercise 6 — advanced. Show that the exponent \(1/(k+1)\) cannot be improved over the corrected class for odd positive integers \(k\). Use \(G=1,F=\rho t^k/k!\) and a compact packet with time width \(h=\rho^{-1/(k+1)}\).

Solution. The pure time word of length \(k+1\) is exactly \(\rho\), so (1.2) holds throughout the rectangle at every frequency. All required derivative bounds hold; the normalized coefficient jet is one and all its bad derivatives are zero. Since \(k\) is odd, \(\partial_tF=\rho t^{k-1}/(k-1)!\geq0\), and both quotient inequalities hold. Choose a fixed unit \(\phi(T,z)\) compactly supported near zero, and define \(u_\rho(t,z)=h^{-1/2}\phi(t/h,z)\). Its norm is exactly one. On the fixed coordinates \(T=t/h\), the time derivative and multiplication by \(F\) both have factor \(h^{-1}=\rho^{1/(k+1)}\), while the \(D_z\) term has factor one. The triangle inequality therefore gives an equation norm at most \(C\rho^{1/(k+1)}\). These packets lie inside one fixed compact rectangle for large \(\rho\). A uniform larger gain would contradict this upper bound. The example establishes sharpness for each of these odd integer classes; it makes no assertion of a sharper even-class classification.
