Recovering localized norms and controlling partition commutators

A phase-space partition does not give a pointwise partition of functions after quantization. The images of compactly supported symbols spread in space, and products of their operators contain composition errors. We need an actual reconstruction with a small operator remainder. The same construction lets us compare the two weighted families and factor the leading partition commutators through their local weights.

The geometric and derivative providers are Covering by curved Hamiltonian neighborhoods, Theorem 1.1 and Sections 2–7, and Cutoffs transported through adaptive neighborhoods, Theorem 1.1. We use the coefficient left-composition formula (B8b) and Hilbert bound (B26) in When a moving symbol scale controls an operator. Those are exact declared calculus interfaces, with their stated lower foundations. Their constants are independent of the Hilbert coefficient dimension. The new reconstruction, mixed-weight comparison and both commutator estimates are proved below. They give the full conclusions of Hörmander [H, Lemmas 27.6.2–27.6.3], including the case-I arguments left to the reader there.

1. The two families and the exact estimates

Write \(t=x_1,w=(x',\xi')\). Fix an integer \(k\geq1\). Let \(0<\lambda<1\), and let \(q\) be real and smooth, with global bounds

\[ |\partial_t^a\partial_w^\beta q| \leq C_{a\beta}\lambda^{|\beta|-2}. \tag{1.1} \]

The exponent in [H, Proposition 27.6.1] counts transverse derivatives: \(|\alpha'+\beta'|-2\). Thus (1.1) is its actual derivative class, with time treated as a parameter. Retain its sign orientation and finite-bracket lower bound on \(\{|t|<1,|w|<\lambda^{-1}\}\). The leaves are the real functions \(q_1=\tau,q_2=q(t,w)\), and the finite words are evaluated at \(\tau=0\).

Fix \(\rho\geq1\) before making \(\lambda\) sufficiently small. Use the cover theorem's finite type-I family \(\varphi_a\), with center scales \(m_a\), and type-II family \(\Phi_j,\psi_j,\Psi_j\), with center scales \(M_j\). Put

\[ \begin{gathered} 0<\kappa_1<\kappa<\kappa_2<1/(k+1),\qquad R_i=\lambda^{-\kappa_i},\quad g_i=|dw|^2/R_i^2,\\ \Psi=\sum_j\Psi_j, \qquad \Psi_j=\psi_j\prod_{l<j}(1-\psi_l),\\ H_\lambda=h(t,\lambda x',\lambda D'),\qquad U=H_\lambda u,\\ U_1=(I-\Psi(t,x',D'))U,\qquad U_2=\Psi(t,x',D')U,\\ P=D_t+iq(t,x',D'),\qquad g=[P,\Psi(t,x',D')]U. \end{gathered} \tag{1.2} \]

All quantizations here are left quantizations in the transverse variables; time is a parameter. Assume \(h\in C_c^\infty\), supported where \(|t|\leq1/2,|w|\leq1/2\). Its rescaled symbol is supported in the cover's \(\overline\Omega\), where \(\Omega=\{|t|<1/2,|w|<\lambda^{-1}/2\}\). All its jets vanish at any support-boundary point outside \(\Omega\), since it is smooth and zero beyond that boundary. Thus the finite composition arguments below also cover support touching that boundary; they do not presume an unstated interior margin for \(h\).

Define the weighted localized energies

\[ N_1=\sum_a\|m_a\varphi_a(t,x',D')U_1\|^2, \qquad N_2=\sum_j\|M_j\Phi_j(t,x',D')U_2\|^2. \tag{1.3} \]

Norms include time and all spatial variables. Each center scale is a constant within its component; no derivative of that constant is taken.

Theorem 1.1. The following estimates hold with constants independent of the fixed \(\rho\), the permitted sufficiently small \(\lambda\), and the number of selected pieces:

\[ \begin{gathered} \|g\|^2\leq C(N_1+N_2+\|u\|^2),\\ \|U_1\|^2\leq C\sum_a\|\varphi_a(t,x',D')U_1\|^2 +C\lambda^2\|u\|^2,\\ \|U_2\|^2\leq C\sum_j\|\Phi_j(t,x',D')U_2\|^2 +C\lambda^2\|u\|^2,\\ \sum_a\|P\varphi_a(t,x',D')U_1\|^2 \leq C(\|PU_1\|^2+N_1+\|u\|^2),\\ \sum_j\|P\Phi_j(t,x',D')U_2\|^2 \leq C(\|PU_2\|^2+N_2+\|u\|^2). \end{gathered} \tag{1.4} \]

These are the complete partition and commutator conclusions. They do not yet assert the localized gain or the general sufficient theorem, which require the subsequent local-model argument.

2. Uniform coefficient calculus and polynomial size bounds

For a fixed transverse radius \(r\geq1\), the constant metric \(g_r=|dw|^2/r^2\) has Planck factor \(r^{-2}\leq1\). Its local and temperateness constants are uniform. The coefficient formula (B8b), followed by the change between left and Weyl quantization, gives a finite expansion whose \(N\)-th remainder has amplitude \(r^{-2N}\) times the two input amplitudes. The Hilbert bound (B26) gives

\[ \|a(t,x',D')\|_{L^2(H_1)\to L^2(H_2)} \leq C p_{\leq J}(a;1,g_r). \tag{2.1} \]

The constants are independent of \(r,t\) and \(\dim H_i\). Integrating the squared fixed-time bound proves the same assertion on the full time-space norm. No time derivative is supplied by (2.1); whenever one is needed below it is given by the transported cutoff theorem.

In particular a family with bounded overlap and uniformly bounded derivatives defines a uniformly bounded column from \(\mathbb C\) to \(\ell^2\), and a uniformly bounded row in the reverse direction. For a matrix of entries supported in intersections of two such families, only a bounded number of entries are nonzero at any point. The operator norm of each differentiated coefficient matrix is bounded by its finite Euclidean entry norm there. The same reasoning applies to all its derivatives. This verifies its Hilbert-valued symbol bound and lets (2.1) control the entire operator matrix. It does not sum nonlocal outputs pointwise.

The cutoffs of family II belong to \(S(1,g_2)\), hence also to \(S(1,g_1)\), since \(R_1<R_2\). Family I belongs to \(S(1,g_1)\). Ordered products used below have the same bounds. At a phase point, only indices whose cutoff supports contain that point can contribute a nontrivial factor or a nonzero jet to the product rule. Their number is bounded by the overlap bound, independently of the ordering and the total family size. Every fixed derivative thus has only a bounded number of nonzero product terms. The columns and rows have uniform coefficient norms as well. The rescaled \(h\) belongs to either class, since \(\lambda R_i\leq1\).

We also need a polynomial bound on the weights when a composition remainder has lost its support. The anisotropic bracket bound of An adaptive scale for repeated brackets, Lemma 1.1, applies with amplitude \(\lambda^{-2}\), unit time derivative scale, transverse derivative scale \(\lambda\), and time-frequency derivative scale \(\lambda^2\). The time pair and each transverse pair have contraction factor at most one. On \(|\tau|\leq\lambda^{-2}\), both real leaves have the required bounds. Consequently every defining word at \(\tau=0\) is \(O(\lambda^{-2})\). Taking its root and using \(\rho\geq1\) gives

\[ 1\leq m_a,M_j\leq C\lambda^{-2} \tag{2.2} \]

after making \(\lambda\) small for fixed \(\rho\). The lower bound follows from the original finite-type lower bound. It is enough here that both weights and their inverses grow at most polynomially; (2.2) gives a simple common choice.

Each additional product-contraction order between the bounded cutoff symbols gives \(R_i^{-2}=\lambda^{2\kappa_i}\). Given any fixed desired power \(L\), take a finite expansion order large enough that

\[ 2\kappa_1N-2\geq L. \tag{2.3} \]

Then even multiplication of its remainder by the largest weight in (2.2) leaves an \(O(\lambda^L)\) operator bound. All row and matrix remainder norms are coefficient norms, so no factor equal to the number of pieces is introduced. The fixed expansion order may change the constant, but remains independent of the parameters.

3. A finite reconstruction with a small remainder

We first prove a general device for either family. Write its cutoffs as \(\theta_b\), its radius as \(r\), and let \(B\) be either \(I-\Psi(t,x',D')\) or \(\Psi(t,x',D')\), as appropriate. On the phase region where the symbol of \(BH_\lambda\) can have nonzero finite composition coefficients, the cores \(\{\theta_b=1\}\) cover. For family I this is \(\Omega\cap\operatorname{supp}(1-\Psi)\); for family II it is \(\Omega\cap\operatorname{supp}\Psi\). The cover theorem includes the closure of the transition, so neither assertion discards the meeting boundary.

Choose an ordering and put

\[ \gamma_b=\theta_b\prod_{c<b}(1-\theta_c^2), \qquad s=\sum_b\gamma_b\theta_b =1-\prod_b(1-\theta_b^2). \tag{3.1} \]

At a point with some \(\theta_b=1\), the last product vanishes and \(s=1\). The formula is finite and exact. The \(\gamma_b\) are supported in the corresponding cutoff supports and have uniform row bounds in \(S(1,g_r)\). Write \(\Theta\) for the cutoff operator column, \(\Gamma\) for the \(\gamma\) operator row, and set

\[ S_0=\Gamma\Theta,\qquad E=I-S_0. \tag{3.2} \]

Both operators have uniform norms by Section 2. The principal symbol of \(E\) is \(1-s\), which vanishes on the relevant covered region. The symbol of \(BH_\lambda\), expanded to any fixed order, has coefficients built from derivatives of its two input symbols; their support is in the intersection of their supports. Every coefficient therefore lies in the same covered region, except at boundary points where all jets of \(h\) vanish. All derivatives of \(1-s\) vanish there as well wherever such a coefficient can be nonzero: the smooth function is zero throughout the covered region and hence has zero jets at its boundary.

This yields, for every integer \(N\),

\[ \|E^N BH_\lambda u\|\leq C_N r^{-2N}\|u\|. \tag{3.3} \]

Here is the finite-order justification. In a full left-product expansion for \(E BH_\lambda\), its zeroth contraction coefficient is zero. The remaining coefficients each have at least one factor \(r^{-2}\) and are still built from derivatives of the input symbols on that same support. Multiplying by \(E\) again kills the zeroth contraction of each such supported coefficient, adding at least one more factor. Induct through \(N\) products, retaining as many finite expansion terms as needed to make the final remainders \(O(r^{-2N})\). Formula (B8b) bounds those remainders in the Hilbert coefficient norm, and (2.1) gives (3.3). This is a finite expansion and an induction; no convergence of an infinite symbol series is assumed.

The exact finite operator identity

\[ I=\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}E^lS_0+E^N \tag{3.4} \]

now gives

\[ BH_\lambda u=A\Theta BH_\lambda u+R_Nu, \quad A=\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}E^l\Gamma, \quad \|R_N\|\leq C_Nr^{-2N}. \tag{3.5} \]

The row \(A\) has a norm bounded by a fixed finite sum of products of the uniform norms of \(E,\Gamma\). Choose \(N\) with \(2\kappa_1N\geq1\); then \(r^{-2N}\leq\lambda\) for either family. Apply the triangle inequality and square. With \(B=I-\Psi\) and \(B=\Psi\), this proves both norm-recovery lines of (1.4).

We shall also use (3.5) after multiplication by other cutoff columns. Expanding the finite row \(A\) to any prescribed accuracy, its \(b\)-th finite symbol coefficients are supported in \(\operatorname{supp}\theta_b\): the initial \(\gamma_b\) has that support, and multiplication and differentiation cannot enlarge it in a finite composition coefficient. Its coefficient seminorms are uniform by the same finite product estimates. Exact operator tails have no such support claim; they are bounded separately using (2.2)–(2.3). This distinction is necessary for the next comparison.

4. The mixed weighted comparison

The type-II admissible-cell lower bound and the type-I scale comparison in the cover theorem give

\[ M_j\leq C m_a \quad\text{if }\operatorname{supp}\Phi_j \cap\operatorname{supp}\varphi_a\ne\varnothing. \tag{4.1} \]

Indeed at a meeting point the actual bracket scale is at least \(cM_j\) by admissibility, and at most \(Cm_a\) by the case-I enlarged-cell comparison. The point need not itself be in case I. In contrast, comparable \(M\) values throughout intersecting type-II cells, and comparable \(m\) values throughout intersecting type-I cells, give their two-sided same-family ratios. These are exactly the geometric interfaces used below.

Apply the type-I reconstruction (3.5) to \(U_1\), multiply its output by the column \((M_j\Phi_j(t,x',D'))_j\), and insert \(m_a^{-1}m_a\) on each reconstructed input. The resulting matrix has finite leading coefficients

\[ c_{ja}^0=(M_j/m_a)\Phi_j\gamma_a. \tag{4.2} \]

Every higher retained coefficient has the same support intersection and the same constant ratio. That ratio is bounded by (4.1). The two separate bounded-overlap bounds ensure a uniformly bounded number of nonzero entries at any phase point, and all their derivatives have uniform \(g_1\) bounds. Formula (2.1) bounds the entire matrix. Choose the reconstruction and subsequent product orders large enough to apply (2.3) to all remaining weighted tails. Their operator norms are at most a fixed constant. Thus

\[ \sum_j\|M_j\Phi_j(t,x',D')U_1\|^2 \leq C(N_1+\|u\|^2). \tag{4.3} \]

There is no assumption that each type-II cell meets only a bounded number of type-I cells. Many may meet its long tube. What is used in the matrix symbol estimate is bounded simultaneous multiplicity of each family at a point. The Hilbert-valued operator bound then controls all nonlocal interactions. The discarded tails are likewise coefficient-norm bounds, rather than component estimates followed by an uncontrolled sum.

5. The commutator with the separating partition

The exact time commutator is multiplication by \(D_t\Psi\). For the transverse part the first left-composition term gives

\[ [P,\Psi(t,x',D')] =\operatorname{Op}_L((D_t+H_q)\Psi)+T, \qquad \|T\|\leq C. \tag{5.1} \]

To verify the remainder amplitude, the scalar zeroth product cancels in \(i[q,\Psi]\). Its first contraction is \(H_q\Psi\), because \(D=-i\partial\). Each second derivative of \(q\), and its higher transverse derivatives measured at \(g_2\), is uniformly bounded by (1.1) and \(\lambda R_2\leq1\). The second contraction with \(\Psi\) therefore has amplitude at most \(CR_2^{-2}\). The full finite integral remainder in the composition theorem is applied to these differentiated factors: the two derivatives on \(q\) have amplitude one and the two on \(\Psi\) have amplitude \(R_2^{-2}\), in either order of the scalar product. The joint oscillatory remainder bound preserves that product amplitude. Thus the entire remainder is in \(S(R_2^{-2},g_2)\), with no claim of convergence of an infinite expansion. Formula (2.1) proves the asserted norm bound. This also shows why using only the much larger zeroth amplitude \(\lambda^{-2}\) at a prematurely truncated expansion would be insufficient.

Let \(\mathcal T=D_t+H_q\). The transported cutoff theorem proves that

\[ r_j=M_j^{-1}\mathcal T\Psi_j \quad\text{is a bounded row in }S(1,g_2), \qquad \mathcal T\Psi=\sum_jr_jM_j\Phi_j. \tag{5.2} \]

The symbol identity holds because \(\Phi_j=1\) in a neighborhood of \(\operatorname{supp}\Psi_j\), which also contains the support of its differentiated component. In the left product of \(r_j\) and \(\Phi_j\), every positive contraction coefficient is zero: the derivatives of \(\Phi_j\) vanish on that neighborhood. Use (2.2)–(2.3) to bound the finite operator remainders after restoring \(M_j\). Consequently (5.1)–(5.2), the row bound, and the uniform bound on \(U=H_\lambda u\) give

\[ \|g\|^2 \leq C\sum_j\|M_j\Phi_j(t,x',D')U\|^2+C\|u\|^2. \tag{5.3} \]

Since \(U=U_1+U_2\), the squared triangle inequality and (4.3) bound the right side by \(C(N_1+N_2+\|u\|^2)\). This proves the first line of (1.4), with both families and the actual global commutator.

6. Equation commutators for each family

We give one construction and verify its inputs separately for the two cases. Use \((\theta_b,w_b,r,B)=(\varphi_a,m_a,R_1,I-\Psi)\) in case I, and \((\Phi_j,M_j,R_2,\Psi)\) in case II. The transported theorem gives

\[ d_b=w_b^{-1}\mathcal T\theta_b\in S(1,g_r), \tag{6.1} \]

with a uniform column bound, by bounded overlap. Same-family scale comparability holds on meeting supports. On the appropriate covered region, the ordered identity (3.1) yields

\[ \mathcal T\theta_b =\sum_c a_{bc}^0w_c\theta_c, \qquad a_{bc}^0=(w_b/w_c)d_b\gamma_c. \tag{6.2} \]

Each entry is supported in the intersection of the corresponding two family supports. The ratio and all differentiated coefficients are uniformly bounded there. Thus \((a_{bc}^0)\) belongs to the uniform Hilbert matrix class \(S(1,g_r)\).

At the operator level, replace the ordered identity by the finite reconstruction (3.5) and expand the resulting products. All retained entries remain on the same support intersections, with bounded weight ratios. Choose enough orders for the weighted tails using (2.3). This gives a bounded operator matrix \(A_r\) and a bounded remainder column \(S_r\) such that

\[ \big(\operatorname{Op}_L(\mathcal T\theta_b)U_i\big)_b =A_r\big(w_c\theta_c(t,x',D')U_i\big)_c+S_ru. \tag{6.3} \]

This construction covers the full time derivative in (6.1); it does not infer any time bound from transverse symbol seminorms.

As in (5.1), the difference between \([P,\theta_b]\) and \(\operatorname{Op}_L(\mathcal T\theta_b)\) is a uniformly bounded column. Its second-contraction amplitude is \(R_1^{-2}\) in case I and \(R_2^{-2}\) in case II. Bounded overlap supplies the coefficient column norm, and the Hilbert calculus supplies its operator norm. Equations (6.3) and \(\|U_i\|\leq C\|u\|\) therefore prove

\[ \sum_b\|[P,\theta_b(t,x',D')]U_i\|^2 \leq C\sum_c\|w_c\theta_c(t,x',D')U_i\|^2+C\|u\|^2. \tag{6.4} \]

Finally the exact operator identity

\[ P\theta_b(t,x',D')U_i =\theta_b(t,x',D')PU_i+[P,\theta_b(t,x',D')]U_i \tag{6.5} \]

and the unweighted cutoff column bound control the first term by \(C\|PU_i\|^2\) after squaring and summing. Substituting each of the two families into (6.4) proves the last two lines of (1.4). The case-I proof uses its own radius throughout, so it supplies the omitted source argument without assigning the stronger type-II metric to the small-gradient cutoffs. Theorem 1.1 is proved. ∎

7. Graded exercises with complete solutions

Exercise 1 — basic. Set three primitive partition values equal to \(1/2\). Compute their ordered components and the complement of their sum. Why does this not yet give a partition of every phase point?

Solution. The components are \(1/2,1/4,1/8\), their sum is \(7/8\), and the complement is \(1/8\). The sum is one only where at least one primitive value is one. The type-I core cover handles the remaining region; the source does not discard it.

Exercise 2 — intermediate. Derive (3.4) with \(N=3\) directly. Which part is an exact operator identity, and which part needs symbol calculus?

Solution. Since \(S_0=I-E\), the sum \(S_0+ES_0+E^2S_0\) telescopes to \(I-E^3\). Adding \(E^3\) gives \(I\), without commutative operator algebra assumptions. The small estimate for \(E^3BH_\lambda\) needs the vanishing finite symbol coefficients, the three contraction factors and the full remainder/operator bounds. A small principal symbol alone would not prove it.

Exercise 3 — intermediate. Let \(R_1=\lambda^{-1/8}\), and suppose the worst lost weight is \(C\lambda^{-2}\). How many contraction orders suffice to leave an \(O(\lambda)\) remainder?

Solution. The amplitude is \(\lambda^{N/4-2}\). We require \(N/4-2\geq1\), so \(N=12\) suffices. It need not be optimal for a particular product, but it handles the declared worst bound. All twelve orders are finite; no infinite expansion is used.

Exercise 4 — advanced. At one phase point take two type-I cutoff values and two type-II cutoff values all equal to one, with weights \(m=(2A,3A),M=(A,2A)\), \(A>0\). Compute the leading mixed matrix (4.2) for the ordering with \(\gamma=(1,0)\), and its exact operator norm. State its scope.

Solution. The matrix is \[ \begin{pmatrix}1/2&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \|c^0\|=\sqrt5/2. \tag{7.1} \] Its nonzero column has squared Euclidean norm \(5/4\), giving the exact norm; the common amplitude \(A\) cancels. This is pointwise coefficient algebra. It does not by itself specify an admissible symbol or prove an operator bound for a whole phase-space family. The derivative and Hilbert coefficient estimates in Section 4 supply that additional argument.

Exercise 5 — advanced. Explain why bounded overlap at a phase point does not justify \(\sum_b\|\theta_b(t,x',D')v\|^2\leq C\|v\|^2\) by a pointwise support argument. Give the actual justification used here.

Solution. Quantization is an integral operator in the transverse variables. Its output need not vanish outside the spatial projection of the symbol support, so its outputs cannot be counted by the phase overlap number at a fixed spatial point. Bounded overlap instead bounds the norm of the symbol column and each of its derivatives in \(\ell^2\). The dimension-independent Hilbert operator theorem applies to that column and proves the desired full operator bound. Its finite product theorem then controls reconstruction and the nonlocal tails in the same norm.

Exercise 6 — advanced. Verify the principal commutator sign in (5.1), and show where the time derivative enters if \(q\) is independent of time but the cutoff is not.

Solution. The first left-product term gives \([q,a]\) with symbol \((1/i)\{q,a\}=-iH_qa\). Multiplication by \(i\) in \(P\) makes it \(H_qa\). Independently, \([D_t,a(t,x',D')]\) is exactly \((D_ta)(t,x',D')\). Thus the leading sum is \((D_t+H_q)a\). Even with time-independent \(q\), this separate derivative can be large; the normalized cutoff transport bounds, rather than the transverse operator theorem, control it.
