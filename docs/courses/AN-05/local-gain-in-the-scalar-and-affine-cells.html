Local gain in the scalar and affine cells

The partition estimates reduce the general problem to two local equations. A small transverse gradient lets us freeze the phase variables and use a signed scalar equation. A large gradient gives an affine differential model after a canonical change. Its Taylor error is harmless only after localization; the canonical change and the Fourier support condition must also be checked.

We retain the exact notation and hypotheses of Recovering localized norms and controlling partition commutators, Theorem 1.1. The scalar provider is A derivative scale for signed one-dimensional equations, Theorem 4.2. The geometric providers are An adaptive scale for repeated brackets, Proposition 3.1 and Theorem 4.1; An explicit canonical cell for a large transverse gradient, Lemma 2.1 and Theorem 5.1; and Normalizing the two-variable affine model, Sections 1–6. The full analytic affine provider is From normalized cells to a two-variable subelliptic estimate, Theorem 1.1. We use the exact coefficient left-product and Hilbert operator interfaces (B8b), (B26) in When a moving symbol scale controls an operator, with their declared lower foundations.

1. The two localized conclusions

Fix the integer \(k\geq1\), and set \(\alpha=1/(k+1)\). Write

\[ \begin{gathered} P=D_t+iq(t,x',D'),\qquad U=H_\lambda u,\\ U_1=(I-\Psi(t,x',D'))U,\qquad U_2=\Psi(t,x',D')U. \end{gathered} \tag{1.1} \]

All transverse quantizations are left quantizations and \(D=-i\partial\). All norms include time and the transverse variables. The selected scalar cutoffs are \(\varphi_a\), with constant center scales \(m_a\), and the affine cutoffs are \(\Phi_j\), with constant scales \(M_j\). Use the three radii \(R_1=\lambda^{-\kappa_1},R=\lambda^{-\kappa},R_2=\lambda^{-\kappa_2}\), where \(0<\kappa_1<\kappa<\kappa_2<\alpha\).

Theorem 1.1. There are \(\rho_0,C\), independent of \(\lambda\) and the number of selected pieces, such that for \(\rho\geq\rho_0\) and then sufficiently small \(\lambda\),

\[ \begin{aligned} \rho^{2\alpha}\sum_a\|m_a\varphi_a(t,x',D')U_1\|^2 &\leq C\left(\sum_a\|P\varphi_a(t,x',D')U_1\|^2+\|u\|^2\right),\\ \rho^{2\alpha}\sum_j\|M_j\Phi_j(t,x',D')U_2\|^2 &\leq C\left(\sum_j\|P\Phi_j(t,x',D')U_2\|^2+\|u\|^2\right). \end{aligned} \tag{1.2} \]

These are both conclusions of Hörmander [H, Lemma 27.6.4]. Their subsequent use in the general sufficient estimate requires the separate assembly argument.

Fix the smooth cutoff used in the geometric cover. Since its support is compact in \((-1,1)\), choose once and for all \(c_0<1\) containing that support. A localized output has exact time support \(|m_a(t-t_a)|<c_0\) or \(|M_j(t-t_j)|<c_0\). Quantization does not enlarge time support because time is a parameter. For an affine cutoff its physical or Fourier distinguished-coordinate support will be specified separately below.

2. Finite localization remainders

For \(r=R_1\) or \(R_2\), put \(g_r=|dw|^2/r^2\). Its Planck factor is \(r^{-2}\). The exact finite coefficient product theorem supplies an arbitrary prescribed power of that factor; the Hilbert theorem bounds the resulting operator rows, columns and matrices uniformly. Each center weight is between \(1\) and \(C\lambda^{-2}\), as proved in Section 2 of the preceding partition lesson. The permitted radius exponents are fixed. Thus a sufficiently high finite product order absorbs any fixed polynomial loss in \(\lambda^{-1}\).

We will use a cutoff \(\widehat\theta_b\), equal to one in a neighborhood of \(\operatorname{supp}\theta_b\), and supported in a fixed enlargement of the cell. The cutoff and its derivatives have the corresponding \(g_r\) bounds. Section 2 of Covering by curved Hamiltonian neighborhoods proves the coefficient, Taylor and bracket bounds on every fixed canonical enlargement, with constants depending only on that fixed enlargement. Its original-domain margins justify those enlarged supports. One may therefore enlarge the exact canonical coordinates for the affine cells; no physical homothety of a curved cell is substituted. All finite coefficients of \((I-\widehat\theta_b(t,x',D'))a_b(t,x',D')\theta_b(t,x',D')\) vanish if \(a_b\) is supported on neither side but has controlled full derivatives: every retained coefficient contains a jet of \(\theta_b\) where all jets of \(1-\widehat\theta_b\) vanish. The full remainder, rather than just its formal series, is retained.

Here we also need affine symbols which grow linearly in phase space. We explain why they cause no missing operator-domain assertion. In the coordinate/gauge case such a symbol is affine in frequency, with coefficients depending on the distinguished coordinate and at most linearly on the other coordinates. Its left product with \(\theta_b\) terminates after the first frequency contraction. In the Fourier case it is affine in spatial coordinates, with smooth bounded frequency coefficients and at most linear frequency factors in the other directions. Move each linear spatial factor through its Fourier multiplier using the exact identity

\[ [x_l,A(D')]=i(\partial_{\xi_l}A)(D'). \tag{2.1} \]

It then multiplies the output cutoff symbol. That symbol has compact phase support, so its position factors and every derivative have a fixed polynomial bound in \(\lambda^{-1}\). Apply the ordinary finite coefficient product bound to these bounded symbols and their bounded multiplier coefficients. Linear frequency factors are handled by the analogous exact derivative identity. The remaining finite coefficients agree with the usual left-product formula, and the remainder has arbitrarily high \(\lambda\) order after enough contractions. This argument proves a bounded localized operator; it never assigns an unbounded affine symbol to \(S(\lambda^{-2},g_r)\) on the whole phase space.

For clarity, the compact supports and extensions used here have polynomial size. The selected centers lie in a fixed enlargement of \(\{|t|<1,|w|<\lambda^{-1}\}\). The canonical tube's long radius is \(\rho A_2\leq C\lambda^{-1}/M\), because the original first transverse derivative bounds give \(A_2\leq C\lambda^{-1}/(\rho M)\). The other radii are at most \(\lambda^{-1}\). The canonical orbit functions have uniformly bounded derivatives on a fixed interval in \(x_2/a\); extend them with a fixed smooth cutoff there. Their values and all derivatives remain uniformly bounded, and the explicit triangular coordinate map remains globally invertible. Its higher derivatives may grow linearly in the remaining coordinates; the preceding exact linear-factor calculation handles that growth. Extend the two one-variable affine coefficients with cutoffs on fixed enlarged normalized intervals; their derivatives retain polynomial parameter bounds. No global estimate for the original symbol outside its prescribed hypotheses is inferred from the local geometry.

These arguments can be used directly in Hilbert coefficient norms. Alternatively, individual rapidly small tails can be summed after the following explicit polynomial count. A fixed shrink of the selected cells is disjoint. A scalar cell has volume at least \(c/M\geq c\lambda^2\); an affine cell has canonical volume at least \(cR_2^{2n-2}/M\geq c\lambda^2\), and its canonical map preserves that volume. All shrunken cells lie in a fixed enlargement of the phase domain, of volume at most \(C\lambda^{-(2n-2)}\). Thus their number is at most \(C\lambda^{-2n}\). Consequently an individual tail \(C_L\lambda^L\|u\|\), with an arbitrarily prescribed \(L\), gives a summed squared tail at most \(C_L\lambda^{2L-2n}\|u\|^2\). Choose \(L\) after this count and any weight losses. This is a proved polynomial bound, not an uncontrolled summation over nonlocal outputs.

We will record such fully bounded tails as

\[ \sum_b\|E_bu\|^2\leq C\|u\|^2. \tag{2.2} \]

Their constants are uniform after fixing the order of all finite expansions and then taking \(\lambda\) sufficiently small for the already fixed \(\rho\).

3. The complete small-gradient argument

Fix a scalar center \((t_a,w_a)\), write \(m=m_a\), and put

\[ f_a(T)=m^{-1}q(t_a+T/m,w_a). \tag{3.1} \]

The stable-scale theorem proves exact pure-time attainment at this center. Hence

\[ \max_{0\leq l\leq k}|f_a^{(l)}(0)|=\rho. \tag{3.2} \]

Indeed some \(|\partial_t^l q(t_a,w_a)|=\rho m^{l+1}\), while every such derivative is at most that quantity for its order. The original derivative bound and the finite-type scale lower bound give

\[ |f_a^{(k+1)}| \leq C\lambda^{-2}m^{-k-2} \leq C'\rho/m\longrightarrow0. \tag{3.3} \]

This holds on \(|T|<1\). For fixed \(\rho\), choose \(\lambda\) small enough that it is at most one. The favorable scalar sign orientation is inherited at the fixed transverse point \(w_a\). Theorem 4.2 of the scalar provider applies with the exact center parameter \(\rho\), not a differently rooted maximum.

Let \(v_a=\varphi_a(t,x',D')U_1\). Its time support is compact in the rescaled interval, and its transverse variables are passive parameters. Rescale time with its exact unitary constant and integrate the scalar inequality over those parameters. Multiplying back by \(m\) gives

\[ \rho^\alpha\|m v_a\| \leq C\|(D_t+iq(t,w_a))v_a\|. \tag{3.4} \]

No compact spatial support is needed for this scalar estimate.

On a fixed enlargement of \(\operatorname{supp}\varphi_a\), the small-gradient derivative theorem and the mean-value formula give

\[ q(t,w)-q(t,w_a)\in S(\varepsilon_\lambda m,g_{R_1}), \qquad \varepsilon_\lambda=C\rho R_1/R\longrightarrow0. \tag{3.5} \]

For its value use \(|\nabla_wq|\leq C\rho m/R\) and the displacement \(CR_1\). For a differentiated symbol, the normalized estimate is \(C\rho m(R_1/R)^{|\beta|}\leq C\rho m R_1/R\) for \(|\beta|\geq1\). The fixed time enlargement uses the already proved extended-cell Taylor bounds. Introduce the larger cutoff from Section 2. The localized difference divided by \(m\) is a uniformly bounded diagonal Hilbert symbol with norm \(C\varepsilon_\lambda\). Its off-cell composition with \(\varphi_a\) has all retained coefficients zero. Full finite tails give

\[ \sum_a\|(q(t,x',D')-q(t,w_a))v_a\|^2 \leq C\varepsilon_\lambda^2\sum_a\|m_a v_a\|^2+C\|u\|^2. \tag{3.6} \]

The error tails act on \(U_1\), whose norm is at most \(C\|u\|\) by the partition theorem. Their coefficient norms, or the explicit count in Section 2, justify the final sum.

Substitute \(P\) in (3.4), square and sum, and use (3.6). Make \(\lambda\) smaller for fixed \(\rho\) so that \(C\varepsilon_\lambda^2\leq\rho^{2\alpha}/2\). Absorb this term. The first inequality of (1.2) follows, including the case with no transverse variables.

4. The affine estimate with its actual jet and rectangle

Fix an affine center and suppress its index. Let \(M,A_2,B_2,b,L_0,N\) be the scales from the affine normalization lesson:

\[ \begin{gathered} L_0=(\rho M)^{1/2},\qquad B_2=(\rho A_2)^{-1},\\ b=\min(B_2,L_0^{-1}),\qquad N=A_2L_0,\\ B_2'=\min(B_2,R_2^{-1}),\qquad W=B_2/B_2'\geq1. \end{gathered} \tag{4.1} \]

The real affine expression is \(\mathcal L(t,z,\xi)=Q(t,z,0)+c(t,z)\xi\), where \(c=\partial_\xi Q(t,z,0)\). After \(T=M(t-t_j),Z=B_2z\), it is \(M(F_0(T,Z)+F_1(T,Z)D_Z)\). The normalization lesson supplies \(G=F_1+D/N^2>0\), with a single fixed correction \(D\), on the larger coefficient rectangle. On \(|T|<1,|Z|<W\), it proves the full real-leaf bracket lower bound \(c\rho\), uniform bounds \(|D^\beta F_0|\leq C_\beta\rho\), small bad derivatives of \(G\), and

\[ c_J\leq\max_{0\leq l\leq k}|\partial_T^lG(T,Z)|\leq C_J. \tag{4.2} \]

The quotient errors in its (4.5), (4.8) tend to zero on every threshold \(G>C_*\rho^{-k-1}\), where \(C_*>0\) is fixed. This follows by replacing the reciprocal threshold factors in its explicit (4.10) by the corresponding fixed powers of \(C_*^{-1}\). We use this quantitative conclusion, rather than silently replacing a threshold by an equivalent one.

The analytic parent theorem requires a center jet maximum exactly one on a full square. Here the center maximum is only between fixed constants. At a point \((T_0,Z_0)\) in a fixed inner part of the rectangle choose a fixed small \(a>0\) and set

\[ J_a=\max_{0\leq l\leq k}a^l|\partial_T^lG(T_0,Z_0)|, \qquad b_a=aJ_a. \tag{4.3} \]

Take \(a<1\). Then \(a^kc_J\leq J_a\leq C_J\), so \(b_a\) has fixed positive lower and upper bounds. Choose \(a\) small enough that both \(a\) and \(aC_J\) fit inside the fixed time and spatial margins left by \(c_0\). With

\[ T=T_0+aS,\qquad Z=Z_0+b_aY, \qquad \widetilde F=aF_0,\quad \widetilde G=(a/b_a)G, \tag{4.4} \]

where the coefficients on the right are evaluated at these transformed arguments, the full square \(|S|,|Y|<1\) lies in the original rectangle. The center pure-time jet maximum of \(\widetilde G\) is exactly one. All coefficient and bad-derivative bounds are unchanged up to fixed factors. The coordinate/frequency change is symplectic, and the operator satisfies

\[ a\{D_T+i(F_0+GD_Z)\} =D_S+i(\widetilde F+\widetilde G D_Y). \tag{4.5} \]

Every word with \(r\) leaves acquires precisely \(a^r\), since the second real leaf and the conjugate time frequency both acquire \(a\). As \(r\leq k+1\), the full bracket lower bound becomes at least \(c'\rho\), with a fixed \(c'>0\). Use the parent parameter \(\widetilde\rho=c'\rho\). Its quotient is \(b_aF_0/G\), with time derivative \(ab_a\partial_T(F_0/G)\). Its exact threshold corresponds to \(G=(b_a/a)\widetilde\rho^{-k-1}\). Section 4 of the normalization lesson, with the quantitative threshold observation above, makes both required errors at most one after \(\lambda\) is sufficiently small for fixed \(\rho\). Every hypothesis of the analytic parent has now been checked on the actual transformed square.

Apply that theorem on a fixed inner square and undo (4.4), with its constant unitary Jacobian. This gives a uniform \(\rho^\alpha\) estimate for functions supported in the corresponding inner physical rectangle. To obtain it on \(|T|<c_0,|Z|<c_0W\), cover that rectangle by fixed-size time and spatial patches. Their centers can be kept a fixed distance from the original boundary because \(W\geq1\). Use a smooth squared partition of unity with bounded overlap and derivatives controlled by the fixed lower bounds for \(a,b_a\). The number of spatial patches may grow with \(W\), but the pointwise squared derivative sums remain bounded. These are multiplication cutoffs for a differential operator. Their commutators are exactly \(D_T\theta+iG D_Z\theta\), with bounded coefficient because \(G\) is uniformly bounded. Sum the squared local estimates and absorb their \(C\|v\|^2\) error for large \(\rho\). We obtain

\[ \rho^\alpha\|v\| \leq C\|\{D_T+i(F_0+GD_Z)\}v\|, \quad \operatorname{supp}v\subset\{|T|<c_0,|Z|<c_0W\}. \tag{4.6} \]

The argument includes both branches of the long spatial rectangle. It uses no growing polynomial-translation constant.

Undo the physical dilation. The constant correction contributes

\[ \frac{M}{B_2}\frac{D}{N^2}D_z =\frac{D}{A_2}D_z. \tag{4.7} \]

Therefore

\[ \rho^\alpha\|Mv\| \leq C\left(\|(D_t+i\mathcal L(t,z,D_z))v\| +\|A_2^{-1}D_zv\|\right) \tag{4.8} \]

for compact support \(|M(t-t_j)|<c_0,|B_2'z|<c_0\). The remaining spatial coordinates are passive parameters; integrate over them without changing the constant. The preceding proof also works for any fixed slightly larger \(c_0'<1\), which will be used in the Fourier cutoff step.

5. The exact coordinate and gauge transfer

First consider the explicit canonical map without a Fourier interchange. Write original coordinates as \((X',\Xi')\), canonical coordinates as \((x',\xi')\), and \(z=x_2\). The canonical-cell lesson gives

\[ \begin{gathered} X_2=z,\qquad X_l=x_l+af_l(z/a),\\ \Xi_l=\xi_l+ag_l(z/a)\quad(l\geq3),\\ \Xi_2=\xi_2+ag_2(z/a) +\sum_{l\geq3}\{g_l'(z/a)x_l-f_l'(z/a)\xi_l\}. \end{gathered} \tag{5.1} \]

The \(a\) in this section is the orbit amplitude from that lesson; it is independent of the small fixed normalization constant used in Section 4. Let \(s(X)\) be the triangular physical coordinate change \(s_2(X)=X_2,s_l(X)=X_l-af_l(X_2/a)\). Its Jacobian determinant is exactly one. Define

\[ \begin{aligned} S(x')={}&a\sum_{l\geq3}g_l(z/a)x_l\\ &+a^2\int_0^{z/a}\left(g_2(v)+\sum_{l\geq3}g_l(v)f_l'(v)\right)\,dv. \end{aligned} \tag{5.2} \]

The canonical one-form calculation in the cell lesson shows that the momentum after applying \(s\) is \(\xi+\nabla S\). The distinguished new frequency, as a function of old phase variables, is consequently

\[ \eta_2(X,\Xi) =\Xi_2+\sum_{l\geq3}f_l'(X_2/a)\Xi_l -(\partial_zS)(s(X)). \tag{5.3} \]

The exact unitary map from new to old functions is

\[ (Vv)(X')=e^{iS(s(X'))}v(s(X')). \tag{5.4} \]

The volume-preserving substitution and modulus-one phase prove unitarity. With the smooth extensions described in Section 2, both maps preserve Schwartz functions for each fixed set of parameters. Differentiate (5.4). The vector field \(\partial_{X_2}+\sum f_l'(X_2/a)\partial_{X_l}\) acts on \(v\circ s\) as \(\partial_zv\), and on \(S\circ s\) as \(\partial_zS\). The two phase terms cancel in (5.3). Thus

\[ \eta_2(X,D')V=VD_z, \qquad (\mathcal L\circ\chi^{-1})(t,X,D')V =V\mathcal L(t,z,D_z). \tag{5.5} \]

Both identities use left coefficient order and are exact. Time commutes with \(V\), since the canonical map is independent of time. No general nonlinear quantization covariance is invoked.

Let \(v_j=\Phi_j(t,X,D')U_2\). Its physical output has \(X_2=z\) in the prescribed inner interval, because its left symbol vanishes identically outside that physical projection. Consequently \(V^{-1}v_j\) has the exact support required in (4.8), together with its exact time support. Applying (4.8) and (5.5) gives

\[ \rho^\alpha\|M_jv_j\| \leq C\left(\|(D_t+i(\mathcal L_j\circ\chi_j^{-1})(t,X,D'))v_j\| +\|A_{2,j}^{-1}\eta_{2,j}(X,D')v_j\|\right). \tag{5.6} \]

The phase translation to the actual selected center is implemented by a constant translation and modulation, which are unitary and leave the same identities intact. Pair relabeling and a simultaneous spatial/frequency reflection have the corresponding exact unitary coordinate actions.

6. The Fourier case and its support repair

The remaining preliminary canonical choice is \(T_0(X,\Xi)=(\Xi,-X)\), with \(\chi=T_0\circ\widehat\chi\) and \(\widehat\chi\) of the form (5.1). Use the unitary transverse Fourier transform with kernel \((2\pi)^{-(n-1)/2}e^{-iY\cdot X}\). Its exact actions send \(D_X\) to multiplication by \(Y\) and multiplication by \(X\) to \(-D_Y\), implementing precisely \(T_0\).

Before applying that transform put the distinguished derivative to the right of its coefficient. In the intermediate coordinates the affine symbol in (5.5) is a sum of a scalar function of \(X_2\), terms \(A_l(t,X_2)\Xi_l\), and terms \(B_l(t,X_2)X_l\) for \(l\geq3\). Only \(A_2(t,X_2)\Xi_2=c(t,X_2)\Xi_2\) has a noncommuting coefficient in its own variable. Replacing \(cD_2\) by \(D_2c\) changes the operator by \(-i\partial_2c\). From the normalized derivative bounds,

\[ |\partial_zc| =M|\partial_ZF_1|\leq CM/\rho\leq CM. \tag{6.1} \]

This bounded multiplication error can be absorbed in (4.8) for sufficiently large \(\rho\). The exact gauge/coordinate transfer of the right-ordered operator equals the right-ordered intermediate expression, by the same chain rule. Under Fourier transformation it becomes precisely the left operator with symbol \(\mathcal L\circ\chi^{-1}\): the distinguished position factor is then on the left, and all other coordinate/frequency pairs commute with their coefficients. The term \(\eta_2\) has coefficient one on its distinguished derivative, so no ordering error enters it. The Fourier-transferred estimate has the same right side as (5.6), initially for functions whose transverse Fourier transform has the required distinguished-coordinate support.

That support is not automatic for \(v_j=\Phi_jU_2\). In this Fourier case the old frequency \(\Xi_2\) is \(-z\), so the symbol support of \(\Phi_j\) has \(|B_2'\Xi_2|<c_0\). Choose a fixed even \(h\in C_c^\infty((-c_0',c_0'))\), with \(c_0<c_0'<1\), equal to one on a neighborhood of \([-c_0,c_0]\). Put

\[ K_j=h(B_{2,j}'D_2),\qquad w_j=K_jv_j. \tag{6.2} \]

Now the Fourier transform of \(w_j\) has exact support \(|B_2'\Xi_2|<c_0'\). The time support is unchanged. Moreover every retained coefficient of \((K_j-I)\Phi_j\) is zero: the derivatives of \(h-1\) vanish throughout the symbol support. The frequency cutoff derivatives have the \(g_{R_2}\) bounds because \(B_2'\leq R_2^{-1}\). The full finite product remainder supplies any prescribed \(\lambda\) order, including after multiplication by \(\rho^\alpha M_j\).

We also need this small difference after applying the two operators on the right. Their Fourier-transferred symbols have the structure described in Section 2. Commuting a linear position factor through a frequency coefficient gives exact derivative terms; inserting \(\Phi_j\) then makes every coefficient bounded with polynomial parameter losses. Thus their compositions with \((K_j-I)\Phi_j\) have zero retained coefficients and rapidly small full remainders. Equivalently the only nonzero commutator of the transferred affine expression with \(K_j\) is the distinguished position term, proportional to \(B_2'c(-D_2)h'(B_2'D_2)\); the analogous commutator for \(\eta_2\) has the constant coefficient in place of \(c\). Their symbols vanish in a neighborhood of \(\operatorname{supp}\Phi_j\), and the same finite remainder proof applies. Every sign in these commutators follows from (2.1); their bounds depend only on the magnitudes.

Apply the legitimate Fourier-supported estimate to \(w_j\), replace it by \(v_j\) using these bounded tails, and restore the ordering error already absorbed in (6.1). We obtain

\[ \rho^\alpha\|M_jv_j\| \leq C\left(\|(D_t+i(\mathcal L_j\circ\chi_j^{-1})(t,X,D'))v_j\| +\|A_{2,j}^{-1}\eta_{2,j}(X,D')v_j\|+\|E_ju\|\right), \tag{6.3} \]

with the summed tail bound (2.2). This is the same localized conclusion as (5.6), with its actual support condition repaired.

7. The full affine Taylor remainder and absorption

Set \(r_j=q-\mathcal L_j\circ\chi_j^{-1}\). In canonical coordinates its value is \(Q_j(t,z,x'',\xi')-Q_j(t,z,0)-\xi_2\partial_{\xi_2}Q_j(t,z,0)\). The canonical residual theorem and the mean-value formula along the remaining transverse variables give

\[ r_j\circ\chi_j =\sum_{l\geq3}x_lT_{j,l}+\sum_{l\geq2}\xi_lS_{j,l}. \tag{7.1} \]

Each coefficient and each additional transverse derivative has the bound \(C_\beta\rho M_j L_{0,j}^{-1-|\beta|}\), with the distinguished spatial derivative having its sharper \(b_j\) factor. These are full smooth Taylor coefficients obtained by integrating the corresponding derivatives along the segment; the residual's value at the axis is exactly zero. On the doubled cutoff cell, the displayed linear variables have size at most \(CR_2\), while the distinguished coordinate retains its long permitted radius. Hence the normalized \(g_{R_2}\) derivatives of (7.1) have size at most

\[ CM_j\delta_\lambda, \qquad \delta_\lambda=\sup_j\rho R_2/L_{0,j}\longrightarrow0 \tag{7.2} \]

for fixed \(\rho\). The inverse canonical derivative bounds transfer them to the physical metric, with uniform constants because \(R_2/a_j\to0\) on these cells. The long-direction factors are retained by the explicit coordinate calculation; they are no larger than the fixed-cell bounds already proved for transported cutoffs. In particular the localized \(r_j\) belongs to \(S(CM_j,g_{R_2})\) for small \(\lambda\).

Choose the larger cutoff from Section 2 and multiply this local symbol by it. The resulting global symbol divided by \(M_j\) has uniformly bounded seminorms. Its diagonal Hilbert operator therefore bounds its action on \((M_jv_j)_j\). The difference between this localized operator and \(r_jv_j\) has zero finite coefficients near \(\operatorname{supp}\Phi_j\). For the original \(q\) use its global bounded symbol estimates. For the affine part use the exact linear-factor remainder argument of Section 2; it does not require a globally bounded affine symbol. Full finite tails and (2.2) prove

\[ \sum_j\|r_j(t,X,D')v_j\|^2 \leq C\left(\sum_j\|M_jv_j\|^2+\|u\|^2\right). \tag{7.3} \]

The distinguished new frequency has size at most \(CR_2\) on the enlarged cutoff support. Its inverse canonical derivatives give the same local \(g_{R_2}\) class. Since \(A_{2,j}>R^{-1}\),

\[ \frac{R_2}{A_{2,j}M_j} <\frac{R_2R}{M_j}\longrightarrow0. \tag{7.4} \]

Indeed \(\kappa+\kappa_2<2\alpha\), while \(M_j\geq c(\lambda^{-2}/\rho)^\alpha\). Apply the same larger-cutoff and full-tail argument, including its exact linear spatial/frequency calculation in the Fourier case. It gives

\[ \sum_j\|A_{2,j}^{-1}\eta_{2,j}(X,D')v_j\|^2 \leq C\left(\sum_j\|M_jv_j\|^2+\|u\|^2\right). \tag{7.5} \]

The symbol identity \(D_t+i\mathcal L_j\circ\chi_j^{-1}=P-ir_j(t,X,D')\) is also an exact left-operator identity. Square (5.6), (6.3), sum over their respective centers, and use (7.3), (7.5), (2.2). We obtain

\[ (\rho^{2\alpha}-C_1)\sum_j\|M_jv_j\|^2 \leq C_2\left(\sum_j\|Pv_j\|^2+\|u\|^2\right). \tag{7.6} \]

Choose \(\rho\) so large that \(C_1\leq\rho^{2\alpha}/2\), then take \(\lambda\) small enough for all preceding fixed-\(\rho\) thresholds. This proves the second inequality of (1.2). Combined with Section 3, it completes Theorem 1.1. ∎

8. Graded exercises with complete solutions

Exercise 1 — basic. If the center scale is attained by the \(l\)-th pure-time derivative, compute the corresponding derivative of \(f_a\) in (3.1). Why does the scalar parameter equal \(\rho\)?

Solution. Differentiation gives \(f_a^{(l)}(0)=m^{-l-1}\partial_t^lq(t_a,w_a)\). Attainment makes its absolute value \(\rho\); each other derivative through \(k\) is at most \(\rho\) by the defining scale. The maximum is therefore exactly \(\rho\), without a common root being applied to different derivative orders.

Exercise 2 — intermediate. Compute the correction in physical variables when \(G=F_1+D/N^2\), \(N=A_2(\rho M)^{1/2}\), and \(B_2=(\rho A_2)^{-1}\).

Solution. The coefficient of \(D_z\) changes by \((M/B_2)D/N^2\). Substitution gives \(M\rho A_2D/(A_2^2\rho M)=D/A_2\), exactly (4.7). It is a derivative error, rather than a bounded multiplication error, and is retained as such until (7.5).

Exercise 3 — intermediate. For \(k=2\), suppose the center jet magnitudes are \((2,3,5)\) and take the fixed small constant in (4.3) to be \(a=1/10\). Find \(J_a,b_a\) and the normalized center jet maximum.

Solution. The weighted magnitudes are \((2,3/10,1/20)\), so \(J_a=2\) and \(b_a=1/5\). The transformed magnitudes are \((a/b_a)(2,3/10,1/20)=(1,3/20,1/40)\). Their maximum is exactly one. The corresponding square has original half-widths \(1/10\) in time and \(1/5\) in space; these widths must fit in the coefficient rectangle.

Exercise 4 — advanced. Verify the gauge sign in (5.4) directly for the simpler map with no shear and phase \(S(z)\). Compute \((D_z-S'(z))e^{iS(z)}v\).

Solution. Since \(D_z=-i\partial_z\), differentiating the phase gives \(D_z(e^{iS}v)=e^{iS}(S'v+D_zv)\). Subtracting \(S'e^{iS}v\) leaves \(e^{iS}D_zv\). The positive phase in the map from new to old functions gives the cancellation in (5.5); its inverse uses the negative phase.

Exercise 5 — advanced. Why does symbol frequency support fail to give the exact Fourier support needed in Section 6? Give the repair and its full error estimate.

Solution. A left-quantized symbol depending on position multiplies a frequency-localized kernel; multiplication by its position coefficient can spread the output's Fourier transform. The symbol's distinguished-frequency support therefore does not alone impose a sharp Fourier support on \(\Phi_jU_2\). The multiplier \(h(B_2'D_2)\) does impose that support exactly. Because \(h=1\) on a neighborhood of the symbol's support, every retained coefficient of \((h(B_2'D_2)-I)\Phi_j\) vanishes. The full finite coefficient remainder is \(O(\lambda^L)\) for any prescribed \(L\), after choosing the finite expansion order. Polynomial weight losses and the proved polynomial family count can be absorbed by choosing \(L\) larger; (2.2) bounds the summed error. The transferred affine and frequency operators require the same full composition check, supplied in Section 6.

Exercise 6 — advanced. Let \(k=3\), \(\kappa=1/10\), \(\kappa_2=1/5\), and \(M\geq c\rho^{-1/4}\lambda^{-1/2}\). Compute the upper asymptotic bound in (7.4), and give the order of a summed tail with individual norm at most \(C\lambda^L\|u\|\) in spatial dimension \(n=3\).

Solution. Here \(R R_2=\lambda^{-3/10}\), so \(R R_2/M\leq C\rho^{1/4}\lambda^{1/5}\), tending to zero for fixed \(\rho\). The family count is at most \(C\lambda^{-6}\). Its summed squared tail is at most \(C_L\lambda^{2L-6}\|u\|^2\). Taking \(L\geq3\) makes it uniformly bounded; taking \(L>3\) makes it tend to zero. Any additional polynomial weight loss is included before this choice of \(L\).
