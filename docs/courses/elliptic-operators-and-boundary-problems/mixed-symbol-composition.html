Composition of mixed symbols with two different remainder estimates

This lesson proves the two composition assertions on the original mixed symbol spaces, with all four orders real and all position-variable estimates global. It uses the quadratic-multiplier estimates in Sections 7–8 of Quadratic Fourier multipliers at a moving scale, the distributional quantization identities of Section 4 of Two measuring scales, one Weyl product and Section 8 of Two measuring scales, one Weyl product, and the Schwartz operator argument of Sections 3–5 of From symbol estimates to operators on every Sobolev scale. Every metric, weight, parameter, and domain verification needed to apply those results is supplied below. No positive frequency-decay exponent in every direction is assumed merely because the symbols are mixed symbols.

The Fourier convention is û(ξ)=∫e−ix⋅ξu(x)dx\widehat u(\xi)=\int e^{-ix\cdot\xi}u(x)\,dx, with inverse coefficient (2π)−n(2\pi)^{-n}, and Dj=−i∂jD_j=-i\partial_j. Fourier inversion on Schwartz space, its tempered-distribution extension, the corresponding kernel correspondence, ordinary Lebesgue integration and differentiation rules, and the already proved course Gauss theorem are the explicit entry mathematics. The proof does not claim to supply those general foundations again.

1. The symbols, their topology, and the exact target

Fix n≥1n\geq1 and fixed finite-dimensional complex Hermitian spaces E0,E1,E2E_0,E_1,E_2. A symbol aa takes values in Hom⁡(E1,E2)\operatorname{Hom}(E_1,E_2), and bb in Hom⁡(E0,E1)\operatorname{Hom}(E_0,E_1); their displayed product is in Hom⁡(E0,E2)\operatorname{Hom}(E_0,E_2). Operator norms are used throughout, so ∥ab∥≤∥a∥∥b∥\|ab\|\leq\|a\|\|b\|. Square matrix symbols are included without a change of convention.

Write ξ=(ξ′,ξn)\xi=(\xi',\xi_n) and retain

R(ξ)=1+|ξ|,T(ξ′)=1+|ξ′|,Wp,q;α=Rp−αnTq−|α′|.(MC1) R(\xi)=1+|\xi|,\qquad T(\xi')=1+|\xi'|, \qquad W_{p,q;\alpha}=R^{p-\alpha_n}T^{q-|\alpha'|}. \tag{MC1}

For n=1n=1, ξ′∈ℝ0\xi'\in\mathbb R^0, T=1T=1, and α′\alpha' is empty. For every p,q∈ℝp,q\in\mathbb R, the space Sp,qS^{p,q} consists of smooth functions on all of ℝxn×ℝξn\mathbb R_x^n\times\mathbb R_\xi^n such that

pp,q,L(f)=max|α|+|β|≤Lsupx,ξ∥∂xβ∂ξαf(x,ξ)∥R(ξ)p−αnT(ξ′)q−|α′|<∞(L≥0).(MC2) p_{p,q,L}(f)=\max_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq L} \sup_{x,\xi}\frac{\|\partial_x^\beta\partial_\xi^\alpha f(x,\xi)\|} {R(\xi)^{p-\alpha_n}T(\xi')^{q-|\alpha'|}}<\infty \qquad(L\geq0). \tag{MC2}

No weight is differentiated; the lack of differentiability of 1+|ξ|1+|\xi| at the origin is irrelevant to this definition and the estimates below. These seminorms define a Fréchet space: a Cauchy sequence has derivatives uniformly convergent on every compact set; the fundamental theorem of calculus on coordinate segments identifies the successive derivatives of the limit; passing to limits in every weighted bound then gives convergence in (MC2). The product rule gives a continuous bilinear pointwise product Sp,q×Sr,s→Sp+r,q+sS^{p,q}\times S^{r,s}\to S^{p+r,q+s}, because the exponents of each of the two weights add exactly in each Leibniz term. This multiplication preserves the order of the two matrix factors.

Theorem. Let m,m′,μ,μ′∈ℝm,m',\mu,\mu'\in\mathbb R, a∈Sm,m′a\in S^{m,m'}, and b∈Sμ,μ′b\in S^{\mu,\mu'}. There is a unique mixed symbol cc representing their left operator composition, and

Op⁡(a)Op⁡(b)=Op⁡(c),c∈Sm+μ,m′+μ′,c−ab∈Sm+μ,m′+μ′−1.(MC3) \begin{gathered} \operatorname{Op}(a)\operatorname{Op}(b)=\operatorname{Op}(c), \qquad c\in S^{m+\mu,m'+\mu'},\\ c-ab\in S^{m+\mu,m'+\mu'-1}. \end{gathered} \tag{MC3}

The operator equality holds on 𝒮\mathcal S and on 𝒮′\mathcal S', with the continuous extensions constructed below. If in addition

∂ξja∈Sm−1,m′(1≤j≤n),(MC4) \partial_{\xi_j}a\in S^{m-1,m'}\qquad(1\leq j\leq n), \tag{MC4}

then the same actual remainder, without changing cc, satisfies

c−ab∈Sm+μ−1,m′+μ′.(MC5) c-ab\in S^{m+\mu-1,m'+\mu'}. \tag{MC5}

Every asserted map has finite input-seminorm bounds. More precisely, for each output order LL some finite JJ and CLC_L, depending only on the fixed orders, dimensions, and derivative order, satisfy

pm+μ,m′+μ′,L(c)≤CLpm,m′,J(a)pμ,μ′,J(b),pm+μ,m′+μ′−1,L(c−ab)≤CLpm,m′,J(a)pμ,μ′,J(b),pm+μ−1,m′+μ′,L(c−ab)≤CL∑j=1npm−1,m′,J(∂ξja)pμ,μ′,J+1(b)under (MC4).(MC6) \begin{aligned} p_{m+\mu,m'+\mu',L}(c) &\leq C_Lp_{m,m',J}(a)p_{\mu,\mu',J}(b),\\ p_{m+\mu,m'+\mu'-1,L}(c-ab) &\leq C_Lp_{m,m',J}(a)p_{\mu,\mu',J}(b),\\ p_{m+\mu-1,m'+\mu',L}(c-ab) &\leq C_L\sum_{j=1}^n p_{m-1,m',J}(\partial_{\xi_j}a) p_{\mu,\mu',J+1}(b) \quad\text{under (MC4)}. \end{aligned} \tag{MC6}

The integer JJ can be enlarged to accommodate the finite derivative shifts used in a displayed inequality. No bound requires an infinite collection of seminorms for a fixed output seminorm. Constants in the last line use the actual strengthened seminorms from (MC4); those seminorms are not inferred from the original class.

2. Exact comparison with bracket weights and metric symbols

Let R0=(1+|ξ|2)1/2R_0=(1+|\xi|^2)^{1/2}, T0=(1+|ξ′|2)1/2T_0=(1+|\xi'|^2)^{1/2}. Since 1+r2≤(1+r)2≤2(1+r2)1+r^2\leq(1+r)^2\leq2(1+r^2) for r≥0r\geq0, both R/R0R/R_0 and T/T0T/T_0 lie in [1,2][1,\sqrt2]. For real u=p−αnu=p-\alpha_n, v=q−|α′|v=q-|\alpha'|, and t+=max⁡(t,0)t_+=\max(t,0), t−=max⁡(−t,0)t_-=\max(-t,0), taking the appropriate positive or negative powers gives

2−(u−+v−)/2R0uT0v≤RuTv≤2(u++v+)/2R0uT0v.(MC7) 2^{-(u_-+v_-)/2}R_0^uT_0^v \leq R^uT^v \leq2^{(u_++v_+)/2}R_0^uT_0^v. \tag{MC7}

For a fixed derivative, division gives the two seminorm comparisons ∥f∥R,T≤2(u−+v−)/2∥f∥R0,T0\|f\|_{R,T}\leq2^{(u_-+v_-)/2}\|f\|_{R_0,T_0} and ∥f∥R0,T0≤2(u++v+)/2∥f∥R,T\|f\|_{R_0,T_0}\leq2^{(u_++v_+)/2}\|f\|_{R,T}. Thus the identity on the same smooth functions is a continuous linear bijection with continuous inverse between the two presentations. It preserves pointwise multiplication, all derivatives, and left quantization, because none of those operations or arguments is changed. It therefore transfers (MC3)–(MC6) with the explicitly displayed constants for each derivative, including all negative orders. In dimension one the factors involving vv can be omitted because T=T0=1T=T_0=1. This is precisely the weight morphism proved in Section 1 of Inverting mixed symbols without commuting matrix factors; it does not replace the original weights silently.

Define the positive quadratic metric and weight on phase space by

g(x,ξ)(v,w)=|v|2+T(ξ′)−2|w′|2+R(ξ)−2wn2,Mp,q(x,ξ)=R(ξ)pT(ξ′)q.(MC8) g_{(x,\xi)}(v,w)=|v|^2+T(\xi')^{-2}|w'|^2+R(\xi)^{-2}w_n^2, \qquad M_{p,q}(x,\xi)=R(\xi)^pT(\xi')^q. \tag{MC8}

The metric definition of S(Mp,q,g)S(M_{p,q},g) uses all multilinear directional derivatives, divided by Mp,qM_{p,q} and by the product of the corresponding metric lengths. It equals Sp,qS^{p,q} with an exact seminorm comparison. A coordinate direction has length one in an xjx_j direction, T−1T^{-1} in a tangential frequency direction, and R−1R^{-1} in the normal frequency direction. Hence a coordinate derivative bound follows from the directional bound with constant one. Conversely, for gg-unit directions V1,…,VkV_1,\ldots,V_k, expand the derivative in the scaled coordinate frame (∂x1,…,∂xn,T∂ξ1,…,T∂ξn−1,R∂ξn)(\partial_{x_1},\ldots,\partial_{x_n},T\partial_{\xi_1},\ldots,T\partial_{\xi_{n-1}},R\partial_{\xi_n}) at the fixed observation point. Each vector of coefficients has Euclidean length at most one and sum of absolute values at most 2n\sqrt{2n}. The multilinear expansion is consequently bounded by (2n)k/2Mp,qpp,q,k(f)(2n)^{k/2}M_{p,q}p_{p,q,k}(f). The scales are fixed numbers at that point, so this calculation does not differentiate the metric. We have proved the identity map and its inverse between the two symbol topologies, including the dimension-dependent directional constants.

We verify every structural hypothesis. Both RR and TT are 1-Lipschitz in their respective frequency variables. If gX(Y−X)1/2≤ϵg_X(Y-X)^{1/2}\leq\epsilon, then the tangential difference is at most ϵTX\epsilon T_X, the normal difference at most ϵRX\epsilon R_X, and the full frequency difference at most 2ϵRX\sqrt2\epsilon R_X, since TX≤RXT_X\leq R_X. For ϵ≤1/4\epsilon\leq1/4, both ratios RY/RX,TY/TXR_Y/R_X,T_Y/T_X lie between 1/21/2 and 3/23/2. The frequency coefficients of the metrics are therefore comparable with a factor at most four, while the base coefficient remains one. This proves slow variation. Each weight RpTqR^pT^q and its reciprocal is locally comparable with factor at most 2|p|+|q|2^{|p|+|q|}, proving local metric continuity of the weight.

For arbitrary frequencies ξ,η\xi,\eta, the triangle inequality gives

max⁡(R(ξ)R(η),R(η)R(ξ),T(ξ′)T(η′),T(η′)T(ξ′))≤1+|ξ−η|.(MC9) \max\left(\frac{R(\xi)}{R(\eta)},\frac{R(\eta)}{R(\xi)}, \frac{T(\xi')}{T(\eta')},\frac{T(\eta')}{T(\xi')}\right) \leq1+|\xi-\eta|. \tag{MC9}

For example R(ξ)≤R(η)+|ξ−η|≤R(η)(1+|ξ−η|)R(\xi)\leq R(\eta)+|\xi-\eta|\leq R(\eta)(1+|\xi-\eta|), since R(η)≥1R(\eta)\geq1; the other ratios follow in the same way. With the standard symplectic form σ((v,w),(v1,w1))=w⋅v1−w1⋅v\sigma((v,w),(v_1,w_1))=w\cdot v_1-w_1\cdot v, direct quadratic duality gives

gXσ(v,w)=TX2|v′|2+RX2vn2+|w|2.(MC10) g_X^\sigma(v,w)=T_X^2|v'|^2+R_X^2v_n^2+|w|^2. \tag{MC10}

Its displacement term dominates |ξ−η|2|\xi-\eta|^2, at either base point. Combining (MC9) with (1+d)2≤2(1+d2)(1+d)^2\leq2(1+d^2) proves gY≤2gX(1+gYσ(X−Y))g_Y\leq2g_X(1+g_Y^\sigma(X-Y)) and Mp,q(Y)≤2(|p|+|q|)/2Mp,q(X)(1+gYσ(X−Y))(|p|+|q|)/2M_{p,q}(Y)\leq2^{(|p|+|q|)/2}M_{p,q}(X)(1+g_Y^\sigma(X-Y))^{(|p|+|q|)/2}. The same estimates apply to reciprocal weights. These are the exact symplectic temperateness conditions, with finite exponents and constants for every real p,qp,q.

The uncertainty parameter hg=sup⁡(g/gσ)1/2h_g=\sup(g/g^\sigma)^{1/2} is T−1T^{-1} if n≥2n\geq2, because T≤RT\leq R, and is R−1R^{-1} if n=1n=1. It is at most one. Thus the same-metric instance of Section 7 of Two measuring scales, one Weyl product applies without a missing compatibility condition: Section 3 of Two measuring scales, one Weyl product proves that the cross tests are automatic when both metrics equal the same symplectically temperate metric. It yields the Weyl symbol product with remainder in S(Mm,m′Mμ,μ′hg,g)S(M_{m,m'}M_{\mu,\mu'}h_g,g). This confirms the metric meaning of the first gain. That theorem alone only established the operator product for Schwartz symbols; Section 6 below proves the required common-domain identity for the present global mixed symbols. The stronger gain in (MC5) needs the parameter argument below rather than an assertion about the ordinary value of hgh_g.

3. A uniform quadratic multiplier on the transformed variables

Use z=(y,η)∈ℝ2nz=(y,\eta)\in\mathbb R^{2n}, with the same metric (MC8), now based on η\eta. For the Fourier-dual variables (py,pη)(p_y,p_\eta), set

At(py,pη)=tpy⋅pη,Tt=exp⁡(it⟨Dy,Dη⟩),−1≤t≤1.(MC11) A_t(p_y,p_\eta)=t\,p_y\cdot p_\eta,\qquad T_t=\exp(i t\langle D_y,D_\eta\rangle),\qquad -1\leq t\leq1. \tag{MC11}

The symmetric map for this quadratic form is t/2t/2 times the swap of the two blocks. For t≠0t\ne0, the phase-dual form in the convention of Section 1 of Quadratic Fourier multipliers at a moving scale is therefore

gzAt(v,w)=4t2(T(η′)2|v′|2+R(η)2vn2+|w|2),ht(z)={|t|/(2T(η′)),n≥2,|t|/(2R(η)),n=1.(MC12) g_z^{A_t}(v,w)=\frac4{t^2} \left(T(\eta')^2|v'|^2+R(\eta)^2v_n^2+|w|^2\right), \qquad h_t(z)=\begin{cases}|t|/(2T(\eta')),&n\geq2,\\|t|/(2R(\eta)),&n=1.\end{cases} \tag{MC12}

Indeed substituting the swap into gz(B(py,pη))g_z(B(p_y,p_\eta)) gives t2(|pη|2+T−2|py′|2+R−2py,n2)/4t^2(|p_\eta|^2+T^{-2}|p_y'|^2+R^{-2}p_{y,n}^2)/4; dualizing this diagonal positive form gives the first formula. The second is its maximal coordinate ratio with (MC8). Thus gz≤gzAtg_z\leq g_z^{A_t}.

The dual displacement in (MC12) dominates 4|η−ζ|24|\eta-\zeta|^2 for every 0<|t|≤10<|t|\leq1. Consequently the very same ratio estimate (MC9) proves both conditions G13 of Section 5 of Quadratic Fourier multipliers at a moving scale uniformly in tt, globally at every observation point zz. The metric exponent can be one and the weight exponent (|p|+|q|)/2(|p|+|q|)/2; the constants in the preceding section remain valid. Slow variation and local weight continuity do not depend on tt at all. At t=0t=0 the multiplier is the identity, treated directly rather than through a zero positive weight.

Apply Sections 7–8 of Quadratic Fourier multipliers at a moving scale with E0=ℝ2nE_0=\mathbb R^{2n} and weight R(η)pT(η′)qR(\eta)^pT(\eta')^q. Their constants and finite derivative orders depend only on the structural constants and dimension, which have just been made uniform for 0<|t|≤10<|t|\leq1. In each fixed output seminorm this gives

pp,q,L(Ttu)≤CLpp,q,JL(u),pp,q,L(Ttu−u)≤CL|t|pp,q,JL(u),pp,q,L(Ttu−u−it⟨Dy,Dη⟩u)≤CLt2pp,q,JL(u).(MC13) \begin{aligned} p_{p,q,L}(T_tu)&\leq C_Lp_{p,q,J_L}(u),\\ p_{p,q,L}(T_tu-u)&\leq C_L|t|p_{p,q,J_L}(u),\\ p_{p,q,L}(T_tu-u-it\langle D_y,D_\eta\rangle u) &\leq C_Lt^2p_{p,q,J_L}(u). \end{aligned} \tag{MC13}

The second and third follow from their sharper remainder weights MhtM h_t and Mht2M h_t^2, using ht≤|t|/2h_t\leq|t|/2. The directional-to-coordinate comparison from Section 2 supplies exactly the seminorms written here. All three bounds include t=0t=0 by their direct identities. To pass the scalar Gauss estimate to matrices, apply it to each scalar function ⟨u(⋅)v,w⟩\langle u(\cdot)v,w\rangle for fixed unit vectors v,wv,w in the corresponding Hermitian spaces. Each of its derivative seminorms is bounded by the matrix seminorm. Taking the supremum over those vectors gives the operator-norm estimate, with the same constant. This proves the finite-dimensional extension without interchanging matrix factors.

For precision about the meaning of this extension, any mixed symbol has polynomial growth in η\eta, uniformly in yy, and hence defines a tempered distribution. Choose smooth compact cutoffs χy,χη\chi_y,\chi_\eta, both equal to one near the origin, and multiply by χy(y/j)χη(η/j)\chi_y(y/j)\chi_\eta(\eta/j) for integers j≥1j\geq1. They remain in a bounded mixed-symbol set. To verify this, a frequency cutoff derivative with multiindex α\alpha has magnitude at most a fixed derivative constant times j−|α|j^{-|\alpha|} and is supported where |η|≤Cj|\eta|\leq Cj, for a fixed CC. On that support, RαnT|α′|≤(1+C)|α|j|α|R^{\alpha_n}T^{|\alpha'|}\leq(1+C)^{|\alpha|}j^{|\alpha|}, so its magnitude is at most its fixed constant times R−αnT−|α′|R^{-\alpha_n}T^{-|\alpha'|}. The zero-order cutoff is bounded as well. Base cutoff derivatives are uniformly bounded because j−1≤1j^{-1}\leq1. The product rule proves every required seminorm bound. These approximants converge locally smoothly and in tempered distributions, the latter by their common polynomial bound and the rapid decrease of any Schwartz test function. Their Gauss limits converge locally smoothly by Section 7 of Quadratic Fourier multipliers at a moving scale, while their quadratic multipliers converge in tempered distributions because that Fourier multiplier acts continuously there. The two limits agree as distributions on each compact set and hence globally. Thus TtT_t here is exactly its distributional Fourier multiplier, not an unrelated extension.

It follows that TsTt=Ts+tT_sT_t=T_{s+t} whenever the parameters in use are finite; for |s|,|t|,|s+t|≤1|s|,|t|,|s+t|\leq1, all three maps have the bounds already proved. In particular, for small hh, Tt+hu−Ttu=Tt(Thu−u)T_{t+h}u-T_tu=T_t(T_hu-u), using bounds on a fixed finite parameter interval if an endpoint is crossed. The same computation (MC12) with |t|≤2|t|\leq2 still gives ht≤1h_t\leq1 and uniform structural constants, so no new theorem is needed at the endpoints. Equations (MC13) give continuity and, from the third bound, differentiation in the Fréchet symbol topology:

ddtTtu=Tt(i⟨Dy,Dη⟩u).(MC14) \frac{d}{dt}T_tu=T_t\bigl(i\langle D_y,D_\eta\rangle u\bigr). \tag{MC14}

The differentiation is valid in every output seminorm; each estimate uses only finitely many input seminorms. This proves parameter continuity at zero and the uniform bounds required for integration over the entire closed parameter interval.

4. The parameter-dependent product before restricting to the diagonal

Define

F(x,ξ;y,η)=a(x,η)b(y,ξ),ℬt(x,ξ;y,η)=Tt(y,η)F(x,ξ;y,η).(MC15) F(x,\xi;y,\eta)=a(x,\eta)b(y,\xi),\qquad \mathcal B_t(x,\xi;y,\eta)=T_t^{(y,\eta)}F(x,\xi;y,\eta). \tag{MC15}

Here (x,ξ)(x,\xi) are parameters, and TtT_t acts only on the indicated (y,η)(y,\eta) variables. At fixed parameters the transformed-variable weight is R(η)mT(η′)m′R(ξ)μT(ξ′)μ′R(\eta)^mT(\eta')^{m'}R(\xi)^\mu T(\xi')^{\mu'}. The last two factors are positive constants with respect to the transformed variables, so they do not change any metric or weight structural constant. Product differentiation in the transformed variables gives derivatives of aa in η\eta followed by derivatives of bb in yy, with the displayed matrix order unchanged. Thus all source seminorms required by Section 3 are bounded by a finite product of seminorms of a,ba,b.

The exact pre-diagonal derivative estimate, for arbitrary multiindices α,β,γ,δ\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta, is

∥∂xβ∂ξα∂ηγ∂yδℬt(x,ξ;y,η)∥≤Cpm,m′,J(a)pμ,μ′,J(b)R(η)m−γnT(η′)m′−|γ′|R(ξ)μ−αnT(ξ′)μ′−|α′|,0≤t≤1.(MC16) \begin{split} &\|\partial_x^\beta\partial_\xi^\alpha \partial_\eta^\gamma\partial_y^\delta\mathcal B_t(x,\xi;y,\eta)\|\\ &\quad\leq C\,p_{m,m',J}(a)p_{\mu,\mu',J}(b) R(\eta)^{m-\gamma_n}T(\eta')^{m'-|\gamma'|} R(\xi)^{\mu-\alpha_n}T(\xi')^{\mu'-|\alpha'|}, \qquad 0\leq t\leq1. \end{split} \tag{MC16}

To prove every part of this estimate, commute ∂yδ∂ηγ\partial_y^\delta\partial_\eta^\gamma with the constant-coefficient multiplier, first for compactly supported functions and then by its distributional continuity. Their application to FF gives exactly (∂xβ∂ηγa)(x,η)(∂yδ∂ξαb)(y,ξ)(\partial_x^\beta\partial_\eta^\gamma a)(x,\eta) (\partial_y^\delta\partial_\xi^\alpha b)(y,\xi), with the matrix product in its original order. The first factor has transformed weight R(η)m−γnT(η′)m′−|γ′|R(\eta)^{m-\gamma_n}T(\eta')^{m'-|\gamma'|}; the second has the fixed parameter factor in (MC16), with no cost for its δ\delta base derivatives. Apply (MC13) with those exact shifted real exponents, using Section 2 for coordinate derivatives if needed. This proves (MC16).

The parameter derivatives in (MC16) are actual derivatives, not formal labels. On any compact parameter set, the map (x,ξ)↦F(x,ξ;⋅,⋅)(x,\xi)\mapsto F(x,\xi;\cdot,\cdot) is smooth into the Fréchet space with weight R(η)mT(η′)m′R(\eta)^mT(\eta')^{m'}. For example Taylor’s formula in one parameter with its integral remainder, measured in any source seminorm, bounds the difference-quotient error by |h||h| times a finite supremum of the corresponding next two parameter derivatives on a compact parameter neighborhood. Those derivative bounds follow directly from (MC2); the fixed ξ\xi factors are bounded there. Induction over derivatives proves the smoothness assertion. Continuous linear application of TtT_t therefore commutes with all parameter derivatives. The uniform estimates (MC13) and the same argument give joint local continuity of every such derivative with respect to tt, including t=0t=0. They also justify (MC14) with parameters present.

Set

Ct(a,b)(x,ξ)=ℬt(x,ξ;x,ξ),c=C1(a,b),C0(a,b)=ab.(MC17) C_t(a,b)(x,\xi)=\mathcal B_t(x,\xi;x,\xi),\qquad c=C_1(a,b),\qquad C_0(a,b)=ab. \tag{MC17}

Differentiation of the diagonal restriction replaces each base derivative by ∂x+∂y\partial_x+\partial_y and each frequency derivative by ∂ξ+∂η\partial_\xi+\partial_\eta. Expand these sums with their binomial coefficients. For a total frequency derivative κ=α+γ\kappa=\alpha+\gamma, the exponents in (MC16) add on the diagonal to m+μ−κnm+\mu-\kappa_n and m′+μ′−|κ′|m'+\mu'-|\kappa'|. The total base derivative causes no weight cost. There are finitely many terms, each bounded by (MC16), so

sup0≤t≤1pm+μ,m′+μ′,L(Ct(a,b))≤CLpm,m′,J(a)pμ,μ′,J(b).(MC18) \sup_{0\leq t\leq1}p_{m+\mu,m'+\mu',L}(C_t(a,b)) \leq C_Lp_{m,m',J}(a)p_{\mu,\mu',J}(b). \tag{MC18}

The same argument proves bounded-set local smooth continuity of these products in both factors, by the corresponding Gauss property and the locally uniform derivative bounds. For fixed inputs the parameter curve is also continuous in every target symbol seminorm: apply the semigroup identity and the second bound in (MC13) to each of the differentiated terms used in (MC16), obtaining a bound CL|t−s|pm,m′,J(a)pμ,μ′,J(b)C_L|t-s|p_{m,m',J}(a)p_{\mu,\mu',J}(b) for the target seminorm of Ct(a,b)−Cs(a,b)C_t(a,b)-C_s(a,b). The unchanged parameter weight factors cancel exactly as in (MC18). This works for arbitrary shifted orders as well, so it supplies continuity in every remainder space used below. The bounded-input local continuity assertion does not assert compact-support density in the full symbol topology.

5. The exact first remainder and both distinct order gains

Apply (MC14) to (MC15). Since the derivatives in yy only hit bb and those in η\eta only hit aa, the convention D=−i∂D=-i\partial gives the exact identity

i⟨Dy,Dη⟩F=∑j=1n(∂ηja)(x,η)(Dyjb)(y,ξ).(MC19) i\langle D_y,D_\eta\rangle F =\sum_{j=1}^n(\partial_{\eta_j}a)(x,\eta) (D_{y_j}b)(y,\xi). \tag{MC19}

Indeed i(−i)(−i)=−ii(-i)(-i)=-i, the coefficient of the derivative on bb in the right side. The fundamental theorem of calculus in (MC14), followed by the diagonal restriction, therefore yields

The integral is an integral of smooth functions with derivatives locally uniformly continuous in tt, and all symbol seminorms have uniform majorants. 6. The operator identity on its full stated domain

For any mixed symbol aa, every derivative has polynomial frequency growth uniformly in xx. For example RmTm′≤Rm++m+′R^mT^{m'}\leq R^{m_++m'_+}, and differentiation only lowers these exponents. Define left quantization on vector-valued Schwartz functions by

Op⁡(a)u(x)=(2π)−n∫eix⋅ξa(x,ξ)û(ξ)dξ.(MC23) \operatorname{Op}(a)u(x)=(2\pi)^{-n}\int e^{ix\cdot\xi}a(x,\xi)\widehat u(\xi)\,d\xi. \tag{MC23}

This integral and all its base derivatives are absolutely convergent. To control a Schwartz seminorm sup⁡x∥xγ∂xβOp⁡(a)u(x)∥\sup_x\|x^\gamma\partial_x^\beta\operatorname{Op}(a)u(x)\|, first expand ∂xβ\partial_x^\beta, which produces only finitely many base derivatives of aa and polynomial frequency factors. Transfer xγx^\gamma from the exponential by integration by parts in ξ\xi. Each term contains finitely many derivatives of aa, one polynomial, and a derivative of û\widehat u. Choose a Schwartz seminorm of û\widehat u whose negative weight exceeds the polynomial growth plus n+1n+1; the integrand is then bounded by a constant times an integrable negative power of RR, uniformly in xx. Boundary terms vanish by that same decay. This proves a finite-seminorm bound and continuity Op⁡(a):𝒮(E1)→𝒮(E2)\operatorname{Op}(a):\mathcal S(E_1)\to\mathcal S(E_2), jointly in the symbol and the input. This is the proof of Section 3 of From symbol estimates to operators on every Sobolev scale applied to the actual polynomial bounds here; it does not place the mixed class inside an inapplicable classical positive-ρ\rho symbol class.

For Schwartz symbols, Fubini applied to the two Fourier integrals, as in Section 5 of From symbol estimates to operators on every Sobolev scale, gives the left composition symbol

c(x,ξ)=(2π)−n∬ei(x−y)⋅(η−ξ)a(x,η)b(y,ξ)dydη=C1(a,b)(x,ξ).(MC24) c(x,\xi)=(2\pi)^{-n}\iint e^{i(x-y)\cdot(\eta-\xi)}a(x,\eta)b(y,\xi)\,dy\,d\eta =C_1(a,b)(x,\xi). \tag{MC24}

Absolute convergence holds for these symbol inputs in the relevant integrations. Equation (MC24) is also (MC21) at t=1t=1, by z=y−xz=y-x and the frequency shift. The phase is therefore the left-quantization phase, not the Weyl phase.

Choose smooth compact cutoffs in both variables tending to one, and let ak,bka_k,b_k be their products with a,ba,b. The cutoff bounds proved in Section 3 show that the symbols remain bounded in their original mixed classes and converge locally smoothly. Their products ck=C1(ak,bk)c_k=C_1(a_k,b_k) remain bounded in the target mixed class and converge locally smoothly to cc, by Section 4. For any fixed u∈𝒮u\in\mathcal S, the operators of all three sequences converge on uu in Schwartz space. To see this explicitly, their integrals and derivatives converge locally in xx by dominated convergence and the uniform polynomial bounds. The preceding Schwartz estimates uniformly bound every output Schwartz seminorm. Outside a large base ball, a seminorm with one higher power of 1+|x|1+|x| makes the tail uniformly small; inside that ball the local smooth convergence applies. This proves convergence in each Schwartz seminorm.

Joint continuity also controls the varying inner input in a product: for every target Schwartz seminorm, the uniformly bounded symbols aka_k give a common finite collection of input seminorms. Thus Op⁡(ak)(Op⁡(bk)u−Op⁡(b)u)→0\operatorname{Op}(a_k)(\operatorname{Op}(b_k)u-\operatorname{Op}(b)u)\to0, while (Op⁡(ak)−Op⁡(a))Op⁡(b)u→0(\operatorname{Op}(a_k)-\operatorname{Op}(a))\operatorname{Op}(b)u\to0. Passing to the limit in the Schwartz-symbol identities proves (MC3) as an operator identity on 𝒮\mathcal S.

We also supply the adjoint domain needed for 𝒮′\mathcal S'. The distributional kernel identity proved in Section 3 of From symbol estimates to operators on every Sobolev scale, equation E13, gives

a†=exp⁡(i⟨Dx,Dξ⟩)(a*),⟨Op⁡(a)u,v⟩=⟨u,Op⁡(a†)v⟩(u,v∈𝒮),(MC25) a^\dagger=\exp(i\langle D_x,D_\xi\rangle)(a^*), \qquad \langle\operatorname{Op}(a)u,v\rangle =\langle u,\operatorname{Op}(a^\dagger)v\rangle \quad(u,v\in\mathcal S), \tag{MC25}

with inner products linear in the first variable. That identity is an identity of tempered kernels and applies to our polynomially growing symbols. The multiplier in (MC25) is exactly (MC11) at t=1t=1, on the full (x,ξ)(x,\xi) phase space with (MC8), so Section 3 proves a†∈Sm,m′a^\dagger\in S^{m,m'}. Its Schwartz action is continuous by (MC23). Transposition of that action, using the adjoint pairing, therefore defines a continuous action of Op⁡(a)\operatorname{Op}(a) on 𝒮′\mathcal S'. It agrees with (MC23) on Schwartz functions by (MC25).

The identity on 𝒮\mathcal S implies that the adjoint of Op⁡(c)\operatorname{Op}(c) there equals the reversed composition of these continuous Schwartz adjoints: test it against two arbitrary Schwartz functions and use (MC25) twice. Transposing that identity proves Op⁡(a)Op⁡(b)=Op⁡(c)\operatorname{Op}(a)\operatorname{Op}(b)=\operatorname{Op}(c) on 𝒮′\mathcal S'. Both compositions are defined because each factor is continuous on the indicated common domain.

Finally, the left symbol-to-kernel map is injective: take the partial inverse Fourier transform in ξ\xi and then the invertible linear substitution z=x−yz=x-y, as in Section 3 of From symbol estimates to operators on every Sobolev scale, E12. Reversing these two operations recovers the symbol distribution uniquely from the kernel. The tempered kernel correspondence identifies the operator with its kernel; hence another symbol giving the same operator has the same kernel and is equal to cc. This proves the claimed uniqueness. The same argument shows that this left composition is associative and has the constant identity matrix as its unit whenever the matrix sizes are compatible, although neither fact is needed for the remainder estimates.

There is an exact map to the already proved Weyl product, not merely a similarity of its estimates. On full phase space write Qs=exp⁡(is⟨Dx,Dξ⟩)Q_s=\exp(is\langle D_x,D_\xi\rangle), and put aW=Q−1/2aa_W=Q_{-1/2}a, bW=Q−1/2bb_W=Q_{-1/2}b. 7. An exact product showing the necessity of the stronger hypothesis

For a fixed v∈ℝnv\in\mathbb R^n, a constant matrix BB, and a frequency-dependent symbol a(ξ)a(\xi), Fourier modulation gives the exact identity
