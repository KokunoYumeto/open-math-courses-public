Reducing first-order boundary data to a split trace

The index of a first-order elliptic boundary problem can be computed after a sequence of explicit deformations. Each deformation has to preserve two structures at once: the interior symbol must remain invertible, and the boundary symbol must remain an isomorphism on the decaying Cauchy data. This lesson constructs the whole sequence. It normalizes the normal coefficient, freezes the collar symbol, collapses all spectral rates while keeping the Calderón projection fixed, rotates the boundary isomorphism after a zero-index stabilization, and reaches the split trace model used by doubling.

The formulas keep every bundle map and every matrix factor in its typed order. In particular, the inverse of the boundary symbol is defined only on the stable bundle, the two off-diagonal blocks of the rotation have different source and target bundles, and a quantized projection is never treated as an exact operator projection.

The named prerequisites are Stable modes and the algebra of boundary data, Cauchy data from jumps and residues, Fredholm boundary problems with first-order Calderón defects, Finite defects under perturbation, Symbols, finite defects, and the index on a closed manifold, The Bott operator, suspension, and reduction of the index to Euclidean space, and Doubling a boundary problem and computing its index. We use Dt=−i∂tD_t=-i\partial_t, an inward collar coordinate t≥0t\geq0, and complex bundles.

1. The starting problem and the reduction theorem

Let XX be a compact smooth manifold with boundary YY. Let E,F→XE,F\to X and G→YG\to Y be smooth complex bundles. Consider a first-order elliptic operator and an order-zero boundary operator

(P,B)1:H1(X;E)→L2(X;F)⊕H1/2(Y;G),B=ℬγ0.(BR1) (P,B)_1:H^1(X;E)\longrightarrow L^2(X;F)\oplus H^{1/2}(Y;G), \qquad B=\mathcal B\gamma_0 . \tag{BR1}

Here ℬ∈Ψphg0(Y;E|Y,G)\mathcal B\in\Psi_{\mathrm{phg}}^0(Y;E|_Y,G). Write pp for the principal symbol of PP. Ellipticity at a nonzero conormal makes

c(y,t)=p(y,t,0,dt):E(y,t)→F(y,t)invertible on a sufficiently small collar.(BR2) c(y,t)=p(y,t,0,dt):E_{(y,t)}\longrightarrow F_{(y,t)} \quad\text{invertible on a sufficiently small collar.} \tag{BR2}

After using cc to identify the target with the source in that collar, the boundary principal symbol has the form

p̃(y,0,η,ζ)=ζIE−a(y,η),q(y,η)=12πi∫Γ+(zIE−a(y,η))−1dz.(BR3) \widetilde p(y,0,\eta,\zeta)=\zeta I_E-a(y,\eta),\qquad q(y,\eta)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma_+} (zI_E-a(y,\eta))^{-1}\,dz . \tag{BR3}

The contour surrounds the spectrum of a(y,η)a(y,\eta) in the upper half-plane. The stable Cauchy bundle is L=ran⁡qL=\operatorname{ran}q over T*Y\0T^*Y\setminus0. If b=σ0(ℬ)b=\sigma_0(\mathcal B), the complementing condition is precisely that b|L:L→π*Gb|_L:L\to\pi^*G be a bundle isomorphism.

Stable reduction theorem. There is a bundle H→XH\to X, a zero-index auxiliary first-order problem on HH, and a continuous Fredholm deformation of the stabilized problem to a split bundle-trace problem. If (Psp,Bsp)(P_{\mathrm{sp}},B_{\mathrm{sp}}) is the endpoint and P̂sp\widehat P_{\mathrm{sp}} is its geometric double, then

ind⁡(P,B)=ind⁡(Psp,Bsp)=ind⁡P̂sp=sind⁡(p̂sp).(BR4) \boxed{ \operatorname{ind}(P,B) =\operatorname{ind}(P_{\mathrm{sp}},B_{\mathrm{sp}}) =\operatorname{ind}\widehat P_{\mathrm{sp}} =\operatorname{sind}(\widehat p_{\mathrm{sp}}).} \tag{BR4}

The theorem assumes that the original complementing operator BB exists. Section 10 records the exact symbol-bundle obstruction exposed when no such BB exists.

2. Normal coefficient normalization and collar freezing

Choose a collar Y×[0,δ)tY\times[0,\delta)_t. The isomorphism c−1:F|Y×[0,δ)→E|Y×[0,δ)c^{-1}:F|_{Y\times[0,\delta)}\to E|_{Y\times[0,\delta)} is used as a collar frame for the target. This is a change of bundle coordinates, so it does not alter the operator, its domain, its kernel, or its cokernel. In this target frame the collar operator can be written

Pb=Dt−A(t)+R0(t),σ1(Pb)(y,t,η,ζ)=ζIE−a(y,t,η),(BR5) P^b=D_t-A(t)+R_0(t), \qquad \sigma_1(P^b)(y,t,\eta,\zeta)=\zeta I_E-a(y,t,\eta), \tag{BR5}

where A(t)A(t) is tangential of order one and R0(t)R_0(t) has order zero. No factor of the original normal coefficient has been discarded: it remains the target identification cc. Every normalized symbol deformation below is transported back to FF by left multiplication with cc, so the global target bundle stays fixed.

Let χ∈Cc∞([0,δ))\chi\in C_c^\infty([0,\delta)), with χ=1\chi=1 for 0≤t≤δ/30\leq t\leq\delta/3 and χ=0\chi=0 for t≥2δ/3t\geq2\delta/3. For 0≤r≤10\leq r\leq1, define

ar(y,t,η)=a(y,t,η)+rχ(t)(a(y,0,η)−a(y,t,η)).(BR6) a_r(y,t,\eta) =a(y,t,\eta)+r\chi(t)\bigl(a(y,0,\eta)-a(y,t,\eta)\bigr). \tag{BR6}

This is a deformation of the full original principal symbol, not a replacement by a rescaled symbol. To prove ellipticity, restrict to the compact collar cosphere |η|2+ζ2=1|\eta|^2+\zeta^2=1. Since the original symbol is invertible there, there is M<∞M<\infty such that

sup⁡∥(ζIE−a(y,t,η))−1∥≤M.(BR7) \sup\|(\zeta I_E-a(y,t,\eta))^{-1}\|\leq M . \tag{BR7}

Uniform continuity gives, after decreasing δ\delta,

∥rχ(t)(a(y,0,η)−a(y,t,η))∥<12Mon the collar cosphere.(BR8) \|r\chi(t)(a(y,0,\eta)-a(y,t,\eta))\| <\frac{1}{2M} \quad\text{on the collar cosphere.} \tag{BR8}

Factor the deformed symbol on the right of the original inverse:

ζIE−ar=(ζIE−a)[IE−(ζIE−a)−1rχ(t)(a(y,0,η)−a(y,t,η))].(BR9) \zeta I_E-a_r =(\zeta I_E-a) \left[I_E-(\zeta I_E-a)^{-1} r\chi(t)(a(y,0,\eta)-a(y,t,\eta))\right]. \tag{BR9}

The bracket is invertible by its Neumann series. Thus every ara_r is elliptic. At t=0t=0, the symbol is always a(y,0,η)a(y,0,\eta), so the stable bundle and b|Lb|_L do not change. Transporting this principal deformation back by cc, and interpolating the lower-order terms, gives an operator-norm continuous Fredholm path on the original source and target bundles. At r=1r=1, the tangential operator is independent of tt on the smaller collar. All cutoff-transition terms are supported where t≥δ/3t\geq\delta/3.

3. The smooth stable projection and the fixed-projection collapse

For a fixed (y0,η0)(y_0,\eta_0), choose a contour Γ+\Gamma_+ that separates the upper and lower spectra of a(y0,η0)a(y_0,\eta_0). Invertibility on this compact contour persists in a parameter neighborhood. Differentiating the resolvent gives

∂ν(zI−a)−1=(zI−a)−1(∂νa)(zI−a)−1.(BR10) \partial_\nu(zI-a)^{-1} =(zI-a)^{-1}(\partial_\nu a)(zI-a)^{-1}. \tag{BR10}

Repeated differentiation under the contour integral proves that qq is smooth without choosing eigenvalue branches. Two local contours give the same projection on their overlap because both enclose exactly the upper spectrum. Positive homogeneity follows by scaling both the symbol and the contour:

q(y,ρη)=12πi∫ρΓ+(zI−ρa(y,η))−1dz=q(y,η),ρ>0.(BR11) q(y,\rho\eta) =\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\rho\Gamma_+} (zI-\rho a(y,\eta))^{-1}\,dz =q(y,\eta),\qquad \rho>0 . \tag{BR11}

Choose Q∈Ψphg0(Y;E|Y)Q\in\Psi_{\mathrm{phg}}^0(Y;E|_Y) with principal symbol qq, and choose Λ∈Ψphg1(Y;E|Y)\Lambda\in\Psi_{\mathrm{phg}}^1(Y;E|_Y) with positive scalar principal symbol λ(y,η)IE\lambda(y,\eta)I_E. The operator QQ need not satisfy Q2=QQ^2=Q; only its principal symbol enters the following ellipticity calculation.

At the symbol level define, for 0≤r≤10\leq r\leq1,

a⟨r⟩=(1−r)a+irλ(2q−IE).(BR12) a^{\langle r\rangle} =(1-r)a+i r\lambda(2q-I_E). \tag{BR12}

The contour formula makes qq a holomorphic function of aa, so aq=qaaq=qa. On the primary summand for an eigenvalue ξ\xi, with nilpotent part NξN_\xi, the restriction of (BR12) is

((1−r)ξ+irλsgn(Imξ))I+(1−r)Nξ.(BR13) \left((1-r)\xi+i r\lambda\, \operatorname{sgn}(\operatorname{Im}\xi)\right)I +(1-r)N_\xi . \tag{BR13}

Its only eigenvalue stays strictly in the same half-plane as ξ\xi. Therefore every a⟨r⟩a^{\langle r\rangle} has no real eigenvalue, and its upper spectral projection is exactly qq. This primary-block calculation is the needed proof; convexity of spectra of arbitrary nonnormal matrices would not suffice.

Let ϕ(t)=1\phi(t)=1 on a smaller collar and have support inside the region where A(t)=A(0)A(t)=A(0). Quantize the deformation by

Ar=(1−rϕ)A+rϕ(iΛQ−iΛ(IE−Q)).(BR14) A_r=(1-r\phi)A +r\phi\bigl(i\Lambda Q-i\Lambda(I_E-Q)\bigr). \tag{BR14}

Its principal tangential symbol is (BR12) with rr replaced by rϕ(t)r\phi(t). Hence Dt−ArD_t-A_r remains elliptic, its stable projection at the boundary remains qq, and the original ℬ\mathcal B remains complementing throughout. The exact order ΛQ\Lambda Q and Λ(IE−Q)\Lambda(I_E-Q) has been retained.

4. Replacing the interior partial operators

In the annulus where a collar cutoff varies, a tangential operator acts in yy while tt is only a parameter. This is a partial pseudodifferential operator. Its support stays away from YY, so it can be approximated by ordinary operators without changing any boundary symbol.

For a tangential order-one symbol k(y,t,η)k(y,t,\eta), let ω(η,εζ)\omega(\eta,\varepsilon\zeta) be the angular cutoff from the partial-operator approximation theorem, and set

kε(y,t,η,ζ)=k(y,t,η)ω(η,εζ).(BR15) k_\varepsilon(y,t,\eta,\zeta) =k(y,t,\eta)\omega(\eta,\varepsilon\zeta). \tag{BR15}

After multiplying on both sides by the fixed annular cutoff, KεK_\varepsilon is an ordinary full-variable operator and, for every real ss,

∥Kε−K∥Hs→Hs−1≤Csε.(BR16) \|K_\varepsilon-K\|_{H^s\to H^{s-1}} \leq C_s\varepsilon . \tag{BR16}

The estimates are uniform in the compact deformation parameter because only finitely many uniform symbol seminorms occur. The image of the parameter interval is a compact subset of the Fredholm locus. A finite operator-norm neighborhood cover therefore supplies one positive tolerance for the entire path. Choose ε\varepsilon below that tolerance. The ordinary approximating path is Fredholm and has the same index at every parameter. This step changes no collar principal symbol, no boundary map, and no stable bundle.

5. The inverse boundary symbol and its rotation

Let b:π*E→π*Gb:\pi^*E\to\pi^*G be the order-zero principal boundary symbol. Its restriction b|L:L→π*Gb|_L:L\to\pi^*G is a smooth homogeneous bundle isomorphism. Define

s:π*G→π*E,s=(b|L)−1,with image in L.(BR17) s:\pi^*G\longrightarrow\pi^*E,\qquad s=(b|_L)^{-1}, \quad\text{with image in }L. \tag{BR17}

bs=IG,qs=s,sbq=q.(BR18) bs=I_G,\qquad qs=s,\qquad sbq=q . \tag{BR18}

The last identity follows because qe∈Lqe\in L and ss is the inverse of bb there. Expressions such as qbqb or sqsq are not used because their source and target bundles do not match.

6. Extending the boundary bundle and adding zero index

The bundle G→YG\to Y has a smooth complement G′G' with G⊕G′≅Y×ℂNG\oplus G'\cong Y\times\mathbb C^N. This follows from a finite trivializing cover and the explicit embedding-and-orthogonal-complement construction in the Bott lesson. Take

H=X×ℂN,H|Y→κG⊕G′.(BR23) H=X\times\mathbb C^N,\qquad H|_Y\xrightarrow{\ \kappa\ }G\oplus G'. \tag{BR23}

Let qG:H|Y→Gq_G:H|_Y\to G and ιG:G→H|Y\iota_G:G\to H|_Y be the projection and inclusion supplied by this splitting, and put

ΠG=ιGqG,ΠG2=ΠG,H|Y=ran⁡ΠG⊕ker⁡ΠG.(BR24) \Pi_G=\iota_Gq_G,\qquad \Pi_G^2=\Pi_G,\qquad H|_Y=\operatorname{ran}\Pi_G\oplus\ker\Pi_G . \tag{BR24}

Choose an order-one operator ΛH\Lambda_H, block diagonal near YY for G⊕G′G\oplus G', with the same positive scalar principal symbol λ(y,η)IH\lambda(y,\eta)I_H as Λ\Lambda. Form the split auxiliary problem whose collar part is

PHb=Dt+iΛH,BH=0.(BR25) P_H^b=D_t+i\Lambda_H,\qquad B_H=0 . \tag{BR25}

Complete it elliptically in the interior as in the split-model theorem. Its stable boundary space is zero, its boundary target is the zero bundle, and that theorem gives

ind⁡(PH,0)=0,ind⁡((P,B)⊕(PH,0))=ind⁡(P,B).(BR26) \operatorname{ind}(P_H,0)=0,\qquad \operatorname{ind}\bigl((P,B)\oplus(P_H,0)\bigr) =\operatorname{ind}(P,B). \tag{BR26}

This is the only stabilization used below. It changes no index.

7. The full two-by-two normal homotopy

8. Ellipticity, boundary bijectivity, and index tracking

The principal tangential generator in (BR31) is

𝔰τg=(cos⁡τsgsin⁡τιGg).(BR34) \mathfrak s_\tau g =\binom{\cos\tau\,sg}{\sin\tau\,\iota_Gg}. \tag{BR34}

(Pτ,Bτ)1:H1(X;E⊕H)→L2(X;F⊕H)⊕H1/2(Y;G)Fredholm.(BR36) (P_\tau,B_\tau)_1: H^1(X;E\oplus H)\longrightarrow L^2(X;F\oplus H)\oplus H^{1/2}(Y;G) \quad\text{Fredholm}. \tag{BR36}

The ordinary interior approximations from Section 4 can be made uniformly along this path. They preserve (BR37), because they lie in one fixed Fredholm neighborhood and are supported away from the boundary symbol.

9. The endpoint is the split trace model
