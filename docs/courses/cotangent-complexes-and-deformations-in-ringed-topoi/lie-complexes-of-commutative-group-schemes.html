Lie complexes of commutative group schemes

Written by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic) on 21 October 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. Public domain (CC0).

Let \(G\) be a group scheme over \(S\), flat and locally of finite presentation. Conjugation makes \(G\) act on its co-Lie complex \(\ell_G\), and this coadjoint action governs how the co-Lie complex of a \(G\)-torsor is twisted (The adjoint co-Lie complex of a group scheme). When \(G\) is commutative the action is trivial in the strongest sense: the co-Lie complex of every \(G\)-torsor, over a scheme or over a diagram of schemes, is the pullback of \(\ell_G\), and the adjoint co-Lie complex is \(\ell_G\) with the trivial action. The proof is a shear argument. For a torsor over a diagram, the isomorphism \((g,x)\mapsto(gx,x)\) identifies the cotangent complex with a pullback of \(\ell_G\); when \(G\) is commutative, \(G\) acts on the nerve of the action as well, and the same argument on the nerve computes the descended co-Lie complex.

The second part of the lesson treats group schemes over diagrams of schemes: families of group schemes linked by homomorphisms. Their co-Lie complexes and Lie complexes are defined levelwise, the shear identifies their cotangent complexes, and for commutative groups the co-Lie complex is additive: a sum of homomorphisms induces the sum of the maps. Additivity computes the Lie complexes of two diagrams that are built from a commutative group scheme \(G\) by additions alone. For the nerve of \(G\), the Lie complex is the nerve of the Lie complex of \(G\). For the spectrum of \(G\), a diagram of type \(C_\bullet(S_1)\) whose values are products of copies of \(G\), the Lie complex is the spectrum of the Lie complex of \(G\). The second computation is the input for the Lie complexes of module schemes, on which a ring scheme acts as well.

Conventions

\(S\) is a scheme, \(T\) the topos of sheaves on \((\mathrm{Sch}/S)_{\mathrm{fppf}}\) with its structure ring \(\mathcal O\), and \(\varepsilon\) the comparison morphisms between big fppf and Zariski topoi. Group schemes over \(S\), or over an \(S\)-scheme, are flat and locally of finite presentation. Diagrams are diagrams of \(S\)-schemes, with their morphisms to \(S\). Morphisms of diagrams of a given type, Zariski topoi, cotangent complexes of morphisms of diagrams and functoriality maps are those of Deformations of diagrams of schemes; the fppf topoi \(\operatorname{Top}(X_{\mathrm{fppf}})\) and \(\operatorname{Top}^{\mathrm o}(X_{\mathrm{fppf}})\) of a diagram \(X\), and \(\varepsilon\colon\operatorname{Top}(X_{\mathrm{fppf}})\to\operatorname{Top}(X_{\mathrm{Zar}})\), are those of Construction 4.1 of Quasi-coherent and perfect complexes on big fppf sites.

Functoriality maps of squares of diagrams. Let \(f\circ a=b\circ f_1\) be a commutative square of morphisms of diagrams, of any types, with \(a\colon X_1\to X\), \(b\colon Y_1\to Y\), \(f\colon X\to Y\) and \(f_1\colon X_1\to Y_1\). The ring maps give a map of ring pairs from \(a^{-1}(f^{-1}\mathcal O_Y\to\mathcal O_X)\) to \(f_1^{-1}\mathcal O_{Y_1}\to\mathcal O_{X_1}\) on \(\operatorname{Top}(X_{1,\mathrm{Zar}})\), and as for schemes it gives the functoriality map \(La^*L_{X/Y}\to L_{X_1/Y_1}\) (Theorem 2.2 of The cotangent complex). Restriction to a level is the inverse image of a morphism of ringed topoi and preserves the polynomial constructions, so the level \(i\) of this map is the functoriality map of the square of schemes at \(i\) (Proposition 1.2 (1) of the lesson on deformations of diagrams). A map in the derived category of the Zariski topos of a diagram is an isomorphism if all its levels are. So if the square is cartesian at every level and \(f\) is flat at every level, its functoriality map is an isomorphism (Proposition 1.4 there). Functoriality maps compose, and the functoriality map of a square whose horizontal sides are isomorphisms is an isomorphism. For a morphism of diagrams \(g\colon X\to Y\) of type \(u\) and \(K\in D^-(Y_{\mathrm{Zar}})\), \(Lg^*K\) has the levels \(Lg_i^*K_{u(i)}\), by the argument of Lemma 4.2 (1) of the lesson on big fppf sites, and derived pullbacks compose [Stacks, Tag 0D6D].

Constant diagrams. For a scheme \(Z\) and a small category \(I\), \(Z_I\) is the constant diagram with value \(Z\) and identity maps. A module of \(\operatorname{Top}((Z_I)_{\mathrm{Zar}})\) is a family of \(\mathcal O_Z\)-modules \(M_i\) with maps \(M_j\to M_i\) for the arrows \(i\to j\) of \(I\), and a module of \(\operatorname{Top}^{\mathrm o}((Z_I)_{\mathrm{fppf}})\) is a family of \(\mathcal O\)-modules \(M_i\) of \(T/Z\) with maps \(M_i\to M_j\). For \(I=\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\), the modules of \(\operatorname{Top}((Z_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}})_{\mathrm{Zar}})\) are the cosimplicial \(\mathcal O_Z\)-modules and those of \(\operatorname{Top}^{\mathrm o}((Z_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}})_{\mathrm{fppf}})\) the simplicial \(\mathcal O\)-modules of \(T/Z\). The inverse image along the morphism \(Z_I\to Z\) of type \(I\to\{\ast\}\) is the constant functor \(c\).

Duals. For a diagram \(Y\) and a module \(M\) of \(T\), \(M_Y\) is the module with the levels \(M|_{Y_i}\) and identity maps; \(\mathcal O_Y\) is the ring of both fppf topoi of \(Y\). The functor \(R\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}^{\mathrm o}\colon\) \(D(\operatorname{Top}(Y_{\mathrm{fppf}}))^{\mathrm{op}}\times D^+(\operatorname{Top}^{\mathrm o}(Y_{\mathrm{fppf}}))\to\) \(D(\operatorname{Top}^{\mathrm o}(Y_{\mathrm{fppf}}))\) is that of Definition 1.4 of A duality formula for diagrams in a topos, for the diagram of \(T\) defined by \(Y\). By Lemma 1.5 there, \(R\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}^{\mathrm o}(E,\mathcal O_Y)\) is \(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}^{\mathrm o}(E,J_Y)\) for a bounded below complex \(J\) of injective \(\mathcal O\)-modules of \(T\) resolving \(\mathcal O\); its level \(i\) is \(R\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(E_i,\mathcal O)\), and its map along \(\alpha\colon i\to j\) is the composition with the structure map \(Y_\alpha^*E_j\to E_i\) of \(E\) (Definition 1.1 there).

Co-Lie complexes \(\ell_G\), co-Lie modules \(\omega_G\) and the maps \(\ell(u)\) of homomorphisms are those of The co-Lie complex of a group scheme. Torsors over diagrams, the morphism \(a\colon N(G,X)\to Y\), the co-Lie complex \(\ell_{X/Y}\) of a torsor over a diagram, the universal torsor \(P=N(G,G)\to N=N(G)\), the classifying category \(B=B[G,S]^{\mathrm o}\) and the adjoint co-Lie complex \(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G\in D^b(B)\) are those of The adjoint co-Lie complex of a group scheme.

1. Group schemes over diagrams

Definition 1.1. Let \(Y\colon I\to(\mathrm{Sch}/S)\) be a diagram. A group scheme over \(Y\) is a diagram \(H\colon I\to(\mathrm{Sch}/S)\) with an \(I\)-morphism \(f\colon H\to Y\) and, for every object \(i\), a structure of group scheme over \(Y_i\) on \(f_i\colon H_i\to Y_i\), such that for every arrow \(\alpha\colon i\to j\) the morphism \(H_\alpha\colon H_i\to H_j\) is a homomorphism over \(Y_\alpha\): it carries the unit and the multiplication of \(H_i\) to those of \(H_j\). It is commutative if every \(H_i\) is. A homomorphism \(u\colon H\to H'\) of group schemes over \(Y\) is an \(I\)-morphism over \(Y\) whose components are homomorphisms. The units \(e_i\) and the multiplications form \(I\)-morphisms \(e\colon Y\to H\) and \(m\colon H\times_YH\to H\), where \(H\times_YH\) is the levelwise fibre product.

The co-Lie complex of \(H\) is \(\ell_H=Le^*L_{H/Y}\in D(Y_{\mathrm{Zar}})\), and its Lie complex is \[ \begin{aligned}\ell^!_H&=R\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}^{\mathrm o}(L\varepsilon^*\ell_H,\mathcal O_Y)\\&\in D(\operatorname{Top}^{\mathrm o}(Y_{\mathrm{fppf}})).\end{aligned} \] A homomorphism \(u\colon H\to H'\) gives \(\ell(u)\colon\ell_{H'}\to\ell_H\), the image under \(Le^*\) of the functoriality map \(Lu^*L_{H'/Y}\to L_{H/Y}\), through \(Le^*Lu^*=Le'^*\), and \(\ell^!(u)\colon\ell^!_H\to\ell^!_{H'}\), the map induced by \(L\varepsilon^*\ell(u)\).

Examples 1.2. (a) For a group scheme \(G\) over \(S\) and a diagram \(Y\), the diagram \(G_Y\) with the levels \(G\times_SY_i\) and the maps \(\mathrm{id}\times Y_\alpha\) is a group scheme over \(Y\).

(b) Let \(G\) be a commutative group scheme over \(S\). Its nerve \(N(G)\), with \(N(G)_n=G^n\), the fibre product over \(S\), has faces and degeneracies that are homomorphisms (Section 3 of Multisimplicial objects and spectral diagrams). So \(N(G)\) is a commutative group scheme over \(S_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}\).

(c) For \(G\) as in (b), let \(G(1)\) be the spectrum of the commutative group object \(G\) of the category of \(S\)-schemes (Section 3 there) and \(C_\bullet(G(1))\) the corresponding diagram of type \(C_\bullet(S_1)\) (Definition 2.1 of Graded monoids and the nerves of their actions). Its values are products of copies of \(G\) and its maps are homomorphisms (Lemma 3.2). So \(C_\bullet(G(1))\) is a commutative group scheme over \(S_K\), where \(K=\int C_\bullet(S_1)\).

Proposition 1.3 (levels). Let \(f\colon H\to Y\) be a group scheme over a diagram \(Y\).

  1. The level \(i\) of \(\ell_H\) is \(\ell_{H_i}\), perfect with tor amplitude in \([-1,0]\). Its structure map along \(\alpha\colon i\to j\) is the image under \(Le_i^*\) of the functoriality map of the square formed by \(H_\alpha\), \(Y_\alpha\), \(f_i\) and \(f_j\), through \(Le_i^*LH_\alpha^*=LY_\alpha^*Le_j^*\); it is an isomorphism if this square is cartesian. For a homomorphism \(u\), the level \(i\) of \(\ell(u)\) is \(\ell(u_i)\).
  2. The level \(i\) of \(\ell^!_H\) is \(R\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(L\varepsilon^*\ell_{H_i},\mathcal O)\), perfect with tor amplitude in \([0,1]\). Its map along \(\alpha\) is induced by the structure map of \(L\varepsilon^*\ell_H\) along \(\alpha\), and it is an isomorphism if the square of (1) is cartesian. The level \(i\) of \(\ell^!(u)\) is induced by \(L\varepsilon^*\ell(u_i)\).
  3. For a functor \(v\colon I'\to I\), \(H\circ v\) is a group scheme over \(Y\circ v\), and \(\ell_{H\circ v}\) and \(\ell^!_{H\circ v}\) are the restrictions of \(\ell_H\) and \(\ell^!_H\) along \(v\).

Proof. (1) By Proposition 1.2 (1) of the lesson on deformations of diagrams, \(L_{H/Y}\) has the levels \(L_{H_i/Y_i}\), with the functoriality maps as structure maps. It is a complex of flat modules, so \(Le^*L_{H/Y}=e^*L_{H/Y}\) has the levels \(e_i^*L_{H_i/Y_i}=\ell_{H_i}\) and the stated structure maps. Perfectness is Proposition 1.2 (1) of the lesson on the co-Lie complex. If the square is cartesian, the structure map is the isomorphism \(LY_\alpha^*\ell_{H_j}\cong\ell_{H_i}\) of Proposition 1.4 (2) there, \(H_j\) being flat over \(Y_j\). The levels of \(\ell(u)\) are computed by the levels of the functoriality map (Conventions).

(2) \(L\varepsilon^*\ell_H\) has the levels \(L\varepsilon^*\ell_{H_i}\) (Lemma 4.2 (1) of the lesson on big fppf sites), perfect with tor amplitude in \([-1,0]\) (Theorem 3.1 there). The description of the levels and maps is that of the Conventions. The dual of a perfect complex is perfect [Stacks, Tag 08JJ], and locally it is the dual complex of a complex of finite free modules in degrees \(-1\) and \(0\). Composition with an isomorphism is invertible.

(3) The diagram \(Y\circ v\) maps to \(Y\) by the morphism of type \(v\) with identity components, and its inverse image is the restriction along \(v\). By the Conventions of the lesson on the adjoint co-Lie complex, it carries \(L_{H/Y}\) to \(L_{H\circ v/Y\circ v}\). It commutes with \(Le^*\) and \(L\varepsilon^*\), and with \(R\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}^{\mathrm o}\) by Lemma 3.1 of the lesson on the duality formula. \(\square\)

Proposition 1.4 (the shear). Let \(f\colon H\to Y\) be a group scheme over a diagram \(Y\).

  1. There is an isomorphism \(\psi_H\colon L_{H/Y}\to f^*\ell_H\) in \(D(H_{\mathrm{Zar}})\), composed of functoriality maps, their inverses and identifications of derived pullbacks.
  2. For a homomorphism \(u\colon H\to H'\) over \(Y\), with \(f'\colon H'\to Y\), the square formed by \(\psi_H\), \(Lu^*\psi_{H'}\), the functoriality map \(Lu^*L_{H'/Y}\to L_{H/Y}\) and \(f^*\ell(u)\colon f^*\ell_{H'}=Lu^*f'^*\ell_{H'}\to f^*\ell_H\) commutes.

Proof. (1) Let \(p_1,p_2\colon H\times_YH\to H\) be the projections and \(\sigma\colon H\times_YH\to H\times_YH\) the shear, given on points by \(\sigma(g,h)=(g,gh)\). It is an \(I\)-morphism because the maps \(H_\alpha\) are homomorphisms, and an isomorphism, with inverse \((g,h)\mapsto(g,g^{-1}h)\). Moreover \(p_1\sigma=p_1\) and \(p_2\sigma=m\). Let \(L_1\) be the cotangent complex of \(p_1\). The square formed by \(p_2\), \(f\), \(p_1\) and \(f\) is cartesian at every level and \(f\) is flat, so its functoriality map \(\varphi\colon Lp_2^*L_{H/Y}\to L_1\) is an isomorphism (Conventions). The square formed by \(\sigma\), \(\mathrm{id}_H\) and \(p_1\) twice has an invertible functoriality map \(\varphi_\sigma\colon L\sigma^*L_1\to L_1\), since \(\sigma\) is an isomorphism. So \[ \begin{aligned}\psi'\colon&Lm^*L_{H/Y}=L\sigma^*Lp_2^*L_{H/Y}\\&\xrightarrow{\ L\sigma^*\varphi\ }L\sigma^*L_1\xrightarrow{\ \varphi_\sigma\ }L_1\\&\xrightarrow{\ \varphi^{-1}\ }Lp_2^*L_{H/Y}\end{aligned} \] is an isomorphism. Let \(s=(\mathrm{id},ef)\colon H\to H\times_YH\), so that \(ms=\mathrm{id}\) and \(p_2s=ef\). Then \(\psi_H=Ls^*\psi'\) goes from \(Ls^*Lm^*L_{H/Y}=L_{H/Y}\) to \(Ls^*Lp_2^*L_{H/Y}=Lf^*Le^*L_{H/Y}=f^*\ell_H\) [Stacks, Tag 0D6D], \(f\) being flat at every level.

(2) The morphism \(u\times_Yu\) commutes with \(p_1\), \(p_2\), \(\sigma\), \(m\) and \(s\), because \(u\) is a homomorphism. Functoriality maps compose, so the functoriality maps of the squares formed by \(u\), \(u\times_Yu\) and these morphisms are compatible with \(\varphi\), \(\varphi_\sigma\) and the identifications of derived pullbacks. Pulling back along \(s\) gives the square. \(\square\)

Lemma 1.5 (products and sums). Let \(H\) and \(H'\) be group schemes over \(Y\), with structure morphisms \(f\), \(f'\) and units \(e\), \(e'\).

  1. A homomorphism that factors through the unit, such as \(e'f\colon H\to H'\), induces \(0\).
  2. Let \(p\), \(p'\) be the projections of \(H\times_YH'\), \(\iota=(\mathrm{id},e'f)\) and \(\iota'=(ef',\mathrm{id})\). Then \(\ell(p)+\ell(p')\colon\ell_H\oplus\ell_{H'}\to\ell_{H\times_YH'}\) is an isomorphism, with inverse \((\ell(\iota),\ell(\iota'))\).
  3. If \(H\) is commutative, then \(\ell(u+v)=\ell(u)+\ell(v)\) for homomorphisms \(u,v\colon H''\to H\), where \(u+v=m\circ(u,v)\). In particular \(\ell(-u)=-\ell(u)\).
  4. Let \(H\) be commutative, \(P\) and \(P'\) finite sets and \(n=(n_{p'p})\in\mathbb Z^{P'\times P}\). Let \(H^P\) be the fibre product over \(Y\) of copies of \(H\) indexed by \(P\), and \(n_H\colon H^P\to H^{P'}\) the homomorphism \((g_p)_p\mapsto(\sum_pn_{p'p}g_p)_{p'}\). Under the isomorphisms \(\ell_{H^P}\cong\ell_H^{\oplus P}\) of (2), \(\ell(n_H)\colon\ell_H^{\oplus P'}\to\ell_H^{\oplus P}\) maps the summand \(p'\) to the summand \(p\) by multiplication by \(n_{p'p}\).

The same statements hold for \(\ell^!\), with the arrows reversed: \(\ell^!(n_H)\) maps the summand \(p\) to the summand \(p'\) by \(n_{p'p}\).

Proof. (1) The trivial group \(Y\) over \(Y\) has \(L_{Y/Y}=0\), the identity being étale (Proposition 5.1 of The cotangent complex), so \(\ell_Y=0\), and \(\ell(vw)=\ell(w)\ell(v)\) (Proposition 1.3 of the lesson on the co-Lie complex, at every level).

(2) At every level, Lemma 3.1 of the lesson on the adjoint co-Lie complex, over \(Y_i\) in place of \(S\), shows that the sum of the functoriality maps of \(p_i\) and \(p'_i\) is an isomorphism \(Lp_i^*L_{H_i/Y_i}\oplus Lp_i'^*L_{H'_i/Y_i}\cong L_{H_i\times H'_i/Y_i}\). Its image under \(Le_i^*\), for the unit \((e_i,e'_i)\), is the level \(i\) of \(\ell(p)+\ell(p')\), which is therefore an isomorphism. Further \(\ell(\iota)\ell(p)=\ell(p\iota)=\mathrm{id}\) and \(\ell(\iota)\ell(p')=\ell(p'\iota)=\ell(e'f)=0\) by (1), and symmetrically for \(\iota'\). So \((\ell(\iota),\ell(\iota'))\) is a left inverse of an isomorphism, hence its inverse.

(3) For \(H'=H\), the multiplication \(m\) is a homomorphism, \(H\) being commutative, and \(m\iota=m\iota'=\mathrm{id}\). So \((\ell(\iota),\ell(\iota'))\circ\ell(m)=(\mathrm{id},\mathrm{id})\), and by (2), \(\ell(m)=\ell(p)+\ell(p')\). With \(w=(u,v)\colon H''\to H\times_YH\), which is a homomorphism with \(pw=u\) and \(p'w=v\), \[ \begin{aligned}\ell(u+v)&=\ell(w)\ell(m)\\&=\ell(w)\ell(p)+\ell(w)\ell(p')\\&=\ell(u)+\ell(v).\end{aligned} \] By (1), \(\ell(u)+\ell(-u)=\ell(0)=0\).

(4) The composite of \(n_H\) with the projection \(\mathrm{pr}_{p'}\) of \(H^{P'}\) is \(\sum_pn_{p'p}\mathrm{pr}_p\). By (3), \(\ell(n_H)\ell(\mathrm{pr}_{p'})=\sum_pn_{p'p}\ell(\mathrm{pr}_p)\), and by (2), applied inductively to products of several factors, the maps \(\ell(\mathrm{pr}_p)\) are the inclusions of the summands. For \(\ell^!\), apply \(R\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}^{\mathrm o}(L\varepsilon^*(-),\mathcal O_Y)\), which is additive and carries direct sums to direct sums. \(\square\)

Proposition 1.6 (the tensor formula). Let \(H\) be a group scheme over a diagram \(Y\) and \(E=L\varepsilon^*\ell_H\). For \(M\in D^+(\operatorname{Top}^{\mathrm o}(Y_{\mathrm{fppf}}))\) there is an isomorphism, natural in \(M\), \[ \ell^!_H\otimes^{\mathbf L}M\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }R\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}^{\mathrm o}(E,M). \] Here \(\otimes^{\mathbf L}\) is the derived tensor product of the ringed topos \(\operatorname{Top}^{\mathrm o}(Y_{\mathrm{fppf}})\); its level \(i\) is the derived tensor product of the levels. In particular, for \(N\in D^+(T)\), \(R\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}^{\mathrm o}(E,N_Y)\cong\ell^!_H\otimes^{\mathbf L}N_Y\).

Proof. Let \(P\to M\) be a quasi-isomorphism from a K-flat complex with flat terms [Stacks, Tag 06YS]. Its levels \(P_i\) are K-flat. Indeed, for a level \(i\) let \(i^{\mathrm o}_!\) be the exact left adjoint of the level functor, with \((i^{\mathrm o}_!Q)_j=\bigoplus_{f\colon i\to j}f_!Q\) (Proposition 4.4 (b) of Morphisms of fibred topoi and modules on total topoi). For an acyclic complex \(A\) of modules of the level \(i\), \(i^{\mathrm o}_!A\) is acyclic, so \(i^{\mathrm o}_!A\otimes P\) is acyclic, and its level \(i\), tensor products being levelwise, contains \(A\otimes P_i\) as the direct summand indexed by the identity of \(i\); so \(A\otimes P_i\) is acyclic. Hence the levels \(P_i\) compute the derived tensor products of the levels. Let \(J_{\mathcal O}\) and \(J_M\) be bounded below complexes of injective modules resolving \(\mathcal O_Y\) and \(M\); their levels are injective (Lemma 1.3 of the lesson on the duality formula). The map \(P\to J_M\) extends along the map \(P=\mathcal O_Y\otimes P\to J_{\mathcal O}\otimes P\), a quasi-isomorphism because \(P\) is K-flat, to a map \(J_{\mathcal O}\otimes P\to J_M\), unique up to homotopy, \(J_M\) being K-injective [Stacks, Tag 070J]. The map of the statement is the composite \[ \begin{aligned}&\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}^{\mathrm o}(E,J_{\mathcal O})\otimes P\\&\quad\to\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}^{\mathrm o}(E,J_{\mathcal O}\otimes P)\\&\quad\to\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}^{\mathrm o}(E,J_M),\end{aligned} \] where the first map sends \(\varphi\otimes x\) to \(y\mapsto\varphi(y)\otimes x\) on every level; it is compatible with the maps of the levels, since those of \(J_{\mathcal O}\otimes P\) are the tensor products of those of \(J_{\mathcal O}\) and \(P\). By Lemma 1.5 of the lesson on the duality formula, its level \(i\) is the canonical map \(R\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(E_i,\mathcal O)\otimes^{\mathbf L}M_i\to\) \(R\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(E_i,M_i)\), an isomorphism because \(E_i\) is perfect [Stacks, Tag 08JJ]. So the map is an isomorphism. For \(N\in D^+(T)\), take \(P=P'_Y\) for a K-flat complex \(P'\) of flat modules of \(T\) representing \(N\) [Tag 06YS]: the levels of \(P'_Y\), the restrictions of \(P'\) to the \(T/Y_i\), are K-flat [Tag 0G7E], so \(P'_Y\) is K-flat, tensor products being levelwise. \(\square\)

2. Torsors under commutative groups

Proposition 2.1 (the shear of a torsor). Let \(G\) be a group scheme over \(S\) and \(f\colon X\to Y\) a torsor under \(G\) over a diagram \(Y\) (Definition 1.1 of the lesson on the adjoint co-Lie complex), with \(\pi_X\colon X\to S\). There is an isomorphism \(L_{X/Y}\cong L\pi_X^*\ell_G\) in \(D(X_{\mathrm{Zar}})\).

Proof. Let \(G\times_SX\) be the diagram with the levels \(G\times_SX_i\), and \(\mathrm{act},\mathrm{pr}\colon G\times_SX\to X\) the action and the projection, \(I\)-morphisms because the maps \(X_\alpha\) are equivariant. The morphism \(\theta\colon G\times_SX\to X\times_YX\), \((g,x)\mapsto(gx,x)\), is an \(I\)-morphism and an isomorphism at every level (Definition 1.1 of the lesson on torsors), with \(q_1\theta=\mathrm{act}\) and \(q_2\theta=\mathrm{pr}\) for the projections \(q_1,q_2\) of \(X\times_YX\). Regard \(X\times_YX\) as a diagram over \(X\) through \(q_2\) and \(G\times_SX\) through \(\mathrm{pr}\). The following functoriality maps are isomorphisms, by the Conventions: that of the square formed by \(q_1\), \(f\), \(q_2\), \(f\), cartesian at every level with \(f\) flat (Proposition 1.3 (1) of the lesson on torsors), from \(Lq_1^*L_{X/Y}\) to the cotangent complex \(L_2\) of \(q_2\); that of \(\theta\), from \(L\theta^*L_2\) to the cotangent complex \(L_3\) of \(\mathrm{pr}\), \(\theta\) being an isomorphism over \(X\); and that of the square formed by the projection \(G\times_SX\to G\) of type \(I\to\{\ast\}\), the structure morphism of \(G\), \(\mathrm{pr}\) and \(\pi_X\), from the pullback of \(L_{G/S}\) to \(L_3\), cartesian at every level with \(G\) flat over \(S\). Together they give \[ \begin{aligned}&L\,\mathrm{act}^*L_{X/Y}=L\theta^*Lq_1^*L_{X/Y}\\&\quad\cong L\theta^*L_2\cong L_3\cong L\mathrm{pr}_G^*L_{G/S}.\end{aligned} \] Pull back along \(s=(e\pi_X,\mathrm{id})\colon X\to G\times_SX\), with \(\mathrm{act}\circ s=\mathrm{id}\) and \(\mathrm{pr}_G\circ s=e\pi_X\): \(L_{X/Y}\cong L\pi_X^*Le^*L_{G/S}=L\pi_X^*\ell_G\) [Stacks, Tag 0D6D]. \(\square\)

Theorem 2.2 (torsors under a commutative group). Let \(G\) be a commutative group scheme over \(S\), \(f\colon X\to Y\) a torsor under \(G\) over a diagram \(Y\colon I\to(\mathrm{Sch}/S)\), and \(g\colon Y\to S\) the structure morphism. Then the co-Lie complex of the torsor is \[ \ell_{X/Y}\cong Lg^*\ell_G. \] In particular this holds for a \(G_Y\)-torsor over a scheme \(Y\).

Proof. Let \(G\) act on the levels \(N_n(G,X_i)=G^n\times_SX_i\) of the nerve \(N(G,X)\) by \(h\cdot(g;x)=(g;hx)\). The map of \(N(G,X)\) along an arrow \((\alpha,\gamma)\) sends \((g;x)\) to \((N(G)(\alpha)(g);X_\gamma(g_{\alpha(0)}\cdots g_1x))\), and \[ \begin{aligned}X_\gamma(g_{\alpha(0)}\cdots g_1hx)&=X_\gamma(hg_{\alpha(0)}\cdots g_1x)\\&=h\,X_\gamma(g_{\alpha(0)}\cdots g_1x),\end{aligned} \] because \(G\) is commutative and \(X_\gamma\) is equivariant. So the maps of \(N(G,X)\) are equivariant. The level \((n,i)\) of \(\varphi=N(G,f)\colon N(G,X)\to N(G,Y)\) is \(\mathrm{id}\times f_i\), the base change of \(f_i\) along \(G^n\times_SY_i\to Y_i\), a torsor by Lemma 1.2 (1) of the lesson on torsors, and it is invariant. So \(\varphi\) is a torsor under \(G\) over the diagram \(N(G,Y)\), indexed by \(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\times I\). By Proposition 2.1, \(L_{N(G,X)/N(G,Y)}\cong L\pi^*\ell_G\), where \(\pi\colon N(G,X)\to S\) is the structure morphism. Since \(\pi=g\circ a\), with \(a\) flat at every level (Lemma 1.2 (3) of the lesson on the adjoint co-Lie complex), \(L\pi^*\ell_G=a^*Lg^*\ell_G\). The object \(Lg^*\ell_G\) lies in \(D^b_{\mathrm{qc}}(Y_{\mathrm{Zar}})\), its levels being perfect with tor amplitude in \([-1,0]\). By Theorems 1.4 (1) and 1.5 (1) there, \(\ell_{X/Y}\) is the unique object of \(D^b_{\mathrm{qc}}(Y_{\mathrm{Zar}})\), up to isomorphism, whose image under \(a^*\) is isomorphic to \(L_{N(G,X)/N(G,Y)}\). So \(\ell_{X/Y}\cong Lg^*\ell_G\). \(\square\)

Corollary 2.3 (the adjoint co-Lie complex of a commutative group). Let \(G\) be commutative, so that \(G^{\mathrm o}=G\).

  1. The co-Lie complex of the universal torsor is \(\ell_{P/N}\cong\pi_N^*\ell_G\), where \(\pi_N\colon N\to S\).
  2. Let \(p^*\colon D^b(T)\to D^b(B)\) give a complex of \(\mathcal O\)-modules the trivial action. Then \(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G\cong p^*L\varepsilon^*\ell_G\).
  3. In particular \(G\) acts trivially on \(H^0(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G)=\varepsilon^*\omega_G\).

Proof. (1) \(P\to N\) is a torsor under \(G^{\mathrm o}=G\) over the simplicial scheme \(N\) (Construction 2.1 there), so Theorem 2.2 applies, with \(N\to S\) flat at every level. (2) For a module \(M\) of \(T\), \(p^*M\) is \(M\) with the identity as action, a module of \(B\); this is the functor of Proposition 6.1 of Cohomology of equivariant modules, for the simplicial topos of the nerve of \([G,S]^{\mathrm o}\), and it is exact. Its nerve has the levels \(\pi_n^*M\) and the canonical maps (proof of Proposition 6.1 there). So \(\operatorname{ner}(p^*L\varepsilon^*\ell_G)\cong\pi_N^*L\varepsilon^*\ell_G\), which is isomorphic to \(L\varepsilon^*\pi_N^*\ell_G\) [Stacks, Tag 03FH] and by (1) to \(L\varepsilon^*\ell_{P/N}\cong\operatorname{ner}(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G)\) (Proposition 2.2 (2) of the lesson on the adjoint co-Lie complex). The nerve is fully faithful on \(D^b(B)\) (Proposition 1.5 (3) of Equivariant cotangent complexes), so \(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G\cong p^*L\varepsilon^*\ell_G\). (3) follows from (2), \(p^*\) being exact. \(\square\)

On \(H^0\) this is the elementary fact that conjugation by a point of a commutative group is the identity, so that the coadjoint action \(c(\operatorname{int}(g))\) of the lesson on the adjoint co-Lie complex is trivial. Corollary 2.3 is stronger: the whole complex, with its action, is isomorphic to the trivial one. For a noncommutative group the conclusion fails already on \(H^0\) (Exercise 4.3).

3. Nerves and spectra

In this section \(G\) is a commutative group scheme over \(S\). For a complex \(M\) of \(\mathcal O\)-modules of \(T\), \(M\langle1\rangle\) is the complex of simplicial modules obtained by applying the nerve termwise, and \(C_\bullet(M(1))\) the complex of modules of type \(C_\bullet(S_1)\) obtained by applying the spectrum termwise. Both are formed with finite sums and maps given by integer matrices, so they are exact functors and act on derived categories: \(D(\mathcal O)\to D(\operatorname{Top}^{\mathrm o}((S_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}})_{\mathrm{fppf}}))\) and \(D(\mathcal O)\to D(\operatorname{Top}^{\mathrm o}((S_K)_{\mathrm{fppf}}))\). For a complex \(E\) of \(\mathcal O_S\)-modules let \(I(E)\) be the complex of cosimplicial modules with the levels \(E^{[n]}=\bigoplus_{k\in[n]}E\), on which \(\alpha\colon[m]\to[n]\) maps the summand \(j\) to the summand \(\alpha(j)\) by the identity, and \(\Sigma\colon I(E)\to cE\) the sum of the summands. Thus \(I(E)\) is the image of \(E\) under the left adjoint of the restriction to the level \(0\), and \(\Sigma\) is the adjunction map of \(cE\), whose level \(0\) is \(E\).

Proposition 3.1 (the nerve). Let \(N=N(G)\), a commutative group scheme over \(S_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}\), and let \(E\) be a bounded above complex of flat \(\mathcal O_S\)-modules representing \(\ell_G\).

  1. There is a distinguished triangle \(\ell_N\to I(E)\xrightarrow{\Sigma}cE\to\ell_N[1]\) in \(D((S_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}})_{\mathrm{Zar}})\). So \(\ell_N\) is isomorphic to the complex \(\ker\Sigma\), with the levels \(\{(x_k)\in E^{[n]}:\sum_kx_k=0\}\).
  2. There is an isomorphism \(\ell^!_N\cong(\ell^!_G)\langle1\rangle\) in \(D(\operatorname{Top}^{\mathrm o}((S_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}})_{\mathrm{fppf}}))\), where \(\ell^!_G=R\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(L\varepsilon^*\ell_G,\mathcal O)\).

Proof. (1) The unit \(e\colon S_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}\to N\) is a \(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\)-morphism (Example 1.2 (b)). The morphism \(N\to S\) of type \(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\{\ast\}\) and the \(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\)-morphism \(N\to S_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}\) give the same ring of \(\operatorname{Top}(N_{\mathrm{Zar}})\), with the levels \(\pi_n^{-1}\mathcal O_S\), so \(L_{N/S}=L_{N/S_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}}\) and \(Le^*L_{N/S}=\ell_N\). Consider the triangle (3.1) of Theorem 3.3 of the lesson on the adjoint co-Lie complex. By Corollary 2.3 (1), \(\ell_{P/N}\cong\pi_N^*\ell_G\), and by the naturality of the adjunction map, (3.1) is isomorphic to a distinguished triangle \[ \begin{aligned}&L_{N/S}\to i_{0!}E\xrightarrow{\ \epsilon\ }\pi_N^*E\\&\quad\to L_{N/S}[1],\end{aligned} \] where \(\epsilon\) is the adjunction map of \(\pi_N^*E\). The level \(n\) of \(i_{0!}E\) is \(\bigoplus_{k=0}^n\pi_n^*E\), the summand \(k\) being that of the vertex \(v_k\), and on this summand \(\epsilon\) is the structure map of \(\pi_N^*E\) along \(v_k\), the identity of \(\pi_n^*E\) (Section 3 there). These complexes have flat levels, so \(Le^*\) is computed by \(e^*\). Since \(\pi_ne_n=\mathrm{id}_S\), \(e^*i_{0!}E=I(E)\), \(e^*\pi_N^*E=cE\) and \(e^*\epsilon=\Sigma\). This gives the triangle. The map \(\Sigma\) is surjective at every level, so \(0\to\ker\Sigma\to I(E)\to cE\to0\) is a short exact sequence of complexes, which gives a distinguished triangle with the same second map [Stacks, Tag 0152]. Two distinguished triangles with the same second map have isomorphic first vertices [Stacks, Tag 014A].

(2) The complex \(\ker\Sigma\) has flat levels, being a direct summand of \(I(E)\) at every level, so \(L\varepsilon^*\ell_N\cong\varepsilon^*\ker\Sigma=\ker\varepsilon^*\Sigma\). Let \(J\) be a bounded below complex of injective \(\mathcal O\)-modules of \(T\) resolving \(\mathcal O\), and \(V=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}^\bullet(\varepsilon^*E,J)\), which represents \(\ell^!_G\) [Stacks, Tag 070J]. Write \(K_n\) for the level \(n\) of \(\ker\varepsilon^*\Sigma\). By the Conventions, \(\ell^!_N\) is represented by the complex with the levels \(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}^\bullet(K_n,J)\). The sequence \(0\to K_n\to(\varepsilon^*E)^{[n]}\to\varepsilon^*E\to0\) is split in every degree, so this level is \(V^{[n]}/V\), the quotient by the diagonal. Its map along \(\alpha\colon[m]\to[n]\) is the composition with the map \(K_m\to K_n\) induced by \(I(\varepsilon^*E)\), and it sends \((x_k)_{k\in[n]}\) to \((x_{\alpha(j)})_{j\in[m]}\). The difference map \[ \begin{aligned}&\delta_n\colon V^{[n]}/V \to V^n,\\ &(x_0,\ldots,x_n)\\ &\quad \mapsto(x_1-x_0,\ldots,x_n-x_{n-1})\end{aligned} \] is an isomorphism. Put \(g_k=x_k-x_{k-1}\). Then \(x_{\alpha(j)}-x_{\alpha(j-1)}=\sum_{\alpha(j-1)<a\leq\alpha(j)}g_a\), which is the nerve formula for the action of \(\alpha\) on \(V\langle1\rangle\) (Conventions of the lesson on graded rings). So the maps \(\delta_n\) form an isomorphism of complexes of simplicial modules onto \(V\langle1\rangle\), and \(\ell^!_N\cong(\ell^!_G)\langle1\rangle\). \(\square\)

The normalized complex of the simplicial module \(V\langle1\rangle\) is \(V\) placed in degree \(1\) (Lemma 3.4 of the lesson on multisimplicial objects): up to this shift, the Lie complex of the nerve is the Lie complex of \(G\).

Lemma 3.2 (the spectrum is a matrix diagram). For every object \(k\) of \(K=\int C_\bullet(S_1)\) there is a finite set \(P(k)\), and for every arrow \(b\colon k\to k'\) a matrix \(n^b\in\mathbb Z^{P(k')\times P(k)}\), with \(n^{\mathrm{id}}=1\) and \(n^{b'b}=n^{b'}n^b\), such that for every commutative group object \(G\) of a category with finite products, \(C_\bullet(G(1))(k)=G^{P(k)}\) and \(C_\bullet(G(1))(b)\) is the homomorphism \(n^b_G\) of Lemma 1.5 (4). The object \(o=(0,([-1],()))\), the \(0\)-simplex \([-1]\) of the nerve of \(\bar\Delta'\) with the empty multi-index, has \(P(o)\) a point and \(C_\bullet(G(1))(o)=G\).

Proof. By Section 3 of the lesson on multisimplicial objects, \(G\langle n\rangle(p_0,\ldots,p_{n-1})=G^P\) with \(P=\prod_{l<n}\{1,\ldots,p_l\}\), and the faces and degeneracies in each coordinate act by the formulas of the nerve, which send a family \((g_a)\) to a family of sums of its entries: integer matrices that do not depend on \(G\). The spectrum \(G(1)\) has the components \(G\langle n\rangle\) and identities, up to the identifications of index sets, as structure maps. By Definition 2.1 of the lesson on graded monoids, \(C_q(G(1))(\sigma,x)=\) \(G(1)^{(k_0)}(x)=\) \(G\langle k_0\rangle(x)\) for \(\sigma=([k_0-1]\to\cdots)\), and the maps of \(C_\bullet(G(1))\) are composites of structure maps of \(G(1)\) and of multisimplicial operators. So all values are products \(G^{P(k)}\) and all maps are given by integer matrices \(n^b\) independent of \(G\). For \(G=\mathbb Z\) in the category of sets, \(n^b_{\mathbb Z}\) is multiplication by the matrix \(n^b\), so the functoriality of \(C_\bullet(\mathbb Z(1))\) gives \(n^{\mathrm{id}}=1\) and \(n^{b'b}=n^{b'}n^b\). For \(o\), \(k_0=0\) and \(G\langle0\rangle=G\). \(\square\)

Lemma 3.3 (cohomology of the Lie complex of the spectrum). Let \(Y=\ell^!_{C_\bullet(G(1))}\), an object of \(D^{[0,1]}(\operatorname{Top}^{\mathrm o}((S_K)_{\mathrm{fppf}}))\). For every \(i\) there is an isomorphism of modules of type \(C_\bullet(S_1)\), natural in \(G\), \[ H^i(Y)\cong C_\bullet\bigl(H^i(\ell^!_G)(1)\bigr). \]

Proof. By Proposition 1.3 (2), the level \(k\) of \(Y\) is \(\ell^!_{G^{P(k)}}\), and its map along \(b\colon k\to k'\) is \(\ell^!\) of the homomorphism \(n^b_G\) (Lemma 3.2), which is the map of \(\operatorname{Top}^{\mathrm o}\) along \(b\), the base \(S_K\) being constant. By Lemma 1.5 (2) and (4), \(\ell^!_{G^{P(k)}}\cong(\ell^!_G)^{\oplus P(k)}\), and under these isomorphisms \(\ell^!(n^b_G)\) is the map \((y_p)\mapsto(\sum_pn^b_{p'p}y_p)_{p'}\). Taking \(H^i\) gives the modules \(H^i(\ell^!_G)^{P(k)}\) with the maps \(n^b\), which is \(C_\bullet(H^i(\ell^!_G)(1))\) by Lemma 3.2 for the abelian sheaf \(H^i(\ell^!_G)\). The isomorphisms are \(\mathcal O\)-linear, being induced by maps of \(\mathcal O\)-modules. \(\square\)

To pass from cohomology to the complex itself we use the adjunction formulas of Diagonal rings and adjunction formulas, with \(\mathcal O\)-coefficients. For \(a\in\{0,1\}\) let \(\mathcal M_a(\mathcal O)\) be the category of \(\mathcal O\)-modules of type \(C_\bullet(S_a)\), the functors \(\int C_\bullet(S_a)\to\operatorname{Mod}(\mathcal O)\), and \(U\) the functor forgetting the \(\mathcal O\)-structure, to the category \(\mathcal M_a\) of that lesson. The constant functor \(c\), the translations \((1)\), \((-1)\) and the reduction are defined by the same formulas and are \(\mathcal O\)-linear. The modules of \(\operatorname{Top}^{\mathrm o}((S_K)_{\mathrm{fppf}})\) are the objects of \(\mathcal M_1(\mathcal O)\), \(S\) being the final object of \(T\).

Lemma 3.4 (constant objects with coefficients).

  1. The constant functor \(c\colon D^-(\mathcal O)\to D^-(\mathcal M_0(\mathcal O))\) is fully faithful, with a left adjoint \(\Lambda_{\mathcal O}\) such that \(U\Lambda_{\mathcal O}=\Lambda U\). An object \(X\) is in its essential image if and only if \(UX\) is in the essential image of \(c\colon D^-(\mathcal A)\to D^-(\mathcal M_0)\).
  2. The composite of \(c\) and \((1)\), \(D^-(\mathcal O)\to D^-(\mathcal M_1(\mathcal O))\), is fully faithful. An object \(Y\) is in its essential image if and only if every \(H^i(Y)\) is isomorphic, as an object of \(\mathcal M_1\), to \(C_\bullet(G_i(1))\) for some abelian sheaf \(G_i\); then \(Y\cong C_\bullet(H(1))\) with \(H=\Lambda_{\mathcal O}(Y^{\mathrm{red}}(-1))\).

Proof. (1) The colimit over \(\int C_\bullet(S_0)\) of \(\mathcal O\)-modules is computed on the underlying abelian sheaves and is left adjoint to \(c\). The category of \(\mathcal O\)-modules satisfies AB5, so by Theorem 2.3 and Corollary 2.4 of Derived colimits over a small category the colimit has a left derived functor \(\Lambda_{\mathcal O}\) on \(D^-(\mathcal M_0(\mathcal O))\), computed by bounded above complexes of induced diagrams, and left adjoint to \(c\). Induced diagrams of \(\mathcal O\)-modules are induced diagrams of abelian sheaves, so \(U\Lambda_{\mathcal O}X=\Lambda UX\), \(\Lambda\) being the left derived functor of the colimit on \(D^-(\mathcal M_0)\) (Proposition 3.4 (1) of the lesson on diagonal rings); on complexes of induced diagrams both adjunctions are induced by the adjunction of the colimit and \(c\), so \(U\) carries the unit and the counit to those of \(\Lambda\) and \(c\). A map of \(D(\mathcal O)\) or of \(D(\mathcal M_0(\mathcal O))\) is an isomorphism if its image under \(U\) is. The image under \(U\) of the counit \(\Lambda_{\mathcal O}cH\to H\) is the counit of \(\Lambda\) and \(c\) at \(UH\), an isomorphism by Proposition 3.4 (3) there; so the counit is an isomorphism and \(c\) is fully faithful. The object \(X\) is in the essential image exactly when its unit \(X\to c\Lambda_{\mathcal O}X\) is an isomorphism, that is, when the unit of \(UX\) is, which characterizes the essential image of \(c\) on \(D^-(\mathcal M_0)\).

(2) Proposition 1.1 (3) of the lesson on diagonal rings holds for \(\mathcal O\)-modules with the same proof: reduction followed by \((-1)\) is left adjoint to \((1)\), both are exact and \(\mathcal O\)-linear, and the unit and counit act termwise. So \((1)\colon D(\mathcal M_0(\mathcal O))\to D(\mathcal M_1(\mathcal O))\) is fully faithful, with essential image the \(Y\) with reduced cohomology, and \(Y\cong X(1)\) for \(X=Y^{\mathrm{red}}(-1)\). The composite with \(c\) is fully faithful by (1). If every \(H^i(UY)\) is isomorphic to some \(C_\bullet(G_i(1))\), then \(UY\) is in the essential image of \(c\) followed by \((1)\) (Proposition 3.6 (2) there); so the cohomology of \(Y\) is reduced, and \(UX\) is in the essential image of \(c\). By (1), \(X\cong c\Lambda_{\mathcal O}X\), and \(Y\cong(cH)(1)=C_\bullet(H(1))\), with \(H=\Lambda_{\mathcal O}X\), the last equality being that of the proof of Proposition 3.6 (2) there, applied termwise. The converse holds because \((1)\) and \(c\) are exact. \(\square\)

Proposition 3.5 (the spectrum). There is an isomorphism \[ \ell^!_{C_\bullet(G(1))}\cong C_\bullet\bigl(\ell^!_G(1)\bigr) \] in \(D(\operatorname{Top}^{\mathrm o}((S_K)_{\mathrm{fppf}}))=D(\mathcal M_1(\mathcal O))\). In particular \(U\ell^!_{C_\bullet(G(1))}\cong(cU\ell^!_G)(1)\) in \(D(\mathcal M_1)\).

Proof. Let \(Y=\ell^!_{C_\bullet(G(1))}\). By Lemma 3.3, every \(H^i(Y)\) is isomorphic to \(C_\bullet(H^i(\ell^!_G)(1))\). By Lemma 3.4 (2), \(Y\cong C_\bullet(H(1))\) for some \(H\in D^-(\mathcal O)\). Evaluation at the object \(o\) of Lemma 3.2 is an exact functor to \(D(\mathcal O)\), and it carries \(C_\bullet(H(1))\) to \(H\) and \(Y\) to \(\ell^!_G\) (Proposition 1.3 (3), the value of \(C_\bullet(G(1))\) at \(o\) being \(G\)). So \(H\cong\ell^!_G\), and \(Y\cong C_\bullet(\ell^!_G(1))\). \(\square\)

4. Exercises

4.1. Let \(R\) be a ring, \(S=\operatorname{Spec}R\), \(r\geq1\) and \(G=\mathbf G_m^r=\operatorname{Spec}R[t_1^{\pm1},\ldots,t_r^{\pm1}]\). For an integer matrix \(M\in\mathbb Z^{r\times r}\) let \(u_M\colon G\to G\) be the homomorphism with \(u_M^*(t_i)=\prod_jt_j^{M_{ij}}\).

(a) Show that \(\ell_G\) is the free module \(\omega_G\) in degree \(0\), with the basis \(dt_1,\ldots,dt_r\) restricted to the unit, and that \(\ell(u_M)\) has the matrix \(M\) in this basis: \(\ell(u_M)(dt_i)=\sum_jM_{ij}\,dt_j\).

(b) Check Lemma 1.5 (3) directly: \(u_M+u_{M'}=u_{M+M'}\) and \(\ell(u_{M+M'})=\ell(u_M)+\ell(u_{M'})\).

4.2. Let \(R\) be a ring, \(c\in R^\times\), \(n\geq1\), \(Y=\operatorname{Spec}R\) and \(X=\operatorname{Spec}B\), \(B=R[t]/(t^n-c)\), with the action of \(\mu_n=\operatorname{Spec}R[z]/(z^n-1)\) given by \(t\mapsto zt\).

(a) Show that \(X\) is a \(\mu_n\)-torsor over \(Y\).

(b) Show that \(L_{X/Y}\) is represented by the complex \(B\,\bar e\to B\,dt\), \(\bar e\mapsto nt^{n-1}dt\), in degrees \(-1\) and \(0\), where \(\bar e\) is the class of \(t^n-c\), and that it is isomorphic to \(B\xrightarrow{n}B\).

(c) Using Example 3.2 of the lesson on the co-Lie complex, \(\ell_{\mu_n}\cong[R\xrightarrow{n}R]\), compare (b) with Proposition 2.1 and Theorem 2.2.

4.3. Let \(k\) be a field and \(G=\operatorname{Spec}k[b,a^{\pm1}]\) the group of affine maps \(x\mapsto ax+b\), with the product \((b,a)(b',a')=(b+ab',aa')\). Show that the coadjoint action of \(G\) on \(\omega_G\) is not trivial, and deduce that Corollary 2.3 fails for \(G\): \(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G\) is not isomorphic to \(p^*L\varepsilon^*\ell_G\).

5. Complete solutions

4.1. (a) \(G\) is smooth over \(S\), so \(L_{G/S}=\Omega_{G/S}[0]\), free on \(dt_1,\ldots,dt_r\), and \(\ell_G=e^*\Omega_{G/S}[0]=\omega_G[0]\) is free on the restrictions of the \(dt_i\) to the unit \(t=1\) (Proposition 1.5 of the lesson on the co-Lie complex). By Proposition 1.3 there, \(\ell(u_M)\) is the map on differentials restricted to the unit. Now \(d(\prod_jt_j^{M_{ij}})=\prod_jt_j^{M_{ij}}\sum_jM_{ij}\,dt_j/t_j\), which at \(t=1\) is \(\sum_jM_{ij}\,dt_j\).

(b) On points, \((u_M+u_{M'})(t)_i=\) \(u_M(t)_iu_{M'}(t)_i=\) \(\prod_jt_j^{M_{ij}+M'_{ij}}\), the group law being the product. So \(u_M+u_{M'}=u_{M+M'}\), and by (a) \(\ell(u_{M+M'})\) has the matrix \(M+M'\), the sum of the matrices of \(\ell(u_M)\) and \(\ell(u_{M'})\).

4.2. (a) \(B\) is free over \(R\) with the basis \(1,t,\ldots,t^{n-1}\), so \(X\to Y\) is finite locally free and surjective. The map \(\mu_n\times X\to X\times_YX\), \((z,t)\mapsto(zt,t)\), corresponds to \(B\otimes_RB\to R[z]/(z^n-1)\otimes_RB\), \(t\otimes1\mapsto zt\), \(1\otimes t\mapsto t\). Since \(t\) is a unit of \(B\), with \(t^{-1}=c^{-1}t^{n-1}\), the inverse sends \(z\mapsto(t\otimes1)(1\otimes t)^{-1}\), which satisfies \(z^n=(t^n\otimes1)(1\otimes t^n)^{-1}=c\cdot c^{-1}=1\). So condition 1 of Definition 1.1 of the lesson on torsors holds, and condition 2 holds after the fppf covering \(X\to Y\), over which the diagonal is a section.

(b) \(X\) is cut out of \(\mathbf A^1_R\) by the nonzerodivisor \(t^n-c\), monic in \(t\), so formula (7.3) of The cotangent complex gives \(L_{X/Y}\cong[B\,\bar e\to B\,dt]\), \(\bar e\mapsto d(t^n-c)=nt^{n-1}dt\). In \(B\), \(nt^{n-1}=nct^{-1}\). With the bases \(\bar e'=c^{-1}\bar e\) and \(\theta=t^{-1}dt\), the differential is \(\bar e'\mapsto c^{-1}nct^{-1}dt=n\theta\). So \(L_{X/Y}\cong[B\xrightarrow{n}B]\).

(c) Proposition 2.1 predicts \(L_{X/Y}\cong\pi_X^*\ell_{\mu_n}=[B\xrightarrow{n}B]\), which (b) confirms. The basis element \(\theta=t^{-1}dt\) is the invariant form \(dt/t\): along the orbit map \(z\mapsto zt\) of a point \(t\), it pulls back to \(dz/z\), which is \(dz\) at \(z=1\). Theorem 2.2 gives \(\ell_{X/Y}\cong\ell_{\mu_n}=[R\xrightarrow{n}R]\), \(\mu_n\) being commutative and \(Y\) being \(S\); its pullback to \(X\) is the complex of (b).

4.3. For \(g=(0,a)\), with \(g^{-1}=(0,a^{-1})\), and a point \(h=(b',a')\), \[ \begin{aligned}ghg^{-1}&=(ab',aa')(0,a^{-1})\\&=(ab',a').\end{aligned} \] So \(\operatorname{int}(g)\) multiplies the coordinate \(b\) by \(a\) and fixes the coordinate \(a'\). On \(\omega_G\), with the basis \(db\), \(da\) restricted to the unit \((0,1)\), \(c(\operatorname{int}(g))\) is \(db\mapsto a\,db\), \(da\mapsto da\). Hence \(c(\operatorname{int}(g))\neq\mathrm{id}\) as soon as \(a\neq1\), and the action \(m_{(g)}=c(\operatorname{int}(g))\) on \(H^0(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G)=\varepsilon^*\omega_G\) (Proposition 2.4 of the lesson on the adjoint co-Lie complex) is not trivial. An isomorphism \(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G\cong p^*L\varepsilon^*\ell_G\) in \(D^b(B)\) would give an isomorphism of modules of \(B\) on \(H^0\), compatible with the actions, between \(\varepsilon^*\omega_G\) with a nontrivial action and a module with the trivial action; this is impossible, since a map compatible with the actions carries the identity action to the identity action. So Corollary 2.3 needs commutativity, and so does the equivariance used in the proof of Theorem 2.2: for \(G\) acting on itself, \(g_1hx\neq hg_1x\) in general.

The triviality of the adjoint co-Lie complex of a commutative group scheme, the isomorphism \(\ell_{X/Y}\cong Lg^*\ell_G\) for torsors under commutative groups, the co-Lie complexes of group schemes over diagrams and the Lie complexes of the nerve and of the spectrum of a commutative group scheme are due to Illusie, in his work on the cotangent complex, where the triviality of the adjoint co-Lie complex is proved by an argument of Deligne with base change for classifying topoi. The proofs here use the shear isomorphism for torsors over diagrams instead, and they deduce the Lie complex of the spectrum from its cohomology and the adjunction formulas for constant objects.
