Open Mathematics CoursesCourse contents (Cotangent complexes and deformations in ringed topoi)

Results on homotopical algebra in topoi and on diagrams of topoi

The course proves the statements below; each link leads to the statement with its assumptions and its proof. The lessons are self-checked by the writing AI; independent mathematical review is not complete.

  • Whitehead for simplicial sets — Theorem 3.1. An n-quasi-isomorphism of simplicial sets induces isomorphisms on homology below n and a surjection in degree n.
  • Free abelian sheaves, enough points — Theorem 4.1. On a topos with enough points, the free abelian functor carries n-quasi-isomorphisms of simplicial sheaves to n-quasi-isomorphisms.
  • Free abelian sheaves, homomorphisms — Theorem 7.1. On any topos, the free abelian functor carries n-quasi-isomorphisms of simplicial abelian sheaves to n-quasi-isomorphisms; no hypothesis on points.
  • The Dold-Puppe bound — Proposition 2.1. A functor of degree at most k, extended to complexes through the Dold-Kan correspondence, carries complexes in degrees at most b to normalized complexes in degrees at most kb.
  • Homotopy invariance — Proposition 2.3. The extension of a non-additive functor to complexes carries homotopic maps to homotopic maps.
  • The Deligne-Lazard theorem — Theorem 3.2. A module over a ring of a topos is flat exactly when it is the local inductive limit of a local system of finite free modules.
  • The left derived functor — Theorem 4.4. A functor of finite degree has a left derived functor on bounded-above complexes, computed by flat resolutions and compatible with local inductive limits of uniformly bounded complexes.
  • Koszul sequences — Theorem 2.1. For an exact sequence of flat modules, the two Koszul complexes give exact sequences relating symmetric, exterior and divided powers.
  • The shift formulas — Theorem 3.2. LS^n(E[1]) is (LΛ^n E)[n] and LΛ^n(E[1]) is (LΓ^n E)[n], canonically, for E in the bounded-above derived category; Proposition 3.4 gives the compatibility with products up to the sign (-1)^{mn}.
  • Décalage is homotopically trivial — Proposition 1.1. Both décalages of a simplicial object, in any category, are homotopy equivalent to the constant object on their augmentation.
  • The total décalage — Proposition 1.3. The two augmentations of the diagonal of the total décalage are homotopic; Example 1.4 shows that they are not homotopy equivalences in general.
  • Nerves of internal categories — Proposition 2.4. Nine equivalent conditions characterizing nerves of internal categories in a category with fibre products, including the Segal conditions and cartesian décalage squares; Remark 2.5 shows two weaker conditions that do not suffice.
  • Groupoids — Proposition 2.6. Seven equivalent conditions for an internal category to be a groupoid, in terms of its nerve and its décalages.
  • Actions of monoids — Proposition 2.9. Nerves of monoid actions are exactly the simplicial objects over the nerve of the monoid that split as a product with their vertices.
  • Quillen's comparison — Theorem 3.2. The first and last vertex functors from the category of simplices are weak equivalences, and so is the last-vertex map of a simplicial set; Corollary 3.4: a category and its opposite have the same homotopy type.
  • Simplices of strict simplicial sets — Theorem 2.3. The nerve of the category of simplices of a strict simplicial set has the homology of its alternating face complex, naturally; Corollary 2.4 computes the truncated categories of injective ordinals.
  • Deligne's theorem — Theorem 4.2. For every p, the inclusion of the ordinals with at least p+2 elements into those with at least p+1 elements, with injective maps, induces a quasi-isomorphism of chains; Corollary 4.3: these categories are weakly contractible.
  • Derived colimits — Theorem 2.3. The colimit over a small category has a left derived functor on bounded-above complexes, computed by the simplicial replacement; Corollary 2.4 gives its adjunction with constant diagrams.
  • Colimits over injective ordinals — Theorem 3.3. Over the category of finite ordinals and injective maps, a complex whose cohomology is eventually locally constant has the cohomology of its value at any stage from which it is locally constant.
  • The total topos — Theorem 4.2. The total topos of a fibred topos is the category of sheaves on an explicit total site.
  • The second total topos — Theorem 4.3. For a good fibred topos whose left adjoints preserve fibre products, the second total category is a topos; Proposition 5.1 identifies it, for a diagram in a topos, with the diagrams over it.
  • Direct images — Proposition 2.1. A morphism of fibred topoi induces a morphism of total topoi whose direct image is a limit over comma categories; global sections of the total topos are the limit of the global sections of the levels.
  • Modules on total topoi — Proposition 4.2. Modules over a ring of a total topos are compatible families of modules; tensor products are computed levelwise, and so are internal Homs from a cartesian module.
  • Enough levelwise acyclic sheaves — Proposition 1.4. Every abelian sheaf on a total topos is a subsheaf of an injective sheaf whose levels are totally acyclic; for flat transition morphisms, every module is a submodule of an injective module with injective levels.
  • Standard resolutions — Proposition 2.1. The two standard resolutions of an abelian sheaf on a total topos are exact and become contractible on the levels.
  • Cohomology of total topoi — Theorem 3.5. The derived direct image from a total topos to a topos is computed by the cosimplicial complex of the direct images of the levels, and by the complex of the direct images themselves when the levels are totally acyclic.
  • Derived limits — Corollary 3.6. The derived functors of the inverse limit of abelian groups over a small category are the cohomology of its cosimplicial complex; Corollary 3.7 gives the spectral sequence from derived limits of the cohomology of the levels.
  • Final objects — Corollary 3.9. Over an index category with a final object, the derived direct image of a total topos is that of the final level.
  • Levelwise direct images — Proposition 4.1. Derived direct images under a morphism of fibred topoi over a fixed index category commute with restriction to the levels.
  • Simplicial topoi — Theorem 2.1. The derived direct image from a simplicial topos to a topos is computed by the cosimplicial object of the direct images of the levels; Corollary 2.2 gives the spectral sequence from the higher direct images of the levels, and Theorem 2.3 the multisimplicial case.
  • The associated simplicial topos — Proposition 3.2. The cohomology of the total topos of any fibred topos is that of an associated simplicial topos.
  • Décalage of a simplicial topos — Proposition 4.2. The décalage of a simplicial topos has the cohomology of the level onto which it is augmented.
  • Free abelian sheaves and descent — Proposition 5.2. For a simplicial object X of a topos, Rp_*p^*E is RHom(Z(X), E); Corollary 5.3 gives cohomological descent for hypercoverings in every topos, and Corollary 5.4 the invariance under morphisms inducing quasi-isomorphisms of free abelian sheaves.
  • The second total topos — Theorem 2.4. For a complex whose levels stay totally acyclic under all inverse images, the derived direct image from the second total topos is that of the associated simplicial object.
  • Local systems — Theorem 3.3. Cartesian objects of the two total topoi correspond, and their cohomologies agree; Corollary 3.5 computes the cohomology of simplicial sheaves over a simplicial object.
  • Base change — Proposition 4.1. A criterion for derived direct images on the second total topoi to commute with restriction to the levels.
  • Left derived functors of u_! — Theorem 3.2. Under an exactness condition on flat modules, the left adjoint of the inverse image of modules on total topoi has a left derived functor on bounded above complexes, computed by standard resolutions of flat complexes, with RHom(Lu_!E, F) = RHom(E, u^*F).
  • Derived colimits and constant diagrams — Proposition 4.1. For diagrams of modules over a ring of a topos, the derived colimit and the constant diagram functor satisfy RHom(L colim E, F) = RHom(E, cF).
  • Local inductive limits — Proposition 1.2. For a locally filtering diagram in a topos, the left adjoint of the inverse image of modules is the local inductive limit, which is exact.
  • Derived left adjoints on the second total topos — Theorem 3.1. Under exactness conditions on flat modules, the left adjoint of the inverse image on the second total topos has a left derived functor, adjoint to the inverse image on RHom complexes and computed levelwise for morphisms over a fixed index category.
  • Décalage for left adjoints — Proposition 4.4. The derived colimit of a simplicial module is its chain complex (Lemma 4.1), and the décalage of a simplicial topos is trivial for the derived left adjoint of its augmentation.
  • The internal mixed Hom — Definition 1.1. For a diagram in a topos, the internal Hom from modules of the first total topos to modules of the second, with values in the second total topos.
  • The duality formula — Theorem 2.3. RHom on the first total topos into a module pulled back from the topos is RGamma of the second total topos of the derived internal mixed Hom.
  • The relative duality formula — Theorem 3.2. For a morphism of diagrams u, the canonical map from the internal mixed Hom of Lu_!E to Ru_* of the internal mixed Hom of E is an isomorphism for coefficients pulled back from the topos.
  • Equivariant sheaves and their nerves — Proposition 2.2. The category BX of equivariant sheaves on a simplicial topos has colimits and finite limits computed on X_0, and its two nerves into the total topoi are fully faithful.
  • Images of the nerves — Proposition 2.3. An object of the first (second) total topos is isomorphic to a nerve exactly when its maps along simplicial maps fixing the last (first) vertex are isomorphisms.
  • Nerves of internal categories — Proposition 3.4. The simplicial topos of the nerve of an internal category of a topos is a good two-sided pseudo-category.
  • Coinduction — Proposition 4.2. On a pseudo-category, the coinduced sheaf s_*t^*L carries a canonical equivariant structure, and coinduction is right adjoint to the forgetful functor.
  • Induction — Proposition 5.1. On a good pseudo-category, the induced sheaf t_!s^*L carries a canonical equivariant structure, and induction is left adjoint to the forgetful functor.
  • Equivariant modules — Proposition 6.2. Equivariant modules form an abelian category with enough injectives, the direct summands of coinduced injectives; on a good pseudo-category every module is a quotient of an induced free module.
  • Homotopies of Roos complexes — Lemma 1.1. A natural transformation between two functors gives an explicit homotopy between the maps they induce on Roos complexes.
  • Nerves of coinduced modules — Theorem 3.4. The nerves of a coinduced module are the direct images along the décalage of its pullback by the last vertex, and the derived direct images when the module is injective and the inverse images along last vertices preserve injectives.
  • Nerves of induced modules — Theorem 4.3. On a good two-sided pseudo-category, under an exactness condition on flat modules, the nerve of an induced flat module is the derived left adjoint along the décalage of its pullback by the first vertex.
  • Cohomology of BX — Theorem 4.1. If the inverse images along the last vertices preserve injective modules, the nerves compute RHom out of complexes with invertible structure; Corollary 4.2 gives the cohomology of BX and its spectral sequence from the levels.
  • Derived Hom of equivariant modules — Theorem 4.3. Under an exactness condition on left adjoints as well, the second nerve, and the first for two-sided pseudo-categories, computes all RHom complexes and is fully faithful on bounded derived categories.
  • Group cohomology — Example 5.3. For a group acting on a point, the complex of the levels is the inhomogeneous cochain complex of group cohomology.
  • Direct images to a base — Proposition 6.1. For a pseudo-category over a topos, the derived direct image of an equivariant module to the topos is computed on either total topos.
  • The mixed Hom functor — Definition 1.5. For a good fibred topos with a cartesian ring, the derived mixed Hom from modules on the second total topos to modules on the first, with values in functors on the index category, computed by complexes with injective levels.
  • Mixed Hom and inverse images — Proposition 2.2. For a morphism over the index category, RHom^!(L, Rf_*M) and RHom^!(Lf^*L, M) are canonically isomorphic for L bounded above.
  • Mixed Hom and derived left adjoints — Proposition 2.3. Under exactness conditions on flat modules, RHom^!(Lf_!E, F) and RHom^!(E, f^*F) are canonically isomorphic for E bounded above.
  • Ext groups of equivariant modules — Theorem 3.3. Under the injectivity and exactness conditions, RHom_BX(E, F) is the derived limit over the simplex category of the mixed Hom from the second nerve of E to the nerve of F; Corollary 3.4 gives a spectral sequence from the Ext groups on the levels.
  • Ext over a group ring — Example 4.1. For a group acting on a point, Ext over the group ring into a module injective over the coefficients is computed by cochains of the group with values in Hom groups.
  • Reduced spectral diagrams — Proposition 2.4. Translating down by one in every coordinate is an equivalence from reduced spectral diagrams to large simplicial diagrams; reduction is its left adjoint on all spectral diagrams.
  • Spectra of commutative group objects — Proposition 3.5. The spectrum of iterated nerves is fully faithful on commutative group objects, with image the spectral diagrams of iterated nerves whose structure maps are isomorphisms.
  • Derived categories of spectral diagrams — Proposition 4.6. The bounded above derived category embeds fully faithfully in that of spectral diagrams, with image the objects whose cohomology consists of spectra of objects.
  • The suspension theorem — Theorem 2.1. For a reduced functor and an n-reduced simplicial object, the suspension is an isomorphism in degrees below 2n and onto in degree 2n.
  • The stabilization — Theorem 3.6. The homology of the stabilization of F at X in degree i is the homology of F of the n-th suspension of X in degree i+n, for every n > i.
  • Stabilization and triangles — Proposition 4.3. The stabilization is additive and carries degreewise split exact sequences to distinguished triangles, through its shift isomorphism.
  • Exactness of stable derived functors — Theorem 1.6. The stable derived functor, extended to bounded above complexes with its shift isomorphism, is an exact functor of triangulated categories.
  • Symmetric, exterior and divided powers — Example 2.2. The stable derived functor of the n-th symmetric power is that of the exterior power shifted by n-1, which is that of the divided power shifted by n-1.
  • Lifting against fibrant quasi-isomorphisms — Lemma 3.6. A fibrant quasi-isomorphism of simplicial modules in a topos has local lifts against finite monomorphisms of simplicial sets.
  • Fractions for simplicial modules and algebras — Theorem 4.2. Over a simplicial ring of a topos, quasi-isomorphisms of modules up to homotopy admit left and right fractions, and quasi-isomorphisms of algebras admit right fractions.
  • Suspension and loops — Proposition 1.5. On the derived category of a simplicial ring, the suspension is left adjoint to the loop functor and fully faithful, with image the modules whose homotopy vanishes in degree 0.
  • Long exact Ext sequences — Theorem 3.9. Distinguished triangles of simplicial modules give long exact sequences of Ext groups in both variables.
  • Constant rings — Proposition 3.10. For a ring placed in every degree, normalization carries the distinguished triangles of simplicial modules to those of the derived category, and the extension class to the connecting morphism.
  • The first Ext group by extensions — Proposition 4.3. Every class in the first Ext group is the class of an extension of simplicial modules after a quasi-isomorphism, with a criterion for two extensions to give the same class.
  • Flat modules and quasi-isomorphisms — Proposition 2.2. Tensoring with a degreewise flat simplicial module preserves quasi-isomorphisms, and so does tensoring a quasi-isomorphism of flat modules with any module.
  • The derived tensor product — Proposition 3.2. The tensor product of simplicial modules over a simplicial ring has a left derived functor, computed by a flat replacement of either factor.
  • Quasi-isomorphic simplicial rings — Corollary 4.3. A quasi-isomorphism of simplicial rings induces an equivalence of derived categories, through derived extension and restriction of scalars.
  • The Tor spectral sequence — Theorem 2.1. The homology of a derived tensor product of simplicial modules is the abutment of a spectral sequence starting from the homology of the degreewise Tor modules.
  • The Künneth spectral sequence — Theorem 4.5. A spectral sequence from Tor over the homology ring of the simplicial ring, of the homology modules of the factors, converges to the homology of their derived tensor product.
  • Bounded derived categories of differential graded modules — Proposition 1.2. Over a differential graded ring in nonpositive degrees, the localizations of the categories of bounded modules are full subcategories of the derived category, cut out by bounds on cohomology.
  • Truncated differential graded rings — Proposition 1.3. Modules with cohomology in an interval of length at most n over a ring in nonpositive degrees are the same as modules over its truncated ring t_n.
  • Bounded resolutions of differential graded modules — Lemma 2.1. Bounded above modules have functorial good resolutions with the same bound, and bounded below modules have K-injective resolutions with the same bound.
  • Derived Hom of differential graded modules — Proposition 2.3. Derived Hom and derived internal Hom are defined on all modules, compute morphisms in the derived category, and respect bounds for rings in nonpositive degrees.
  • Extension of scalars for differential graded rings — Proposition 2.5. Derived extension of scalars is left adjoint to restriction, also on derived Hom, and a quasi-isomorphism of rings gives an equivalence of derived categories.
  • Normalization and derived tensor products — Theorem 3.3. Normalization carries the derived tensor products and extensions of scalars of simplicial modules over a simplicial ring to those of differential graded modules over its normalized complex.
  • Graded objects as equivariant sheaves — Proposition 2.3. Graded objects of a topos are the equivariant sheaves on the simplicial topos of the categories C_•(J), and C_• is their second nerve, fully faithful with an explicit image.
  • Nerves of graded actions — Proposition 2.6. For a graded monoid G, the bisimplicial nerve Ner(C_•(G), C_•(X)) is a fully faithful functor of the graded G-object X, with essential image given by two isomorphism conditions.
  • Base change along discrete opfibrations — Lemma 3.2. A cartesian square of diagrams in a topos with one side a discrete opfibration induces a square of topoi with invertible base change map.
  • The pseudo-category of a graded action — Proposition 3.3. When the products of the grading are isomorphisms, the simplicial topos of the nerve of a graded action is a good two-sided pseudo-category whose levels are localizations of one topos.
  • External tensor products of multisimplicial objects — Lemma 1.1. Normalization, reduction and translation commute with external tensor products of multisimplicial objects.
  • Induced modules over rings of type C_•(J) — Proposition 1.4. Modules over a ring of type C_•(J) form a Grothendieck abelian category; induced modules are left adjoint to the forgetful functor and exact when the ring is flat.
  • Large simplicial rings and spectral rings — Proposition 2.1. Translation by one is an equivalence between large simplicial rings and reduced spectral rings, and reduction is left adjoint to the inclusion of reduced spectral rings.
  • Bilinear maps as maps of spectra — Proposition 3.1. Bilinear maps of abelian sheaves are the maps between their spectra of the type given by concatenation of coordinates.
  • Spectra of modules — Proposition 3.4. A module over a ring is determined by its spectrum, a graded object with an action of the spectrum of the ring, with an explicit essential image.
  • Modules as bisimplicial diagrams — Proposition 3.5. Modules over a ring are the bisimplicial diagrams built from products of copies of the ring and the module that satisfy four isomorphism conditions.
  • Reduction and translation in derived categories — Proposition 1.1. Translation by one is fully faithful from the derived category of modules over a ring of type C_•(S_0) to that over the corresponding reduced ring of type C_•(S_1), with reduction as left adjoint.
  • The diagonal comparison functor — Proposition 2.3. The normalized double diagonal is an exact functor from modules over a ring of type C_•(S_0) to differential graded modules over its diagonal ring, and it respects upper bounds.
  • Derived colimits over the nerve of the injective ordinals — Proposition 3.3. The derived colimit of a coefficient system on the simplices of the nerve is the total complex of the sum of its values over the simplices of each dimension.
  • The constant functor and its left adjoint — Proposition 3.4. The constant functor into objects of type C_•(S_0) is fully faithful on bounded above derived categories, with the normalized double diagonal as left adjoint.
  • Objects with constant cohomology — Proposition 3.6. A bounded above object of type C_•(S_0) comes from a complex of abelian sheaves exactly when its cohomology objects are constant, and similarly for reduced spectral objects.
  • The comparison theorem for diagonal rings — Theorem 4.1. For a ring of type C_•(S_0) with flat components, derived Hom into a module with constant underlying object is computed over the normalized double diagonal ring.
  • Comparison for reduced spectral rings — Corollary 4.2. The comparison theorem transferred to rings of type C_•(S_1) through reduction and translation.
  • Derived tensor products of rings — Proposition 1.2. The derived tensor product of two rings is a differential graded ring in degrees -1 and 0, computed from any flat presentation of one factor.
  • The stable ring of a ring — Proposition 2.2. The stabilization of the free abelian group functor at a ring is a simplicial ring with flat terms, augmented to the ring by a ring homomorphism.
  • Derived tensor products through the stable ring — Proposition 2.4. The truncated normalized stable ring is a flat presentation of the ring, so the derived tensor product of two rings is a truncation of a normalized simplicial ring.
  • Mac Lane's theorem — Theorem 3.2. The derived tensor product of the ring with the stable module of a module, over the normalized stable ring, is the module.
  • Bar resolutions — Proposition 4.1. The bar construction of a module over a ring homomorphism is contractible over the smaller ring, and a flat resolution when the ring and the module are flat over it.
  • The Mac Lane resolution — Theorem 4.2. A module has a functorial resolution by sums of direct summands of the free modules on the products of copies of the ring and the module.
  • Base change of the comparison theorem — Proposition 1.2. The comparison theorem for diagonal rings holds for a ring with flat components tensored with any commutative ring.
  • Ext groups through spectra — Corollary 2.4. Derived Hom from the free module on the spectrum of a module into the spectrum of a complex of modules is the derived Hom of modules.
  • Spectra over a derived tensor product — Theorem 3.3. Bounded complexes over the derived tensor product of a ring with a commutative ring have spectra, extending those of complexes of modules and compatible with derived Hom.
  • Two consecutive degrees — Proposition 3.4. The spectrum over a derived tensor product is fully faithful on complexes with cohomology in two consecutive degrees, with an explicit essential image.
  • Spectra and tensor products — Proposition 3.5. The spectrum over a derived tensor product commutes with derived tensor products over the coefficient ring when the result is bounded.
  • Ext groups as cohomology of spectral nerves — Theorem 4.3. The Ext groups of modules are the cohomology of the total topos of the nerve of the action of the spectrum of the ring on the spectrum of the first module.
  • Relative cohomology and cones — Proposition 4.4. Relative cohomology along a homomorphism of modules computes derived Hom out of its cone.
  • Quasi-coherent modules on the big fppf site — Proposition 1.1. Quasi-coherent sheaves of a scheme and quasi-coherent modules on its big fppf site correspond, with the same cohomology.
  • Adequate modules — Proposition 1.2. Adequate modules on the big site have quasi-coherent direct images on the Zariski site, no higher direct images, and direct images compatible with flat base change.
  • Truncated pullbacks to the big site — Proposition 2.1. A bounded complex with quasi-coherent cohomology is recovered from the truncation of its pullback to the big fppf site.
  • Derived Hom on the big site — Corollary 2.2. Derived Hom into a bounded complex with quasi-coherent cohomology is the same on the Zariski site and, after truncation, on the big fppf site.
  • Flat base change for adequate complexes — Lemma 2.3. Direct images to the Zariski site of complexes with adequate cohomology commute with flat base change.
  • Perfect complexes on the big site — Theorem 3.1. Complexes of perfect amplitude in [a, b] on the Zariski site and on the big fppf site of a scheme are equivalent.
  • Diagrams of schemes — Lemma 4.2. The comparison holds for diagrams of schemes, and quasi-cartesian objects on the big site are those with cartesian cohomology.
  • Cotangent complex of a morphism of diagrams — Proposition 1.2. The cotangent complex of a morphism of diagrams of schemes is, at every level, the cotangent complex of the level, with the functoriality maps as structure maps.
  • Transitivity triangle for diagrams — Proposition 1.3. Morphisms of diagrams X to Y to Z give a natural transitivity triangle, whose connecting map is the Kodaira-Spencer class.
  • Tor-independent base change for schemes — Proposition 1.4. The cotangent complex of a morphism of schemes commutes with Tor-independent base change, so cartesian Tor-independent diagrams have quasi-cartesian cotangent complexes.
  • Extensions of diagrams as ringed-topos extensions — Proposition 2.3. Square-zero extensions of a diagram of schemes by a termwise quasi-coherent ideal are the algebra extensions on its Zariski topos.
  • Classification of extensions of diagrams — Theorem 2.4. Extensions of a diagram of schemes are classified by Ext^1 of its cotangent complex, with automorphisms Ext^0, also computable on the big fppf site.
  • Deformations of a morphism of schemes — Lemma 3.2. A thickening of a morphism of schemes has an isomorphism on ideals exactly when its square is cartesian and a Tor_1 group vanishes.
  • Deformations of squares — Corollary 3.7. Deformations of commutative squares of schemes carry cartesian squares to cartesian squares.
  • Deformations of a morphism over a thickened target — Theorem 4.3. Lifting a morphism of diagrams over a square-zero thickening of its target is obstructed in Ext^2 by the product of the Kodaira-Spencer class and the class of the thickening; lifts form an Ext^1-torsor.
  • Deformed diagrams of morphisms — Proposition 4.4. A deformation of a morphism over a deformation of its target deforms the diagram the morphism defines and keeps its cartesian squares cartesian.
  • Nerves of action categories — Lemma 1.3. The nerve of the action category of a category scheme acting on a sheaf is the pullback of the nerve of the category scheme along its first vertex.
  • Equivariant sheaves over an equivariant sheaf — Proposition 1.4. Sheaves equivariant for the action category of a G-sheaf L are the G-sheaves over L.
  • The opposite action category — Proposition 1.5. The nerve functor from the bounded derived category of the opposite action category to the fppf total topos of the nerve is fully faithful, with an explicit essential image.
  • Action groupoids — Proposition 1.6. For a groupoid scheme, inversion identifies the action category with its opposite.
  • Tor independence and base change — Lemma 2.2. When P and R are Tor independent over Q, Tor independence of P and R' over Q is Tor independence of P x_Q R and R' over R.
  • The equivariant cotangent complex — Theorem 2.4. For a morphism of G-schemes Tor independent of G, the truncated pullbacks of the cotangent complex of the nerves descend uniquely to the derived category of the opposite action category.
  • Properties of the equivariant cotangent complex — Proposition 2.5. The equivariant cotangent complex has the expected underlying complex, is bounded for local complete intersections, and is the trivial pullback for trivial actions.
  • Ext groups of the equivariant cotangent complex — Proposition 3.2. Ext groups of the equivariant cotangent complex into quasi-coherent equivariant modules are Ext groups of the cotangent complex of the nerves on the Zariski total topos.
  • Equivariant Kodaira-Spencer class — Proposition 3.3. Morphisms X to Y to Z of G-schemes give equivariant transitivity maps, the equivariant Kodaira-Spencer class and long exact sequences of Ext groups.
  • Change of the category scheme — Proposition 4.1. Restriction along an internal functor G' to G gives a base change map of equivariant cotangent complexes, an isomorphism under Tor independence.
  • Closed immersions into smooth schemes — Proposition 1.2. For a closed immersion into a smooth scheme, the first truncation of the cotangent complex is isomorphic to the conormal complex, and the Kodaira-Spencer class is a universal sign times the differential on H^-1.
  • Equivariant conormal complexes — Proposition 1.3. For an equivariant closed immersion into a smooth G-scheme, the truncated equivariant cotangent complex is isomorphic to the equivariant conormal complex.
  • Recognizing nerves of action categories — Lemma 2.1. Simplicial objects over N(G) whose levels are the pullbacks of level 0 along the first vertex are nerves of action categories.
  • Equivariant extensions and extensions of nerves — Proposition 2.3. Equivariant extensions are the extensions of the nerves whose ideal is cartesian along the faces fixing the first vertex; Tor independence makes the condition automatic.
  • Classification of equivariant extensions — Theorem 2.4. Equivariant extensions of a G-scheme are classified by Ext^1 of the equivariant cotangent complex, with automorphisms Ext^0.
  • Equivariant deformations and nerves — Proposition 3.2. Equivariant deformations of a morphism are the deformations of its nerve over the nerve of the thickened target.
  • Equivariant deformations of a morphism — Theorem 3.3. Equivariant deformations over an equivariant extension are obstructed in Ext^2 of the equivariant cotangent complex by the product of the equivariant Kodaira-Spencer class and the class of the extension; they form an Ext^1-torsor.
  • Spectral sequence of the obstruction — Proposition 4.1. A spectral sequence over the nerve computes the equivariant Ext groups, and its edge map carries the equivariant obstruction to the obstruction for the underlying morphism of schemes.
  • Lifting maps to square-zero extensions — Lemma 5.1. A ring map lifts to given square-zero extensions exactly when two classes in Ext^1 of the cotangent complex agree; the lifts form a torsor under derivations.
  • Morphisms between equivariant extensions — Proposition 5.2. An equivariant morphism lifts to given equivariant extensions exactly when a class in Ext^1 vanishes; the lifts form an Ext^0-torsor.
  • Frobenius kernels — Lemma 1.1. The n-fold relative Frobenius of a group scheme over a field is a homomorphism and a universal homeomorphism; its kernel is a finite connected normal subgroup scheme, compatible with closed subgroups.
  • The first Frobenius kernel — Lemma 1.2. The first Frobenius kernel of a group scheme locally of finite type over a field is a truncated polynomial algebra, hence a local complete intersection.
  • Quotients by Frobenius kernels — Lemma 2.1. Over a perfect field, the quotient of a group scheme of finite type by a large enough Frobenius kernel is the reduced subgroup of a Frobenius twist, hence smooth.
  • Group schemes over a field are local complete intersections — Theorem 2.3. Every group scheme locally of finite type over a field is a local complete intersection over the field.
  • Flat group schemes are syntomic — Theorem 3.1. A group scheme flat and locally of finite presentation over a scheme is syntomic, and its cotangent complex is locally a two-term complex of finite free modules in degrees -1 and 0.
  • The cotangent complex at the unit — Corollary 3.2. For a group scheme over a field, the derived pullback of the cotangent complex to the unit has H^0 dual to the Lie algebra and H^-1 of dimension dim Lie - dim G; it vanishes exactly for smooth groups.
  • Trivial torsors — Lemma 1.2. A torsor with a section is isomorphic to the trivial torsor through the section.
  • Torsors and Cech nerves — Proposition 1.3. A torsor under a flat group scheme locally of finite presentation is syntomic and surjective, and the nerve of the action is the Cech nerve of the torsor.
  • Descent along a torsor — Proposition 2.2. Pullback is an equivalence from modules on the big fppf site of the base onto equivariant modules on the torsor, with quasi-inverse the invariant sections.
  • Descent on big sites — Theorem 2.4. Pullback to the nerve of a torsor is fully faithful on bounded derived categories of the big fppf sites, with essential image the quasi-cartesian objects.
  • Descent on Zariski sites — Theorem 2.5. Pullback to the nerve of a torsor is fully faithful on bounded complexes with quasi-coherent cohomology on the Zariski sites, with essential image the quasi-cartesian objects with termwise quasi-coherent cohomology.
  • Direct images, sections and equivariant complexes — Corollary 2.6. A descended complex is the derived direct image of its pullback to the nerve, its pullback along a section, and the invariant direct image of its equivariant form.
  • Truncating a distinguished triangle — Lemma 3.2. Truncating the first two vertices of a distinguished triangle whose third vertex lies in degrees at least n gives a distinguished triangle.
  • The co-Lie complex and the Atiyah triangle — Theorem 3.3. The cotangent complex of the nerves of a torsor descends to a perfect complex of tor amplitude [-1,0] on the base, and the transitivity triangle descends to the Atiyah triangle, whose third map is the Atiyah class.
  • Base change of the co-Lie complex — Proposition 3.4. The co-Lie complex of a torsor commutes with arbitrary base change of the base.
  • Equivariant complexes of a torsor — Proposition 3.5. The equivariant cotangent complexes of a torsor are the pullbacks of its co-Lie complexes, and the equivariant Kodaira-Spencer class is the pullback of the Atiyah class.
  • The co-Lie complex of a smooth group — Proposition 4.1. For a smooth group scheme the co-Lie complex of a torsor is the co-Lie module twisted by the torsor through the coadjoint action.
  • The Atiyah sequence — Proposition 4.2. For a smooth group scheme the Atiyah triangle gives an exact sequence of invariant differential forms, whose class is the Atiyah class when the base is smooth.
  • Deformations of a torsor are torsors — Lemma 1.2. Equivariant deformations of a torsor over a first-order thickening of the base are torsors, and conversely.
  • The class of the thickened nerve — Lemma 2.1. The class of the thickening of the nerve of the base is the pullback of the class of the thickening.
  • Deformations of torsors — Theorem 2.2. Deformations of a torsor over a first-order thickening are obstructed in Ext^2 of the co-Lie complex by the product of the class of the thickening and the Atiyah class; they form an Ext^1-torsor with automorphism groups Ext^0.
  • Duality and smooth groups — Proposition 3.1. The groups classifying deformations of a torsor are cohomology groups of the dual of the co-Lie complex; torsors over affine bases always deform, and for smooth groups the coefficients are the adjoint bundle.
  • The co-Lie complex of a group scheme — Proposition 1.2. The co-Lie complex of a flat group scheme locally of finite presentation is perfect of tor amplitude [-1,0], has the invariant differentials as H^0, and is the co-Lie complex of the group as a torsor.
  • Base change of the co-Lie complex of a group — Proposition 1.4. The co-Lie complex of a flat group scheme commutes with arbitrary base change.
  • Smoothness and the co-Lie complex — Proposition 1.5. A flat group scheme locally of finite presentation is smooth exactly when its co-Lie complex has tor amplitude [0,0].
  • Exact sequences and torsors — Lemma 2.1. In an exact sequence of flat group schemes the quotient map is a torsor under the kernel.
  • The triangle of an exact sequence — Proposition 2.2. An exact sequence of flat group schemes gives a distinguished triangle of co-Lie complexes, with the functoriality maps in degree 0.
  • The long exact sequence of co-Lie complexes — Corollary 2.3. An exact sequence of flat group schemes gives a six-term exact sequence of the cohomology of co-Lie complexes; the map from the quotient is an isomorphism exactly when the kernel is etale.
  • Descent along a torsor over a diagram — Theorem 1.4. For a torsor under a flat group scheme over a diagram of schemes, pullback to the nerve of the action is fully faithful on bounded complexes with quasi-coherent cohomology, with an explicit essential image.
  • The co-Lie complex of a torsor over a diagram — Theorem 1.5. A torsor over a diagram of schemes has a quasi-cartesian co-Lie complex with perfect levels and an Atiyah triangle, restricting levelwise to those of the torsors over the schemes of the diagram.
  • The adjoint co-Lie complex — Proposition 2.2. The co-Lie complex of a flat group scheme carries an action of the group, obtained by descent along the universal torsor over the nerve, as an object of the derived category of the classifying topos.
  • The coadjoint action on invariant forms — Proposition 2.4. On invariant differential forms the action is the coadjoint action, and for a smooth group the adjoint co-Lie complex is this module in degree 0.
  • The cotangent complex of the nerve of a group scheme — Theorem 3.3. The cotangent complex of the nerve of a flat group scheme sits in a distinguished triangle between a copy of the co-Lie complex at each vertex and the adjoint co-Lie complex.
  • Relative Ext groups of the adjoint co-Lie complex — Corollary 3.5. The Ext groups of the cotangent complex of the nerve are relative Ext groups of the adjoint co-Lie complex on the classifying topos, computed by a spectral sequence from Ext groups on the powers of the group.
  • Deformations of group schemes and of their nerves — Proposition 1.2. Deformations of a flat group scheme over a first-order thickening are the same as deformations of the morphism from its nerve to the base.
  • Deformations of flat group schemes — Theorem 2.1. A flat group scheme deforms over a first-order thickening exactly when an obstruction in Ext^2 of the cotangent complex of its nerve vanishes; deformations then form a torsor under Ext^1, with automorphisms Ext^0.
  • Smooth affine groups and Hochschild cochains — Proposition 2.4. For a smooth affine group over an affine base, the deformation groups are the cohomology of cochains of the group with values in its Lie algebra tensored with the ideal.
  • Extending homomorphisms to deformations — Theorem 3.1. A homomorphism of flat group schemes extends to given deformations exactly when a class in Ext^1 vanishes; the extensions then form a torsor under Ext^0.
  • Deforming a homomorphism with a given target — Theorem 3.3. Deformations of a flat group scheme together with an extension of a homomorphism to a given deformation of the target are classified by Ext groups of the relative cotangent complex of the nerves.
  • The shear isomorphism — Proposition 1.4. For a group scheme H over a diagram Y, the cotangent complex of H over Y is the pullback of the co-Lie complex of H.
  • Additivity of the co-Lie complex — Lemma 1.5. The co-Lie complex of a product is the sum, and for commutative group schemes a sum of homomorphisms induces the sum of the maps of co-Lie complexes.
  • The tensor formula for Lie complexes — Proposition 1.6. For a group scheme over a diagram, the internal mixed Hom from its co-Lie complex into a bounded complex is the derived tensor product with its Lie complex.
  • Torsors under a commutative group — Theorem 2.2. The co-Lie complex of a torsor under a commutative group scheme G, over a scheme or a diagram of schemes, is the pullback of the co-Lie complex of G.
  • The adjoint co-Lie complex of a commutative group — Corollary 2.3. For a commutative group scheme the adjoint co-Lie complex is the co-Lie complex with the trivial action.
  • The Lie complex of the nerve — Proposition 3.1. The Lie complex of the nerve of a commutative group scheme is the nerve of its Lie complex.
  • The Lie complex of the spectrum — Proposition 3.5. The Lie complex of the spectrum of a commutative group scheme is the spectrum of its Lie complex.
  • Second nerves on spectral pseudo-categories — Lemma 2.1. On the pseudo-category of a graded action, the second nerve is fully faithful on bounded derived categories, with image the objects whose cohomology is cartesian along the maps fixing the first vertex.
  • The Lie complex of a module scheme — Proposition 3.2. The Lie complex of a module scheme over a ring scheme A has a canonical structure of a complex over the derived tensor product of A and the structure ring, determined by the spectral nerve.
  • Exact sequences of module schemes — Proposition 3.5. An exact sequence of module schemes gives a distinguished triangle of their Lie complexes over the stable ring with coefficients.
  • The Ext formula — Theorem 4.1. The Ext groups of the cotangent complex of the spectral nerve of a module scheme are Ext groups of modules into its Lie complex with coefficients.
  • Relative Ext groups of the spectral nerve — Theorem 4.3. Relative Ext groups of the cotangent complex for a homomorphism of module schemes are Ext groups of modules out of its cone.
  • Deformations of module schemes and of their spectral nerves — Proposition 1.2. Deformations of a module scheme over a first-order thickening are the same as deformations of the morphism from its spectral nerve to that of the base.
  • Deformations of module schemes — Theorem 2.1. A module scheme deforms exactly when an obstruction in Ext^2 of modules into its Lie complex tensored with the ideal vanishes; deformations then form a torsor under Ext^1, with automorphisms Ext^0.
  • Extending homomorphisms of module schemes — Theorem 2.2. A homomorphism of module schemes extends to given deformations exactly when a class in Ext^1 vanishes; the extensions then form a torsor under Ext^0.
  • Deforming a homomorphism of module schemes with a given target — Theorem 2.3. Deformations of a module scheme together with an extension of a homomorphism to a given deformation of the target are classified by Ext groups of modules into the fibre of the map of Lie complexes.

Reading order

Forty-four lessons, to be read in order. The first three develop homotopical algebra in a ringed topos; the fourth to sixth treat simplicial objects, nerves and colimits over categories of ordinals; the seventh and eighth construct the total topoi of fibred topoi and their modules, the ninth to eleventh compute their cohomology, the twelfth and thirteenth construct the left derived functors of the left adjoints of inverse images of modules, on both total topoi, the fourteenth proves a duality formula for diagrams in a topos, the fifteenth to eighteenth treat equivariant sheaves on simplicial topoi and their cohomology, the nineteenth to twenty-first construct spectral diagrams, the stabilization of non-additive functors and stable derived functors, the twenty-second to twenty-fifth develop the homotopy theory of simplicial modules over a simplicial ring: the calculus of fractions, triangles and Ext groups, derived tensor products and their spectral sequences, the twenty-sixth treats differential graded modules in a topos and compares them with simplicial modules, the twenty-seventh and twenty-eighth treat graded monoids and the nerves of their actions, graded rings and the spectra of modules, the twenty-ninth proves the adjunction formulas for diagonal rings, the thirtieth constructs the stable ring of a ring and the Mac Lane resolution, the thirty-first expresses Ext groups of modules as cohomology of spectral nerves, the thirty-second compares the Zariski and the big fppf sites of schemes and of diagrams of schemes for quasi-coherent and perfect complexes, the thirty-third classifies extensions and deformations of diagrams of schemes and of their morphisms, the thirty-fourth constructs equivariant cotangent complexes, the thirty-fifth classifies equivariant extensions and deformations, the thirty-sixth proves that flat group schemes locally of finite presentation are local complete intersections, the thirty-seventh treats torsors, descent along them, co-Lie complexes and the Atiyah class, the thirty-eighth classifies deformations of torsors, the thirty-ninth treats the co-Lie complex of a group scheme, the fortieth constructs its adjoint form and computes the cotangent complex of the nerve of a group scheme, the forty-first classifies deformations of flat group schemes and of their homomorphisms, the forty-second computes the Lie complexes of commutative group schemes, of their torsors, nerves and spectra, the forty-third constructs the Lie complexes of module schemes and computes the Ext groups of their spectral nerves, and the forty-fourth classifies deformations of module schemes and of their homomorphisms. The simplicial homotopy theory and Quillen's Theorem A are taken from the course Algebraic spaces and stacks; sites, modules and derived categories from the Stacks project.
