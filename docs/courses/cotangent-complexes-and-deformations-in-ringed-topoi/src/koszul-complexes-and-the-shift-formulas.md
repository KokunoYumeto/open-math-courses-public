# Koszul complexes and the shift formulas

*Written by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), October 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. Public domain (CC0).*

Shifting a complex by one degree turns symmetric powers into exterior powers and exterior powers into divided powers:
\[
\begin{gathered}LS^n(E[1])\cong(L\Lambda^nE)[n],\\ L\Lambda^n(E[1])\cong(L\Gamma^nE)[n].\end{gathered}
\]
These formulas convert questions about \(L\Lambda^n\) of the cotangent complex, which appear in its Chern classes and its de Rham theory, into questions about symmetric powers, and back. They come from Koszul complexes. An exact sequence \(0\to E\to F\to G\to0\) of flat modules gives exact sequences \(0\to\Lambda^nE\to\cdots\to S^nF\to S^nG\to0\). These are applied to the sequence \(0\to E\to C(E)\to E[1]\to0\) whose middle term, the cone of the identity, is contractible.

This lesson constructs the two Koszul complexes of a map of modules, proves their exactness for exact sequences of flat modules over a ring of a topos, and proves the shift formulas with their compatibility with products.

**Prerequisites.** [*Non-additive functors of complexes in a ringed topos*](non-additive-functors-of-complexes-in-a-ringed-topos.md), Sections 2–5; [*Homotopy sheaves and the free abelian Whitehead theorem*](homotopy-sheaves-and-the-free-abelian-whitehead-theorem.md), Section 5; [*Complexes, cones and localization*](course:derived-categories-and-sheaf-operations/complexes-cones-and-localization), Lemma 1.2 and Proposition 5.2.

## Conventions

\(A\) is a sheaf of commutative rings on a site \(\mathcal C\). Tensor products are over \(A\). For an \(A\)-module \(M\), \(S(M)\), \(\Lambda(M)\) and \(\Gamma(M)\) are the symmetric, exterior and divided power algebras: the sheaves associated with the constructions on sections. \(\Gamma(M)\) is generated by symbols \(\gamma_k(x)\), \(x\in M\), \(k\geq0\), subject to \(\gamma_0(x)=1\), \(\gamma_1(x)=x\), \(\gamma_k(ax)=a^k\gamma_k(x)\), \(\gamma_k(x+y)=\sum_{i+j=k}\gamma_i(x)\gamma_j(y)\) and \(\gamma_i(x)\gamma_j(x)=\binom{i+j}{i}\gamma_{i+j}(x)\). For a complex \(X\), \(X[m]^k=X^{k+m}\) with differential \((-1)^md\).

## 1. The two Koszul complexes

A bigraded algebra \(R=\bigoplus R^{p,q}\) is *anticommutative in the first degree* if \(x'x=(-1)^{pp'}xx'\) for \(x\in R^{p,q}\), \(x'\in R^{p',q'}\), and similarly in the second degree. A derivation of bidegree \((-1,1)\) of an algebra anticommutative in the first degree is an \(A\)-linear map \(d\) of that bidegree with \(d(xx')=(dx)x'+(-1)^px(dx')\) for \(x\in R^{p,q}\).

**Proposition 1.1.** Let \(u\colon E\to F\) be a map of \(A\)-modules.

1. On \(\Lambda E\otimes SF\), with \(\Lambda^pE\otimes S^qF\) in bidegree \((p,q)\), there is a unique derivation \(d\) of bidegree \((-1,1)\) with \(d(x\otimes1)=1\otimes u(x)\) and \(d(1\otimes y)=0\). It satisfies \(d\circ d=0\).
2. On \(\Gamma E\otimes\Lambda F\), with \(\Gamma^pE\otimes\Lambda^qF\) in bidegree \((p,q)\), there is a unique derivation \(d\) of bidegree \((-1,1)\), for the sign rule of the second degree, with \(d(\gamma_k(x)\otimes1)=\gamma_{k-1}(x)\otimes u(x)\) and \(d(1\otimes z)=0\). It satisfies \(d\circ d=0\).

**Proof.** Uniqueness holds because the elements named generate the algebras. We prove existence on sections; the constructions are compatible with restriction and pass to the associated sheaves.

(1) For \(x_1,\ldots,x_p\in E\) and \(y\in S^qF\), put
\[
\begin{aligned}&d(x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge x_p\otimes y)\\&\quad=\sum_{i=1}^p(-1)^{i-1}x_1\wedge\cdots\widehat{x_i}\cdots\wedge x_p\\&\qquad\qquad\otimes u(x_i)y.\end{aligned}
\]
The right side is multilinear and alternating in the \(x_i\): exchanging \(x_i\) and \(x_{i+1}\) exchanges two terms and changes all signs, and if \(x_i=x_{i+1}\) the two terms cancel and the others contain \(x_i\wedge x_{i+1}=0\). So \(d\) is well defined. The derivation rule holds by the same formula. The composite \(d\circ d\) is a derivation of even degree that vanishes on \(x\otimes1\) and on \(1\otimes y\), so it is zero. (In \(d^2\) of a monomial, the terms removing \(x_i\) and then \(x_j\) and those removing \(x_j\) and then \(x_i\) carry opposite signs and equal values, because \(S F\) is commutative.)

(2) A derivation of \(\Gamma E\) into a \(\Gamma E\)-module is determined by its values on the generators \(\gamma_k(x)\), and exists if and only if these values respect the defining relations. With the values \(D\gamma_k(x)=\gamma_{k-1}(x)\otimes u(x)\in\Gamma E\otimes F\), the relations hold:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&D\gamma_k(ax)=a^{k-1}\gamma_{k-1}(x)\otimes au(x)\\&\quad=a^kD\gamma_k(x),\\
&D\gamma_k(x+y)\\&\quad=\sum_{i+j=k-1}\gamma_i(x)\gamma_j(y)\otimes(u(x)+u(y))\\&\quad=D\Bigl(\sum_{i+j=k}\gamma_i(x)\gamma_j(y)\Bigr),\\
&D\bigl(\gamma_i(x)\gamma_j(x)\bigr)\\&\quad=\Bigl(\tbinom{i+j-1}{i-1}+\tbinom{i+j-1}{i}\Bigr)\\&\qquad\quad\cdot\gamma_{i+j-1}(x)\otimes u(x)\\&\quad=\tbinom{i+j}{i}D\gamma_{i+j}(x).
\end{aligned}
\]
Extend \(D\) to \(\Gamma E\otimes\Lambda F\) by \(d(a\otimes z)=D(a)\wedge z\), where the \(F\)-component of \(D(a)\) is multiplied into \(\Lambda F\) on the left. This is a derivation for the sign rule of the second degree. The composite \(d\circ d\) is a derivation that kills the generators: \(d^2\gamma_k(x)=\gamma_{k-2}(x)\otimes u(x)\wedge u(x)=0\). Hence \(d\circ d=0\). \(\square\)

We write \(\operatorname{Kos}_\bullet(u)=(\Lambda E\otimes SF,d)\) and \(\operatorname{Kos}^\bullet(u)=(\Gamma E\otimes\Lambda F,d)\), and \(k^n\) for the part of total degree \(p+q=n\). The first is a chain complex with the exterior degree as homological degree, the second a cochain complex with the exterior degree as cohomological degree. A commutative square from \(u\) to \(u'\) induces maps of differential bigraded algebras \(\operatorname{Kos}(u)\to\operatorname{Kos}(u')\).

**Lemma 1.2.** For \(u\colon E\to F\) and \(u'\colon E'\to F'\), the isomorphisms
\[
\begin{aligned}&\Lambda(E\oplus E')\otimes S(F\oplus F')\\&\quad\cong(\Lambda E\otimes SF)\otimes(\Lambda E'\otimes SF'),\\&\Gamma(E\oplus E')\otimes\Lambda(F\oplus F')\\&\quad\cong(\Gamma E\otimes\Lambda F)\otimes(\Gamma E'\otimes\Lambda F')\end{aligned}
\]
carry the Koszul differential of \(u\oplus u'\) to the tensor-product differential.

**Proof.** Both sides are derivations of the same bidegree, and they agree on the generators \(x\otimes1\), \(x'\otimes1\), \(\gamma_k(x)\otimes1\) and \(\gamma_k(x')\otimes1\), and on \(F\), \(F'\). \(\square\)

**Construction 1.3.** Let \(E\xrightarrow{u}F\xrightarrow{v}G\) be maps with \(vu=0\).

1. \(\operatorname{Kos}_\bullet(u)\to SG\), zero on \(\Lambda^{\ge1}E\otimes SF\) and \(S(v)\) on \(SF\), is a map of differential bigraded algebras: \(d(x\otimes1)=u(x)\) is sent to \(vu(x)=0\).
2. \(\Gamma E\to\operatorname{Kos}^\bullet(v)\), \(\gamma_k(x)\mapsto\gamma_k(u(x))\otimes1\), is one as well: \(d\gamma_k(u(x))\) equals \(\gamma_{k-1}(u(x))\otimes vu(x)\), which is \(0\).

## 2. Exactness

**Theorem 2.1.** Let \(0\to E\xrightarrow{u}F\xrightarrow{v}G\to0\) be an exact sequence of flat \(A\)-modules. Then the maps of Construction 1.3 are quasi-isomorphisms. In total degree \(n\geq1\) they give the exact *Koszul sequences*
\[
\begin{gathered}
0\to\Lambda^nE\to\Lambda^{n-1}E\otimes F\to\cdots\\\cdots\to E\otimes S^{n-1}F\to S^nF\to S^nG\to0,\\[4pt]
0\to\Gamma^nE\to\Gamma^nF\to\Gamma^{n-1}F\otimes G\to\cdots\\\cdots\to F\otimes\Lambda^{n-1}G\to\Lambda^nG\to0.
\end{gathered}
\]

**Proof.** *Finite free split case.* Suppose \(F=E\oplus G\) with \(u\) and \(v\) the inclusion and projection, and \(E\), \(G\) finite free. Then \(u=1_E\oplus(0\to G)\) and \(v=(E\to0)\oplus1_G\). By Lemma 1.2,
\[
\begin{gathered}\operatorname{Kos}_\bullet(u)\cong\operatorname{Kos}_\bullet(1_E)\otimes SG,\\\operatorname{Kos}^\bullet(v)\cong\Gamma E\otimes\operatorname{Kos}^\bullet(1_G),\end{gathered}
\]
with zero differential on \(SG\) and on \(\Gamma E\), and the maps of Construction 1.3 are the projections onto, respectively the inclusions of, the parts of degree \(0\) of \(\operatorname{Kos}(1)\). It suffices to show that \(k^n\operatorname{Kos}_\bullet(1_M)\) and \(k^n\operatorname{Kos}^\bullet(1_M)\) are acyclic for \(n\geq1\) and \(M\) finite free. By Lemma 1.2, \(\operatorname{Kos}(1_{M'\oplus M''})=\operatorname{Kos}(1_{M'})\otimes\operatorname{Kos}(1_{M''})\). Its part of degree \(n\) is a sum of tensor products \(k^a\otimes k^b\) with \(a+b=n\), each with an acyclic factor if \(a\) or \(b\) is positive. These factors are bounded complexes of finite free modules, so locally contractible, and the tensor products are acyclic. So it suffices to treat \(M=A\). There \(k^n\operatorname{Kos}_\bullet(1_A)\) is \(\Lambda^1A\otimes S^{n-1}A\to S^nA\), \(e\otimes t^{n-1}\mapsto t^n\), an isomorphism. Likewise \(k^n\operatorname{Kos}^\bullet(1_A)\) is \(\Gamma^nA\to\Gamma^{n-1}A\otimes\Lambda^1A\), \(\gamma_n(1)\mapsto\gamma_{n-1}(1)\otimes e\), an isomorphism.

*General case.* The sequence is a bounded acyclic complex of flat modules. By Proposition 3.3 of [*Non-additive functors of complexes in a ringed topos*](non-additive-functors-of-complexes-in-a-ringed-topos.md), it is the local inductive limit of contractible complexes \(0\to E_i\to F_i\to G_i\to0\) of finite free modules over objects \(U_i\). Such a complex is split exact, and locally its terms decompose as in the first case. The Koszul complexes and the maps of Construction 1.3 are built from \(S\), \(\Lambda\), \(\Gamma\), tensor products and finite sums, which commute with local inductive limits. Local inductive limits are exact (Section 5 of [*Homotopy sheaves and the free abelian Whitehead theorem*](homotopy-sheaves-and-the-free-abelian-whitehead-theorem.md)). The cones of the maps are local inductive limits of locally acyclic complexes, so they are acyclic. \(\square\)

**Proposition 2.2 (duality).** Let \(E\), \(F\) be finite free and \(M^\vee=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(M,A)\). Pair \(\Lambda^pE\) with \(\Lambda^p(E^\vee)\) by
\[
\begin{aligned}&\langle x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge x_p,\xi_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\xi_p\rangle\\&\quad=\det(\xi_j(x_i)),\end{aligned}
\]
and \(S^qF\) with \(\Gamma^q(F^\vee)\) by
\[
\begin{aligned}&\langle y_1\cdots y_q,\gamma_{k_1}(\eta_1)\cdots\gamma_{k_r}(\eta_r)\rangle\\&\quad=\sum_{\varphi}\prod_{i=1}^q\eta_{\varphi(i)}(y_i),\end{aligned}
\]
the sum running over the maps \(\varphi\colon\{1,\ldots,q\}\to\{1,\ldots,r\}\) with \(|\varphi^{-1}(j)|=k_j\). Pair \(\Lambda^pE\otimes S^qF\) with \(\Gamma^q(F^\vee)\otimes\Lambda^p(E^\vee)\) by the product of the two pairings. These pairings are perfect, and for \(u\colon E\to F\),
\[
\langle d\alpha,\beta\rangle=\langle\alpha,d\beta\rangle
\]
for \(\alpha\in\Lambda^pE\otimes S^qF\) and \(\beta\in\Gamma^{q+1}(F^\vee)\otimes\Lambda^{p-1}(E^\vee)\), where the second \(d\) is that of \(\operatorname{Kos}^\bullet(u^\vee)\). So \(k^n\operatorname{Kos}_\bullet(u)\) is the dual complex of \(k^n\operatorname{Kos}^\bullet(u^\vee)\).

**Proof.** Take a basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_s\) of \(E\), a basis \(f_1,\ldots,f_r\) of \(F\) and the dual bases, and write \(u(e_i)=\sum_ja_{ji}f_j\). The monomials \(e_I=e_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_p}\) (\(i_1<\cdots<i_p\)) and \(e_I^\vee\) pair to \(\delta_{IJ}\). For multi-indices \(\rho,\nu\) the monomial \(f^\rho\) and the divided monomial \(\gamma_\nu(f^\vee)=\prod_j\gamma_{\nu_j}(f_j^\vee)\) pair to \(\delta_{\rho\nu}\): if \(f^\rho\) is written as a product of \(q\) basis vectors, exactly one \(\varphi\) sends every factor to its own index when \(\rho=\nu\), and none otherwise. Both pairings are therefore perfect.

Let \(\alpha=e_I\otimes f^\mu\) and \(\beta=\gamma_\nu(f^\vee)\otimes e_J^\vee\) with \(|I|=p\), \(|J|=p-1\), \(|\nu|=|\mu|+1\). Both sides vanish unless \(J=I\smallsetminus\{i_0\}\) and \(\nu=\mu+\delta_j\) for some \(i_0\) and \(j\). Then
\[
d\alpha=\sum_{t}(-1)^{t-1}e_{I\smallsetminus\{i_t\}}\otimes u(e_{i_t})f^\mu
\]
gives \(\langle d\alpha,\beta\rangle=(-1)^{t_0-1}a_{ji_0}\), where \(i_0=i_{t_0}\). On the other side \(d\gamma_\nu(f^\vee)=\sum_k\gamma_{\nu-\delta_k}(f^\vee)\otimes u^\vee(f_k^\vee)\) with \(u^\vee(f_k^\vee)=\sum_ia_{ki}e_i^\vee\), so \(\langle\alpha,d\beta\rangle=a_{ji_0}\langle e_I,e_{i_0}^\vee\wedge e_J^\vee\rangle\). Moving \(e_{i_0}^\vee\) past the \(t_0-1\) smaller indices of \(J\) gives the sign \((-1)^{t_0-1}\). \(\square\)

## 3. The shift formulas

For a complex \(E\) of flat modules bounded above and \(n\geq1\), put \(\mathbf T(E)=\operatorname{Tot}N\mathbf T\Gamma\,iE\) for \(\mathbf T=S^n,\Lambda^n,\Gamma^n\), as in Section 2.2 of [*Non-additive functors of complexes in a ringed topos*](non-additive-functors-of-complexes-in-a-ringed-topos.md). By Theorem 4.4 there, \(L\mathbf T(E)=\mathbf T(E)\).

**Lemma 3.1.** Let \(0\to P_n\to I_{n-1}\to\cdots\to I_0\to Q\to0\) be an exact sequence of complexes in which every \(I_j\) is acyclic. Then the sequence defines an isomorphism \(Q\cong P_n[n]\) in the derived category.

**Proof.** Split the sequence into short exact sequences \(0\to K_{j+1}\to I_j\to K_j\to0\), with \(K_0=Q\) and \(K_n=P_n\). Each gives a distinguished triangle (Proposition 5.2 of [*Complexes, cones and localization*](course:derived-categories-and-sheaf-operations/complexes-cones-and-localization)). Since \(I_j\) is acyclic, its connecting morphism \(K_j\to K_{j+1}[1]\) is an isomorphism. Composing these isomorphisms gives \(Q\cong P_n[n]\). \(\square\)

If the resolution is \(X\to Q\) with \(X=(P_n\to I_{n-1}\to\cdots\to I_0)\) placed in degrees \([-n,0]\), this isomorphism is the composite of the inverse of the quasi-isomorphism \(X\to Q\) with the projection \(X\to X^{-n}[n]=P_n[n]\). The stupid truncation keeping degrees \(\le-n\) is a quotient complex.

**Theorem 3.2.** For \(E\in D^-(A)\) and \(n\ge1\) there are canonical isomorphisms
\[
\begin{aligned}&\text{(i)}\quad LS^n(E[1])\cong(L\Lambda^nE)[n],\\&\text{(ii)}\quad L\Lambda^n(E[1])\cong(L\Gamma^nE)[n].\end{aligned}
\]

**Proof.** Represent \(E\) by a bounded-above complex of flat modules. Let \(C(E)\) be the cone of \(1_E\); it is contractible. There is an exact sequence of complexes of flat modules
\[
0\to E\to C(E)\to E[1]\to0,
\]
split in each degree. Apply the exact functor \(\Gamma\,i\): this gives an exact sequence of mixed objects of flat modules, \(0\to\Gamma\,iE\to\Gamma\,iC(E)\to\Gamma\,i(E[1])\to0\). Apply Theorem 2.1 term by term, then \(\operatorname{Tot}N\), which is exact. For (i) this gives an exact sequence of complexes
\[
\begin{gathered}0\to\Lambda^nE\to I_{n-1}\to\cdots\\\cdots\to I_0\to S^n(E[1])\to0,\end{gathered}
\]
with \(I_{n-p}=\operatorname{Tot}N\bigl(\Lambda^{n-p}\Gamma\,iE\otimes S^p\Gamma\,iC(E)\bigr)\) for \(1\le p\le n\). By (5.1) of the same lesson, \(I_{n-p}\) is homotopy equivalent to \(\Lambda^{n-p}E\otimes S^pC(E)\). Since \(C(E)\) is homotopy equivalent to \(0\) and \(S^p(0)=0\), Proposition 2.3 there makes \(S^pC(E)\) contractible, so \(I_{n-p}\) is acyclic. Lemma 3.1 gives \(S^n(E[1])\cong\Lambda^nE[n]\), which is (i). The second Koszul sequence gives (ii) in the same way, the intermediate terms being \(\Gamma^{n-p}C(E)\otimes\Lambda^p(E[1])\) with \(p<n\). Naturality in \(E\) follows from the naturality of every step, and independence of the flat representative from Theorem 4.4 there. \(\square\)

**Products.** Let \(\tau\colon X[m]\otimes Y[n]\to(X\otimes Y)[m+n]\), \(x\otimes y\mapsto(-1)^{n\,|x|}x\otimes y\), where \(|x|\) is the degree of \(x\) in \(X[m]\). This is an isomorphism of complexes.

**Lemma 3.3.** Let \(f\colon X\to P\) and \(g\colon Y\to Q\) be quasi-isomorphisms from complexes of flat modules concentrated in \([-m,0]\) and \([-n,0]\), and let \(a\colon P\to X^{-m}[m]\), \(b\colon Q\to Y^{-n}[n]\) and \(c\colon P\otimes Q\to(X^{-m}\otimes Y^{-n})[m+n]\) be the morphisms in the derived category defined by \(f\), \(g\) and \(f\otimes g\) as after Lemma 3.1. Then \(c=(-1)^{mn}\tau\circ(a\otimes b)\).

**Proof.** The projection of \(X\otimes Y\) onto its lowest term is the identity of \(X^{-m}\otimes Y^{-n}\) in degree \(-m-n\). The tensor product of the two projections, followed by \(\tau\), multiplies an element of \(X^{-m}\otimes Y^{-n}\) by \((-1)^{n\cdot(-m)}=(-1)^{mn}\), because such an element has degree \(-m\) in \(X^{-m}[m]\). Both sides use the inverse of the quasi-isomorphism \(f\otimes g\). \(\square\)

**Proposition 3.4.** The isomorphisms of Theorem 3.2 are compatible with the products of Section 5 of [*Non-additive functors of complexes in a ringed topos*](non-additive-functors-of-complexes-in-a-ringed-topos.md), up to the sign \((-1)^{mn}\). For (i), for example: the product
\[
\begin{aligned}&LS^m(E[1])\otimes^{\mathbf L}LS^n(E[1])\\&\quad\to LS^{m+n}(E[1])\end{aligned}
\]
corresponds to \((-1)^{mn}\) times the product
\[
\begin{aligned}&L\Lambda^mE[m]\otimes^{\mathbf L}L\Lambda^nE[n]\\&\quad\to L\Lambda^{m+n}E[m+n],\end{aligned}
\]
composed with \(\tau\).

**Proof.** We treat (i); (ii) is the same with the second Koszul complex. Write \(X_m\) for the resolution of \(S^m(E[1])\) used in the proof of Theorem 3.2: the complex \(\Lambda^mE\to I_{m-1}\to\cdots\to I_0\) in degrees \([-m,0]\), with its quasi-isomorphism \(X_m\to S^m(E[1])\). These complexes are the totals of the parts of total degree \(m\) of the Koszul algebra of \(\Gamma\,iE\to\Gamma\,iC(E)\), followed by Construction 1.3. The Koszul algebra is a differential bigraded algebra, and Construction 1.3 is a map of algebras. With (5.1), the multiplication therefore gives a map of complexes \(X_m\otimes X_n\to X_{m+n}\) covering the product \(S^m(E[1])\otimes S^n(E[1])\to S^{m+n}(E[1])\). On the lowest terms it is the product \(\Lambda^mE\otimes\Lambda^nE\to\Lambda^{m+n}E\).

The complexes are bounded above with flat terms, so \(X_m\otimes X_n\to S^m(E[1])\otimes S^n(E[1])\) is a quasi-isomorphism that computes the derived tensor product. It is a resolution of length \(m+n\) with lowest term \(\Lambda^mE\otimes\Lambda^nE\). The projections onto lowest terms commute with the product map, since it preserves degree \(-(m+n)\). Hence the morphism defined by the resolution \(X_{m+n}\), composed with the product, equals the product on lowest terms composed with the morphism \(c\) defined by \(X_m\otimes X_n\). By Lemma 3.3, \(c=(-1)^{mn}\tau\circ(a\otimes b)\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are the isomorphisms of Theorem 3.2 for \(m\) and \(n\). \(\square\)

## 4. Exercises

**Exercise 4.1.** Let \(A\) be a ring and \(f\in A\) a nonzerodivisor. Write down \(\operatorname{Kos}_\bullet\) of \(f\colon A\to A\) in total degree \(n\), and compare it with the Koszul complex of the element \(f\).

**Exercise 4.2.** Use Theorem 3.2 to compute \(LS^2(A[1])\) and \(L\Lambda^2(A[1])\) for \(A\) a ring.

**Exercise 4.3.** Over \(\mathbb F_2\), show that \(\Lambda^2\) and \(\Gamma^2\) differ, and explain why the second shift formula needs \(\Gamma\) rather than \(S\).

## 5. Complete solutions

**4.1.** In total degree \(n\), \(\operatorname{Kos}_\bullet(f)\) is \(\Lambda^1A\otimes S^{n-1}A\to S^nA\), \(e\otimes t^{n-1}\mapsto ft^n\). Identifying both terms with \(A\), it is multiplication by \(f\): the Koszul complex of \(f\), which is a resolution of \(A/f\) because \(f\) is a nonzerodivisor.

**4.2.** By (i), \(LS^2(A[1])\cong\Lambda^2(A)[2]=0\). By (ii), \(L\Lambda^2(A[1])\cong\Gamma^2(A)[2]\cong A[2]\), free of rank one on \(\gamma_2(1)\).

**4.3.** For \(M=\mathbb F_2\), \(\Lambda^2M=0\), while \(\Gamma^2M=\mathbb F_2\gamma_2(1)\) and \(S^2M=\mathbb F_2\). The canonical algebra map \(S(M)\to\Gamma(M)\) sends \(x^2\) to \(x\cdot x=2\gamma_2(x)\), which is \(0\) in characteristic \(2\); so \(S^2M\to\Gamma^2M\) is zero, and \(S^2\) cannot replace \(\Gamma^2\) in (ii). By Exercise 4.2, \(L\Lambda^2(\mathbb F_2[1])\cong\mathbb F_2[2]\). The second Koszul sequence, which is exact over every ring, is the one that involves \(\Gamma\).
