# The adjoint co-Lie complex of a group scheme

*Written by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic) on 21 October 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. Public domain (CC0).*

Let \(G\) be a group scheme over \(S\), flat and locally of finite presentation. Conjugation makes \(G\) act on its co-Lie complex \(\ell_G\); on the module of invariant differential forms this is the coadjoint action. This lesson constructs \(\ell_G\) with this action, as an object of the derived category of the classifying topos of \(G\), and uses it to compute the cotangent complex of the nerve \(N(G)\) over \(S\). The construction is descent along a torsor over a simplicial scheme. The nerve \(P=N(G,G)\) of \(G\) acting on itself by left translations maps to \(N(G)\), and in each degree this map is a torsor under the opposite group \(G^{\mathrm o}\), acting by right translations. The first section extends descent along torsors, and the co-Lie complex and the Atiyah class of a torsor, from schemes to diagrams of schemes. The co-Lie complex of \(P\) over \(N(G)\) is the Zariski form of the adjoint co-Lie complex. The main theorem is a distinguished triangle
\[
\begin{aligned}&L_{N(G)/S}\to i_{0!}\ell_G\\&\quad\to\ell_{P/N(G)}\to L_{N(G)/S}[1],\end{aligned}
\]
in which \(i_{0!}\ell_G\) has a copy of \(\ell_G\) at each vertex of each simplex and the second map is the adjunction map. It identifies the Ext groups of \(L_{N(G)/S}\) with relative Ext groups of the adjoint co-Lie complex. A spectral sequence computes these from the Ext groups of \(\ell_G\) on the schemes \(G^n\). By the deformation theory of diagrams, the obstructions to deforming the morphism \(N(G)\to S\) over a thickening of \(S\), and the classification of these deformations, lie in such Ext groups of \(L_{N(G)/S}\).

## Conventions

\(S\) is a scheme and \(G\) a group scheme over \(S\), flat and locally of finite presentation, with structure morphism \(\pi\colon G\to S\) and unit \(e\colon S\to G\). Its opposite group \(G^{\mathrm o}\) is the scheme \(G\) with the multiplication \(g*h=hg\); it is again flat and locally of finite presentation, and a left action of \(G^{\mathrm o}\) is a right action of \(G\). \(T\) is the big fppf topos of \(S\), and \(\varepsilon\) the comparison morphisms between big fppf and Zariski topoi.

Diagrams of schemes, their Zariski topoi, morphisms of diagrams of a given type and cotangent complexes of morphisms of diagrams are those of [*Deformations of diagrams of schemes*](deformations-of-diagrams-of-schemes.md). For a diagram \(X\colon I\to(\mathrm{Sch})\) and an object \(i\) of \(I\), \(i^*\) is the restriction to the level \(i\). \(D_{\mathrm{qc}}(X_{\mathrm{Zar}})\) is the full subcategory of objects with termwise quasi-coherent cohomology, and \(D^b_{\mathrm{qc}}\) its bounded part. A map in the derived category of a Zariski topos of a diagram is an isomorphism if and only if its restrictions to all levels are, cohomology being computed levelwise. For a functor \(u\colon I'\to I\), the diagram \(X\circ u\) maps to \(X\) by the morphism of type \(u\) with identity components; its inverse image is the restriction along \(u\). It is the inverse image of a morphism of ringed topoi, so it is exact and preserves the polynomial constructions; hence it carries the cotangent complex of a morphism of diagrams \(X\to Y\) to that of the restricted morphism, as in the proof of Proposition 1.2 (1) of the lesson on deformations of diagrams.

Simplicial schemes are diagrams indexed by \(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\); for \(\alpha\colon[m]\to[n]\) a simplicial scheme \(N\) has \(N(\alpha)\colon N_n\to N_m\), and \(v_k\colon[0]\to[n]\) is the vertex \(k\). Nerves of actions are those of [*Equivariant cotangent complexes*](equivariant-cotangent-complexes.md): for a \(G\)-scheme \(X\), \(N_n(G,X)=G^n\times_SX\), the vertex \(k\) of \((g_1,\ldots,g_n;x)\) is \(g_k\cdots g_1x\), and \(N(G,X)(\alpha)(g;x)=\) \(\bigl(N(G)(\alpha)(g);g_{\alpha(0)}\cdots g_1x\bigr)\) (Lemma 1.3 there). Torsors, the comparison of a torsor with the nerve of its action, and the co-Lie complex \(\ell_{X/Y}\) of a torsor over a scheme are those of [*Torsors, co-Lie complexes and the Atiyah class*](torsors-co-lie-complexes-and-the-atiyah-class.md), with its notation \(\varphi\), \(q\), \(a=q\varphi\) and \(t_{[n}=\tau_{\geq n}\). We write \(B\) for the classifying category \(B[G,S]^{\mathrm o}\). By (0.1) of the lesson on torsors, a module of \(B\) is an \(\mathcal O\)-module \(M\) of \(T\) with a map \(m\colon\pi^*M\to\pi^*M\) on \(G\), whose values \(m_{(g)}\) at points \(g\) of \(G\) satisfy \(m_{(e)}=\mathrm{id}\) and \(m_{(h)}\circ m_{(g)}=m_{(gh)}\): a right action of \(G\) on \(M\). Its underlying module is \(UM=M\).

## 1. Torsors over diagrams

**Definition 1.1.** Let \(I\) be a small category, \(H\) a group scheme over \(S\), flat and locally of finite presentation, and \(Y\colon I\to(\mathrm{Sch}/S)\) a diagram of schemes over \(S\). A *torsor under \(H\) over \(Y\)* is a diagram \(X\colon I\to(\mathrm{Sch}/S)\) with an action of \(H\) on every \(X_i\), such that every \(X_\alpha\) is a morphism of \(H\)-schemes, together with an \(I\)-morphism \(f\colon X\to Y\) of diagrams over \(S\) such that every \(f_i\colon X_i\to Y_i\) is an \(H_{Y_i}\)-torsor (Definition 1.1 of the lesson on torsors).

In this section \(f\colon X\to Y\) is a torsor under \(G\) over \(Y\). The nerves of the actions form a diagram \(N(G,X)\colon\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\times I\to(\mathrm{Sch})\), with the values \(N_n(G,X_i)\) and, for an arrow \((\alpha,\gamma)\colon([n],i)\to([m],j)\), given by \(\alpha\colon[m]\to[n]\) in \(\Delta\) and \(\gamma\colon i\to j\) in \(I\), the morphism \(N_m(G,X_\gamma)\circ N(G,X_i)(\alpha)\); here \(N(G,X_\gamma)\) is the morphism of nerves induced by the morphism \(X_\gamma\) of \(G\)-schemes, which commutes with the simplicial maps. In the same way we have \(N(G,Y)\), with \(N_n(G,Y_i)=G^n\times_SY_i\), and \(N(G)\), the diagram \(([n],i)\mapsto G^n\), constant in \(i\). The morphisms \(\varphi=N(G,f)\colon N(G,X)\to N(G,Y)\) and the projection \(N(G,Y)\to N(G)\) are \((\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\times I)\)-morphisms, and \(q\colon N(G,Y)\to Y\), with the components \(q_{(n,i)}\colon G^n\times_SY_i\to Y_i\), and \(a=q\varphi\), with \(a_{(n,i)}=f_i\circ v_0\), are morphisms of type the projection \(p\colon\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\times I\to I\). The restriction of \(N(G,X)\) to \(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\times\{i\}\), its *column* at \(i\), is the simplicial scheme \(N(G,X_i)\), and the restriction of \(a\) is the morphism \(a^{(i)}\colon N(G,X_i)\to Y_i\) of the lesson on torsors for the torsor \(f_i\).

**Lemma 1.2.**

1. A morphism of \(G_V\)-torsors over a scheme \(V\) is an isomorphism.
2. For every arrow \(\gamma\colon i\to j\) of \(I\), the square formed by \(X_\gamma\), \(Y_\gamma\), \(f_i\) and \(f_j\) is cartesian.
3. For every arrow \(c\to c'\) of \(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\times I\), the square formed by the maps of \(N(G,X)\) and \(N(G,Y)\) along it and by \(\varphi_c\), \(\varphi_{c'}\) is cartesian. Every \(\varphi_c\) and every \(a_c\) is flat, and every \(a_{(0,i)}=f_i\) is flat and surjective.

**Proof.** (1) Let \(u\colon X_1\to X_2\) be a morphism of \(G_V\)-torsors. By Proposition 1.3 (1) of the lesson on torsors, \(\{X_1\to V\}\) is an fppf covering. After base change along it, \(X_1\times_VX_1\to X_1\) has the diagonal section \(\delta\), and \(u'=u\times_VX_1\) is a morphism of \(G_{X_1}\)-torsors. Since \(u'\) is equivariant, \(u'\circ\psi_\delta=\psi_{u'\delta}\), and both \(\psi\) are isomorphisms by Lemma 1.2 (2) of the lesson on torsors. So \(u'\) is an isomorphism, and so is \(u\) [Tag 02L4].

(2) By Lemma 1.2 (1) of the lesson on torsors, \(X_j\times_{Y_j}Y_i\to Y_i\) is a \(G_{Y_i}\)-torsor, and \((X_\gamma,f_i)\colon X_i\to X_j\times_{Y_j}Y_i\) is a morphism of \(G_{Y_i}\)-torsors, \(X_\gamma\) being equivariant. By (1) it is an isomorphism.

(3) An arrow of \(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\times I\) is the composite of an arrow \((\alpha,\mathrm{id}_i)\) and an arrow \((\mathrm{id},\gamma)\), and composites of cartesian squares are cartesian. For \((\alpha,\mathrm{id}_i)\) the square is cartesian by Lemma 3.1 (1) of the lesson on torsors. For \((\mathrm{id}_{[n]},\gamma)\) it is the product with \(G^n\) over \(S\) of the square of (2). The \(\varphi_{(n,i)}=\mathrm{id}_{G^n}\times f_i\) are flat, \(f_i\) being flat (Proposition 1.3 (1) of the lesson on torsors), and \(v_0\colon G^n\times_SX_i\to X_i\) is a base change of the flat \(G^n\to S\). \(\square\)

**Lemma 1.3 (direct images along a projection).** Let \(K\) and \(I\) be small categories, \(Z\colon K\times I\to(\mathrm{Sch})\) and \(Y\colon I\to(\mathrm{Sch})\) diagrams, and \(a\colon Z\to Y\) a morphism of type the projection \(K\times I\to I\) whose components \(a_{(k,i)}\) are flat. For an object \(i\) of \(I\) let \(Z^{(i)}\colon K\to(\mathrm{Sch})\) be the column \(k\mapsto Z_{(k,i)}\), \(\iota_i^*F=F^{(i)}\) the restriction of an object of \(\operatorname{Top}(Z_{\mathrm{Zar}})\) to it, and \(a^{(i)}\colon Z^{(i)}\to Y_i\) the morphism with the components \(a_{(k,i)}\).

1. \(\iota_i^*\) carries injective abelian sheaves to injective abelian sheaves.
2. For every abelian sheaf \(F\) on \(\operatorname{Top}(Z_{\mathrm{Zar}})\), \(i^*a_*F=a^{(i)}_*F^{(i)}\), naturally in \(F\). For \(F\in D^+(Z_{\mathrm{Zar}})\) there is an isomorphism \(i^*Ra_*F\cong Ra^{(i)}_*F^{(i)}\), natural in \(F\).
3. Under these isomorphisms, for \(E\in D^+(Y_{\mathrm{Zar}})\) the restriction to the level \(i\) of the adjunction map \(E\to Ra_*a^*E\) is the adjunction map \(E_i\to Ra^{(i)}_*a^{(i)*}E_i\), and for \(F\in D^+(Z_{\mathrm{Zar}})\) the restriction to the column \(i\) of the adjunction map \(a^*Ra_*F\to F\) is the adjunction map \(a^{(i)*}Ra^{(i)}_*F^{(i)}\to F^{(i)}\).

**Proof.** (1) For an abelian sheaf \(L\) on \(\operatorname{Top}(Z^{(i)}_{\mathrm{Zar}})\), put
\[
(\iota_{i!}L)_{(k,i')}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\colon i'\to i}Z_{(\mathrm{id}_k,\gamma)}^{-1}L_k,
\]
where the structure map along an arrow \((\kappa,\gamma')\colon(k,i')\to(k',i'')\) sends the summand of \(\gamma''\colon i''\to i\) to the summand of \(\gamma''\gamma'\) by \(Z_{(\mathrm{id}_k,\gamma''\gamma')}^{-1}(L_\kappa)\), using \(Z_{(\kappa,\gamma''\gamma')}=Z_{(\kappa,\mathrm{id}_i)}\circ Z_{(\mathrm{id}_k,\gamma''\gamma')}\). As in Proposition 3.1 (1) of [*Fibred topoi and their total topoi*](fibred-topoi-and-their-total-topoi.md), the conditions on composites follow from those of \(L\), and a map \(L\to F^{(i)}\) extends uniquely to \(\iota_{i!}L\to F\), by \(F_{(\mathrm{id}_k,\gamma)}\circ Z_{(\mathrm{id}_k,\gamma)}^{-1}(-)\) on the summand of \(\gamma\); so \(\iota_{i!}\) is left adjoint to \(\iota_i^*\). It is exact, inverse images and sums of abelian sheaves being exact, so \(\iota_i^*\) preserves injectives [Tag 015Z].

(2) By Proposition 2.1 of [*Morphisms of fibred topoi and modules on total topoi*](morphisms-of-fibred-topoi-and-modules-on-total-topoi.md), \((a_*F)_i\) is the limit of the contravariant functor \(((k,i'),\gamma)\mapsto\gamma_*a_{(k,i')*}F_{(k,i')}\) on the category \(\mathcal C\) of pairs of an object \((k,i')\) and an arrow \(\gamma\colon i'\to i\). Let \(\mathcal D\subseteq\mathcal C\) be the full subcategory of the objects \(((k,i),\mathrm{id}_i)\), isomorphic to \(K\). The arrows from an object \(((k,i'),\gamma)\) to objects of \(\mathcal D\) are the pairs \((\kappa,\gamma)\) with \(\kappa\colon k\to k'\); they form a category isomorphic to the category of arrows out of \(k\), which is nonempty and connected, having the initial object \(\mathrm{id}_k\). So \(\mathcal D^{\mathrm{op}}\) is initial in \(\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}\) [Tag 09WP], and the limit may be computed on \(\mathcal D\) [Tag 002R]: \((a_*F)_i=\varprojlim_ka_{(k,i)*}F_{(k,i)}\), with the maps adjoint to the \(F_{(\kappa,\mathrm{id}_i)}\). By Corollary 2.2 (a) there, this is \(a^{(i)}_*F^{(i)}\). For \(F\in D^+\), choose a bounded below complex \(J\) of injective abelian sheaves with a quasi-isomorphism \(F\to J\). The functor \(i^*\) is exact and preserves injectives (Lemma 1.1 of [*Cohomology of total topoi*](cohomology-of-total-topoi.md)), and so is \(\iota_i^*\), by (1). So \(i^*Ra_*F=i^*a_*J=a^{(i)}_*J^{(i)}\), and \(J^{(i)}\) is a bounded below complex of injectives quasi-isomorphic to \(F^{(i)}\), so this is \(Ra^{(i)}_*F^{(i)}\). Derived direct images of complexes of modules are those of the underlying complexes of abelian sheaves [Tag 0D6J], the topoi being topoi of sheaves on sites (Theorem 4.2 of the lesson on fibred topoi).

(3) The functor \(a^*\) is exact, the \(a_{(k,i)}\) being flat, and \((a^*E)^{(i)}=a^{(i)*}E_i\). In the proof of Proposition 2.1 of the lesson on morphisms of fibred topoi, the component at \(((k,i),\mathrm{id}_i)\) of the level \(i\) of the adjunction map \(E\to a_*a^*E\) is the adjunction map of \(a_{(k,i)}\), and the level \((k,i)\) of the adjunction map \(a^*a_*F\to F\) is \(a_{(k,i)}^*\) of the projection of \((a_*F)_i\) to \(a_{(k,i)*}F_{(k,i)}\) followed by the adjunction map of \(a_{(k,i)}\). These are the descriptions of the adjunction maps of \(a^{(i)}\). With \(a^*E\to J\) and \(F\to J\) injective resolutions as in (2), the same holds for the derived functors. \(\square\)

**Theorem 1.4 (descent along a torsor over a diagram).** Let \(f\colon X\to Y\) be a torsor under \(G\) over \(Y\).

1. For every \(E\in D^b_{\mathrm{qc}}(Y_{\mathrm{Zar}})\) the adjunction map \(E\to Ra_*a^*E\) is an isomorphism. Hence \(a^*\colon D^b_{\mathrm{qc}}(Y_{\mathrm{Zar}})\to D(N(G,X)_{\mathrm{Zar}})\) is fully faithful.
2. Let \(K\in D^b(N(G,X)_{\mathrm{Zar}})\) be an object whose columns \(K^{(i)}\) are quasi-cartesian with termwise quasi-coherent cohomology. Then \(Ra_*K\in D^b_{\mathrm{qc}}(Y_{\mathrm{Zar}})\), its level \(i\) is \(Ra^{(i)}_*K^{(i)}\), and the adjunction map \(a^*Ra_*K\to K\) is an isomorphism. The essential image of \(a^*\) on \(D^b_{\mathrm{qc}}(Y_{\mathrm{Zar}})\) consists of these objects.
3. An object \(E\) of \(D^b_{\mathrm{qc}}(Y_{\mathrm{Zar}})\) is quasi-cartesian if and only if \(a^*E\) is quasi-cartesian along every arrow \((\mathrm{id}_{[n]},\gamma)\) of \(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\times I\).

**Proof.** By Lemma 1.2 (3), \(a^*\) is exact, so it is its own derived functor, left adjoint to \(Ra_*\) [Tag 07A6].

(1) By Lemma 1.3 (3), the level \(i\) of \(E\to Ra_*a^*E\) is the adjunction map \(E_i\to Ra^{(i)}_*a^{(i)*}E_i\), an isomorphism by Corollary 2.6 (1) of the lesson on torsors, since \(E_i\in D^b_{\mathrm{qc}}((Y_i)_{\mathrm{Zar}})\). For \(E,F\in D^b_{\mathrm{qc}}(Y_{\mathrm{Zar}})\), the map \(u\mapsto a^*u\) followed by the adjunction bijection \(\operatorname{Hom}(a^*E,a^*F)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(E,Ra_*a^*F)\) is composition with the adjunction map of \(F\), a bijection.

(2) By Theorem 2.5 of the lesson on torsors, \(K^{(i)}\cong a^{(i)*}E_i\) for some \(E_i\in D^b_{\mathrm{qc}}((Y_i)_{\mathrm{Zar}})\). By Lemma 1.3 (2) and Corollary 2.6 (1) there, \((Ra_*K)_i\cong Ra^{(i)}_*K^{(i)}\cong E_i\). If \(K\) lies in \(D^{[r,t]}\), then \(a^{(i)*}H^s(E_i)=H^s(K^{(i)})\) vanishes for \(s\notin[r,t]\). Its level \(0\) is \(f_i^*H^s(E_i)\), and \(f_i^*\) is faithful on quasi-coherent modules, \(f_i\) being flat and surjective (Remark 3.5 of the lesson on deformations of diagrams). So \(E_i\in D^{[r,t]}\) for all \(i\), and \(Ra_*K\in D^b_{\mathrm{qc}}(Y_{\mathrm{Zar}})\). By Lemma 1.3 (3), the column \(i\) of \(a^*Ra_*K\to K\) is the adjunction map of \(a^{(i)}\) at \(K^{(i)}\cong a^{(i)*}E_i\). By the triangle identities its composite with \(a^{(i)*}\) of the adjunction map at \(E_i\), an isomorphism, is the identity; so it is an isomorphism, and so is \(a^*Ra_*K\to K\). Conversely, the columns \(a^{(i)*}E_i\) of \(a^*E\) are quasi-cartesian with termwise quasi-coherent cohomology by Theorem 2.5 of the lesson on torsors.

(3) For \(\gamma\colon i\to j\), \(a_{(n,j)}\circ N(G,X)_{(\mathrm{id},\gamma)}=Y_\gamma\circ a_{(n,i)}\), so the structure map of \(a^*E\) along \((\mathrm{id}_{[n]},\gamma)\) is \(a_{(n,i)}^*\) of the map \(LY_\gamma^*E_j\to E_i\) [Tag 0D6D]. If the latter is an isomorphism, so is the former. Conversely, for \(n=0\), \(a_{(0,i)}=f_i\) is flat and surjective, and the cone of \(LY_\gamma^*E_j\to E_i\) has quasi-coherent cohomology; if \(f_i^*\) of it vanishes, it vanishes. \(\square\)

**Theorem 1.5 (the co-Lie complex of a torsor over a diagram).** Let \(f\colon X\to Y\) be a torsor under \(G\) over a diagram \(Y\) of \(S\)-schemes.

1. There is an object \(\ell_{X/Y}\) of \(D^b_{\mathrm{qc}}(Y_{\mathrm{Zar}})\) with an isomorphism \(a^*\ell_{X/Y}\cong L_{N(G,X)/N(G,Y)}\), unique up to unique isomorphism: the *co-Lie complex* of \(X\) over \(Y\). It is quasi-cartesian, \(\ell_{X/Y}\cong Ra_*L_{N(G,X)/N(G,Y)}\), and its level \(i\) is the co-Lie complex \(\ell_{X_i/Y_i}\) of the lesson on torsors, through isomorphisms under which the structure map along \(\gamma\colon i\to j\) is the base change isomorphism \(LY_\gamma^*\ell_{X_j/Y_j}\cong\ell_{X_i/Y_i}\) of Proposition 3.4 there, \(X_i\) being identified with \(X_j\times_{Y_j}Y_i\) by Lemma 1.2 (2). Its levels are perfect with tor amplitude in \([-1,0]\).
2. For every integer \(n\) there is an object \(\ell_n(X/S)\) of \(D^b_{\mathrm{qc}}(Y_{\mathrm{Zar}})\) with an isomorphism \(a^*\ell_n(X/S)\cong t_{[n}L_{N(G,X)/N(G)}\), and there are maps \(\ell_n(X/S)\to\ell_{n+1}(X/S)\) whose images under \(a^*\) are the truncation maps, all unique up to unique isomorphism. Moreover \(\ell_n(X/S)\cong Ra_*t_{[n}L_{N(G,X)/N(G)}\), with the levels \(\ell_n(X_i/S)\).
3. For every \(n\leq-1\) there are unique maps forming a distinguished triangle
\[
\begin{aligned}&\quad t_{[n}L_{Y/S}\\ &\quad \xrightarrow{\ \beta_n\ }\ell_n(X/S)\\ &\quad \quad \xrightarrow{\ \gamma_n\ }\ell_{X/Y}\\ &\quad \xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{at}_n\ }t_{[n}L_{Y/S}[1]\end{aligned} \tag{1.1}
\]
whose image under \(a^*\) is the triangle that Lemma 3.2 of the lesson on torsors makes from the transitivity triangle of \(N(G,X)\to N(G,Y)\to N(G)\), through the isomorphisms of (1), (2) and \(a^*t_{[n}L_{Y/S}\cong t_{[n}L\varphi^*L_{N(G,Y)/N(G)}\). Its restriction to every level \(i\) is the triangle (3.1) of the lesson on torsors for \(X_i\to Y_i\to S\). The family \(\mathrm{at}(X/Y/S)=(\mathrm{at}_n)_{n\leq-1}\) is the *Atiyah class* of \(X\) over \(Y\).

**Proof.** (1) Let \(L=L_{N(G,X)/N(G,Y)}\). By the Conventions, its column at \(i\) is \(L_{N(G,X_i)/N(G,Y_i)}\), which lies in \(D^{[-1,0]}\), is quasi-cartesian and has termwise quasi-coherent cohomology (Lemma 3.1 (2), (4) of the lesson on torsors). Theorem 1.4 (2) gives \(\ell_{X/Y}=Ra_*L\) with \(a^*\ell_{X/Y}\cong L\), unique up to unique isomorphism by Theorem 1.4 (1), with the levels \(Ra^{(i)}_*L_{N(G,X_i)/N(G,Y_i)}\cong\ell_{X_i/Y_i}\) (Theorem 3.3 (1) there), perfect with tor amplitude in \([-1,0]\). The structure map of \(L\) along \((\mathrm{id}_{[n]},\gamma)\) is the functoriality map of the square formed by the maps of \(N(G,X)\) and \(N(G,Y)\) along this arrow and by \(\varphi_{(n,i)}\), \(\varphi_{(n,j)}\) (Proposition 1.2 (1) of the lesson on deformations of diagrams). This square is cartesian and \(\varphi_{(n,j)}\) is flat (Lemma 1.2 (3)), so the map is an isomorphism (Proposition 1.4 there). Hence \(a^*\ell_{X/Y}\) is quasi-cartesian along these arrows, and \(\ell_{X/Y}\) is quasi-cartesian by Theorem 1.4 (3). The structure map along \(\gamma\) and the base change isomorphism are two isomorphisms \(LY_\gamma^*\ell_{X_j/Y_j}\to\ell_{X_i/Y_i}\) whose images under \(a^{(i)*}\) are the same functoriality map of the nerves; by the uniqueness in Proposition 3.4 of the lesson on torsors they agree.

(2) The columns of \(t_{[n}L_{N(G,X)/N(G)}\) are the \(t_{[n}L_{N(G,X_i)/N(G)}\), restriction being exact. They lie in \(D^{[n,0]}\), are quasi-cartesian and have termwise quasi-coherent cohomology (Lemma 3.1 (4), (5) of the lesson on torsors). Theorem 1.4 gives \(\ell_n(X/S)=Ra_*t_{[n}L_{N(G,X)/N(G)}\) and, by full faithfulness, the transition maps; the levels are identified by Theorem 3.3 (2) there.

(3) The functoriality map \(q^*L_{Y/S}\to L_{N(G,Y)/N(G)}\) of the commutative square formed by \(q\), the projection \(N(G,Y)\to N(G)\) and the structure morphisms to \(S\) has the columns of Lemma 3.1 (3) of the lesson on torsors, which are isomorphisms; so it is an isomorphism, and \(a^*L_{Y/S}=\) \(L\varphi^*q^*L_{Y/S}\cong\) \(L\varphi^*L_{N(G,Y)/N(G)}\). The third vertex \(L\) of the transitivity triangle of \(N(G,X)\to N(G,Y)\to N(G)\) (Proposition 1.3 of the lesson on deformations of diagrams) lies in \(D^{\geq-1}\subseteq D^{\geq n}\). So Lemma 3.2 of the lesson on torsors gives a distinguished triangle with the vertices \(a^*t_{[n}L_{Y/S}\), \(a^*\ell_n(X/S)\) and \(a^*\ell_{X/Y}\). The three objects \(t_{[n}L_{Y/S}\), \(\ell_n(X/S)\) and \(\ell_{X/Y}\) lie in \(D^b_{\mathrm{qc}}(Y_{\mathrm{Zar}})\), \(L_{Y/S}\) having termwise quasi-coherent cohomology (Proposition 1.2 (2) of the lesson on deformations of diagrams). By Theorem 1.4 (1) the three maps are \(a^*\) of unique maps \(\beta_n\), \(\gamma_n\), \(\mathrm{at}_n\). Complete \(\beta_n\) to a distinguished triangle with third vertex \(C\in D^b_{\mathrm{qc}}(Y_{\mathrm{Zar}})\). Its image under the exact functor \(a^*\) is distinguished, so there is an isomorphism \(a^*C\cong a^*\ell_{X/Y}\) compatible with the maps [Tag 014A]. By full faithfulness it is \(a^*\) of an isomorphism \(C\cong\ell_{X/Y}\) compatible with the maps, and (1.1) is isomorphic to a distinguished triangle. The restriction to the level \(i\) is exact, and it carries (1.1) to a distinguished triangle whose image under \(a^{(i)*}\) is the triangle of the column \(i\); by the uniqueness in Theorem 3.3 (3) of the lesson on torsors, it is the triangle (3.1) there. \(\square\)

## 2. The adjoint co-Lie complex

**Construction 2.1 (the universal torsor).** Let \(N=N(G)\) and \(P=N(G,G)\), the nerve of \(G\) acting on itself by left multiplication, with \(\varphi=N(G,\pi)\colon P\to N\). So \(P_n=G^n\times_SG\), \(\varphi_n\) is the projection to \(G^n=N_n\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}&P(\alpha)(g_1,\ldots,g_n;x)\\&\quad=\bigl(N(\alpha)(g);g_{\alpha(0)}\cdots g_1x\bigr).\end{aligned}
\]
Let \(G^{\mathrm o}\) act on every \(P_n\) by \(h\star(g;x)=(g;xh)\). The maps \(P(\alpha)\) are \(G^{\mathrm o}\)-equivariant, left and right multiplications commuting, and \(\varphi_n\) is invariant. The map \(\varphi_n\) is the base change along \(\pi_n\colon N_n=G^n\to S\) of \(\pi\colon G\to S\), on which \(G^{\mathrm o}\) acts by \(h\star x=xh\): the trivial \(G^{\mathrm o}_S\)-torsor, with the section \(e\). So every \(\varphi_n\) is a \(G^{\mathrm o}_{N_n}\)-torsor (Lemma 1.2 (1) of the lesson on torsors), and \(\varphi\colon P\to N\) is a torsor under \(G^{\mathrm o}\) over the simplicial scheme \(N\), in the sense of Definition 1.1 with \(I=\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\). By Proposition 1.3 (2) of the lesson on torsors, applied to the \(G_S\)-torsor \(\pi\) with the left action, the vertices \((P(v_0),\ldots,P(v_n))\colon P_n\to G^{n+1}\) form an isomorphism of simplicial schemes from \(P\) onto the Čech nerve of \(\pi\).

**Proposition 2.2.** Let \(\ell_{P/N}\in D^b_{\mathrm{qc}}(N_{\mathrm{Zar}})\) be the co-Lie complex of the torsor \(P\) over \(N\) (Theorem 1.5 (1), for the group \(G^{\mathrm o}\)).

1. \(\ell_{P/N}\) is quasi-cartesian, and its level \(n\) is isomorphic to \(\pi_n^*\ell_G\), perfect with tor amplitude in \([-1,0]\). Its level \(0\) is the co-Lie complex \(\ell_{G/S}\) of the \(G^{\mathrm o}_S\)-torsor \(\pi\), isomorphic to \(\ell_G\).
2. There is an object \(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G\) of \(D^b(B)\) with an isomorphism \(\operatorname{ner}(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G)\cong L\varepsilon^*\ell_{P/N}\), unique up to unique isomorphism: the *adjoint co-Lie complex* of \(G\). Its underlying object \(U\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G\) is \(L\varepsilon^*\ell_G\).

**Proof.** (1) By Theorem 1.5 (1), \(\ell_{P/N}\) is quasi-cartesian with the levels \(\ell_{P_n/N_n}\). By Proposition 3.4 of the lesson on torsors, for the base change of \(\pi\) along \(\pi_n\), \(\ell_{P_n/N_n}\cong L\pi_n^*\ell_{G/S}=\pi_n^*\ell_{G/S}\), \(\pi_n\) being flat. By Proposition 1.2 (3) of [*The co-Lie complex of a group scheme*](the-co-lie-complex-of-a-group-scheme.md), in its form for the right multiplication, \(\ell_{G/S}\cong Le^*L_{G/S}=\ell_G\), perfect with tor amplitude in \([-1,0]\) (Proposition 1.2 (1) there).

(2) By Lemma 4.2 (1), (3) of the lesson on big fppf sites, \(L\varepsilon^*\ell_{P/N}\) has the levels \(L\varepsilon^*\pi_n^*\ell_G\), which lie in \(D^{[-1,0]}\), and it is quasi-cartesian, so its cohomology modules are cartesian. The simplicial scheme \(N\) is \(N(G,S)\) for the trivial action, and by Proposition 1.5 (3) of the lesson on equivariant cotangent complexes, \(\operatorname{ner}\colon D^b(B)\to D^b(\operatorname{Top}(N_{\mathrm{fppf}}))\) is fully faithful with an essential image containing \(L\varepsilon^*\ell_{P/N}\). The level \(0\) of the nerve of a module is its underlying module (Proposition 1.5 (2) there, the first vertex of \(N_0\) being the identity). \(\square\)

For points of \(G\) with values in an \(S\)-scheme \(V\) and a morphism \(\rho\colon G_V\to G_V\) of \(V\)-schemes fixing \(e_V\), let \(c(\rho)=e_V^*(d\rho)\colon\omega_G\otimes\mathcal O_V\to\omega_G\otimes\mathcal O_V\), as in Section 4 of the lesson on torsors; \(c(\rho\rho')=c(\rho')c(\rho)\). For \(g\in G(V)\) let \(\operatorname{int}(g)(h)=ghg^{-1}\). Then \(\operatorname{int}(gh)=\operatorname{int}(g)\operatorname{int}(h)\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}&c(\operatorname{int}(h))\circ c(\operatorname{int}(g))\\&\quad =c(\operatorname{int}(gh)).\end{aligned} \tag{2.1}
\]

**Lemma 2.3 (sections).** Let \(H\) be a group scheme over \(S\), flat and locally of finite presentation, and \(f\colon X\to Y\) an \(H_Y\)-torsor over a scheme \(Y\). For an open \(V\subseteq Y\) and a section \(s\) of \(f\) over \(V\), let \(\psi_s\colon H_V\to X_V\), \(h\mapsto hs\), and let
\[
\begin{aligned}&\tau_s\colon H^0(\ell_{X/Y})|_V \cong s^*\Omega_{X/Y}\\ &\quad =e_V^*\psi_s^*\Omega_{X_V/V}\\ &\quad \xrightarrow{\ e_V^*(d\psi_s)\ }\omega_H\otimes\mathcal O_V,\end{aligned}
\]
where the first map is \(H^0\) of the isomorphism \(\ell_{X/Y}|_V\cong Ls^*L_{X/Y}\) of Theorem 3.3 (1) of the lesson on torsors, with \(H^0(Ls^*L_{X/Y})=s^*\Omega_{X/Y}\) ((0.1) of the lesson on deformations of diagrams and Theorem 6.1 of *The cotangent complex*).

1. \(\tau_s\) is an isomorphism, and \(\tau_{hs}=c(\operatorname{int}(h^{-1}))\circ\tau_s\) for \(h\in H(V)\).
2. Let \(u\colon Y'\to Y\) be a morphism, \(X'=X\times_YY'\) with the projection \(u'\colon X'\to X\), and \(s'\) the section of \(X'\) over \(V'=u^{-1}(V)\) with \(u's'=su\). Then \(\tau_{s'}\circ H^0(b)=u^*\tau_s\) on \(V'\), where \(b\colon Lu^*\ell_{X/Y}\cong\ell_{X'/Y'}\) is the base change isomorphism of Proposition 3.4 of the lesson on torsors and \(H^0(Lu^*\ell_{X/Y})=u^*H^0(\ell_{X/Y})\).

**Proof.** (1) The proof of Proposition 4.1 (2), (3) of the lesson on torsors uses the smoothness of the group only to identify \(\ell_{X/Y}\) and \(L_{X/Y}\) with their \(H^0\). Replace the level \(0\) isomorphism \(\theta\colon f^*\ell_{X/Y}\cong L_{X/Y}\) there by \(H^0(\theta)\colon f^*H^0(\ell_{X/Y})\cong\Omega_{X/Y}\), \(f\) being flat, and the structure maps along the two faces of \(N_1(H,X)\) by their \(H^0\), which are maps of differentials (Theorem 6.1 of *The cotangent complex*). The argument then applies word for word.

(2) Let \(\theta'\) be the level \(0\) isomorphism for \(X'\) and \(c\colon u'^*L_{X/Y}\to L_{X'/Y'}\) the functoriality map, which on \(H^0\) is the map of differentials \(u'^*\Omega_{X/Y}\to\Omega_{X'/Y'}\). By Proposition 3.4 of the lesson on torsors, the level \(0\) of \(a'^*(b)\) satisfies \(\theta'\circ f'^*(b)=c\circ u'^*(\theta)\). With \(s'^*f'^*=\mathrm{id}\) and \(s'^*u'^*=u^*s^*\),
\[
\begin{aligned}\tau_{s'}\circ H^0(b)&=e_{V'}^*(d\psi_{s'})\circ s'^*H^0(c)\\&\qquad\circ u^*s^*H^0(\theta).\end{aligned}
\]
The map \(\psi_{s'}\) is the base change of \(\psi_s\), so \(u'\psi_{s'}=\psi_su_H\) with \(u_H\colon H_{V'}\to H_V\). The functoriality of differentials gives \(d\psi_{s'}\circ\psi_{s'}^*H^0(c)=du_H\circ u_H^*(d\psi_s)\), where \(e_{V'}^*(du_H)\) is the identification \(u^*(\omega_H\otimes\mathcal O_V)=\omega_H\otimes\mathcal O_{V'}\). Pulling back along \(e_{V'}\), with \(\psi_{s'}e_{V'}=s'\) and \(u_He_{V'}=e_Vu\), gives \(e_{V'}^*(d\psi_{s'})\circ s'^*H^0(c)=u^*e_V^*(d\psi_s)\). Hence \(\tau_{s'}\circ H^0(b)=\) \(u^*\bigl(e_V^*(d\psi_s)\circ s^*H^0(\theta)\bigr)=\) \(u^*\tau_s\). \(\square\)

**Proposition 2.4 (the coadjoint action).**

1. \(H^0(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G)\) is isomorphic to the module \(\varepsilon^*\omega_G\) of \(B\) with the structure \(m_{(g)}=c(\operatorname{int}(g))\) at every point \(g\) of \(G\).
2. If \(G\) is smooth over \(S\), then \(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G\cong H^0(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G)[0]\).

The structure of (1) satisfies the rule of a module of \(B\) by (2.1). Dually, \(c(\operatorname{int}(g))\) is the transpose of the adjoint action of \(g\) on the Lie algebra.

**Proof.** (1) Write \(\ell=\ell_{P/N}\) and \(\ell_n\) for its levels. For \(\alpha\colon[m]\to[n]\), \(H^0\) of the structure map \(\ell_\alpha\colon N(\alpha)^*\ell_m\to\ell_n\) is an isomorphism \(N(\alpha)^*H^0(\ell_m)\cong H^0(\ell_n)\) ((0.1) of the lesson on deformations of diagrams), so \(H^0(\ell)\) is a cartesian module, and \(\varepsilon^*H^0(\ell)=\) \(H^0(L\varepsilon^*\ell)\cong\) \(H^0(\operatorname{ner}\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G)=\) \(\operatorname{ner}H^0(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G)\), the nerve being exact. For a module \((M,m)\) of \(B\), the nerve has the level \(1\) module \(\pi^*M\), its structure map along the vertex \(v_0\colon[0]\to[1]\) is the identity and along \(v_1\) it is \(m\) (Definition 2.1 of the lesson on equivariant sheaves, with the reversal of Proposition 1.5 (1) of the lesson on equivariant cotangent complexes). If \(w\) is an isomorphism from \(\operatorname{ner}(M)\) to a module \(E\) of \(\operatorname{Top}(N_{\mathrm{fppf}})\), compatibility with these two maps gives
\[
\begin{aligned}m&=\pi^*w_0^{-1}\circ\bigl(E_{v_0}^{-1}\circ E_{v_1}\bigr)\\&\qquad\circ\pi^*w_0 .\end{aligned}
\]
So it suffices to compute \(H^0(\ell_{v_0})^{-1}\circ H^0(\ell_{v_1})\) on \(\pi^*H^0(\ell_0)\).

Use the isomorphisms of Lemma 2.3 for the group \(G^{\mathrm o}\): \(\tau_e\colon H^0(\ell_0)\cong\omega_G\) on \(N_0=S\), and \(\tau_\sigma\colon H^0(\ell_1)\cong\pi^*\omega_G\) on \(N_1=G\) for the section \(\sigma(g)=(g;e)\) of \(\varphi_1\). By Theorem 1.5 (1), \(\ell_{v_k}\) is the base change isomorphism for the cartesian square formed by \(P(v_k)\colon P_1\to P_0\), \(\pi\colon N_1\to N_0\) and \(\varphi\); here \(P(v_0)(g;x)=x\) and \(P(v_1)(g;x)=gx\). The sections of \(\varphi_1\) lying over \(e\) are \(\sigma\) for \(v_0\), since \(P(v_0)\sigma=e\pi\), and \(\sigma_1\colon g\mapsto(g;g^{-1})\) for \(v_1\), since \(P(v_1)\sigma_1=e\pi\). By Lemma 2.3 (2),
\[
\begin{aligned}\tau_\sigma\circ H^0(\ell_{v_0})&=\pi^*\tau_e,\\ \tau_{\sigma_1}\circ H^0(\ell_{v_1})&=\pi^*\tau_e .\end{aligned}
\]
In \(G^{\mathrm o}\), \(\sigma_1=g^{-1}\star\sigma\), and the inner automorphism \(k\mapsto(g^{-1})^{-1}*k*g^{-1}\) of \(G^{\mathrm o}\) is \(k\mapsto g^{-1}kg\), that is, \(\operatorname{int}(g^{-1})\). Lemma 2.3 (1) for \(G^{\mathrm o}\), whose co-Lie module is \(\omega_G\), gives \(\tau_{\sigma_1}=c(\operatorname{int}(g^{-1}))\circ\tau_\sigma\). By (2.1), \(c(\operatorname{int}(g^{-1}))^{-1}=c(\operatorname{int}(g))\). Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}&H^0(\ell_{v_0})^{-1}\circ H^0(\ell_{v_1})\\&\quad=\pi^*\tau_e^{-1}\circ c(\operatorname{int}(g))\circ\pi^*\tau_e .\end{aligned}
\]
Applying \(\varepsilon^*\) and the formula for \(m\), with \(w_0\) followed by \(\varepsilon^*\tau_e\), gives (1).

(2) If \(G\) is smooth, \(\ell_G\cong\omega_G[0]\) (Proposition 1.5 of the lesson on the co-Lie complex), so the levels of \(\ell_{P/N}\) and of \(L\varepsilon^*\ell_{P/N}\) have no cohomology in degree \(-1\). The level \(0\) of \(\operatorname{ner}H^{-1}(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G)\) is the underlying module of \(H^{-1}(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G)\), which therefore vanishes; and \(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G\) lies in \(D^{[-1,0]}\). \(\square\)

## 3. The cotangent complex of the nerve

Let \(i_0\colon S_{\mathrm{Zar}}\to\operatorname{Top}(N_{\mathrm{Zar}})\) and \(i'_0\colon G_{\mathrm{Zar}}\to\operatorname{Top}(P_{\mathrm{Zar}})\) be the morphisms of the levels \(0\), \(N_0=S\) and \(P_0=G\). Their inverse images \(i_0^*\), \(i'^*_0\) are the restrictions to the level \(0\). They have the left adjoints (Lemma 4.2 (1) of the lesson on big fppf sites)
\[
\begin{aligned}(i_{0!}E)_n&=\bigoplus_{k=0}^n\pi_n^*E,\\ (i'_{0!}F)_n&=\bigoplus_{k=0}^nP(v_k)^*F,\end{aligned}
\]
the summand \(k\) being that of the vertex \(v_k\), with \(N(v_k)=\pi_n\). Every \(P(v_k)\) is flat, being the projection \(G^{n+1}\to G\) under the isomorphism of Construction 2.1, and so is \(\pi_n\). So \(i_{0!}\) and \(i'_{0!}\) are exact, and \(i_0^*\), \(i'^*_0\) carry injective modules to injective modules [Tag 015Z]. On the summand of \(v_k\) at the level \(n\), the adjunction map \(i_{0!}i_0^*K\to K\) is the structure map \(K_{v_k}\colon N(v_k)^*K_0\to K_n\) (Proposition 3.1 (1) of the lesson on fibred topoi), and similarly on \(P\).

**Lemma 3.1 (products).** Let \(X_0,\ldots,X_m\) be flat \(S\)-schemes and \(X=X_0\times_S\cdots\times_SX_m\), with the projections \(p_k\). The sum of the functoriality maps \(p_k^*L_{X_k/S}\to L_{X/S}\) of the squares formed by \(p_k\) and \(\mathrm{id}_S\) is an isomorphism \(\bigoplus_kp_k^*L_{X_k/S}\cong L_{X/S}\).

**Proof.** By induction on \(m\), the case \(m=0\) being trivial. Write \(X=X'\times_SX_m\), with \(p'\colon X\to X'=X_0\times_S\cdots\times_SX_{m-1}\). Let \(w\colon L_{X/S}\to L_{X/X'}\) be the second map of the transitivity triangle of \(X\to X'\to S\) (Proposition 1.3 of the lesson on deformations of diagrams), and \(F_m\colon p_m^*L_{X_m/S}\to L_{X/S}\) the functoriality map of \(p_m\). The composite \(w\circ F_m\) is the functoriality map of the cartesian square formed by \(p_m\), \(p'\) and the structure morphisms (Theorem 2.2 of *The cotangent complex*), an isomorphism by Proposition 1.4 of the lesson on deformations of diagrams, \(X_m\) being flat over \(S\). So \(F_m\circ(w F_m)^{-1}\) is a right inverse of \(w\), and by [Tag 05QT] the sum of \(F_m\) and the first map \(p'^*L_{X'/S}\to L_{X/S}\) of the triangle is an isomorphism \(p'^*L_{X'/S}\oplus p_m^*L_{X_m/S}\cong L_{X/S}\). By induction, \(p'^*L_{X'/S}\) is the sum of the pullbacks of the \(L_{X_k/S}\), \(k<m\), and functoriality maps compose. \(\square\)

**Lemma 3.2.** The adjunction map \(i'_{0!}i'^*_0L_{P/S}\to L_{P/S}\) is an isomorphism.

**Proof.** At the level \(n\) its restriction to the summand of \(v_k\) is the structure map of \(L_{P/S}\) along \(v_k\), the functoriality map of the square formed by \(P(v_k)\) and \(\mathrm{id}_S\) (Proposition 1.2 (1) of the lesson on deformations of diagrams). Under the isomorphism \(P_n\cong G^{n+1}\) of Construction 2.1, \(P(v_k)\) is the projection \(p_k\), and functoriality maps are compatible with this isomorphism (Theorem 2.2 of *The cotangent complex*). So Lemma 3.1, with all \(X_k=G\), shows that the level \(n\) is an isomorphism. \(\square\)

**Theorem 3.3 (the cotangent complex of the nerve).** There is a distinguished triangle in \(D(N_{\mathrm{Zar}})\)
\[
\begin{aligned}&L_{N/S}\longrightarrow i_{0!}i_0^*\ell_{P/N}\\&\quad\xrightarrow{\ \epsilon\ }\ell_{P/N}\xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{at}\ }L_{N/S}[1],\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
\]
where \(\epsilon\) is the adjunction map and \(\mathrm{at}\) is the Atiyah class of the torsor \(P\) over \(N\). Here \(i_0^*\ell_{P/N}\cong\ell_G\), so the level \(n\) of the middle term is \(\bigoplus_{k=0}^n\pi_n^*\ell_G\). At every level the triangle is split: the level \(n\) of \(\epsilon\) has a right inverse and the level \(n\) of \(\mathrm{at}\) vanishes.

**Proof.** By Theorem 3.1 of [*Flat group schemes are local complete intersections*](flat-group-schemes-are-local-complete-intersections.md), \(L_{G/S}\) lies in \(D^{[-1,0]}\). By Lemma 3.1, so do \(L_{N_n/S}\) and \(L_{P_n/S}\), with \(N_n=G^n\) and \(P_n\cong G^{n+1}\). The levels of the nerve \(Q=N(G^{\mathrm o},P)\) are \(Q_{(r,n)}=G^r\times_SP_n\), and \(L_{Q_{(r,n)}/G^r}\cong\operatorname{pr}^*L_{P_n/S}\) by Proposition 1.4 of the lesson on deformations of diagrams, \(G^r\) being flat over \(S\). So all these complexes lie in \(D^{[-1,0]}\), on which \(t_{[-1}\) is the identity. Theorem 1.5 (2), (3) for the \(G^{\mathrm o}\)-torsor \(P\) over \(N\), with \(n=-1\), gives \(\lambda=\ell_{-1}(P/S)\) with \(b^*\lambda\cong L_{Q/N(G^{\mathrm o})}\), where \(b\colon Q\to N\) is the morphism called \(a\) in Section 1, and a distinguished triangle
\[
\begin{aligned}&L_{N/S}\xrightarrow{\ \beta\ }\lambda\xrightarrow{\ \gamma\ }\ell_{P/N}\\&\qquad\xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{at}\ }L_{N/S}[1].\end{aligned}
\]

*Step 1: \(i_0^*(\gamma)\) is an isomorphism.* By Theorem 1.5 (3), the level \(0\) of this triangle is the triangle (3.1) of the lesson on torsors for \(P_0=G\to N_0=S\to S\), whose first vertex \(t_{[-1}L_{S/S}\) vanishes. Rotating it gives a distinguished triangle with the first map \(i_0^*(\gamma)\) and the third vertex \(0\), so \(i_0^*(\gamma)\) is an isomorphism [Tag 05QR].

*Step 2: the adjunction map \(\epsilon_\lambda\colon i_{0!}i_0^*\lambda\to\lambda\) is an isomorphism.* Every \(\varphi_n\) is flat and surjective, being a base change of \(\pi\), which has the section \(e\). Both sides have termwise quasi-coherent cohomology, so it suffices to show that \(\varphi^*(\epsilon_\lambda)\) is an isomorphism (Remark 3.5 of the lesson on deformations of diagrams, applied to the cohomology of the cone at each level). Since \(N(v_k)\varphi_n=\varphi_0P(v_k)\), there is an isomorphism \(\varphi^*i_{0!}E\cong i'_{0!}\varphi_0^*E\), natural in \(E\), and under it \(\varphi^*(\epsilon_K)\) is the adjunction map \(\epsilon'_{\varphi^*K}\) of \(\varphi^*K\), the structure maps of \(\varphi^*K\) being \(\varphi^*\) of those of \(K\). The restriction of \(Q\) to the row \(\{0\}\times\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\) is \(P\), since \(N_0(G^{\mathrm o},P_n)=P_n\), and on it \(b\) restricts to \(\varphi\) and \(N(G^{\mathrm o})\) to the constant diagram \(S\). By the Conventions, restricting \(b^*\lambda\cong L_{Q/N(G^{\mathrm o})}\) to this row gives \(\varphi^*\lambda\cong L_{P/S}\). The adjunction map is natural, so \(\epsilon'_{\varphi^*\lambda}\) is an isomorphism because \(\epsilon'_{L_{P/S}}\) is, by Lemma 3.2.

*Step 3.* The naturality of the adjunction map gives \(\gamma\circ\epsilon_\lambda=\epsilon\circ i_{0!}i_0^*(\gamma)\). By Steps 1 and 2, \(\mu=i_{0!}i_0^*(\gamma)\circ\epsilon_\lambda^{-1}\colon\lambda\to i_{0!}i_0^*\ell_{P/N}\) is an isomorphism with \(\gamma=\epsilon\circ\mu\). So \((\mathrm{id},\mu,\mathrm{id})\) is an isomorphism from the triangle above onto (3.1), with first map \(\mu\beta\), and (3.1) is distinguished. By Proposition 2.2 (1), \(i_0^*\ell_{P/N}\cong\ell_G\).

At the level \(n\), the summand of \(v_0\) maps to \(\ell_n\) by the structure map \(\ell_{v_0}\), an isomorphism, \(\ell_{P/N}\) being quasi-cartesian. So the restriction \(\epsilon|_n\) of \(\epsilon\) to the level \(n\) has a right inverse \(s\), and the restriction of \(\mathrm{at}\) is \(\mathrm{at}|_n=\mathrm{at}|_n\circ\epsilon|_n\circ s=0\) [Tag 0146]. \(\square\)

So \(L_{N(G)/S}\) sits between a copy of \(\ell_G\) at every vertex and the adjoint co-Lie complex, and at every level it is a complement of one summand \(\ell_G\) (Exercise 4.4).

**Corollary 3.4 (Ext groups).** For \(M\in D^+(N_{\mathrm{Zar}})\) there is a long exact sequence
\[
\begin{aligned}&\quad \cdots \to\operatorname{Ext}^j(\ell_{P/N},M)\\ &\quad \xrightarrow{\ i_0^*\ }\operatorname{Ext}^j_S(\ell_G,i_0^*M)\\ &\quad \quad \to\operatorname{Ext}^j(L_{N/S},M)\\ &\quad \to\operatorname{Ext}^{j+1}(\ell_{P/N},M)\\ &\quad \quad \xrightarrow{\ i_0^*\ }\operatorname{Ext}^{j+1}_S(\ell_G,i_0^*M)\\ &\quad \to\cdots,\end{aligned}
\]
in which the maps \(i_0^*\) are restriction to the level \(0\) through \(i_0^*\ell_{P/N}\cong\ell_G\). In the derived category of abelian groups, \(R\operatorname{Hom}(L_{N/S},M)[-1]\) is a cocone of the restriction map \(R\operatorname{Hom}(\ell_{P/N},M)\to R\operatorname{Hom}_S(\ell_G,i_0^*M)\): the Ext groups of \(L_{N/S}\) are relative Ext groups of the adjoint co-Lie complex, shifted by one.

**Proof.** Apply \(\operatorname{Hom}(-,M[j])\) to (3.1) [Tag 0149]. Since \(i_{0!}\) is exact with the exact right adjoint \(i_0^*\), which preserves injectives, \(R\operatorname{Hom}(i_{0!}E,M)=R\operatorname{Hom}_S(E,i_0^*M)\): represent \(M\) by a bounded below complex of injectives \(J\); then \(i_0^*J\) is one, both are K-injective [Tag 070J], and \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(i_{0!}E,J)=\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(E,i_0^*J)\). Under this identification, composition with \(\epsilon\) is the map induced by \(i_0^*\). For the second statement, \(R\operatorname{Hom}(-,M)\), the derived global sections of the derived sheaf Hom, carries distinguished triangles to distinguished triangles [Tag 08JC]. \(\square\)

Write \(T_n=T/N_n\) for the big fppf topos of \(N_n\), so that \(\operatorname{Top}(N_{\mathrm{fppf}})\) is the total topos of the \(T_n\). For complexes \(E\in D^-(B)\) and \(F\in D^+(B)\), the forgetful functor induces a map \(U\colon R\operatorname{Hom}_B(E,F)\to R\operatorname{Hom}_T(UE,UF)\); write \(R\operatorname{Hom}_{B/S}(E,F)\) for a cocone of it and \(\operatorname{Ext}^j_{B/S}(E,F)\) for its cohomology, the *relative Ext groups*.

**Corollary 3.5 (relative Ext groups on the classifying topos).**

1. For \(E\in D^-(B)\) and \(F\in D^+(B)\), \(R\operatorname{Hom}_{B/S}(E,F)\) may be taken to be the total complex of the columns \(n\geq1\) of the double complex (3.2) of the lesson on mixed Hom complexes. There is a convergent spectral sequence
\[
\begin{gathered}E_1^{n,k}\\ =\operatorname{Ext}^k_{T_n}(\pi_n^*UE,\pi_n^*UF)\ \ (n\geq1),\\ E_1^{0,k} =0,\quad E_1^{n,k}\\ \Rightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{n+k}_{B/S}(E,F),\end{gathered}
\]
whose rows \(E_1^{\bullet,k}\) are the cochain complexes of the cosimplicial groups \([n]\mapsto\operatorname{Ext}^k_{T_n}(\pi_n^*UE,\pi_n^*UF)\) in degrees \(n\geq1\).
2. For \(M\in D^+(B)\) there is an isomorphism \(R\operatorname{Hom}(L\varepsilon^*L_{N/S},\operatorname{ner}M)\cong\) \(R\operatorname{Hom}_{B/S}(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G,M)[1]\) in the derived category of abelian groups.
3. Let \(I\) be a quasi-coherent \(\mathcal O_S\)-module, \(\pi_N^*I\) the cartesian module of \(\operatorname{Top}(N_{\mathrm{Zar}})\) with the levels \(\pi_n^*I\), and \(\varepsilon^*I\) the module of \(B\) with the trivial structure. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}&\operatorname{Ext}^j(L_{N/S},\pi_N^*I)\\&\quad \cong\operatorname{Ext}^{j+1}_{B/S}(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G,\varepsilon^*I),\end{aligned}
\]
and the spectral sequence of (1) for these groups has the terms
\[
\begin{aligned}E_1^{n,k}&=\operatorname{Ext}^k_{G^n}(\pi_n^*\ell_G,\pi_n^*I)\\&=H^k\bigl(G^n,\pi_n^*I\otimes^{\mathbf L}(\pi_n^*\ell_G)^\vee\bigr)\end{aligned}
\]
for \(n\geq1\), the Ext and cohomology groups being those of the Zariski topos of \(G^n\).

**Proof.** (1) Let \(X\) be the simplicial topos of the nerve of the internal category \([G,S]^{\mathrm o}\) of \(T\), with the levels \(T_n\) (Proposition 1.5 (1) of the lesson on equivariant cotangent complexes); all its vertices \(X_n\to X_0=T\) are the localizations along \(\pi_n\). It is a good two-sided pseudo-category satisfying the conditions (I) and (E) (Proposition 5.1 of the lesson on cohomology of equivariant modules), and \(BX=B\). Represent \(E\) and \(F\) as in the proof of Theorem 3.3 of the lesson on mixed Hom complexes, \(F\) by a bounded below complex \(I\) of injective modules, and let \(C^{\bullet,\bullet}\) be the double complex (3.2) there (written \(G\) there). By that theorem, \(K=\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet_B(E,I)\) is the kernel of the first coface differential on the column \(C^{\bullet,0}\), and its inclusion is a quasi-isomorphism \(K\to\operatorname{Tot}(C)\). The projection \(\operatorname{Tot}(C)\to C^{\bullet,0}\) is a map of complexes, because the coface differential raises \(n\); its kernel is the total complex \(\operatorname{Tot}(C^{\bullet,\geq1})\) of the columns \(n\geq1\), and the sequence is termwise split. The column \(C^{\bullet,0}=\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(UE,UI)\) computes \(R\operatorname{Hom}_T(UE,UF)\), the modules \(UI^q\) being injective by (I) (Proposition 6.2 (3) of the lesson on equivariant sheaves), and the composite \(K\to C^{\bullet,0}\) is the map induced by \(U\). So the triangle of the termwise split sequence [Tags 014I, 014Q] shows that \(\operatorname{Tot}(C^{\bullet,\geq1})\) is a cocone of \(U\). Filtering it by \(n\), the filtration being finite in each degree as in the proof of Corollary 3.4 of the lesson on mixed Hom complexes, gives the spectral sequence, with the column cohomology \(\operatorname{Ext}^k(X(v_0)^*E,X(v_n)^*F)\) computed there.

(2) Apply \(L\varepsilon^*\), an exact functor, to (3.1). The functor \(L\varepsilon^*\) commutes with \(i_{0!}\), levelwise (Lemma 4.2 (1) of the lesson on big fppf sites) and compatibly with the adjunction maps, the fppf \(i_{0!}\) being defined by the same formula. This gives a distinguished triangle
\[
\begin{aligned}&L\varepsilon^*L_{N/S}\to i_{0!}i_0^*\operatorname{ner}\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G\\&\quad\xrightarrow{\ \epsilon\ }\operatorname{ner}\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G\to L\varepsilon^*L_{N/S}[1].\end{aligned}
\]
As in the proof of Corollary 3.4, \(R\operatorname{Hom}(i_{0!}i_0^*\operatorname{ner}\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G,\operatorname{ner}M)=\) \(R\operatorname{Hom}_T(U\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G,UM)\), the level \(0\) of a nerve being the underlying module. By Theorem 4.3 (2) of the lesson on cohomology of equivariant modules, the nerve induces an isomorphism \(R\operatorname{Hom}_B(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G,M)\cong\) \(R\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{ner}\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G,\operatorname{ner}M)\). Composed with restriction to the level \(0\), it is the map induced by \(U\): on complexes, the nerve of a map of modules of \(B\) has the level \(0\) map itself. So \(R\operatorname{Hom}(L\varepsilon^*L_{N/S},\operatorname{ner}M)[-1]\) and \(R\operatorname{Hom}_{B/S}(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G,M)\) are cocones of the same map [Tag 08JC], hence isomorphic [Tag 014A].

(3) By Lemma 4.2 (2) of the lesson on big fppf sites, with the truncation index \(0\) and \(t_{[0}L\varepsilon^*\pi_N^*I=\varepsilon^*\pi_N^*I\), \(R\operatorname{Hom}(L_{N/S},\pi_N^*I)\cong\) \(R\operatorname{Hom}(L\varepsilon^*L_{N/S},\varepsilon^*\pi_N^*I)\), and \(\varepsilon^*\pi_N^*I=\operatorname{ner}(\varepsilon^*I)\). So (2) gives the isomorphism. In (1), \(\pi_n^*U\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G=L\varepsilon^*\pi_n^*\ell_G\) and \(\pi_n^*U\varepsilon^*I=\varepsilon^*\pi_n^*I\), and Corollary 2.2 (1) of the lesson on big fppf sites, over \(G^n\), identifies the Ext groups over \(T_n\) with the Zariski ones. Finally \(\pi_n^*\ell_G\) is perfect, and [Tag 08DQ] gives the cohomological form. \(\square\)

## 4. Exercises

**4.1.** Let \(G\) be commutative. Show that the structure of \(H^0(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G)\) is trivial, and that if \(G\) is moreover smooth, \(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G\) is isomorphic to \(\varepsilon^*\omega_G\) in degree \(0\) with the trivial structure.

**4.2.** Let \(G=\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_S[a,a^{-1},b]\) with \((a,b)(a',b')=(aa',ab'+b)\), the group of affine transformations \(x\mapsto ax+b\), with unit \((1,0)\). Show that \(\omega_G\) is free on the classes of \(da\) and \(db\), and that for a point \(g=(c,d)\),
\[
\begin{aligned}&c(\operatorname{int}(g))(\alpha\,da+\beta\,db)\\&\quad=(\alpha-d\beta)\,da+c\beta\,db .\end{aligned}
\]
Check (2.1) directly. For \(S=\operatorname{Spec}k\), \(k\) a field, show that the sections of \(H^0(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G)\) fixed by the structure, that is, \(\operatorname{Hom}_B(\mathcal O,H^0(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G))\), form the line \(k\,da\).

**4.3.** Show that \(G\) is étale over \(S\) if and only if \(L_{N(G)/S}=0\), and deduce from Theorem 3.3 that then \(\ell_{P/N}=0\), all Ext groups of Corollary 3.4 vanishing.

**4.4.** (a) Show that at every level \(n\), (3.1) gives an isomorphism \(L_{G^n/S}\cong\bigoplus_{k=1}^n\pi_n^*\ell_G\). (b) Let \(G\) be smooth. Show that the level \(1\) of (3.1) is a short exact sequence
\[
\begin{aligned}0&\to\Omega_{G/S}\to\pi^*\omega_G\oplus\pi^*\omega_G\\&\to\pi^*\omega_G\to0,\end{aligned}
\]
whose second map, in the isomorphisms \(\tau_e\) on the two summands and \(\tau_\sigma\) on the target of the proof of Proposition 2.4, is \((u,v)\mapsto u+c(\operatorname{int}(g))(v)\). Deduce an isomorphism \(\Omega_{G/S}\cong\pi^*\omega_G\).

## 5. Complete solutions

**4.1.** For a commutative group \(\operatorname{int}(g)=\mathrm{id}\), so \(c(\operatorname{int}(g))=\mathrm{id}\), and Proposition 2.4 (1) gives the trivial structure. If \(G\) is also smooth, Proposition 2.4 (2) gives \(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G\cong H^0(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G)[0]\).

**4.2.** \(G\) is the open subscheme \(a\neq0\) of the affine plane, so \(\Omega_{G/S}\) is free on \(da,db\) and \(\omega_G=e^*\Omega_{G/S}\) is free on their classes. The inverse of \(g=(c,d)\) is \((c^{-1},-c^{-1}d)\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}&\operatorname{int}(g)(a,b)\\&\quad=(c,d)(a,b)(c^{-1},-c^{-1}d)\\&\quad=(a,\,cb+d-ad).\end{aligned}
\]
Over \(V\), \(c\) and \(d\) are constants, so \(\operatorname{int}(g)^*da=da\) and \(\operatorname{int}(g)^*db=c\,db-d\,da\); at the unit this is the stated formula. For \(h=(c',d')\), \(gh=(cc',cd'+d)\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}&c(\operatorname{int}(h))c(\operatorname{int}(g))(\alpha,\beta)\\&\quad=(\alpha-d\beta-d'c\beta,\ c'c\beta),\end{aligned}
\]
which is \(c(\operatorname{int}(gh))(\alpha,\beta)\), as \(cd'+d\) is the second coordinate of \(gh\). A map \(\mathcal O\to M\) of modules of \(B\) is a section \(v\) of \(M\) with \(m(\pi^*v)=\pi^*v\) on \(G\). For \(v=\alpha\,da+\beta\,db\) with \(\alpha,\beta\in k\), the universal point \(g=(c,d)\in G(G)\) gives \(\alpha-d\beta=\alpha\) and \(c\beta=\beta\) in \(k[c,c^{-1},d]\), so \(\beta=0\). Conversely \(da\) is fixed. By Proposition 2.4 (1) the fixed sections form \(k\,da\).

**4.3.** The level \(1\) of \(L_{N(G)/S}\) is \(L_{G/S}\), and \(L_{G^n/S}\cong\bigoplus\operatorname{pr}_k^*L_{G/S}\) by Lemma 3.1. So \(L_{N(G)/S}=0\) if and only if \(L_{G/S}=0\). If \(L_{G/S}=0\), then \(\ell_G=Le^*L_{G/S}=0\), and \(G\) is étale by Corollary 2.3 of the lesson on the co-Lie complex. If \(G\) is étale, \(L_{G/S}=0\) (Propositions 5.1 and 10.1 of *The cotangent complex*), so \(L_{N(G)/S}=0\) and \(\ell_G=Le^*L_{G/S}=0\). In (3.1) the first vertex vanishes, so \(\epsilon\) is an isomorphism [Tag 05QR], and its source \(i_{0!}i_0^*\ell_{P/N}\cong i_{0!}\ell_G\) vanishes; so \(\ell_{P/N}=0\). Then the terms \(\operatorname{Ext}^j(\ell_{P/N},M)\) and \(\operatorname{Ext}^j_S(\ell_G,i_0^*M)\) of Corollary 3.4 vanish, and so do the \(\operatorname{Ext}^j(L_{N/S},M)\), as they must.

**4.4.** (a) Restrict (3.1) to the level \(n\): with \(L_n=L_{G^n/S}\), \(A=(i_{0!}i_0^*\ell_{P/N})_n=\bigoplus_{k=0}^n\pi_n^*\ell_0\) and \(\beta\colon L_n\to A\) the first map, it is a distinguished triangle whose second map \(\epsilon|_n\) has the right inverse \(s=\iota_0\circ\ell_{v_0}^{-1}\), \(\iota_0\) being the inclusion of the summand of \(v_0\) (Theorem 3.3). By [Tag 05QT], \((\beta,s)\colon L_n\oplus\ell_n\to A\) is an isomorphism, hence so is \((\beta,\iota_0)\colon L_n\oplus\pi_n^*\ell_0\to A\). Also \((\iota_0,j)\colon\pi_n^*\ell_0\oplus C\to A\) is an isomorphism, \(C\) being the sum of the summands \(k\geq1\) and \(j\) its inclusion. Let \(p_L,p_D\) and \(p'_D,p_C\) be the projections of the two decompositions, so that \(\beta p_L+\iota_0p_D=\mathrm{id}=\iota_0p'_D+jp_C\), \(p_L\beta=\mathrm{id}\), \(p_Cj=\mathrm{id}\) and \(p_L\iota_0=0=p_C\iota_0\). Then \(p_Lj\colon C\to L_n\) and \(p_C\beta\colon L_n\to C\) are inverse: \(p_Ljp_C\beta=p_L(\mathrm{id}-\iota_0p'_D)\beta=\mathrm{id}\) and \(p_C\beta p_Lj=p_C(\mathrm{id}-\iota_0p_D)j=\mathrm{id}\). So \(L_{G^n/S}\cong C\cong\bigoplus_{k=1}^n\pi_n^*\ell_G\).

(b) If \(G\) is smooth, \(L_{G/S}=\Omega_{G/S}\) and \(\ell_G=\omega_G\) are modules in degree \(0\), and so is every level of \(\ell_{P/N}\). The level \(1\) of (3.1) is a distinguished triangle of modules in degree \(0\), and its long exact cohomology sequence [Tag 0149] is the short exact sequence stated. The level \(1\) of \(\epsilon\) is \(\ell_{v_0}\) on the first summand and \(\ell_{v_1}\) on the second. The proof of Proposition 2.4 computes them: \(\tau_\sigma\circ\ell_{v_0}=\pi^*\tau_e\) and \(\tau_\sigma\circ\ell_{v_1}=c(\operatorname{int}(g))\circ\pi^*\tau_e\). So the map is \((u,v)\mapsto u+c(\operatorname{int}(g))(v)\), and its kernel consists of the pairs \((-c(\operatorname{int}(g))(v),v)\). Projecting to the second summand gives \(\Omega_{G/S}\cong\pi^*\omega_G\).
