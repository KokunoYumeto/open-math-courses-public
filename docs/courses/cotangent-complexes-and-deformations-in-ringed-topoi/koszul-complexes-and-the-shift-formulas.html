Koszul complexes and the shift formulas

Written by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic) on 20 October 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. Public domain (CC0).

Shifting a complex by one degree turns symmetric powers into exterior powers and exterior powers into divided powers: \[ \begin{gathered}LS^n(E[1])\cong(L\Lambda^nE)[n],\\ L\Lambda^n(E[1])\cong(L\Gamma^nE)[n].\end{gathered} \] These formulas convert questions about \(L\Lambda^n\) of the cotangent complex, which appear in its Chern classes and its de Rham theory, into questions about symmetric powers, and back. They come from Koszul complexes. An exact sequence \(0\to E\to F\to G\to0\) of flat modules gives exact sequences \(0\to\Lambda^nE\to\cdots\to S^nF\to S^nG\to0\). These are applied to the sequence \(0\to E\to C(E)\to E[1]\to0\) whose middle term, the cone of the identity, is contractible.

This lesson constructs the two Koszul complexes of a map of modules, proves their exactness for exact sequences of flat modules over a ring of a topos, and proves the shift formulas with their compatibility with products.

Prerequisites. Non-additive functors of complexes in a ringed topos, Sections 2–5; Homotopy sheaves and the free abelian Whitehead theorem, Section 5; Complexes, cones and localization, Lemma 1.2 and Proposition 5.2. From the AI-integrated Stacks project: spectral sequences of double complexes with finitely many nonzero terms on each antidiagonal [Stacks, Tag 0132] and bounded above complexes of flat modules are K-flat [Tag 06YQ].

Conventions

\(A\) is a sheaf of commutative rings on a site \(\mathcal C\). Tensor products are over \(A\). For an \(A\)-module \(M\), \(S(M)\), \(\Lambda(M)\) and \(\Gamma(M)\) are the symmetric, exterior and divided power algebras: the sheaves associated with the constructions on sections. \(\Gamma(M)\) is generated by symbols \(\gamma_k(x)\), \(x\in M\), \(k\geq0\), subject to \(\gamma_0(x)=1\), \(\gamma_1(x)=x\), \(\gamma_k(ax)=a^k\gamma_k(x)\), \(\gamma_k(x+y)=\sum_{i+j=k}\gamma_i(x)\gamma_j(y)\) and \(\gamma_i(x)\gamma_j(x)=\binom{i+j}{i}\gamma_{i+j}(x)\). For a complex \(X\), \(X[m]^k=X^{k+m}\) with differential \((-1)^md\).

1. The two Koszul complexes

A bigraded algebra \(R=\bigoplus R^{p,q}\) is anticommutative in the first degree if \(x'x=(-1)^{pp'}xx'\) for \(x\in R^{p,q}\), \(x'\in R^{p',q'}\), and similarly in the second degree. A derivation of bidegree \((-1,1)\) of an algebra anticommutative in the first degree is an \(A\)-linear map \(d\) of that bidegree with \(d(xx')=(dx)x'+(-1)^px(dx')\) for \(x\in R^{p,q}\).

Proposition 1.1. Let \(u\colon E\to F\) be a map of \(A\)-modules.

  1. On \(\Lambda E\otimes SF\), with \(\Lambda^pE\otimes S^qF\) in bidegree \((p,q)\), there is a unique derivation \(d\) of bidegree \((-1,1)\) with \(d(x\otimes1)=1\otimes u(x)\) and \(d(1\otimes y)=0\). It satisfies \(d\circ d=0\).
  2. On \(\Gamma E\otimes\Lambda F\), with \(\Gamma^pE\otimes\Lambda^qF\) in bidegree \((p,q)\), there is a unique derivation \(d\) of bidegree \((-1,1)\), for the sign rule of the second degree, with \(d(\gamma_k(x)\otimes1)=\gamma_{k-1}(x)\otimes u(x)\) and \(d(1\otimes z)=0\). It satisfies \(d\circ d=0\).

Proof. Uniqueness holds because the elements named generate the algebras. We prove existence on sections; the constructions are compatible with restriction and pass to the associated sheaves.

(1) For \(x_1,\ldots,x_p\in E\) and \(y\in S^qF\), put \[ \begin{aligned}&d(x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge x_p\otimes y)\\&\quad=\sum_{i=1}^p(-1)^{i-1}x_1\wedge\cdots\widehat{x_i}\cdots\wedge x_p\\&\qquad\qquad\otimes u(x_i)y.\end{aligned} \] The right side is multilinear and alternating in the \(x_i\): exchanging \(x_i\) and \(x_{i+1}\) exchanges two terms and changes all signs, and if \(x_i=x_{i+1}\) the two terms cancel and the others contain \(x_i\wedge x_{i+1}=0\). So \(d\) is well defined. The derivation rule holds by the same formula. The composite \(d\circ d\) is a derivation of even degree that vanishes on \(x\otimes1\) and on \(1\otimes y\), so it is zero. (In \(d^2\) of a monomial, the terms removing \(x_i\) and then \(x_j\) and those removing \(x_j\) and then \(x_i\) carry opposite signs and equal values, because \(S F\) is commutative.)

(2) A derivation of \(\Gamma E\) into a \(\Gamma E\)-module is determined by its values on the generators \(\gamma_k(x)\), and exists if and only if these values respect the defining relations. With the values \(D\gamma_k(x)=\gamma_{k-1}(x)\otimes u(x)\in\Gamma E\otimes F\), the relations hold: \[ \begin{aligned} &D\gamma_k(ax)=a^{k-1}\gamma_{k-1}(x)\otimes au(x)\\&\quad=a^kD\gamma_k(x),\\ &D\gamma_k(x+y)\\&\quad=\sum_{i+j=k-1}\gamma_i(x)\gamma_j(y)\otimes(u(x)+u(y))\\&\quad=D\Bigl(\sum_{i+j=k}\gamma_i(x)\gamma_j(y)\Bigr),\\ &D\bigl(\gamma_i(x)\gamma_j(x)\bigr)\\&\quad=\Bigl(\tbinom{i+j-1}{i-1}+\tbinom{i+j-1}{i}\Bigr)\\&\qquad\quad\cdot\gamma_{i+j-1}(x)\otimes u(x)\\&\quad=\tbinom{i+j}{i}D\gamma_{i+j}(x). \end{aligned} \] Extend \(D\) to \(\Gamma E\otimes\Lambda F\) by \(d(a\otimes z)=D(a)\wedge z\), where the \(F\)-component of \(D(a)\) is multiplied into \(\Lambda F\) on the left. This is a derivation for the sign rule of the second degree. The composite \(d\circ d\) is a derivation that kills the generators: \(d^2\gamma_k(x)=\gamma_{k-2}(x)\otimes u(x)\wedge u(x)=0\). Hence \(d\circ d=0\). \(\square\)

We write \(\operatorname{Kos}_\bullet(u)=(\Lambda E\otimes SF,d)\) and \(\operatorname{Kos}^\bullet(u)=(\Gamma E\otimes\Lambda F,d)\), and \(k^n\) for the part of total degree \(p+q=n\). The first is a chain complex with the exterior degree as homological degree, the second a cochain complex with the exterior degree as cohomological degree. A commutative square from \(u\) to \(u'\) induces maps of differential bigraded algebras \(\operatorname{Kos}(u)\to\operatorname{Kos}(u')\).

Lemma 1.2. For \(u\colon E\to F\) and \(u'\colon E'\to F'\), the isomorphisms \[ \begin{aligned}&\Lambda(E\oplus E')\otimes S(F\oplus F')\\&\quad\cong(\Lambda E\otimes SF)\otimes(\Lambda E'\otimes SF'),\\&\Gamma(E\oplus E')\otimes\Lambda(F\oplus F')\\&\quad\cong(\Gamma E\otimes\Lambda F)\otimes(\Gamma E'\otimes\Lambda F')\end{aligned} \] carry the Koszul differential of \(u\oplus u'\) to the tensor-product differential.

Proof. Both sides are derivations of the same bidegree, and they agree on the generators \(x\otimes1\), \(x'\otimes1\), \(\gamma_k(x)\otimes1\) and \(\gamma_k(x')\otimes1\), and on \(F\), \(F'\). \(\square\)

Construction 1.3. Let \(E\xrightarrow{u}F\xrightarrow{v}G\) be maps with \(vu=0\).

  1. \(\operatorname{Kos}_\bullet(u)\to SG\), zero on \(\Lambda^{\ge1}E\otimes SF\) and \(S(v)\) on \(SF\), is a map of differential bigraded algebras: \(d(x\otimes1)=u(x)\) is sent to \(vu(x)=0\).
  2. \(\Gamma E\to\operatorname{Kos}^\bullet(v)\), \(\gamma_k(x)\mapsto\gamma_k(u(x))\otimes1\), is one as well: \(d\gamma_k(u(x))\) equals \(\gamma_{k-1}(u(x))\otimes vu(x)\), which is \(0\).

2. Exactness

Theorem 2.1. Let \(0\to E\xrightarrow{u}F\xrightarrow{v}G\to0\) be an exact sequence of flat \(A\)-modules. Then the maps of Construction 1.3 are quasi-isomorphisms. In total degree \(n\geq1\) they give the exact Koszul sequences \[ \begin{gathered} 0\to\Lambda^nE\to\Lambda^{n-1}E\otimes F\to\cdots\\\cdots\to E\otimes S^{n-1}F\to S^nF\to S^nG\to0,\\[4pt] 0\to\Gamma^nE\to\Gamma^nF\to\Gamma^{n-1}F\otimes G\to\cdots\\\cdots\to F\otimes\Lambda^{n-1}G\to\Lambda^nG\to0. \end{gathered} \]

Proof. Finite free split case. Suppose \(F=E\oplus G\) with \(u\) and \(v\) the inclusion and projection, and \(E\), \(G\) finite free. Then \(u=1_E\oplus(0\to G)\) and \(v=(E\to0)\oplus1_G\). By Lemma 1.2, \[ \begin{gathered}\operatorname{Kos}_\bullet(u)\cong\operatorname{Kos}_\bullet(1_E)\otimes SG,\\\operatorname{Kos}^\bullet(v)\cong\Gamma E\otimes\operatorname{Kos}^\bullet(1_G),\end{gathered} \] with zero differential on \(SG\) and on \(\Gamma E\), and the maps of Construction 1.3 are the projections onto, respectively the inclusions of, the parts of degree \(0\) of \(\operatorname{Kos}(1)\). It suffices to show that \(k^n\operatorname{Kos}_\bullet(1_M)\) and \(k^n\operatorname{Kos}^\bullet(1_M)\) are acyclic for \(n\geq1\) and \(M\) finite free. By Lemma 1.2, \(\operatorname{Kos}(1_{M'\oplus M''})=\operatorname{Kos}(1_{M'})\otimes\operatorname{Kos}(1_{M''})\). Its part of degree \(n\) is a sum of tensor products \(k^a\otimes k^b\) with \(a+b=n\), each with an acyclic factor if \(a\) or \(b\) is positive. These factors are bounded complexes of finite free modules, so locally contractible, and the tensor products are acyclic. So it suffices to treat \(M=A\). There \(k^n\operatorname{Kos}_\bullet(1_A)\) is \(\Lambda^1A\otimes S^{n-1}A\to S^nA\), \(e\otimes t^{n-1}\mapsto t^n\), an isomorphism. Likewise \(k^n\operatorname{Kos}^\bullet(1_A)\) is \(\Gamma^nA\to\Gamma^{n-1}A\otimes\Lambda^1A\), \(\gamma_n(1)\mapsto\gamma_{n-1}(1)\otimes e\), an isomorphism.

General case. The sequence is a bounded acyclic complex of flat modules. By Proposition 3.3 of Non-additive functors of complexes in a ringed topos, it is the local inductive limit of contractible complexes \(0\to E_i\to F_i\to G_i\to0\) of finite free modules over objects \(U_i\). Such a complex is split exact, and locally its terms decompose as in the first case. The Koszul complexes and the maps of Construction 1.3 are built from \(S\), \(\Lambda\), \(\Gamma\), tensor products and finite sums, which commute with local inductive limits. Local inductive limits are exact (Section 5 of Homotopy sheaves and the free abelian Whitehead theorem). The cones of the maps are local inductive limits of locally acyclic complexes, so they are acyclic. \(\square\)

Proposition 2.2 (duality). Let \(E\), \(F\) be finite free and \(M^\vee=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(M,A)\). Pair \(\Lambda^pE\) with \(\Lambda^p(E^\vee)\) by \[ \begin{aligned}&\langle x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge x_p,\xi_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\xi_p\rangle\\&\quad=\det(\xi_j(x_i)),\end{aligned} \] and \(S^qF\) with \(\Gamma^q(F^\vee)\) by \[ \begin{aligned}&\langle y_1\cdots y_q,\gamma_{k_1}(\eta_1)\cdots\gamma_{k_r}(\eta_r)\rangle\\&\quad=\sum_{\varphi}\prod_{i=1}^q\eta_{\varphi(i)}(y_i),\end{aligned} \] the sum running over the maps \(\varphi\colon\{1,\ldots,q\}\to\{1,\ldots,r\}\) with \(|\varphi^{-1}(j)|=k_j\). Pair \(\Lambda^pE\otimes S^qF\) with \(\Gamma^q(F^\vee)\otimes\Lambda^p(E^\vee)\) by the product of the two pairings. These pairings are perfect, and for \(u\colon E\to F\), \[ \langle d\alpha,\beta\rangle=\langle\alpha,d\beta\rangle \] for \(\alpha\in\Lambda^pE\otimes S^qF\) and \(\beta\in\Gamma^{q+1}(F^\vee)\otimes\Lambda^{p-1}(E^\vee)\), where the second \(d\) is that of \(\operatorname{Kos}^\bullet(u^\vee)\). So \(k^n\operatorname{Kos}_\bullet(u)\) is the dual complex of \(k^n\operatorname{Kos}^\bullet(u^\vee)\).

Proof. Take a basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_s\) of \(E\), a basis \(f_1,\ldots,f_r\) of \(F\) and the dual bases, and write \(u(e_i)=\sum_ja_{ji}f_j\). The monomials \(e_I=e_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_p}\) (\(i_1<\cdots<i_p\)) and \(e_I^\vee\) pair to \(\delta_{IJ}\). For multi-indices \(\rho,\nu\) the monomial \(f^\rho\) and the divided monomial \(\gamma_\nu(f^\vee)=\prod_j\gamma_{\nu_j}(f_j^\vee)\) pair to \(\delta_{\rho\nu}\): if \(f^\rho\) is written as a product of \(q\) basis vectors, exactly one \(\varphi\) sends every factor to its own index when \(\rho=\nu\), and none otherwise. Both pairings are therefore perfect.

Let \(\alpha=e_I\otimes f^\mu\) and \(\beta=\gamma_\nu(f^\vee)\otimes e_J^\vee\) with \(|I|=p\), \(|J|=p-1\), \(|\nu|=|\mu|+1\). Both sides vanish unless \(J=I\smallsetminus\{i_0\}\) and \(\nu=\mu+\delta_j\) for some \(i_0\) and \(j\). Then \[ d\alpha=\sum_{t}(-1)^{t-1}e_{I\smallsetminus\{i_t\}}\otimes u(e_{i_t})f^\mu \] gives \(\langle d\alpha,\beta\rangle=(-1)^{t_0-1}a_{ji_0}\), where \(i_0=i_{t_0}\). On the other side \(d\gamma_\nu(f^\vee)=\sum_k\gamma_{\nu-\delta_k}(f^\vee)\otimes u^\vee(f_k^\vee)\) with \(u^\vee(f_k^\vee)=\sum_ia_{ki}e_i^\vee\), so \(\langle\alpha,d\beta\rangle=a_{ji_0}\langle e_I,e_{i_0}^\vee\wedge e_J^\vee\rangle\). Moving \(e_{i_0}^\vee\) past the \(t_0-1\) smaller indices of \(J\) gives the sign \((-1)^{t_0-1}\). \(\square\)

3. The shift formulas

For a complex \(E\) of flat modules bounded above and \(n\geq1\), put \(\mathbf T(E)=\operatorname{Tot}N\mathbf T\Gamma\,iE\) for \(\mathbf T=S^n,\Lambda^n,\Gamma^n\), as in Section 2.2 of Non-additive functors of complexes in a ringed topos. By Theorem 4.4 there, \(L\mathbf T(E)=\mathbf T(E)\).

Lemma 3.1. Let \(0\to P_n\xrightarrow{\alpha_n}I_{n-1}\to\cdots\to I_0\xrightarrow{\alpha_0}Q\to0\) be an exact sequence of complexes in which every \(I_j\) is acyclic, \(n\geq1\).

  1. The sequence defines an isomorphism \(\sigma\colon Q\to P_n[n]\) in the derived category, natural for morphisms of such sequences.
  2. Let \(X\) be the total complex of the double complex whose column of index \(a\) is \(P_n\) for \(a=-n\) and \(I_{-a}\) for \(-n<a\leq0\), with the maps of the sequence as horizontal differentials and \((-1)^a\) times the differential of the column of index \(a\) as vertical differential: \(X^k=\bigoplus_a(\text{column }a)^{k-a}\), \(d=d_h+(-1)^ad_v\). The map \(e\colon X\to Q\) given by \(\alpha_0\) on the column of index \(0\) is a quasi-isomorphism, the projection \(\pi\colon X\to P_n[n]\) onto the column of index \(-n\) is a morphism of complexes, and \[ \sigma=(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}\,\pi\circ e^{-1}. \]
  3. Let \(Y\) be the total complex, with the same conventions, of the double complex whose column of index \(c\) is \(I_{n-1-c}\) for \(0\leq c<n\) and \(Q\) for \(c=n\). The map \(e'\colon P_n\to Y\) given by \(\alpha_n\) into the column of index \(0\) is a quasi-isomorphism, the inclusion \(\iota\colon Q[-n]\to Y\) of the column of index \(n\) is a morphism of complexes, and \[ \sigma[-n]=(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}\,e'^{-1}\circ\iota. \]

Proof. (1) Split the sequence into short exact sequences \(0\to K_{j+1}\to I_j\to K_j\to0\), with \(K_0=Q\) and \(K_n=P_n\). Each gives a distinguished triangle whose third morphism is \(\delta_j=(-p)r^{-1}\colon K_j\to K_{j+1}[1]\), where \(r\colon C(K_{j+1}\to I_j)\to K_j\) is the quasi-isomorphism and \(p\) the projection onto \(K_{j+1}[1]\) (Proposition 5.2 of Complexes, cones and localization); on cohomology it is the boundary map of the long exact sequence. Since \(I_j\) is acyclic, \(\delta_j\) is an isomorphism. Put \(\sigma=\delta_{n-1}[n-1]\circ\cdots\circ\delta_1[1]\circ\delta_0\), morphisms being shifted without sign. A morphism of sequences induces morphisms of the short exact sequences, and \(r\) and \(p\) are natural in them.

(2) The cone of \(e\) is isomorphic to the shift by one of the total complex of the double complex obtained by adding \(Q\) as a column of index \(1\), through \((q,x)\mapsto(-q,x)\). That double complex has exact rows and finitely many columns, so its total complex is acyclic [Tag 0132], and \(e\) is a quasi-isomorphism. The column of index \(-n\) receives no horizontal differential and has the differential \((-1)^nd\) of \(P_n[n]\), so \(\pi\) is a morphism of complexes. Put \(\rho_X=\pi\circ e^{-1}\) and \(\varepsilon_n=(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}\).

For \(n=1\), \(X\) is the cone \(C(\alpha_1)\), with \(C(\alpha_1)^k=I_0^k\oplus P_1^{k+1}\) and \(d(y,x)=(dy+\alpha_1x,-dx)\); \(e\) is the map \(r\) and \(\pi\) the map \(p\) of Proposition 5.2 there. So \(\sigma=(-p)r^{-1}=-\rho_X\).

Let \(n\geq2\). Let \(K=K_1\subset I_0\), and let \(R_1=(0\to K\to I_0\to Q\to0)\) and \(R'=\) \((0\to P_n\to I_{n-1}\to\cdots\to I_1\to K\to0)\), of lengths \(1\) and \(n-1\), with total complexes \(X_1\), \(X'\) and maps \(e_1,\pi_1\), \(e_{X'},\pi_{X'}\) as in (2). By definition \(\sigma=\sigma_{R'}[1]\circ\sigma_{R_1}\). Let \(\Phi\colon X\to X_1\) be the identity of \(I_0\) on the column of index \(0\), the corestriction \(I_1\to K\) of \(\alpha_1\) on the column of index \(-1\), and zero on the other columns; it is a morphism of complexes, because \(\alpha_1\alpha_2=0\). Let \(\Psi\colon X\to X'[1]\) be \((-1)^{a+1}\) times the identity from the column of index \(a\leq-1\) of \(X\) to the column of index \(a+1\) of \(X'\), and zero on the column of index \(0\). The vertical differentials agree, \((-1)^a\) for \(X\) and \(-(-1)^{a+1}\) for \(X'[1]\); the horizontal differentials of \(X\) and \(X'[1]\) differ by the sign \(-1\), which the factors \((-1)^{a+1}\) compensate; and the horizontal differential of \(X\) from the column of index \(-1\) to that of index \(0\) is killed by \(\Psi\), while \(X'\) has no column of index \(1\). So \(\Psi\) is a morphism of complexes. On the column of index \(-1\), \(\pi_1\Phi\) and \(e_{X'}[1]\Psi\) are both the corestriction of \(\alpha_1\), and both vanish on the other columns; and \(\pi_{X'}[1]\Psi=(-1)^{n+1}\pi\). With \(e_1\Phi=e\), this gives in the derived category \[ \begin{aligned} \rho_{X'}[1]\circ\rho_{X_1}\circ e&=\pi_{X'}[1]\,e_{X'}[1]^{-1}\,\pi_1\Phi\\ &=\pi_{X'}[1]\Psi=(-1)^{n+1}\pi, \end{aligned} \] so \(\rho_{X'}[1]\circ\rho_{X_1}=(-1)^{n+1}\rho_X\). By the case \(n=1\) and induction, \(\sigma=\) \(\varepsilon_{n-1}\rho_{X'}[1]\circ(-\rho_{X_1})=\) \((-1)^n\varepsilon_{n-1}\rho_X=\) \(\varepsilon_n\rho_X\).

(3) As in (2), the cone of \(e'\) is acyclic, being isomorphic to the total complex of the double complex obtained by adding \(P_n\) as a column of index \(-1\), and \(\iota\) is a morphism of complexes, no horizontal differential leaving the column of index \(n\). Put \(\rho'_Y=e'^{-1}\circ\iota\colon Q[-n]\to P_n\).

For \(n=1\), \(Y\) has \(Y^k=I_0^k\oplus Q^{k-1}\) and \(d(j,q)=(dj,\alpha_0j-dq)\), and \(\mu(j,q)=(-q,j)\) is an isomorphism of \(Y\) onto \(C(\alpha_0)[-1]\), where \(C(\alpha_0)^k=Q^k\oplus I_0^{k+1}\). It carries \(e'\) to \(\kappa\colon P_1\to C(\alpha_0)[-1]\), \(x\mapsto(0,\alpha_1x)\), and \(\iota\) to \(-i[-1]\), \(i\colon Q\to C(\alpha_0)\) the inclusion. On \(C(\alpha_1)\), with \(r\), \(p\) as in (2), the map \((y,x)\mapsto(0,y)\) to \(C(\alpha_0)\), of degree \(-1\), is a homotopy between \(i\circ r\) and \(-\kappa[1]\circ p\): its commutator with the differentials is \((y,x)\mapsto(\alpha_0y,\alpha_1x)\). So \(i=\kappa[1]\circ(-p)r^{-1}=\kappa[1]\circ\sigma\) in the derived category, and \(\rho'_Y=\kappa^{-1}\circ(-i[-1])=-\sigma[-1]\).

Let \(n\geq2\). Let \(K'=K_{n-1}\subset I_{n-2}\), and let \(S_1=(0\to P_n\to I_{n-1}\to K'\to0)\) and \(S'=\) \((0\to K'\to I_{n-2}\to\cdots\to I_0\to Q\to0)\), with total complexes \(Y_1\), \(Y'\) as in (3); by definition \(\sigma=\sigma_{S_1}[n-1]\circ\sigma_{S'}\). Let \(\lambda\colon Y_1\to Y\) be the identity of \(I_{n-1}\) on the column of index \(0\) and the inclusion \(K'\to I_{n-2}\) on the column of index \(1\), and \(j\colon Y'[-1]\to Y\) be \((-1)^c\) times the identity from the column of index \(c-1\) of \(Y'\) to the column of index \(c\) of \(Y\). Both are morphisms of complexes, by the same comparison of signs as in (2), and \(\lambda e'_1=e'\), \(\lambda\iota_1=-j\circ e'_{Y'}[-1]\), \(j\circ\iota_{Y'}[-1]=(-1)^n\iota\). Hence, in the derived category, \[ \begin{aligned}&\iota\\ &\quad =(-1)^nj\,e'_{Y'}[-1]\,\rho'_{Y'}[-1]\\ &\quad =(-1)^{n+1}\lambda\iota_1\rho'_{Y'}[-1]\\ &\quad =(-1)^{n+1}e'\circ\rho'_{Y_1}\circ\rho'_{Y'}[-1],\end{aligned} \] so \(\rho'_Y=(-1)^{n+1}\rho'_{Y_1}\circ\rho'_{Y'}[-1]\). By the case \(n=1\) and induction, \(\sigma[-n]=\) \(\sigma_{S_1}[-1]\circ\sigma_{S'}[-n]=\) \((-\rho'_{Y_1})\circ\varepsilon_{n-1}\rho'_{Y'}[-1]=\) \(\varepsilon_n\rho'_Y\). \(\square\)

Theorem 3.2. For \(E\in D^-(A)\) and \(n\ge1\) there are canonical isomorphisms \[ \begin{aligned}&\text{(i)}\quad LS^n(E[1])\cong(L\Lambda^nE)[n],\\&\text{(ii)}\quad L\Lambda^n(E[1])\cong(L\Gamma^nE)[n].\end{aligned} \]

Proof. Represent \(E\) by a bounded-above complex of flat modules. Let \(C(E)\) be the cone of \(1_E\); it is contractible. There is an exact sequence of complexes of flat modules \[ 0\to E\to C(E)\to E[1]\to0, \] split in each degree. Apply the exact functor \(\Gamma\,i\): this gives an exact sequence of mixed objects of flat modules, \(0\to\Gamma\,iE\to\Gamma\,iC(E)\to\Gamma\,i(E[1])\to0\). Apply Theorem 2.1 term by term, then \(\operatorname{Tot}N\), which is exact. For (i) this gives an exact sequence of complexes \[ \begin{gathered}0\to\Lambda^nE\to I_{n-1}\to\cdots\\\cdots\to I_0\to S^n(E[1])\to0,\end{gathered} \] with \(I_{n-p}=\operatorname{Tot}N\bigl(\Lambda^{n-p}\Gamma\,iE\otimes S^p\Gamma\,iC(E)\bigr)\) for \(1\le p\le n\). By (5.1) of the same lesson, \(I_{n-p}\) is homotopy equivalent to \(\Lambda^{n-p}E\otimes S^pC(E)\). Since \(C(E)\) is homotopy equivalent to \(0\) and \(S^p(0)=0\), Proposition 2.3 there makes \(S^pC(E)\) contractible, so \(I_{n-p}\) is acyclic. Lemma 3.1 gives \(S^n(E[1])\cong\Lambda^nE[n]\), which is (i). The second Koszul sequence gives (ii) in the same way, the intermediate terms being \(\Gamma^{n-p}C(E)\otimes\Lambda^p(E[1])\) with \(p<n\). Naturality in \(E\) follows from the naturality of every step, and independence of the flat representative from Theorem 4.4 there. \(\square\)

Products. Let \(\tau\colon X[m]\otimes Y[n]\to(X\otimes Y)[m+n]\), \(x\otimes y\mapsto(-1)^{n\,|x|}x\otimes y\), where \(|x|\) is the degree of \(x\) in \(X[m]\). This is an isomorphism of complexes, natural in \(X\) and \(Y\). For morphisms \(f\colon Q[-m]\to P\) and \(g\colon Q'[-n]\to P'\), a check of the signs on \(q\otimes q'\) gives \[ \begin{aligned}&\bigl((f\otimes g)\circ\tau^{-1}\bigr)[m+n]\\&\quad =(-1)^{mn}\,\tau\\&\quad \circ(f[m]\otimes g[n]).\end{aligned} \tag{3.1} \]

For double complexes \(D\), \(D'\) with the conventions of Lemma 3.1 (2), let \(D\otimes D'\) be the double complex whose column of index \(c\) is \(\bigoplus_{a+a'=c}D_a\otimes D'_{a'}\), with the tensor product differential of complexes as vertical differential and \(d_h(x\otimes y)=d_hx\otimes y+(-1)^ax\otimes d_hy\) for \(x\) in the column of index \(a\). Then \[ \begin{gathered}\varphi \colon\operatorname{Tot}D\otimes\operatorname{Tot}D'\\ \to\operatorname{Tot}(D\otimes D'),\\ x\otimes y\mapsto(-1)^{ba'}x\otimes y,\end{gathered} \] for \(x\) of vertical degree \(b\) and \(y\) in the column of index \(a'\), is an isomorphism of complexes: on each of the four components of the differential the two signs agree.

Lemma 3.3. Let \(R\colon\) \(0\to\) \(P\to\) \(I_{m-1}\to\) \(\cdots\to\) \(I_0\to\) \(Q\to\) \(0\) and \(R'\colon\) \(0\to\) \(P'\to\) \(I'_{n-1}\to\) \(\cdots\to\) \(I'_0\to\) \(Q'\to\) \(0\) be exact sequences of bounded above complexes of flat modules, with all \(I_j\), \(I'_j\) acyclic and \(m,n\geq1\), and let \(\sigma_R\), \(\sigma_{R'}\) be their isomorphisms (Lemma 3.1).

  1. Let \(D\), \(D'\) be the double complexes of Lemma 3.1 (2) (columns of index \(-m,\ldots,0\) and \(-n,\ldots,0\)). The columns of \(D\otimes D'\), followed by the map to \(Q\otimes Q'\) induced by the two augmentations, form an exact sequence \(R\otimes R'\) of length \(m+n\), from \(P\otimes P'\) to \(Q\otimes Q'\), with acyclic intermediate terms, and its isomorphism is \(\tau\circ(\sigma_R\otimes\sigma_{R'})\).
  2. Let \(D\), \(D'\) be instead the double complexes of Lemma 3.1 (3) (columns of index \(0,\ldots,m\) and \(0,\ldots,n\)). The map \(P\otimes P'\to D_0\otimes D'_0\), followed by the columns of \(D\otimes D'\), form an exact sequence \(R\otimes'R'\) of length \(m+n\), from \(P\otimes P'\) to \(Q\otimes Q'\), with acyclic intermediate terms, and its isomorphism is \((-1)^{mn}\tau\circ(\sigma_R\otimes\sigma_{R'})\).

Proof. In each vertical degree the rows of \(D\) and \(D'\), augmented, are resolutions of flat modules by flat modules; their tensor product computes the Tor of these flat modules, so it is a resolution of their tensor product. Hence \(R\otimes R'\) is exact, and likewise \(R\otimes'R'\), whose rows are tensor products of coresolutions. An intermediate term of \(R\otimes R'\) is a sum of tensor products \(D_a\otimes D'_{a'}\) with \((a,a')\neq(-m,-n)\), each with an acyclic factor; the other factor is a bounded above complex of flat modules, hence K-flat [Tag 06YQ], and the tensor product is acyclic. The same holds for the terms of \(R\otimes'R'\) other than \(Q\otimes Q'\).

(1) With the maps \(e\), \(\pi\) of Lemma 3.1 (2), \(e_{R\otimes R'}\circ\varphi=e_R\otimes e_{R'}\), and \(\pi_{R\otimes R'}\circ\varphi=(-1)^{mn}\tau\circ(\pi_R\otimes\pi_{R'})\): for \(x\in P^b\) in the column of index \(-m\) and \(y\) in the column of index \(-n\), \(\varphi\) gives \((-1)^{-bn}\), while \(\tau\) gives \((-1)^{n(b-m)}\), \(x\) having degree \(b-m\) in \(P[m]\). As \(e_R\otimes e_{R'}\) is a quasi-isomorphism of K-flat complexes, \(\pi_{R\otimes R'}e_{R\otimes R'}^{-1}=\) \((-1)^{mn}\tau\circ(\pi_Re_R^{-1}\otimes\pi_{R'}e_{R'}^{-1})\). By Lemma 3.1 (2) the isomorphism of \(R\otimes R'\) is \(\varepsilon_{m+n}(-1)^{mn}\varepsilon_m\varepsilon_n\,\tau\circ(\sigma_R\otimes\sigma_{R'})\), and \(\varepsilon_{m+n}\varepsilon_m\varepsilon_n=(-1)^{mn}\).

(2) With the maps \(e'\), \(\iota\) of Lemma 3.1 (3), \(\varphi\circ(e'_R\otimes e'_{R'})=e'_{R\otimes'R'}\) and \(\varphi\circ(\iota_R\otimes\iota_{R'})=(-1)^{mn}\iota_{R\otimes'R'}\circ\tau\): for \(x\in Q^b\) in the column of index \(m\) and \(y\) in the column of index \(n\), \(\varphi\) gives \((-1)^{bn}\) and \(\tau\) gives \((-1)^{n(b+m)}\). Hence \(\rho'_{R\otimes'R'}=(-1)^{mn}(\rho'_R\otimes\rho'_{R'})\circ\tau^{-1}\), and by Lemma 3.1 (3) and (3.1) the isomorphism of \(R\otimes'R'\) is \(\varepsilon_{m+n}(-1)^{mn}(-1)^{mn}\varepsilon_m\varepsilon_n\,\tau\circ\) \((\sigma_R\otimes\sigma_{R'})=\) \((-1)^{mn}\tau\circ\) \((\sigma_R\otimes\sigma_{R'})\). \(\square\)

Proposition 3.4. The isomorphisms of Theorem 3.2 are compatible with the products of Section 5 of Non-additive functors of complexes in a ringed topos, as follows. For \(m,n\geq1\), the product \[ \begin{aligned}&LS^m(E[1])\otimes^{\mathbf L}LS^n(E[1])\\&\quad\to LS^{m+n}(E[1])\end{aligned} \] corresponds, through (i), to the product \[ \begin{aligned}&L\Lambda^mE[m]\otimes^{\mathbf L}L\Lambda^nE[n]\\&\quad\to L\Lambda^{m+n}E[m+n]\end{aligned} \] composed with \(\tau\); and the product of \(L\Lambda(E[1])\) corresponds, through (ii), to \((-1)^{mn}\) times the product of \(L\Gamma(E)\), shifted, composed with \(\tau\). That is, \(\sigma\circ(\text{product})=\) \((\text{product})[m+n]\circ\tau\circ(\sigma\otimes\sigma)\) for (i), and the same with the factor \((-1)^{mn}\) for (ii).

Proof. (i) Let \(R_m\) be the sequence of the proof of Theorem 3.2 for \(S^m(E[1])\): its terms are \(\Lambda^mE\) and the complexes \(I_{m-p}\), in the columns of index minus their exterior degree, and its isomorphism is that of (i). These are the parts of total degree \(m\) of the Koszul algebra of \(\Gamma\,iE\to\Gamma\,iC(E)\), followed by Construction 1.3. The multiplication of the Koszul algebra, composed with (5.1), maps \(D_a\otimes D_{a'}\) to the column of index \(a+a'\), since exterior degrees add. It commutes with the horizontal differentials of Lemma 3.3, because the Koszul differential is a derivation with the sign \((-1)^p\) of the exterior degree \(p=-a\); it commutes with the vertical differentials, because (5.1) and the termwise multiplication are morphisms of complexes; and Construction 1.3 is a homomorphism of algebras. So the multiplication is a morphism of sequences \(R_m\otimes R_n\to R_{m+n}\) of length \(m+n\), equal to the product \(\Lambda^mE\otimes\Lambda^nE\to\Lambda^{m+n}E\) on the first terms and to the product \(S^m(E[1])\otimes S^n(E[1])\to S^{m+n}(E[1])\) on the last ones. By the naturality in Lemma 3.1 (1) and Lemma 3.3 (1), \(\sigma_{R_{m+n}}\circ(\text{product})=\) \((\text{product})[m+n]\circ\tau\circ(\sigma_{R_m}\otimes\sigma_{R_n})\). The complexes are bounded above with flat terms, so the tensor products compute the derived tensor products.

(ii) The sequence \(R'_m\) for \(\Lambda^m(E[1])\) is formed by \(\Gamma^mE\) and the parts \(\Gamma^{m-c}C(E)\otimes\Lambda^c(E[1])\) of the second Koszul complex, \(0\leq c\leq m\), after \(\Gamma\,i\) and \(\operatorname{Tot}N\); in the double complex of Lemma 3.1 (3) the part of exterior degree \(c\) is the column of index \(c\). The multiplication of the second Koszul algebra adds exterior degrees, its differential is a derivation with the sign \((-1)^c\) of the exterior degree, and Construction 1.3 is a homomorphism of algebras; so the multiplication is a morphism of sequences \(R'_m\otimes'R'_n\to R'_{m+n}\), and Lemma 3.3 (2) gives the factor \((-1)^{mn}\). \(\square\)

4. Exercises

Exercise 4.1. Let \(A\) be a ring and \(f\in A\) a nonzerodivisor. Write down \(\operatorname{Kos}_\bullet\) of \(f\colon A\to A\) in total degree \(n\), and compare it with the Koszul complex of the element \(f\).

Exercise 4.2. Use Theorem 3.2 to compute \(LS^2(A[1])\) and \(L\Lambda^2(A[1])\) for \(A\) a ring.

Exercise 4.3. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), show that \(\Lambda^2\) and \(\Gamma^2\) differ, and explain why the second shift formula needs \(\Gamma\) rather than \(S\).

Exercise 4.4. Let \(E=Ae_1\oplus Ae_2\), placed in degree \(0\), and let \(x_1,x_2\) be the classes of \(e_1,e_2\) in \(H^{-1}(LS^1(E[1]))=E\), and \(y_1,y_2\) the same classes in \(H^{-1}(L\Lambda^1(E[1]))\). Show that for \(n=1\) both isomorphisms of Theorem 3.2 are the identity of \(E[1]\), and that the isomorphisms for \(n=2\) send the product \(x_1x_2\in H^{-2}(LS^2(E[1]))\) to \(-e_1\wedge e_2\in\Lambda^2E\) and the product \(y_1y_2\in H^{-2}(L\Lambda^2(E[1]))\) to \(e_1e_2\in\Gamma^2E\).

5. Complete solutions

4.1. In total degree \(n\), \(\operatorname{Kos}_\bullet(f)\) is \(\Lambda^1A\otimes S^{n-1}A\to S^nA\), \(e\otimes t^{n-1}\mapsto ft^n\). Identifying both terms with \(A\), it is multiplication by \(f\): the Koszul complex of \(f\), which is a resolution of \(A/f\) because \(f\) is a nonzerodivisor.

4.2. By (i), \(LS^2(A[1])\cong\Lambda^2(A)[2]=0\). By (ii), \(L\Lambda^2(A[1])\cong\Gamma^2(A)[2]\cong A[2]\), free of rank one on \(\gamma_2(1)\).

4.3. For \(M=\mathbb F_2\), \(\Lambda^2M=0\), while \(\Gamma^2M=\mathbb F_2\gamma_2(1)\) and \(S^2M=\mathbb F_2\). The canonical algebra map \(S(M)\to\Gamma(M)\) sends \(x^2\) to \(x\cdot x=2\gamma_2(x)\), which is \(0\) in characteristic \(2\); so \(S^2M\to\Gamma^2M\) is zero, and \(S^2\) cannot replace \(\Gamma^2\) in (ii). By Exercise 4.2, \(L\Lambda^2(\mathbb F_2[1])\cong\mathbb F_2[2]\). The second Koszul sequence, which is exact over every ring, is the one that involves \(\Gamma\).

4.4. For \(n=1\) both sequences are \(0\to E\to C(E)\to E[1]\to0\), with \(C(E)^{-1}=E\), \(C(E)^0=E\) and \(d=1\). Its third morphism \((-p)r^{-1}\) is the identity: \(s(x)=((0,x),-x)\) is a morphism of complexes \(E[1]\to C(E\to C(E))\) with \(r\circ s=1\), so \(r^{-1}=s\) and \(-p\circ s(x)=x\). By Proposition 3.4 (i), the image of \(x_1x_2\) is the product of \(\tau(x_1\otimes x_2)\); since \(x_1\) has degree \(-1\) in \(E[1]\) and the second factor is shifted by \(1\), \(\tau(x_1\otimes x_2)=(-1)^{-1}x_1\otimes x_2\), with product \(-e_1\wedge e_2\). For (ii) the same computation gives \(-e_1e_2\), and the factor \((-1)^{1\cdot1}\) of Proposition 3.4 turns it into \(e_1e_2\).
