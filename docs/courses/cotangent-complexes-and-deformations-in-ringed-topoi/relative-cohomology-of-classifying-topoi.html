Relative cohomology of classifying topoi

Written by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic) on 23 October 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. Public domain (CC0).

Let \(G\) be a group scheme over a scheme \(S\), flat and locally of finite presentation, and \(B\) its classifying topos. The Ext groups of the cotangent complex of the nerve \(N(G)\) into the nerve of a module are relative Ext groups of the adjoint co-Lie complex on \(B\) (Corollary 3.5 of The adjoint co-Lie complex of a group scheme): both are cocones of maps that correspond to each other, and two cocones of one map are isomorphic. Such an isomorphism is not unique, and two of them cannot be compared without more information. This lesson constructs these identifications canonically. Relative Ext groups on \(B\) are computed with the cocone of the unit of the coinduction adjunction on an injective resolution; on the nerve, with the cocone of the unit of the restriction to the level \(0\). For every distinguished triangle that relates an object of the topos of the nerve to the nerve of an object of \(B\) through the adjunction map of the level \(0\), this gives a canonical isomorphism of Ext groups, natural in the triangle and compatible with restriction along homomorphisms of group schemes. As an application, for a homomorphism \(u\colon F\to G\), the groups that classify the deformations of \(G\) together with an extension of \(u\) to a given deformation of \(F\) are relative Ext groups of the adjoint co-Lie complex on the classifying topos of \(G\) modulo that of \(F\). In the same way, for a category scheme acting on schemes, the groups that classify the equivariant deformations of the target of an equivariant morphism are relative Ext groups of the equivariant cotangent complex on the classifying topoi. For two deformations of the target of a homomorphism, the difference of the obstructions is the image of the difference of the deformations under the connecting map of the triangle that relates the adjoint co-Lie complexes of the two groups, with a sign; the proof is a relation between Kodaira–Spencer classes over a fibre product. Finally, the relative Ext groups are relative cohomology groups with coefficients in the internal Hom, by an isomorphism of complexes that is natural in the first argument and compatible with restriction, which gives the forms of these results with dual coefficients.

The lesson proves:

  • relative Ext groups on the classifying topos, computed from an injective resolution \(J\) of the coefficients and the cocone of \(J\to\operatorname{coind}UJ\), with a natural distinguished triangle;
  • on the fppf topos of the nerve, an isomorphism \(\operatorname{Ext}^{j+1}_{B/S}(\lambda,M)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^j(X,\operatorname{ner}M)\) for every distinguished triangle \(X\to i_{0!}i_0^*\operatorname{ner}\lambda\to\operatorname{ner}\lambda\to X[1]\) whose second map is the adjunction map, natural in the triangle;
  • restriction maps along a homomorphism \(u\colon F\to G\) on both sides, which these isomorphisms respect;
  • relative Ext groups and relative cohomology of the classifying topos of \(G\) modulo that of \(F\), their comparison with the Ext groups of the arrow topos of the morphism of nerves, and the deformation groups of the target of a homomorphism as the groups \(\operatorname{Ext}^{j+1}(B_G/B_F;\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G,\varepsilon^*I)\);
  • for a morphism \(X\to Y\) of schemes with an action of a category scheme \(G\), relative Ext groups of the classifying topos of \([G,Y]^{\mathrm o}\) modulo that of \([G,X]^{\mathrm o}\), and the equivariant deformation groups of the target as relative Ext groups of the equivariant cotangent complex;
  • the anticommutation of Kodaira–Spencer classes over a fibre product, the relation between the connecting maps of the triangles of a homomorphism, and the difference of the obstructions for two deformations of the target in relative Ext groups;
  • relative Ext groups as relative cohomology with coefficients in the internal Hom, and the deformation theorems for homomorphisms with dual coefficients.

Conventions

\(S\) is a scheme, \(T\) the big fppf topos of \(S\) with its ring \(\mathcal O\), and \(G\) a group scheme over \(S\), flat and locally of finite presentation, with the classifying topos \(B=B[G,S]^{\mathrm o}\) of the lesson on the adjoint co-Lie complex: a module of \(B\) is a module \(M\) of \(T\) with a right action \(m\) of \(G\). The forgetful functor \(U\colon\operatorname{Mod}(B)\to\operatorname{Mod}(T)\) is exact and has the right adjoint \(\operatorname{coind}\), with the unit \(\eta_E\colon E\to\operatorname{coind}UE\) and the counit \(\mathrm{cu}_L\colon U\operatorname{coind}L\to L\), and \(\mathrm{cu}_{UE}\circ U\eta_E=\mathrm{id}\) (Proposition 4.2 of the lesson on equivariant sheaves). The adjoint of a map \(f\colon E\to\operatorname{coind}L\) is \(\mathrm{cu}_L\circ Uf\). Both \(U\) and \(\operatorname{coind}\) carry injective modules to injective modules, \(B\) being the classifying topos of a good two-sided pseudo-category satisfying (I) and (E) (Proposition 6.2 of the lesson on equivariant sheaves; Proposition 5.1 of the lesson on cohomology of equivariant modules, as in the proof of Corollary 3.5 of the lesson on the adjoint co-Lie complex).

\(N=N(G)\), and \(\mathcal N\) is its fppf topos \(\operatorname{Top}(N_{\mathrm{fppf}})\), ringed by \(\mathcal O\). The nerve \(\operatorname{ner}\colon\operatorname{Mod}(B)\to\operatorname{Mod}(\mathcal N)\) is exact, and its level \(0\) is the underlying module: \(i_0^*\circ\operatorname{ner}=U\), where \(i_0^*\) is the restriction to the level \(0\) (Proposition 1.5 (2) of the lesson on equivariant cotangent complexes). The functor \(i_0^*\) is exact, with the exact left adjoint \(i_{0!}\), \((i_{0!}E)_n=\bigoplus_{k=0}^n\pi_n^*E\) (Proposition 4.2 (b) of the lesson on morphisms of fibred topoi), and with the right adjoint \(i_{0*}\), the direct image of modules along the morphism of ringed topoi from \(T\) to \(\mathcal N\) given by the level \(0\), whose inverse image is \(i_0^*\); its level \(0\) is the identity, by the formula \(i_*(L)_j=\prod_{f\colon i\to j}f_*L\) of Proposition 3.1 (1) of the lesson on fibred topoi. So \(i_0^*i_{0*}=\mathrm{id}\), the counit is the identity, and \(i_0^*\) of the unit \(\nu_K\colon K\to i_{0*}i_0^*K\) is the identity of \(i_0^*K\) by the triangle identity. Since \(i_0^*\) and \(i_{0*}\) have exact left adjoints, \(i_{0!}\) and \(i_0^*\), they carry injective modules to injective modules [Tag 015Z].

For a map of complexes \(f\colon A\to C\), the cocone \(\operatorname{cocone}(f)\) is the complex with the terms \(A^k\oplus C^{k-1}\) and the differential \((a,c)\mapsto(da,f(a)-dc)\). The sequence \(0\to C[-1]\to\operatorname{cocone}(f)\to A\to0\), with \(c\mapsto(0,c)\) and \((a,c)\mapsto a\), is termwise split, and its distinguished triangle is \(\operatorname{cocone}(f)\to A\xrightarrow{f}C\to\operatorname{cocone}(f)[1]\) [Tags 014I, 014Q]: with the splitting \(a\mapsto(a,0)\), the connecting map is \(a\mapsto f(a)\). A commutative square \(b\circ f=f'\circ a\) of maps of complexes induces the map \((a,b)\colon\operatorname{cocone}(f)\to\operatorname{cocone}(f')\). For complexes \(E\), \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(E,\operatorname{cocone}(f))=\) \(\operatorname{cocone}(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(E,f))\), as one checks on the components of the differential. A bounded below complex of injective modules is K-injective [Tag 070J]: maps of complexes into it, up to homotopy, are the maps of the derived category, and \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(E,J)\) computes \(R\operatorname{Hom}(E,J)\) for every complex \(E\). In a short exact sequence of complexes whose third term is acyclic, the first map is a quasi-isomorphism.

1. Relative Ext groups on the classifying topos

Construction 1.1. Let \(M\in D^+(B)\). A resolution of \(M\) is a bounded below complex \(J\) of injective modules of \(B\) with an isomorphism \(M\cong J\) in \(D(B)\); it exists, \(\operatorname{Mod}(B)\) having enough injectives (Proposition 6.2 (2) of the lesson on equivariant sheaves). Put \[ J^{\mathrm{rel}}=\operatorname{cocone}\bigl(\eta_J\colon J\to\operatorname{coind}UJ\bigr), \] a bounded below complex of injective modules, hence K-injective. For a complex \(E\) of modules of \(B\) put \[ \begin{aligned}&R\operatorname{Hom}_{B/S}(E,M)=\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(E,J^{\mathrm{rel}}),\\&\operatorname{Ext}^j_{B/S}(E,M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{D(B)}(E,J^{\mathrm{rel}}[j]).\end{aligned} \]

Lemma 1.2.

  1. For every complex \(E\), \(R\operatorname{Hom}_{B/S}(E,M)=\) \(\operatorname{cocone}\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(E,\eta_J)\bigr)\), and under the adjunction \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(E,\operatorname{coind}UJ)=\) \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(UE,UJ)\) the map \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(E,\eta_J)\) is \(f\mapsto Uf\). So there is a distinguished triangle \[ \begin{aligned}&R\operatorname{Hom}_{B/S}(E,M)\\&\quad\to R\operatorname{Hom}_B(E,M)\\&\quad\xrightarrow{\ U\ }R\operatorname{Hom}_T(UE,UM)\\&\quad\to R\operatorname{Hom}_{B/S}(E,M)[1],\end{aligned} \] natural in the complex \(E\), and \(R\operatorname{Hom}_{B/S}(E,M)\) is a cocone of the map induced by \(U\), as in Corollary 3.5 of the lesson on the adjoint co-Lie complex.
  2. For two resolutions \(J\), \(J'\) of \(M\), a map of complexes \(g\colon J\to J'\) representing the identity of \(M\) exists and is unique up to homotopy, and \((g,\operatorname{coind}Ug)\colon J^{\mathrm{rel}}\to J'^{\mathrm{rel}}\) is a quasi-isomorphism. The induced isomorphisms \(\operatorname{Ext}^j_{B/S}(E,M)\to\operatorname{Ext}^j_{B/S}(E,M)'\) do not depend on \(g\) and are compatible with the triangles of (1).
  3. The functor \(E\mapsto R\operatorname{Hom}_{B/S}(E,M)\) carries distinguished triangles of \(D(B)\) to distinguished triangles of \(D(\mathbf Z)\).

Proof. (1) The first statement is the identity of the Conventions. The adjunction sends \(\varphi\colon E\to\operatorname{coind}UJ\) (in any degree) to \(\mathrm{cu}\circ U\varphi\), and \(\mathrm{cu}\circ U(\eta_J\circ f)=\mathrm{cu}\circ U\eta_J\circ Uf=Uf\). The complexes \(J\), \(\operatorname{coind}UJ\) and \(UJ\) are bounded below complexes of injective modules (Conventions), so the three Hom complexes compute \(R\operatorname{Hom}_B(E,M)\), \(R\operatorname{Hom}_B(E,\operatorname{coind}UJ)\) and \(R\operatorname{Hom}_T(UE,UM)\), and the triangle of the cocone is the triangle of the statement. Naturality in \(E\) holds on the complexes.

(2) \(J'\) is K-injective, so the identity of \(M\), a map \(J\to J'\) in \(D(B)\), is represented by a map of complexes \(g\), unique up to homotopy. Naturality of \(\eta\) gives \(\eta_{J'}\circ g=\operatorname{coind}Ug\circ\eta_J\), so \((g,\operatorname{coind}Ug)\) is a map of cocones. It maps the sequence \(0\to(\operatorname{coind}UJ)[-1]\to J^{\mathrm{rel}}\to J\to0\) to the corresponding one for \(J'\); its outer components are quasi-isomorphisms, \(g\) being one and \(Ug\) being a quasi-isomorphism between bounded below complexes of injectives, hence a homotopy equivalence, which the additive functor \(\operatorname{coind}\) preserves. By the long exact cohomology sequences it is a quasi-isomorphism. If \(h\) is a homotopy from \(g\) to \(g_1\), with components \(h^k\colon J^k\to J'^{k-1}\), then \((x,y)\mapsto(h(x),-\operatorname{coind}Uh(y))\) is a homotopy between the two maps of cocones, by the naturality of \(\eta\) applied to the \(h^k\). Compatibility with the triangles holds on the complexes.

(3) \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(-,J^{\mathrm{rel}})\) computes \(R\operatorname{Hom}(-,J^{\mathrm{rel}})\), which carries distinguished triangles to distinguished triangles [Tag 08JC]. \(\square\)

2. The comparison with the nerve

Construction 2.1. Let \(M\in D^+(B)\) with a resolution \(J\). Choose a bounded below complex \(I\) of injective modules of \(\mathcal N\) with a map of complexes \(\iota\colon\operatorname{ner}J\to I\) that is a quasi-isomorphism and injective in every degree (embed the terms successively into injective modules). Put \[ I^{\mathrm{rel}}=\operatorname{cocone}\bigl(\nu_I\colon I\to i_{0*}i_0^*I\bigr), \] a bounded below complex of injective modules, \(i_{0*}\) and \(i_0^*\) preserving injective modules. Let \(\theta\colon\operatorname{ner}(\operatorname{coind}UJ)\to i_{0*}i_0^*I\) be the map adjoint to \(i_0^*(\iota)\circ\mathrm{cu}_{UJ}\colon U\operatorname{coind}UJ\to UJ\to i_0^*I\), using \(i_0^*\operatorname{ner}=U\). Then \(\nu_I\circ\iota=\theta\circ\operatorname{ner}(\eta_J)\): both maps \(\operatorname{ner}J\to i_{0*}i_0^*I\) have the adjoint \(i_0^*(\iota)\), the first because \(i_0^*\nu_I=\mathrm{id}\), the second because \(\mathrm{cu}\circ U\eta_J=\mathrm{id}\). The nerve commutes with cocones, being exact, so \((\iota,\theta)\) is a map of complexes \[ c\colon\operatorname{ner}(J^{\mathrm{rel}})\longrightarrow I^{\mathrm{rel}}. \]

Lemma 2.2.

  1. \(i_0^*I^{\mathrm{rel}}\) is acyclic, and \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(i_{0!}E,I^{\mathrm{rel}})\) is acyclic for every complex \(E\) of modules of \(T\).
  2. For a complex \(X\) of modules of \(\mathcal N\) with \(i_0^*X\) acyclic, the projection \(I^{\mathrm{rel}}\to I\) induces a quasi-isomorphism \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(X,I^{\mathrm{rel}})\to\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(X,I)\), which computes \(R\operatorname{Hom}(X,\operatorname{ner}M)\).
  3. For a bounded above complex \(\lambda\) of modules of \(B\), the map \[ \begin{aligned}&\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(\lambda,J^{\mathrm{rel}})\\&\quad\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(\operatorname{ner}\lambda,I^{\mathrm{rel}}),\\&\qquad f\longmapsto c\circ\operatorname{ner}(f),\end{aligned} \] is a quasi-isomorphism.

Proof. (1) \(i_0^*\nu_I\) is the identity, so \(i_0^*I^{\mathrm{rel}}\) is the cocone of the identity of \(i_0^*I\), which is acyclic. By the adjunction, \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(i_{0!}E,I^{\mathrm{rel}})=\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(E,i_0^*I^{\mathrm{rel}})\), which computes \(R\operatorname{Hom}(E,i_0^*I^{\mathrm{rel}})=0\), \(i_0^*I^{\mathrm{rel}}\) being a bounded below complex of injective modules.

(2) By the Conventions, \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(X,I^{\mathrm{rel}})\) is the cocone of \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(X,\nu_I)\colon\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(X,I)\to\) \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(X,i_{0*}i_0^*I)\), and the target is \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(i_0^*X,i_0^*I)\), acyclic as in (1). So the projection is a quasi-isomorphism, and \(I\) is a K-injective complex isomorphic to \(\operatorname{ner}M\).

(3) By the Conventions the map is the map of cocones of the commutative square formed by \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(\lambda,\eta_J)\), \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(\operatorname{ner}\lambda,\nu_I)\) and the maps \(f\mapsto\iota\circ\operatorname{ner}(f)\), \(f\mapsto\theta\circ\operatorname{ner}(f)\). The first of these is the comparison map \(R\operatorname{Hom}_B(\lambda,M)\to R\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{ner}\lambda,\operatorname{ner}M)\) induced by the nerve, a quasi-isomorphism by Theorem 4.3 (2) of the lesson on cohomology of equivariant modules. The second becomes, under the adjunctions \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(\lambda,\operatorname{coind}UJ)=\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(U\lambda,UJ)\) and \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(\operatorname{ner}\lambda,i_{0*}i_0^*I)=\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(U\lambda,i_0^*I)\), the composition with the quasi-isomorphism \(i_0^*\iota\colon UJ\to i_0^*I\) between bounded below complexes of injective modules: the adjoint of \(\theta\circ\operatorname{ner}(f)\) is \(i_0^*(\iota)\circ\mathrm{cu}\circ Uf\), and \(\mathrm{cu}\circ Uf\) is the adjoint of \(f\). So it is a quasi-isomorphism too. A map of short exact sequences of complexes whose outer components are quasi-isomorphisms is one in the middle. \(\square\)

Theorem 2.3 (the canonical identification). Let \(\lambda\in D^b(B)\), \(M\in D^+(B)\), and let \[ \begin{aligned}&X\xrightarrow{\ \beta\ }i_{0!}i_0^*\operatorname{ner}\lambda\\&\quad\xrightarrow{\ \epsilon\ }\operatorname{ner}\lambda\xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{at}\ }X[1]\end{aligned} \] be a distinguished triangle of \(D(\mathcal N)\) whose second map is the adjunction map. Then \(i_0^*X=0\), and for every \(j\) there is a unique isomorphism \[ \Theta\colon\operatorname{Ext}^{j+1}_{B/S}(\lambda,M)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^j(X,\operatorname{ner}M) \] such that, for every \(f\), \(\Theta(f)\) is the image under \(I^{\mathrm{rel}}\to I\) of the unique \(\psi\in\operatorname{Hom}(X,I^{\mathrm{rel}}[j])\) with \(\psi[1]\circ\mathrm{at}=c\circ\operatorname{ner}(f)\) in \(\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{ner}\lambda,I^{\mathrm{rel}}[j+1])\).

  1. \(\Theta\) does not depend on the choices of \(J\) and \(I\).
  2. (Naturality.) Let \(X'\to i_{0!}i_0^*\operatorname{ner}\lambda'\to\operatorname{ner}\lambda'\xrightarrow{\mathrm{at}'}\) \(X'[1]\) be a second such triangle, \(b\colon\lambda\to\lambda'\) and \(a\colon X\to X'\) maps with \(\mathrm{at}'\circ\operatorname{ner}(b)=a[1]\circ\mathrm{at}\). Then \(\Theta(f'\circ b)=\Theta'(f')\circ a\) for \(f'\in\operatorname{Ext}^{j+1}_{B/S}(\lambda',M)\).
  3. The image of \(\Theta(f)\) under \(\operatorname{Ext}^j(X,\operatorname{ner}M)\to\) \(\operatorname{Ext}^{j+1}(\operatorname{ner}\lambda,\operatorname{ner}M)\), \(\varphi\mapsto\varphi[1]\circ\mathrm{at}\), is the nerve of the image of \(f\) in \(\operatorname{Ext}^{j+1}_B(\lambda,M)\) under the map of Lemma 1.2 (1).

Proof. The unit \(E\to i_0^*i_{0!}E\) of the adjunction \(i_{0!}\dashv i_0^*\) is the identity, the level \(0\) of \(i_{0!}E\) being \(E\); by the triangle identity, \(i_0^*\epsilon\) composed with it is the identity, so \(i_0^*\epsilon\) is the identity of \(U\lambda\). Applying the exact functor \(i_0^*\) to the triangle gives \(i_0^*X=0\) [Tag 05QR]. By Lemma 2.2 (1), \(\operatorname{Hom}(i_{0!}i_0^*\operatorname{ner}\lambda,I^{\mathrm{rel}}[k])=0\) for all \(k\), so the long exact sequence of \(\operatorname{Hom}(-,I^{\mathrm{rel}}[j+1])\) for the triangle [Tag 0149] shows that \(\psi\mapsto\psi[1]\circ\mathrm{at}\) is a bijection \(\operatorname{Hom}(X,I^{\mathrm{rel}}[j])\to\) \(\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{ner}\lambda,I^{\mathrm{rel}}[j+1])\). By Lemma 2.2 (3), \(f\mapsto c\circ\operatorname{ner}(f)\) is a bijection from \(\operatorname{Ext}^{j+1}_{B/S}(\lambda,M)\), and by Lemma 2.2 (2), the projection is a bijection \(\operatorname{Hom}(X,I^{\mathrm{rel}}[j])\to\) \(\operatorname{Hom}(X,I[j])=\) \(\operatorname{Ext}^j(X,\operatorname{ner}M)\). So \(\Theta\) is the composite of three bijections.

(1) For other choices \(J'\), \(I'\), choose \(g\) as in Lemma 1.2 (2) and a map of complexes \(h\colon I\to I'\) with \(h\circ\iota=\iota'\circ\operatorname{ner}(g)\): it exists because \(\iota\) is injective in every degree and a quasi-isomorphism, so that \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(I,I')\to\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(\operatorname{ner}J,I')\) is surjective in every degree, \(I'\) having injective terms, and a quasi-isomorphism, \(I'\) being K-injective; a surjective quasi-isomorphism of complexes is surjective on cycles of degree \(0\). Then \((h,i_{0*}i_0^*h)\colon I^{\mathrm{rel}}\to I'^{\mathrm{rel}}\) is a map of cocones, by the naturality of \(\nu\), and \((h,i_{0*}i_0^*h)\circ c=c'\circ\operatorname{ner}(g,\operatorname{coind}Ug)\): on the first components this is the choice of \(h\); on the second, both maps \(\operatorname{ner}(\operatorname{coind}UJ)\to i_{0*}i_0^*I'\) have the adjoint \(i_0^*(h\iota)\circ\mathrm{cu}_{UJ}=i_0^*(\iota'\operatorname{ner}g)\circ\) \(\mathrm{cu}_{UJ}=i_0^*(\iota')\circ\mathrm{cu}_{UJ'}\circ\) \(U\operatorname{coind}Ug\), by the naturality of \(\mathrm{cu}\). Composing the characterization of \(\psi\) with this map gives that of \(\psi'\), and the projections to \(I\), \(I'\) correspond under \(h\), which represents the identity of \(\operatorname{ner}M\).

(2) Let \(\psi'\) correspond to \(f'\). Then \((\psi'\circ a)[1]\circ\mathrm{at}=\psi'[1]\circ\mathrm{at}'\circ\) \(\operatorname{ner}(b)=c\circ\operatorname{ner}(f'\circ b)\), so \(\psi'\circ a\) corresponds to \(f'\circ b\), by uniqueness.

(3) The composite of \(c\) with the projection \(I^{\mathrm{rel}}\to I\) is \(\iota\) composed with the nerve of the projection \(J^{\mathrm{rel}}\to J\). So \(\Theta(f)[1]\circ\mathrm{at}\) is \(\iota\) composed with the nerve of the image of \(f\) in \(\operatorname{Hom}(\lambda,J[j+1])=\operatorname{Ext}^{j+1}_B(\lambda,M)\). \(\square\)

Remark 2.4. The triangles of Corollary 3.5 (2) and of Corollary 3.12 (1) of the lesson on the adjoint co-Lie complex, and the triangle of the proof of Theorem 3.1 (3) of the lesson on deformations of flat group schemes, are of this kind after \(L\varepsilon^*\); so the isomorphisms of Ext groups stated there can be taken to be the isomorphisms \(\Theta\), which are canonical and natural.

3. Restriction along a homomorphism

Let \(u\colon F\to G\) be a homomorphism of group schemes over \(S\), flat and locally of finite presentation, \(B_F=B[F,S]^{\mathrm o}\), and \(u^*\colon\operatorname{Mod}(B)\to\operatorname{Mod}(B_F)\) the restriction along \(u\), exact, with \(U_Fu^*=U\) and \(\operatorname{ner}(u^*M)=N(u)^*\operatorname{ner}(M)\) for the morphism of nerves \(N(u)\colon N(F)\to N\) and the inverse image \(N(u)^*\) of the fppf topoi, also exact (Proposition 2.5 of the lesson on the adjoint co-Lie complex). Write \(\mathcal N_F\) for the fppf topos of \(N(F)\), and \(\operatorname{coind}_F\), \(\mathrm{cu}^F\), \(\eta^F\) for the coinduction of \(B_F\). The level \(0\) of \(N(u)\) is the identity of \(S\), so \(i_0^*N(u)^*=i_0^*\) and \(N(u)^*i_{0!}E=i_{0!}E\) for a module \(E\) of \(T\), compatibly with the adjunction maps, the levels \(\bigoplus_k\pi_n^*E\) pulling back to \(\bigoplus_k\pi_{F,n}^*E\). So \(N(u)^*\) carries a triangle as in Theorem 2.3 for \(\lambda\) to one for \(u^*\lambda\).

Construction 3.1. Let \(M\in D^+(B)\), with a resolution \(J\) and \(\iota\colon\operatorname{ner}J\to I\) as in Construction 2.1. Choose a resolution \(J_F\) of \(u^*M\), a map of complexes \(g\colon u^*J\to J_F\) representing the identity of \(u^*M\), and \(\iota_F\colon\operatorname{ner}J_F\to I_F\) as in Construction 2.1 for \(F\). Let \(\kappa\colon u^*\operatorname{coind}UJ\to\operatorname{coind}_FUJ_F\) be the map adjoint to \(Ug\circ\mathrm{cu}_{UJ}\colon U\operatorname{coind}UJ\to UJ\to UJ_F\). Then \(\kappa\circ u^*(\eta_J)=\eta^F_{J_F}\circ g\), both having the adjoint \(Ug\), so \[ \rho=(g,\kappa)\colon u^*(J^{\mathrm{rel}})\longrightarrow J_F^{\mathrm{rel}} \] is a map of complexes, \(u^*\) commuting with cocones. The restriction map is \[ \begin{aligned}&\operatorname{res}\colon R\operatorname{Hom}_{B/S}(E,M)\\&\quad\to R\operatorname{Hom}_{B_F/S}(u^*E,u^*M),\\&\qquad f\longmapsto\rho\circ u^*(f).\end{aligned} \] Choose a map of complexes \(h\colon N(u)^*I\to I_F\) with \(h\circ N(u)^*(\iota)=\iota_F\circ\operatorname{ner}(g)\); it exists as in the proof of Theorem 2.3 (1), \(N(u)^*\iota\) being injective in every degree and a quasi-isomorphism. Let \(\mathrm b\colon N(u)^*i_{0*}i_0^*I\to i_{0*}i_0^*I\) be the map adjoint to the identity of \(i_0^*N(u)^*i_{0*}i_0^*I=i_0^*I\). Then \[ \begin{aligned}&h^{\mathrm{rel}}=\bigl(h,\ i_{0*}i_0^*(h)\circ\mathrm b\bigr)\colon\\&\qquad N(u)^*I^{\mathrm{rel}}\longrightarrow I_F^{\mathrm{rel}}\end{aligned} \] is a map of complexes: both \(\nu_{I_F}\circ h\) and \(i_{0*}i_0^*(h)\circ\mathrm b\circ N(u)^*(\nu_I)\) have the adjoint \(i_0^*h\), since \(i_0^*\nu=\mathrm{id}\) and \(i_0^*\mathrm b=\mathrm{id}\).

Lemma 3.2.

5. Equivariant deformations of the target

Let \(G\) be a category scheme over \(S\) and \(f\colon X\to Y\) a morphism of \(G\)-schemes, with the notation of Equivariant cotangent complexes: \(B_X=B[G,X]^{\mathrm o}\), \(B_Y=B[G,Y]^{\mathrm o}\), \(\varphi=N(G,f)\colon N(G,X)\to N(G,Y)\), and \(f_B^*\colon\operatorname{Mod}(B_Y)\to\operatorname{Mod}(B_X)\) the functor of Lemma 3.1 there, with \(\operatorname{ner}\circ f_B^*=\varphi_{\mathrm{big}}^*\circ\operatorname{ner}\). Definition 5.1. Lemma 5.2. Let \(\lambda\) be a bounded above complex of modules of \(B_Y\), \(M\in D^+(B_Y)\), and \(J\), \(J_X\), \(r\) as in Definition 5.1. Proof. (1) The functors \(\varphi_{\mathrm{big}}^*\circ\operatorname{ner}\) and \(\operatorname{ner}\circ f_B^*\) agree on maps, so the left side is \(s\circ\varphi_{\mathrm{big}}^*(\iota)\circ\operatorname{ner}(f_B^*a)=\iota_X\circ\) \(\operatorname{ner}(r)\circ\operatorname{ner}(f_B^*a)\).

The simplicial topoi of the nerves of the internal categories \([G,Y]^{\mathrm o}\) and \([G,X]^{\mathrm o}\) of \(T\) are good two-sided pseudo-categories satisfying (I) and (E) (Proposition 5.1 there), so by Theorem 4.3 (2) there both maps are quasi-isomorphisms.

(3) By (1) the two maps of (2) form a commutative square with \(a\mapsto r\circ f_B^*a\) and \(\psi\mapsto s\circ\varphi_{\mathrm{big}}^*\psi\), hence a map of the short exact sequences of the two cocones whose outer components are quasi-isomorphisms; so the map of cocones is a quasi-isomorphism. 6. Two deformations of the target

Let \(u\colon F\to G\) be as in Section 3, with the notation of 3.9 and Proposition 3.10 of the lesson on the adjoint co-Lie complex: \(P=N(G,G)\to N\) and \(P_F=N(F,F)\to N(F)\) are the universal torsors under \(G^{\mathrm o}\) and \(F^{\mathrm o}\), \(P'=N(F)\times_NP\), \(h\colon P_F\to P'\) and \(h_G\colon P'\to P\) are the morphisms of 3.9 there, and \(a\colon N(F^{\mathrm o},P_F)\to N(F)\) is the morphism of Section 1 there for the torsor \(P_F\). This section compares the third maps of three triangles there:

  • \(\mathrm{at}\colon\ell_{P/N}\to L_{N/S}[1]\), the third map of the triangle (3.1) of Theorem 3.3 there, for \(G\);
  • \(\delta\colon\ell_u\to LN(u)^*\ell_{P/N}[1]\), the third map of the triangle (3.2);
  • \(\mathrm{at}_u\colon\ell_u\to L_{N(F)/N}[1]\), the third map of the triangle of Theorem 3.11 there.

The comparison is a relation between Kodaira–Spencer classes over a fibre product, with a sign. Through the canonical identifications of Section 2, it turns the difference formula for the obstructions attached to two deformations of the target (Proposition 3.5 of the lesson on deformations of flat group schemes) into a formula in relative Ext groups of the classifying topos of \(F\).

For composable morphisms \(A\to B\to C\) of diagrams of schemes of the same type, write the transitivity triangle (Proposition 1.3 of Deformations of diagrams of schemes) as \[ \begin{aligned}&L_{B/C}|_A\xrightarrow{\ v_{ABC}\ }L_{A/C}\xrightarrow{\ w_{ABC}\ }L_{A/B}\\&\quad\xrightarrow{\ K(A/B/C)\ }L_{B/C}|_A[1],\end{aligned} \] where \(E|_A\) is the derived inverse image of \(E\) along the morphism to the diagram on which \(E\) lives, and \(v_{ABC}\), \(w_{ABC}\) are functoriality maps. For a commutative square of diagrams with the top \(A'\to A\), the bottom \(B'\to B\) and the vertical morphisms \(A'\to B'\), \(A\to B\), its functoriality map is the map \(L_{A/B}|_{A'}\to L_{A'/B'}\) of Proposition 1.2 there. Functoriality maps of composite squares are the composites of the functoriality maps (Theorem 2.2 of The cotangent complex).

Lemma 6.1 (Kodaira–Spencer classes over a fibre product). Let \(X\to Y\), \(Y\to Z_1\), \(Y\to Z_2\), \(Z_1\to W\), \(Z_2\to W\) and \(W\to V\) be morphisms of identity type of diagrams of schemes indexed by one small category, such that the square formed by the four middle morphisms commutes and is cartesian at every level, and every level of \(Z_2\to W\) is flat. 7. Dual coefficients

For modules \(E\), \(L\) of \(B\), write \(\mathcal Hom(E,L)\) for the internal Hom of modules of the ringed topos \(B\), and \(\mathcal Hom^\bullet\) for the internal Hom complex of two complexes. The forgetful functor \(U\) is the restriction to the localization \(B/G_\rho\) (Lemma 3.7 (1) of the lesson on the adjoint co-Lie complex), so \(U\mathcal Hom(E,L)=\mathcal Hom(UE,UL)\) [Tag 0E8H]. Tensor products of modules of \(B\) are computed on the underlying modules [Tag 03EL], so a module of \(B\) is flat if its underlying module is flat, and every object of \(D^-(B)\) is represented by a bounded above complex of flat modules: every module is a quotient of a module \(\operatorname{ind}(Q)\) with \(Q\) free, whose underlying module is flat (proof of Lemma 3.1 (2) of the lesson on equivariant cotangent complexes), and [Tag 05T7] applies. For a homomorphism \(u\colon F\to G\), the restriction \(u^*\) does not change the underlying modules, and it carries tensor products and internal Hom of modules of \(B\) to those of \(B_F\): both are the tensor products and internal Hom of the underlying modules, with the action of \(G\), respectively of \(F\) through \(u\), induced on them.

A map \(K'\to K\) in \(D^-(B)\), or another choice of \(P\), is represented by maps of complexes \(P\leftarrow P''\to P'\) with \(P''\) a bounded above complex of flat modules and the first map a quasi-isomorphism [Tag 05T7]; a quasi-isomorphism \(P''\to P\) induces quasi-isomorphisms of both sides, and another choice of \(J\) is connected by a map as in Lemma 1.2 (2). This gives the independence and the naturality on cohomology.

(2) The restriction of \(R\operatorname{Hom}_{B/S}\) is \(f\mapsto\rho\circ u^*f\) with \(\rho=(g,\kappa)\), and that of the relative cohomology is \(s\mapsto\rho_H\circ u^*s\) with \(\rho_H=(g_H,\kappa_H)\), where \(\kappa_H\) is adjoint to \(Ug_H\circ\mathrm{cu}_{UH}\). By Lemma 7.1 (3), \(u^*\mathcal Hom^\bullet(P,J^{\mathrm{rel}})=\mathcal Hom^\bullet(u^*P,u^*J^{\mathrm{rel}})\), and it suffices to show that \((\mathrm{id},\zeta_F)\circ\mathcal Hom^\bullet(u^*P,\rho)=\) \(\rho_H\circ u^*(\mathrm{id},\zeta)\). On the first components both are \(g_H\). On the second, both are maps \(u^*\mathcal Hom^\bullet(P,\operatorname{coind}UJ)\to\operatorname{coind}_FUH_F\); by the description of \(\zeta\) in the proof of Lemma 7.1 (2), the adjoint of each is the map \(\mathcal Hom^\bullet(UP,U\operatorname{coind}UJ)\to\) \(\mathcal Hom^\bullet(UP,UJ_F)\) given by composition with \(Ug\circ\mathrm{cu}_{UJ}=\mathrm{cu}^F\circ U\kappa\).

(3) Both maps send a section of \(H=\mathcal Hom^\bullet(P,J)\) over \(B\), a map \(P\to J\), to \(g\) composed with its restriction along \(u\). Cocones of equal maps are equal (Definition 4.1).

(4) By Lemma 3.7 (2) of the lesson on the adjoint co-Lie complex, \(K\) is perfect. The functor \(u^*\) is exact, carries tensor products to tensor products and flat modules to flat modules, and carries \(R\mathcal Hom(K,M)\) to \(R\mathcal Hom(u^*K,u^*M)\) through \(g_H\) (Lemma 7.1 (3), also for \(M=\mathcal O\)); so it carries the evaluation map of \(K\) and \(M\) to that of \(u^*K\) and \(u^*M\). The relative cohomology groups are functorial in the coefficients: a map of coefficients in \(D^+\) is represented by a map of their resolutions, unique up to homotopy, which induces maps of the cocones of Construction 1.1 and of Definition 4.1, as in the proof of Lemma 1.2 (2); an isomorphism induces isomorphisms of the cohomology groups, by the five lemma. \(\square\)

In particular (1) and (4) give the isomorphism of Proposition 3.8 of the lesson on the adjoint co-Lie complex by a construction that is natural in the first argument and compatible with restriction along homomorphisms.

Corollary 7.3 (dual coefficients in the deformation theorems).

  1. In Corollary 4.4, \(E^j\cong\) \(H^{j+1}\bigl(B_G/B_F,(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G)^\vee\otimes^{\mathbf L}\varepsilon^*I\bigr)\) for every \(j\): the obstruction to deforming \(G\) together with an extension of \(u\) to a given deformation of \(F\) lies in \(H^3\bigl(B_G/B_F,(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G)^\vee\otimes^{\mathbf L}\varepsilon^*I\bigr)\), the solutions form a torsor under \(H^2\), and their automorphisms form \(H^1\).
  2. In Corollary 6.4, let \(D'\in H^2\bigl(B/S,(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G)^\vee\otimes^{\mathbf L}\varepsilon^*I\bigr)\) correspond to \(D\) under Theorem 7.2 (1), (4), and let \(\operatorname{res}D'\) be its restriction along \(u\). Then \(\omega(u,G')-\omega(u,G'')\) corresponds to \(-\delta_C^\vee(\operatorname{res}D')\in\) \(H^3\bigl(B_F/S,C_u^\vee\otimes^{\mathbf L}\varepsilon^*I\bigr)\), where \(\delta_C^\vee\colon(u^*\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G[1])^\vee\to C_u^\vee\) is the dual of \(\delta_C\), acting on \(H^3\bigl(B_F/S,(u^*\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G[1])^\vee\otimes^{\mathbf L}\varepsilon^*I\bigr)=\) \(H^2\bigl(B_F/S,(u^*\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G)^\vee\otimes^{\mathbf L}\varepsilon^*I\bigr)\).

Proof. (1) Corollary 4.4 and Theorem 7.2 (3), (4), the underlying object \(L\varepsilon^*\ell_G\) of \(\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G\) being perfect (Theorem 3.1 of the lesson on big fppf sites). (2) By Corollary 6.4 the difference corresponds to \(-(\operatorname{res}D)[1]\circ\delta_C\). By Theorem 7.2 (2), \(\operatorname{res}D\) corresponds to \(\operatorname{res}D'\), and by the naturality of Theorem 7.2 (1), (4) in the first argument, applied to \(\delta_C\colon C_u\to u^*\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G[1]\), composition with \(\delta_C\) corresponds to the map induced by \(R\mathcal Hom(\delta_C,\varepsilon^*I)\), that is, by \(\delta_C^\vee\otimes^{\mathbf L}\varepsilon^*I\). The isomorphisms of Theorem 7.2 commute with the shift of the first argument, all the constructions in Lemma 7.1 being compatible with shifts; this identifies \((\operatorname{res}D)[1]\) with \(\operatorname{res}D'\) in the stated groups. The underlying objects of \(u^*\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G\) and \(C_u\) are perfect (Proposition 3.10 (2) of the lesson on the adjoint co-Lie complex). \(\square\)

9. Complete solutions

8.1. A module of \(B[S,S]^{\mathrm o}\) is a module of \(T\) with an action of the trivial group, that is, with no additional data, so \(B_F=T\) and the restriction along the unit forgets the action: \(u^*=U\). The complex \(UJ\) is a bounded below complex of injective modules (Conventions) and represents \(UM\), so it is a resolution of \(u^*M\), and \(g=\mathrm{id}\) represents the identity. With these choices Definition 4.1 is the cocone of \(f\mapsto Uf\), which by Lemma 1.2 (1) is \(R\operatorname{Hom}_{B/S}(\lambda,M)\) up to the identification \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(\lambda,\operatorname{coind}UJ)=\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(U\lambda,UJ)\): the cocone of \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(\lambda,\eta_J)\) is carried isomorphically onto the cocone of \(U\) by the adjunction, which is an isomorphism of complexes.

8.2. For \(u=\mathrm{id}\), \(u^*\) is the identity, \(J_F=J\) and \(g=\mathrm{id}\); the map of Definition 4.1 is the identity of \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(\lambda,J)\), whose cocone is acyclic. In Corollary 4.4 the groups \(E^j\) are then isomorphic to \(\operatorname{Ext}^{j+1}(B/B;\ell^{\mathrm{ad}}_G,\varepsilon^*I)=0\); directly, the relative Ext groups of the arrow topos of an identity vanish by the long exact sequence of Proposition 3.2 of the lesson on the arrow topos, whose second map is then the identity. The obstruction vanishes, the solutions form a torsor under \(0\), and the automorphism group is trivial: given \(F'\), the pair \((G',u')=(F',\mathrm{id})\) is the unique solution up to unique isomorphism.

8.3. On \((x,y)\in J^k\oplus(\operatorname{coind}UJ)^{k-1}\), with \(d(x,y)=(dx,\eta(x)-dy)\), \(\eta=\eta_J\), \(\eta'=\eta_{J'}\) and \(\tilde h=\operatorname{coind}Uh\): \(dH(x,y)=(dh(x),\eta'h(x)+d\tilde h(y))\) and \(Hd(x,y)=(hd(x),-\tilde h\eta(x)+\tilde hd(y))\). Naturality of \(\eta\) applied to the module maps \(h^k\) gives \(\tilde h\eta=\eta'h\), so the sum is \(((dh+hd)(x),(d\tilde h+\tilde hd)(y))=\) \(((g_1-g)(x),\operatorname{coind}U(g_1-g)(y))\), as \(\operatorname{coind}U\) is additive and commutes with differentials. With \(+\tilde h\) in the second component, the terms \(\eta'h(x)\) and \(\tilde h\eta(x)\) would add instead of cancelling.

8.4. For \(f=\mathrm{id}_Y\), \(f_B^*\) is the identity; take \(J_X=J\) and \(r=\mathrm{id}\). The map of Definition 5.1 is then the identity of \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(\lambda,J)\). In the long exact cohomology sequence of the termwise split sequence \(0\to\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(\lambda,J)[-1]\to\operatorname{cocone}\to\) \(\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(\lambda,J)\to0\), the connecting maps are the maps induced by this identity (Conventions), hence bijective, so the cocone is acyclic. By Theorem 5.3 (1) all groups \(E^i\) vanish; directly, the relative Ext groups of the arrow topos of an identity vanish (Exercise 5.3 of the arrow topos lesson). With \(X=Y\), \(f=\mathrm{id}\) and \(h=g\), the triple \((X',i,h')\), an equivariant deformation of \(g\) over \(Z'\), is an equivariant \(Z'\)-extension of \(Y\) for \(kg\) whose ideal map from \(g_B^*K\) is an isomorphism (Definition 3.1 of the lesson on equivariant extensions), and \(f'=\mathrm{id}_{X'}\) satisfies \(f'\circ i=i\circ f\) and \(h'\circ f'=h'\). So it is an equivariant solution. By Theorem 2.2 there the isomorphism classes of equivariant solutions form a torsor under \(E^1=0\), so there is one class, and the automorphism group \(E^0\) of a solution is trivial, so the isomorphism to \((X',\mathrm{id}_{X'})\) is unique.

8.5. The cotangent complex of an identity morphism is zero, so \(L_{Z_1/W}=L_{W/W}=0\), and \(K(Z_1/W/V)\), a map out of it, is zero; the right side of Lemma 6.1 vanishes. With \(Y=Z_2\), the morphism \(Y\to Z_1\) is \(Z_2\to W\) and \(Y\to Z_2\) is the identity, so the square of \(\xi_2\) has the identities as top and bottom, and its functoriality map \(\xi_2\colon L_{Z_2/W}\to L_{Y/Z_1}=L_{Z_2/W}\) is the identity. The left side of Lemma 6.1 is then \(K(Z_2/W/V)|_X[1]\circ K(X/Z_2/W)\), which therefore vanishes. This is the vanishing \(K(Y/W/V)|_X[1]\circ K(X/Y/W)=0\) of the step naturality in the base, for \(Y=Z_2\): there it follows from the factorization of \(K(X/Y/W)\) through \(K(X/Y/V)\) and from \(K(Y/W/V)\circ w_{YWV}=0\).
