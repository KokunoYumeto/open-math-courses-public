A cubic bound for a fixed question distribution

Written by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), October 2026. Self-checked by the writing AI. Public domain (CC0).

This lesson proves Theorem 2.2 of Entangled games and threshold repetition without any external input: for a fixed question distribution \(\mu\), the probability of winning a fraction \(v+\delta\) of the repetitions decays at a rate proportional to \(\delta^3\). The vectors of Conditioning on a tested set depend on both players' questions. Within each connected component of the support of \(\mu\), adjacent question pairs share a one-question approximation, so each vector can be replaced by the vector of one fixed root pair, at a cost controlled along paths in the support. The root vectors define legal one-game strategies, which yields a conditioning estimate with square-root error, and the transfer of From conditioning to a threshold bound turns it into the cubic rate.

1. Support components and component games

Let \(H=\{(x,y):\mu(x,y)>0\}\), joined into a graph by an edge between two pairs that share a question. For the connected components \(H_j\) put \[ \begin{aligned} &\lambda_j=\mu(H_j),\qquad\mu_j=\mu(\,\cdot\mid H_j),\\ &\mu_{j,\min}=\min_{z\in H_j}\mu_j(z), \end{aligned} \] and \(\kappa_\mu=8\max_j(|H_j|-1)/\mu_{j,\min}\). This constant depends on the masses of \(\mu\), not only on its support; it is \(0\) when every component is a single pair.

Lemma 1.1. If \(\mu_X(x)>0\), all pairs of \(H\) with first entry \(x\) lie in one component; likewise for second entries.

Proof. Any two such pairs share the question \(x\), so they are adjacent. \(\square\)

So each player can determine the component from the local question alone. Let \(G_j\) be the game with question distribution \(\mu_j\) and the predicate of \(G\), and \(v_j=\omega^*(G_j)\).

Lemma 1.2. \(\sum_j\lambda_jv_j\le\omega^*(G)\).

Proof. Let \(\varepsilon>0\) and choose finite-dimensional strategies \(\mathcal S_j\) for the finitely many games \(G_j\) with success at least \(v_j-\varepsilon\). Let the players share the tensor product of the states of all \(\mathcal S_j\). On question \(x\), Alice determines the component \(j\) of \(x\) by Lemma 1.1 and applies her measurement of \(\mathcal S_j\) on the \(j\)-th factor; Bob does the same. On a pair of \(H_j\) both use \(\mathcal S_j\), so this strategy for \(G\) succeeds with probability \(\sum_j\lambda_j(\text{success of }\mathcal S_j)\ge\sum_j\lambda_jv_j-\varepsilon\). No supremum needs to be attained. \(\square\)

2. A conditioning estimate through the components

Proposition 2.1 ([OpenAI-TR, Proposition 5.1]). For every finite-dimensional strategy for the \(k\)-fold repetition and every proper set \(C\) of coordinates with \(p=\Pr(E_C)>0\), with \(m=k-|C|\), \(\tau=\tau(C)\) and the average conditional win rate \(\bar s(C)\), \[ \begin{aligned} \bar s(C)&\le v+\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/p)}{2m}}\\ &\quad+4\sqrt{\kappa_\mu\tau}+2\kappa_\mu\tau . \end{aligned} \tag{2.1} \] The bound does not depend on the dimension of the strategy or on \(k\).

Proof. The inputs. Use the construction of Conditioning on a tested set for \(C\), with the mode \(i\) restored in the notation. For a transcript \(r\) of mode \(i\) put \(w_{i,r}=w_i(r)/m\), and for a fresh pair \(z=(x,y)\) write \(\Psi_{i,z}^r=\Psi_{xy}^r\), with the local vectors \(\Psi_{i,x}^r,\Psi_{i,y}^r\) and the acceptance operators \(W_{i,r,z}\) of that lesson, \(0\preceq W_{i,r,z}\preceq I\). Let \[ c_{i,r,z}=\|\Psi_{i,z}^r-\Psi_{i,x}^r\|^2+\|\Psi_{i,z}^r-\Psi_{i,y}^r\|^2 . \] Averaging (2.2) and (2.3) and Lemma 3.1 of that lesson over the modes gives \[ \begin{aligned} &\sum_{i,r,z}w_{i,r}\mu(z)\|\Psi_{i,z}^r\|^2=p,\\ &\sum_{i,r,z}w_{i,r}\mu(z)\,c_{i,r,z}\le2p\tau,\\ &\sum_{i,r,z}w_{i,r}\mu(z)\\ &\quad\cdot\langle\Psi_{i,z}^r,W_{i,r,z}\Psi_{i,z}^r\rangle=p\bar s, \end{aligned} \tag{2.2} \] where \(\bar s=\bar s(C)\).

Replacement by a root. Choose a root \(\rho_j\in H_j\) in each component. Fix \(i,r\) and abbreviate \(\Psi_z=\Psi_{i,z}^r\) and \(c_z=c_{i,r,z}\). If \(z,z'\in H_j\) share the question \(y\), the common local vector \(\Psi_y\) gives \[ \begin{aligned} &\|\Psi_z-\Psi_{z'}\|^2\\ &\quad\le2\|\Psi_z-\Psi_y\|^2+2\|\Psi_y-\Psi_{z'}\|^2\\ &\quad\le2(c_z+c_{z'}), \end{aligned} \] and likewise with \(\Psi_x\) if they share \(x\). A simple path from \(z\) to \(\rho_j\) in \(H_j\) has at most \(|H_j|-1\) edges, and each vertex lies on at most two of them. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality along the path gives \[ \begin{aligned} &\|\Psi_z-\Psi_{\rho_j}\|^2\le4(|H_j|-1)\sum_{a\in H_j}c_a\\ &\quad\le\frac{4(|H_j|-1)}{\mu_{j,\min}}\sum_{a\in H_j}\mu_j(a)c_a . \end{aligned} \] Define \[ \begin{aligned} &E_j=\sum_{i,r}\sum_{z\in H_j}w_{i,r}\mu_j(z)\|\Psi_{i,z}^r-\Psi_{i,\rho_j}^r\|^2,\\ &E=\sum_j\lambda_jE_j . \end{aligned} \] Since \(\sum_z\mu_j(z)=1\), \(\lambda_j\mu_j(a)=\mu(a)\) and \(4(|H_j|-1)/\mu_{j,\min}\le\kappa_\mu/2\), the path estimate and (2.2) give \[ \begin{aligned} E&\le\frac{\kappa_\mu}2\sum_{i,r,a}w_{i,r}\mu(a)c_{i,r,a}\\ &\le\kappa_\mu p\tau . \end{aligned} \tag{2.3} \]

Legal component strategies. Put \[ \begin{aligned} P_j&=\sum_{i,r}\sum_{z\in H_j}w_{i,r}\mu_j(z)\|\Psi_{i,z}^r\|^2,\\ \widehat P_j&=\sum_{i,r}w_{i,r}\|\Psi_{i,\rho_j}^r\|^2,\\ T_j&=\sum_{i,r}\sum_{z\in H_j}w_{i,r}\mu_j(z)\\ &\qquad\cdot\langle\Psi_{i,z}^r,W_{i,r,z}\Psi_{i,z}^r\rangle,\\ \widehat T_j&=\sum_{i,r}\sum_{z\in H_j}w_{i,r}\mu_j(z)\\ &\qquad\cdot\langle\Psi_{i,\rho_j}^r,W_{i,r,z}\Psi_{i,\rho_j}^r\rangle . \end{aligned} \] If \(\widehat P_j>0\), the vector \[ \begin{aligned} \Xi_j&=\widehat P_j^{-1/2}\sum_{i,r}\sqrt{w_{i,r}}\\ &\qquad\cdot|i,r\rangle_A\otimes|i,r\rangle_B\otimes\Psi_{i,\rho_j}^r \end{aligned} \] is a unit vector in a finite-dimensional space, with flag registers \(|i,r\rangle\) held by both players. On question \(x\), Alice reads her register \((i,r)\) and applies the POVM \((\widetilde M_{r,x}^a)_a\) of mode \(i\) to her factor \(\mathcal K_A\); Bob does the same with \((\widetilde N_{r,y}^b)_b\). The state and the register distribution do not depend on the fresh questions, because the vectors are taken at the fixed root \(\rho_j\) and the weights do not involve the fresh pair; each measurement depends only on its own player's question. So this is a legal strategy for \(G_j\). The registers are perfectly correlated, so its success probability is \(\widehat T_j/\widehat P_j\). Hence, also when \(\widehat P_j=0\) (then \(\widehat T_j=0\)), \[ \widehat T_j\le v_j\widehat P_j . \tag{2.4} \]

Comparison. For vectors \(a,b\) and \(0\preceq W\preceq I\), \[ \begin{aligned} &\langle a,Wa\rangle-\langle b,Wb\rangle\\ &\quad=\langle a-b,Wa\rangle+\langle b,W(a-b)\rangle, \end{aligned} \] so \[ \begin{aligned} &|\langle a,Wa\rangle-\langle b,Wb\rangle|\\ &\quad\le(\|a\|+\|b\|)\|a-b\| . \end{aligned} \] With \(W=I\) or \(W=W_{i,r,z}\), the weighted Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives \[ \begin{aligned} &|P_j-\widehat P_j|,\ |T_j-\widehat T_j|\le e_j,\\ &e_j:=\bigl(\sqrt{P_j}+\sqrt{\widehat P_j}\bigr)\sqrt{E_j} . \end{aligned} \] Since \(\sum_j\lambda_jP_j=p\) by (2.2), the triangle inequality in the weighted direct sum of the spaces gives \(\sqrt{\widehat P}\le\sqrt p+\sqrt E\) for \(\widehat P=\sum_j\lambda_j\widehat P_j\), and another Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives \[ \begin{aligned} \sum_j\lambda_je_j&\le\bigl(\sqrt p+\sqrt{\widehat P}\bigr)\sqrt E\\ &\le2\sqrt{pE}+E . \end{aligned} \] By (2.4) and \(v_j\le1\), \(T_j\le\widehat T_j+e_j\le v_j\widehat P_j+e_j\le v_jP_j+2e_j\). Since \(\sum_j\lambda_jT_j=p\bar s\) by (2.2), \[ \begin{aligned} \bar s&\le\sum_j\widetilde\lambda_jv_j+4\sqrt{E/p}\\ &\quad+2E/p, \end{aligned} \tag{2.5} \] where \(\widetilde\lambda_j=\lambda_jP_j/p\).

The component weights. Let \(J_i\) be the component of the fresh pair at mode \(i\). By (2.2) of the previous lesson, \(\lambda_jP_j=\frac1m\sum_i\Pr(E_C,\ J_i=j)\), so \(\widetilde\lambda\) is the average over \(i\) of the law of \(J_i\) given \(E_C\). The \(J_i\) are independent with law \(\lambda=(\lambda_j)\), so Lemma 4.1 of the previous lesson and convexity of total variation give \(\operatorname{TV}(\widetilde\lambda,\lambda)\le\sqrt{\log(1/p)/(2m)}\). Since \(0\le v_j\le1\), \[ \begin{aligned} \sum_j\widetilde\lambda_jv_j&\le\sum_j\lambda_jv_j+\operatorname{TV}(\widetilde\lambda,\lambda)\\ &\le v+\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/p)}{2m}}, \end{aligned} \] by Lemma 1.2. Substituting this and (2.3) into (2.5) proves (2.1). \(\square\)

For fixed \(\mu\) the error in (2.1) is \(O(\sqrt\tau+\tau)\), while (S) has error of order \(\tau^{1/4}\) with a universal constant. Where both apply, the smaller one may be used.

3. The cubic threshold bound

Proof of Theorem 2.2 of Entangled games and threshold repetition. Since \(\log(1/p)\le m\tau\), Proposition 2.1 bounds the average conditional win rate by \[ v+\sqrt{\tau/2}+4\sqrt{\kappa_\mu\tau}+2\kappa_\mu\tau . \] Let \(0<\delta<1-v\) and \(T=\delta^2/(1024(1+\kappa_\mu))\). If \(\tau(C)\le T\), the excess is at most \[ \begin{aligned} &\frac\delta{\sqrt{2048}}+\frac{4\delta}{32}+\frac{2\delta^2}{1024}\\ &\quad\le\frac\delta{32}+\frac\delta8+\frac\delta{512}=\frac{81\delta}{512}<\frac\delta4, \end{aligned} \] using \(\kappa_\mu/(1+\kappa_\mu)\le1\) and \(\delta<1\). Also \(T\le\delta/4\). So the hypothesis (2.1) of Proposition 2.1 of From conditioning to a threshold bound holds, and that proposition gives the rate \[ \begin{aligned} &\frac{\delta T}{16(1+\log d)}=\frac{\delta^3}{c_\mu},\\ &c_\mu=16384(1+\kappa_\mu)(1+\log d) .\qquad\square \end{aligned} \]

4. Exercises

4.1. For the game of Exercise 5.2 of the first lesson, compute the rate of Theorem 2.2.

4.2. Let \(\mu\) be uniform on the pairs \((x,\sigma(x))\) for a bijection \(\sigma\). Show that \(\kappa_\mu=0\) and that (2.1) reads \(\bar s\le v+\sqrt{\log(1/p)/(2m)}\).

4.3. Prove that a simple path in a graph with \(n\) vertices has at most \(n-1\) edges and that each of its vertices lies on at most two of its edges. Deduce the factor \(4(|H_j|-1)\) in the path estimate.

4.4. Show that \(\operatorname{TV}(\widetilde\lambda,\lambda)\le\frac1m\sum_i\operatorname{TV}(P_i,\lambda)\) when \(\widetilde\lambda=\frac1m\sum_iP_i\).

4.5. In the strategy built from \(\Xi_j\), explain why it matters that the weights \(w_{i,r}\) do not depend on the fresh pair.

5. Solutions

4.1. \(d=4\) and \(\kappa_\mu=96\), so the rate is \(\delta^3/(16384\cdot97\cdot(1+\log4))\), about \(\delta^3/(3.8\cdot10^6)\).

4.2. Two different pairs \((x,\sigma(x))\) share no question, so every component is a single pair and \(\kappa_\mu=0\); the terms with \(\kappa_\mu\) vanish.

4.3. The \(n'\le n\) vertices of a simple path are distinct and consecutive vertices are joined, giving \(n'-1\) edges; an inner vertex lies on its two neighbouring edges and an end vertex on one. Along a path \(z_0,\dots,z_\ell\), \[ \begin{aligned} &\|\Psi_{z_0}-\Psi_{z_\ell}\|^2\le\ell\sum_t\|\Psi_{z_t}-\Psi_{z_{t+1}}\|^2\\ &\quad\le\ell\sum_t2(c_{z_t}+c_{z_{t+1}})\le4\ell\sum_ac_a . \end{aligned} \]

4.4. \(\operatorname{TV}\) is a norm distance, \(\frac12\|\cdot\|_1\), so \(\|\frac1m\sum_i(P_i-\lambda)\|_1\le\frac1m\sum_i\|P_i-\lambda\|_1\).

4.5. A strategy for one game must prepare its shared state before the questions are asked. If the weights depended on the fresh pair, \(\Xi_j\) would depend on the questions, and the players could not prepare it.
