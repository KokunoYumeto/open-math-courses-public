Introduction

An action can have a kernel even when its orbits carry no additional stabilizers. Its transformation groupoid retains that kernel as a group of loops at every point. For an ergodic action of a countable abelian group, the kernel is exactly the stabilizer on one invariant conull set. The quotient group acts freely there.

The transformation groupoid also splits measurably into its kernel and its principal orbit relation. This assertion concerns arrows and their endpoints. It does not require a splitting of the original group extension. We will compute the difference for an integer rotation of five points, and for rational rotations of the circle.

The last lesson's Theorem 1.1 proves compatible finite lifts, including their Borelness and exact agreement at earlier stages. Countable abelian groups are amenable by the Haar amenability lesson, Proposition 6.1. Relation Corollary 6.2 then supplies the hyperfinite exhaustion used below. These are proved course prerequisites; the groupoid split is proved here.

There is no Borel transversal meeting each orbit exactly once. If DDD were one, its distinct KKK-translates would form a countably infinite disjoint cover of the circle. Exercise 5.8 (finite classes need not have dyadic size). Level 2. For the five-point pair relation in Section 3, show that no full-unit subrelation can have every class of size two. Explain why compatible lifting still applies to that example, and why an exhaustion whose successive constant class sizes are 2k2^k2k is a stronger requirement than an exhaustion by finite classes.

Solution. A subrelation partitions the five objects. If every class had size two, the total number of objects would be twice the number of classes and hence even, which contradicts five. Since all five points have positive measure, passing to a conull set cannot discard a point. In particular no full-unit stage of class size two exists, and an increasing sequence with all the prescribed class sizes 2k2^k2k cannot exist on this space.

The full five-point relation itself is a finite-class Borel relation; the constant sequence equal to that relation is already a finite exhaustion. Theorem 1.1 of the lifting lesson therefore applies directly. Indeed its least-root construction gives exactly c(j,i)=j−ic(j,i)=j-ic(j,i)=j−i in (3.1). No dyadic-size hypothesis occurs in that proof. Properly ergodic dyadic models and their atomless splitting arguments address another branch; the elementary finite-orbit case must be retained separately when using those source results.
