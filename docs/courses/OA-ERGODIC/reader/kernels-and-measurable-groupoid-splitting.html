Introduction

An action can have a kernel even when its orbits carry no additional stabilizers. Its transformation groupoid retains that kernel as a group of loops at every point. For an ergodic action of a countable abelian group, the kernel is exactly the stabilizer on one invariant conull set. The quotient group acts freely there.

The transformation groupoid also splits measurably into its kernel and its principal orbit relation. This assertion concerns arrows and their endpoints. It does not require a splitting of the original group extension. We will compute the difference for an integer rotation of five points, and for rational rotations of the circle.

The last lesson's Theorem 1.1 proves compatible finite lifts, including their Borelness and exact agreement at earlier stages. Countable abelian groups are amenable by the Haar amenability lesson, Proposition 6.1. Relation Corollary 6.2 then supplies the hyperfinite exhaustion used below. These are proved course prerequisites; the groupoid split is proved here.

There is no Borel transversal meeting each orbit exactly once. If DDD were one, its distinct KKK-translates would form a countably infinite disjoint cover of the circle. Exercise 5.5 (finite roots versus full orbit roots). Level 3. In the rational-rotation example, verify (4.2), prove that every orbit is infinite and null, and explain why compatible finite lifts do not contradict the absence of a Borel transversal.

Solution. The classes of j/n!j/n!j/n!, for 0≤j<n!0\le j<n!0≤j<n!, are distinct, form a subgroup and exhaust KnK_nKn​. Divisibility n!∣(n+1)!n!\mid(n+1)!n!∣(n+1)! gives inclusion. Singletons have Haar measure zero; for example their equal mass would contradict finite total mass if it were positive. Countable additivity makes each full orbit null. A finite-class root can be chosen by a Borel injection because each finite set has a least code. The root of the larger class need not remain the old root. Theorem 1.1 retains the old pair transports by its three-factor extension formula, even while roots change. Every pair eventually has a fixed transport; a single selected point for the entire orbit is never constructed. The transversal contradiction in Section 4 therefore remains valid.

Exercise 5.6 (kernel zero and one-point bases). Level 1. Describe Theorem 2.1 when the action is faithful, and when XXX is one point of positive measure with trivial action. Are the resulting groupoids the same?

Solution. Faithfulness makes H={e}H=\{e\}H={e}. Theorem 1.2 then makes the action free on its conull reduction, and (2.3) identifies its transformation groupoid with its principal orbit relation. For a one-point trivial action, H=GH=GH=G, KKK is trivial and RRR has only the identity pair. The product H×RH\times RH×R is the one-unit group GGG, including every original loop.
