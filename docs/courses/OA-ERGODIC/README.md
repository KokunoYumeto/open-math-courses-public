# Measured relations and operator algebras

This course develops ergodic actions, measured orbit relations, Krieger algebras, diagonal normalizers, groupoid cocycles, isotropy, finite approximation and amenability. Its 57 lessons contain 496 graded exercises with complete solutions and 81 editable mathematical diagrams.

Start with [Orbits, stabilizers, and relation algebras](src/orbits-stabilizers-and-relation-algebras.md), then follow the [HTML reader](reader/index.html). The Markdown lessons can be read on GitHub. For the typeset offline edition, download this directory and open `reader/index.html`; bundled KaTeX styles and fonts require no JavaScript.

The lessons assume measure and integration, functional analysis, standard Borel spaces, Haar measure and von Neumann algebra foundations. Each lesson names its exact additional prerequisites. General modular theory and standard-form implementation belong to the modular course; crossed-product duality and the flow of weights belong to the flow course.

The reading guides connect the proofs, examples and exercises:

- [Actions and factor types](src/type-criteria-for-locally-compact-free-actions.md#reading-from-a-measurable-action-to-its-factor-type): measurable densities, compact models, free diagonals and the four factor types.
- [Orbit algebras and the Krieger construction](src/lifted-relations-and-the-associated-flow.md#reading-the-krieger-construction-from-orbits-to-flow): nonsingular kernels, invariant measures, phase-inclusive normalizers, ratio sets and the associated flow.
- [Measured groupoids and finite approximation](src/balanced-arrays-and-the-hyperfinite-finite-factor.md#reading-finite-approximation-through-to-the-factor): finite-sheet conventions, refining arrays, diagonal generation and matrix chains.
- [Cohomology and factor fields](src/return-arrows-and-factor-field-flows.md#reading-from-finite-corrections-to-flows-and-factor-fields): compatible lifts, subgroup corrections, variable stabilizers and signed flow returns.
- [Amenability](src/haar-averages-and-compact-translation-control.md#proof-dependencies-and-reading-order): discrete means, Haar averaging, closed subgroups and the stated classical structure inputs.
- [Amenability exercises](src/amenable-actions-free-centres-and-isotropy-obstructions.md#three-routes-through-the-exercises): stabilizers, scalar composition defects and finite-dimensional crossed-product constructions.
- [Random operators and isotropy](src/orbit-averaging-and-the-modular-weight-bridge.md#reading-from-transverse-kernels-to-random-operator-centralizers): transverse kernels, Haar product coordinates, averaged commutants, modular orbit averages and strict spectral centralizers.

The [hidden-group lesson](src/principal-groupoids-with-hidden-group-factors.md) studies the precise limits of commuting-copy constructions. It includes complete generating examples with extra commutants, the infinite ambient-trace theorem, normal product-state and expectation obstructions, and the failure of the stabilized one-sided repair. Its general finite-factor theorem excludes a normal expectation from the full operator algebra in every normal unital representation of an infinite-dimensional finite factor, including nonseparable Hilbert spaces.

Its [matrix reduction](src/principal-groupoids-with-hidden-group-factors.md#5h-matrix-coordinates-preserve-the-infinite-trace-obstruction) preserves the two actual finite-factor copies, both relative commutants, generation and the internal involution. The simultaneous matrix coordinates give the whole ambient stable identification; the converse explicitly requires that identification. Every nonzero finite ambient-trace projection in the reduced corner destroys multiplicativity on each reduced copy, as the full positive compression defect shows. Proposition 5H.3 gives the sharp form: the supremum of the unitary compression-defect norms is one in every such corner. Its compact convex proof, two boundary exercises and a second editable diagram specify the exact trace and relative-commutant hypotheses. Three complete boundary exercises and an [editable diagram](src/figures/hidden-factor-matrix-reduction.svg) explain the result. These are restrictions and exact reductions of a hypothetical source pair. The unrestricted existence assertion in Connes's Corollary 11 remains open.

The [factor-quotient criterion](src/principal-groupoids-with-hidden-group-factors.md#5i-testing-an-internal-exchange-in-a-normal-factor-quotient) retains every condition of the two-copy question. A two-by-two algebra detects whether the exchange is internal in the actual coefficient factor. Its centre, both relative commutants and the normal stable ambient isomorphism are distinct tests. The universal binormal algebra and its factor quotients are constructed through normal-marginal GNS representations. Two complete exercises distinguish Hilbert-space implementation from innerness and recover a symmetry from a central projection. The criterion leaves existence of a suitable quotient unresolved.

The [compact-normal quotient proof](src/almost-connected-groups-and-the-solvable-radical.md#1a-compact-normal-quotients-and-connected-solvable-lifts) proves that a compact normal quotient of a connected locally compact group preserves the absence of connected solvable normal subgroups. It gives the full centralizer and component arguments, three worked exercises and an [editable proof diagram](src/figures/compact-normal-centralizer.svg). No countability assumption is imposed. The radical and general Lie approximation are constructed in the same lesson at their explicit Haar and compact-representation prerequisites. The closed rank-one theorem uses the stated root, Lie and geometry inputs.

The [Lie-approximation reductions](src/almost-connected-groups-and-the-solvable-radical.md#1b-lie-approximation-finite-kernels-and-the-countability-reduction) give five complete proofs: finite compact representation kernels, proper compact-kernel quotients, a compact normal kernel with a countable quotient base, direct metrization, and the exact lift from the metrizable case. Two solved exercises explain uncountable compact products and proper open subgroups. These steps impose no countability assumption on the original group. The [metrizable construction](src/almost-connected-groups-and-the-solvable-radical.md#the-metrizable-case-compact-kernels-escape-and-coordinates) proves finite and subgroup trapping, a compact normal kernel with an NSS quotient, weak and strong Gleason metrics, finite-dimensional exponential coordinates and the analytic global group law. Its fifteen proof modules include the regularized bootstrap and all product limits. Four further solved exercises test non-countable atlases, non-locally-connected groups, small torsion and the order of limits. Corollary 1B.21 gives the Lie quotient theorem for arbitrary locally compact Hausdorff groups.

The [solvable-radical construction](src/almost-connected-groups-and-the-solvable-radical.md#1c-constructing-the-closed-solvable-radical) gives nine complete proof units and four solved exercises. The Lie radical is proved closed and algebraically solvable without assuming linearity or a finite centre. One compact Lie quotient then gives a common derived-length bound, which justifies the directed union and closure defining the full locally compact radical. The proof includes nonclosed subgroups and arbitrary compact groups; its Lie-approximation input is Corollary 1B.21 in the same lesson.

The [compact-conjugation and closed rank-one proofs](src/almost-connected-groups-and-the-solvable-radical.md#1d-constructing-a-compact-conjugation-from-root-spaces) construct the conjugation on the actual complex algebra, prove positivity through root strings, and supply a closed split subgroup in the original noncompact semisimple Lie group. The group proof includes nonlinear groups and infinite centres. Five complete worked diagnostics cover root signs, unequal root lengths, generator rescaling, split normalization and the full preimage components. The root-production and geometry prerequisites are explicit.

The [bounded-section proof](src/dense-roof-groups-periods-and-eigenfunctions.md#bounded-sections-on-every-free-orbit) constructs a Borel section meeting every orbit of a free Borel real flow. Orbitwise smoothing, rational levels and a countable greedy selection give consecutive gaps in `[r,3r)`, a Borel successor and an exact suspension chart in the original time, for every `r > 0`. The complete proof, three solved exercises and an [editable diagram](src/figures/all-orbit-bounded-section.svg) require no measure on the flow space. The classical conclusion is credited to Wagh. For two prescribed positive roofs, the programme tiling construction supplies the Borel section, predecessor, successor and original-time suspension chart on every free Borel orbit.

The [topological action exercise](src/borel-carriers-and-uniform-wandering-neighborhoods.md#reading-the-topological-action-exercise) has a completed qualified disposition. Compact extraction is proved under the stated regularity and atomic hypotheses; uniform separation is proved when a positive compact set is supplied, and measurable freeness is proved without Radon regularity. The actual standard atomless counterexample assumes the continuum hypothesis. Existence without CH and a unique intended source countability convention are not claimed.

The course is incomplete. The unrestricted weak-measurable two-copy assertion remains unresolved here. The every-orbit tiling by two prescribed positive rationally independent return lengths is proved in the linked programme construction and used with its exact Borel and real-analysis prerequisites in Theorem 3B.1. The bounded all-orbit section is a separate theorem; the finite displacement, rank and sparse-orbit arguments supply the prescribed tiling. The flow-course constructions are used at their stated scope; the broader continuous-product/book-notation correspondence belongs to that course. Every result retains its stated prerequisites.
