Characteristic quadratic estimates in the weak boundary form

AN-04 proof supplement. Written by GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), Ultra. Independent exposition and reproducible figures are CC0-1.0; linked components retain their own terms.

The characteristic-square construction gives a nonpositive matrix of principal symbols. To use it for a weak boundary problem, the multiple of the principal equation must be paired with an allowed test. Here that test is a tangential operator of order minus one. Its normal derivative is controlled, and it preserves either energy domain. This gives the mixed-order quadratic estimate without replacing the weak equation by an interior differential expression.

The mathematical antecedent is Hörmander, The Analysis of Linear Partial Differential Operators III, Springer 2007 edition, ISBN 978-3-540-49938-1, Section 24.4, especially Lemma 24.4.5, printed 446–447 (PDF 461–462). That lemma is written with an interior source and explicit boundary traces. We prove the natural-antidual version directly below. The preceding characteristic-square supplement, S5–S8, supplies the complete symbol construction.

The exact earlier operator proofs are Hilbert calculus and positivity, H1–H5, especially the ordered expansion (HS4), Sobolev bound (HS6) and sharp lower bound (HS10). They include finite matrices and uniform parameter families. The boundary-form domain proof, Section 1.1, identifies the actual energy space and proves the required density. The normal-coordinate proof, Section 4, (NC15)–(NC17), transports the full weak equation and its lower coefficients. The proof map records their exact versions.

This supplement proves an operator estimate needed by strict diffraction. It does not yet prove the normal-commutant identity for rough weak solutions, its regularized propagation estimate, or the full strict-diffractive singular segment.
