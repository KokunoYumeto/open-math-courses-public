One-dimensional descent and affine concavity

A characteristic curve can contain a whole interval on which its transverse quadratic form vanishes. The correct regularity statement collapses that interval before testing concavity. We prove the statement by adding one real variable, applying the two-dimensional propagation theorem, and returning to the original curve. The auxiliary variable changes neither the distribution's microlocal Sobolev index nor its source index.

The mathematical antecedent is Lars Hörmander, The Analysis of Linear Partial Differential Operators IV, admitted 2009 edition, Theorem 26.9.4 and its descent argument, printed pages 144–145. The compact homogeneous normal form is Proposition 26.4.13, printed pages 101–102. Our argument supplies the tensor-product estimates, homogeneous localization, radial case and passage from local to global concavity explicitly.

We use the exact indefinite factorization and affine geometry, QAF:F0 and T0–A0, homogeneous characteristic tubes, PSG:E0–N2, and reduced-leaf propagation, RLP:T0 and A0. The graph equivalence transports every real Sobolev order. Each dependency is pinned in the proof map.
