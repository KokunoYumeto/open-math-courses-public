Weak local solvability, uniform estimates and finite obstructions

A local existence statement can initially allow a different neighborhood and a different distribution order for every forcing term. This lesson proves that these choices can be made uniform. It also proves that gaining just one derivative in the residual is enough to recover exact solvability subject to finitely many compatibility conditions. The same argument leads to the adjoint estimate used to test microlocal solvability.

The mathematical antecedent is Lars Hörmander, The Analysis of Linear Partial Differential Operators IV, 2009 edition, Section 26.4, Definitions 26.4.1 and 26.4.3, Theorem 26.4.2, Proposition 26.4.4 and Lemma 26.4.5, printed pages 92–95. We retain all five solvability conditions, both directions of graph invariance and the precise weak norm in the adjoint estimate. The geometric companion gives the sign equivalences. The necessity argument uses the uniform adjoint estimate to exclude forbidden sign transitions, including flat transitions.

The earlier test-space companion, TG:B1–B3, supplies complete-metric Baire and the original seminorm topology. The fixed-support companion, KS:S1–S4, supplies actual Hilbert spaces, compact embeddings and dual pairings. The Hilbert companion, CT:T1, proves representation and compact-adjoint facts. The graph lesson, GE:D7–D10, supplies the complete graph calculus. We use these precise results and prove the additional functional-analytic steps below.
