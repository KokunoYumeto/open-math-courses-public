L5.1.8: Lower Darboux integral <= upper integral, with common-refinement proof.

Proposition 5.1.8.

Let f ⁣:[a,b]→Rf \colon [a,b] \to \Rf:[a,b]→R be a bounded function. Let m,M∈Rm, M \in \Rm,M∈R be such that for all x∈[a,b],x \in [a,b]\text{,}x∈[a,b], we have m≤f(x)≤M.m \leq f(x) \leq M\text{.}m≤f(x)≤M. Then
m(b−a)≤∫ab‾f≤∫ab‾f≤M(b−a).\begin{equation*} m(b-a) \leq \underline{\int_a^b} f \leq \overline{\int_a^b} f \leq M(b-a) .\tag{5.2} \end{equation*}m(b−a)≤∫ab​​f≤∫ab​​f≤M(b−a).​(5.2)​

Proof.

By Proposition 5.1.2, for every partition P,P\text{,}P,
m(b−a)≤L(P,f)≤U(P,f)≤M(b−a).\begin{equation*} m(b-a) \leq L(P,f) \leq U(P,f) \leq M(b-a). \end{equation*}m(b−a)≤L(P,f)≤U(P,f)≤M(b−a).​
The inequality m(b−a)≤L(P,f)m(b-a) \leq L(P,f)m(b−a)≤L(P,f) implies m(b−a)≤∫ab‾f.m(b-a) \leq \underline{\int_a^b} f\text{.}m(b−a)≤∫ab​​f. The inequality U(P,f)≤M(b−a)U(P,f) \leq M(b-a)U(P,f)≤M(b−a) implies ∫ab‾f≤M(b−a).\overline{\int_a^b} f \leq M(b-a)\text{.}∫ab​​f≤M(b−a).
The middle inequality in (5.2) is the main point of the proposition. Let P1,P2P_1, P_2P1​,P2​ be partitions of [a,b].[a,b]\text{.}[a,b]. L5.1.10: Integral bound is the just-proved Darboux bound with equal lower/upper integrals, as Definition 5.1.9 specifies.

Proposition 5.1.10.

Let f ⁣:[a,b]→Rf \colon [a,b] \to \Rf:[a,b]→R be a Riemann integrable function. Let m,M∈Rm, M \in \Rm,M∈R be such that m≤f(x)≤Mm \leq f(x) \leq Mm≤f(x)≤M for all x∈[a,b].x \in [a,b]\text{.}x∈[a,b]. Then
m(b−a)≤∫abf≤M(b−a).\begin{equation*} m(b-a) \leq \int_a^b f \leq M(b-a) . \end{equation*}m(b−a)≤∫ab​f≤M(b−a).​

Proof. For an integrable function the lower and upper Darboux integrals are equal to its integral. Substitute this equality in the preceding bound (5.2), proved above. This gives the two asserted inequalities.

L5.1.11: Exact constant integral, proved by coinciding upper/lower bounds.

Example 5.1.11.

We integrate constant functions using Proposition 5.1.8. If f(x)≔cf(x) \coloneqq cf(x):=c for some constant c,c\text{,}c, then we take m=M=c.m = M = c\text{.}m=M=c. In inequality (5.2) all the inequalities must be equalities. Thus fff is integrable on [a,b][a,b][a,b] and ∫abf=c(b−a).\int_a^b f = c(b-a)\text{.}∫ab​f=c(b−a).

L5.1.13: Arbitrarily small Darboux gaps imply integrability.

Proposition 5.1.13.

Let f ⁣:[a,b]→Rf \colon [a,b] \to \Rf:[a,b]→R be a bounded function. Then fff is Riemann integrable if for every ϵ>0,\epsilon > 0\text{,}ϵ>0, there exists a partition PPP of [a,b][a,b][a,b] such that
U(P,f)−L(P,f)<ϵ.\begin{equation*} U(P,f) - L(P,f) < \epsilon . \end{equation*}U(P,f)−L(P,f)<ϵ.​
