Semidefinite division with a nonnegative quotient

A smooth division can have the right remainder and still have a negative quotient. We complete the missing positivity step by proving a flat correction lemma. The correction preserves the exact quotient wherever the divisor changes sign. It also explains why the one-sided remainder cannot generally be removed.

The mathematical antecedent is Lars Hörmander, The Analysis of Linear Partial Differential Operators IV, Springer 2009 eBook, ISBN 978-3-642-00136-9, Proposition 26.5.7, printed 114–115. The proof below supplies the full semidefinite assertion; the preceding lesson supplies its indefinite assertion and the invariant affine construction on the indefinite extension. All exposition and the correction construction here are independently written. Human foundations remain external; the proof map gives exact versions and locators.
