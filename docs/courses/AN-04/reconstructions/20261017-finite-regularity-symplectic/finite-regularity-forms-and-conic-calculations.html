Finite-regularity forms and conic calculations

Hamiltonian identities can be established before choosing Darboux coordinates. This lets us keep the precise differentiability needed for an exterior derivative, distinguish an invertible alternating form from a closed one, and follow the weights in a conic fold chart.

Mathematical source: Lars Hörmander, The Analysis of Linear Partial Differential Operators III, 2007 edition, §21.1, especially Lemma 21.1.5, the homogeneity identities and Example 21.1.11. The exposition and diagram here are independent programme work. The phase-space lesson, prescribed coordinates and folded forms give the complete smooth coordinate theorems.

5. Dilation and a nonclosed rescaling

Here a conic manifold has a smooth positive-dilation action and, around every point, a saturated neighborhood equivariantly diffeomorphic to an open cone in RN∖0\mathbb R^N\setminus0RN∖0. These are the cone charts used for homogeneous coordinate extensions. In such a chart each positive ray meets the unit sphere once, giving its transverse-slice product. On a cotangent bundle the equivalent dilation acts in the covectors while leaving the base fixed.
