Condition Ψ and complex characteristic tubes

A negative value followed by a positive value can be separated by an entire interval of zeros. At such a transition every derivative may vanish. This lesson develops the geometry that keeps these transitions visible under changes of symbol and canonical coordinates. It also constructs one complex normal form along a whole compact characteristic interval.

The mathematical antecedent is Lars Hörmander, The Analysis of Linear Partial Differential Operators IV, 2009 edition, Section 26.4, Definitions 26.4.6 and 26.4.9, Lemmas 26.4.10–26.4.11, Theorem 26.4.12 and Proposition 26.4.13, printed pages 96–102. We prove the closed-set flow step directly, including its Lipschitz version. The analytic nonsolvability theorem 26.4.7 requires a subsequent construction of approximate adjoint solutions; it is not asserted here.

We use the exact coordinate and flow proofs, PC:O0–O2, compact homogeneous tube, TUBE:D0–I3, and smooth complex division, FN:D0–D5. The solvability lesson, LSE:M1 and D0–D2, supplies the precise invariance and estimate used in the analytic necessity argument.
