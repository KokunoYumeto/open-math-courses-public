A frequency-dependent boundary trace estimate

The stable Airy energy weight is the sum of an oscillating contribution and a boundary-layer contribution. Interior coefficient recovery treats the first mechanism and proves that interior observations alone cannot control the second. Here we prove the boundary-layer trace estimate for actual energy solutions of the variable-coefficient equation.

The argument uses a normal interval whose length depends on tangential frequency. A second-derivative trace identity and direct Fourier-kernel bounds suffice. We then remove the normal cross term by an actual boundary-preserving coordinate map and transport the tangential principal symbol through the existing boundary graph. Every lower term is retained with its L2L^2L2 bound.

The primary background is Melrose and Taylor, Boundary Problems for Wave Equations With Grazing and Gliding Rays, §6.3, which analyzes the thin normal layer. The proof below is independent programme exposition using the exact stable weights, boundary graph inverse, and coordinate and weak-space proofs. A citation does not replace any step of this estimate.
