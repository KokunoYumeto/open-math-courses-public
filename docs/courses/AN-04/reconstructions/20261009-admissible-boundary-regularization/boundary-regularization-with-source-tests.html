Boundary regularization with admissible source tests

Original exposition, proofs, exercises and illustration: GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), 9 October 2026. CC0-1.0. The mathematical antecedents are Lars Hörmander, The Analysis of Linear Partial Differential Operators III, corrected 1994 edition, Springer 2007 eBook, Sections 18.3 and 24.7. Its text and figures are not reproduced here.

The Banach-to-normal-graph proof obtains two ordinary normal derivatives from a full local source norm. Here the source is measured only after finitely many boundary operators whose microsupports lie in a prescribed compressed cone. We prove a corresponding ordinary H2H^2H2 bound for a sufficiently negative-order boundary regularization of the solution. The identities keep the full kernels, including their residual terms.

The exact earlier proofs are the resolved kernel and normal-jet calculus, global kernels and all-real quotient bounds, compressed microsupport calculus, common minimal extension and normal recovery, and actual quotient traces. Their transitive Lebl foundation proofs remain external author-edition dependencies.

R9. What this does and does not supply. For a residual AAA, the theorem applies at every required negative boundary order. Moreover each fixed word in DzD_zDz​ and xDxxD_xxDx​, placed to the left of AAA, is still residual. It does not prove arbitrary ordinary normal smoothing: Exercise 3 gives a residual boundary operator for which the third normal derivative grows on a sequence with bounded source and bounded BBB norm. Nor does the construction eliminate the parameter dependence of the finite source tests. The present result removes the need to assume a full local source norm merely to obtain the regularized H2H^2H2 domain. It does not claim the full observation inequality.
