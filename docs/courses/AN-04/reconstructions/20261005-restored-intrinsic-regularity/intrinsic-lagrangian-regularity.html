Recognizing a Lagrangian distribution intrinsically

A phase representation tells us how to produce a singular distribution. An intrinsic definition tells us how to recognize the same object without first choosing a phase. The link is an endpoint regularity condition: applying any sequence of first-order operators whose principal symbols vanish on the Lagrangian leaves the distribution in one fixed Besov space. Near a frequency graph, those operators become frequency derivatives of a symbol.

The exact earlier programme proofs used below are:

  • L2 completeness and compact smooth density, M6–M8, Fourier extension, L1–L3, and dyadic endpoints, B1–B6.
  • Ordinary operator calculus, O0–O6, coordinate and frame transport, T0–T3 and W1–W5, and matrix parametrices and intrinsic localization, K0–K7.
  • The restored phase-space lesson and oscillatory-distribution lesson, together with frequency coordinates, C0–C5, and the fully proved frequency-graph criterion.
  • Prescribed-phase reconstruction, F0–F6, homogeneous symbol transport, H2 and H5–H6, and Sobolev localization and refinement, S0–S5.

The proof map binds each use to its exact source and full dependency chain. These complete programme proofs replace the earlier broad AN-03 references.
