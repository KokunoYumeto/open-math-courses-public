Matrix damping in the boundary commutator estimate

The normal-gauge supplement proves the exact commutator identity on the actual weak graph. This supplement absorbs its complex matrix lower coefficients and a uniform class of regularization errors. It proves a mixed half-order estimate with the incoming term and the full boundary form retained. For Dirichlet data the boundary square has a favorable sign. For Robin data the order-one boundary form remains an explicit term; its control is a further part of strict propagation.

The proof uses the scalar characteristic completion with a positive matrix weight. It does not infer positivity of a block matrix merely from positivity of its matrix-valued polynomial on real normal covariables. Exercise 2 gives a counterexample to that inference.

8. Sources and remaining propagation work

The primary source is Lars Hörmander, The Analysis of Linear Partial Differential Operators III: Pseudo-Differential Operators, Springer 2007, ISBN 978-3-540-49938-1, Section 24.4, especially Lemma 24.4.7, pp. 449–451. Its scalar Dirichlet argument supplies the normal-energy and regularization strategy. The matrix weight, legal matrix test, explicit Robin defect and uniform full-symbol formulation above are independent receiving proofs, with the exact earlier programme arguments linked in the proof map.

MD17 proves the uniform estimate with its actual incoming and boundary contributions. To conclude the full weak strict-propagation theorem, the remaining work must control the homogeneous Robin form, prove the incoming estimate from the earlier interior/reflection results, bound the retained cutoff-source pairings, identify the mixed gain with the energy-b front, and pass to higher-order supported limits on one common smooth neighborhood.
