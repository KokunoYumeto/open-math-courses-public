Fourier–Airy wavefronts and physical time

The distributional kernel theorem defines the normalized Airy operators on every compact distribution. We now locate their singularities, including the limiting tangential singularities at the boundary. We then identify the sign that carries boundary singularities forward in the actual time coordinate. This supplies the smooth-past assertion needed to use transposed boundary comparison.

Melrose–Taylor, Boundary Problems for Wave Equations With Grazing and Gliding Rays, Section 6.5, is the primary antecedent for the wavefront analysis. The proof below uses the exact phases, amplitudes and Airy estimates already established in this programme and derives the time sign in our convention. No general symbolic calculus with frequency gain smaller than base loss is assumed.
