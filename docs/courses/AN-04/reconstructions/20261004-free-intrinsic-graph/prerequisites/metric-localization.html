6. Compact comparison and the local topology

At any fixed point XXX, (1)–(2) give a neighborhood on which the metric is comparable to gXg_XgX​ and the weight is bounded above and bounded away from zero. A finite subcover gives such bounds on a compact set. In fixed coordinates this means that convergence in all the seminorms (3) implies convergence of every derivative uniformly on compact sets.

In particular every compact smooth function is in S(m,g)S(m,g)S(m,g): its derivatives are bounded on its support, where the metric and weight have the stated bounds; all derivatives vanish off the support. On smooth functions the local topology is metrized by

∑j≥12−jmin⁡(1,max⁡∣α∣≤j, ∣X∣≤j∣∂α(a−b)(X)∣).(ML1) \sum_{j\geq1}2^{-j}\min\left(1, \max_{|\alpha|\leq j,\ |X|\leq j} |\partial^\alpha(a-b)(X)|\right). \tag{ML1} j≥1∑​2−jmin(1,∣α∣≤j, ∣X∣≤jmax​∣∂α(a−b)(X)∣).(ML1)

Convergence in every displayed seminorm implies convergence in this metric: choose a finite initial sum with arbitrarily small remaining tail, then make each of its terms small. Conversely its jjj-th term tending to zero forces that seminorm to tend to zero. The triangle inequality follows from min⁡(1,s+t)≤min⁡(1,s)+min⁡(1,t)\min(1,s+t)\leq\min(1,s)+\min(1,t)min(1,s+t)≤min(1,s)+min(1,t).
