Adaptive scales and a principal-type solution estimate

The transverse symbol can be small, have one sign, or factor through a simple zero. One phase-space scale detects these alternatives and lets their different estimates be summed with uniform constants. We construct that scale, prove the factorization with all derivative bounds, and obtain the full small-time solution estimate. The derivative energy retained in the factorized regions is the input needed for the subsequent localized argument.

The antecedents are Lars Hörmander, The Analysis of Linear Partial Differential Operators IV, admitted 2009 edition, Lemma 26.10.2, Proposition 26.10.3 and estimate (26.10.16), printed pages 150–155. This lesson supplies independent proofs and exact connections to the earlier metric calculus, scalar positivity and Nirenberg–Treves estimates. Localized energy and quasiconcavity supplies the measured estimate, canonical barrier, shell test and exact Sobolev-index bootstrap.

The proof map records the complete receiving chain. The Hilbert-valued calculus MP:B1 and MP:B7 controls the countably many localizations without a dimension-dependent coefficient constant. The scalar positivity proof MP:F2–F5 bounds the negative quantization error. The complete Nirenberg–Treves estimates, NTO:E1 and NTO:E3, give both the solution bound and the retained energy.
