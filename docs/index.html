Thompson's group F and amenability

OpenAI's proof (2026) that Thompson's group F is not amenable, answering Geoghegan's 1979 question. The course develops F through dyadic partitions of the unit interval: normalized restrictions, images of fine uniform partitions, elements moving pairs of separated dyadic intervals, and covariance. It constructs an explicit Lipschitz map of the closed unit ball of a Hilbert space into its unit sphere that moves every point by at least 1/2, through a curve with a bounded, uniformly separated tail. It then colors dyadic partitions recursively with this map and proves that one finite set of elements of F has a uniformly large relative boundary on every finite subset, so that F has no Følner sets.

The hyperinvariant subspace problem

OpenAI's negative answer (2026) to the hyperinvariant subspace problem: on every infinite-dimensional separable complex Hilbert space there is a nonzero operator S with ‖S^n‖^{1/n} → 0 whose commutant is a proper, weakly closed, unital, transitive algebra, so S has no hyperinvariant subspace other than 0 and the whole space. The course proves Domar's theorem that a bilateral weighted shift with regular, quadratically decaying logarithmic weights has only the tails as invariant subspaces; builds a measurable field of such shifts over the 2-adic integers with the odometer and proves the estimate ‖S^n‖ ≤ exp(−10⌊(n+1)²/4⌋); shows that operators commuting with the scalar multipliers are fields of operators; and constructs a contraction commuting with S that moves every tail one step backward, so that a hyperinvariant subspace would carry a strictly decreasing label along the odometer, which a probability space does not allow.

Isomorphisms of free group factors

OpenAI's theorem (2026) that the free group factors L(F_n), 2 ≤ n < ∞, are all isomorphic. The first part proves OpenAI's rank step L(F_n) ≅ L(F_{n+1}) for n ≥ 3: free independence and freely generating Haar tuples, a bounded logarithm of the extra generator and a norm bound for free sums that does not depend on the number of summands; trace-preserving polynomial flows with displacement bounds; a small cocycle with a prescribed word value, through free prefixes, Catalan moments and cutoff inversion; and a limit with adaptive error budgets whose tuple generates the whole factor. The second part proves Voiculescu's corner formula, that the corner of L(F_n) cut down by a projection of trace ½ is L(F_{4n−3}), and deduces L(F_2) ≅ L(F_3): the semicircle law, a freeness criterion on the full Fock space, free semicircular families, 2×2 matrices with semicircular and circular entries that are free from matrices over the other variables, and the polar decomposition of a circular element. The third part extends the corner formula to every trace 1/k, with k×k semicircular matrices, and shows that every positive rational number lies in the fundamental group of L(F_2).

Relative bicentralizers and core commutants
